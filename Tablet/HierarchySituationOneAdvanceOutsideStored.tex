\begin{lemma}[Situation 7.1: advance an unstored common color]
Fix a replacement presentation \(P\) and an index
\(1\leq i<\ell-1\).  Suppose a color \(\alpha\notin D_{n,q}\) is missing,
under the coloring of \(P\), at both exact vertices \(y_i\) and \(y_\ell\).
Assume \(\noderef{HierarchyPathNumber}=1\) and the explicit
palette-surplus consequence that every vertex has a missing color under
every proper exactly-one-uncolored replacement coloring.  Then
\[
 i+1\in\noderef{HierarchyEligibleCommonMissingIndices}.
\]
Thus the paper's replacement family contains a member having a color
missing at both \(y_{i+1}\) and \(y_\ell\).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Write \(\mu\) for \(P.\mathit{coloring}\), \(H\) for its hierarchy, and
\(q\) for its last stored index.  Abbreviate
\(P.\mathit{corresponding}.\mathit{replacementETT}\) by
\(\mathcal E_P\), let \(T_n\) be its terminal ladder tree, let
\(D_n=\noderef{TashkinovStoredColors}(\mathcal E_P,n)\), and put
\[
 A=\noderef{HierarchyStarredStage}(H,q),\quad
 \ell=\noderef{HierarchyFullTailLength}(C),\quad
 B=T(y_{\ell-1}),\quad X=T(y_{i+1}),\quad X^-=T(y_i),          \tag{1}
\]
where the last three trees are the corresponding
\noderef{HierarchyFullTailPrefix}es.  Unpacking
\(P.\mathit{sameGood}\) in
\noderef{HierarchyReplacementPresentation} and
\noderef{SameGoodHierarchy} identifies the original and replacement base
cores, divider counts, ordered stages, stored sets, and Gamma pairs.  In
particular it identifies the two last starred stages and hence the
full-tail lengths and every exact prefix in (1).

We derive the prefix chain from the actual hierarchy fields.  The
\(\mathit{baseStage}\) field of \noderef{TashkinovHierarchy} identifies
stage zero with \(T_n\).  Each \(\mathit{nested}\) field is an entrywise
\noderef{TreeSequenceInitialSegment}, each corresponding
\(\mathit{strict}\) field makes that inclusion proper, and
\(\mathit{terminalStage}\) identifies the last stage with \(T\).  If
\(q>0\), the positive branch of \noderef{HierarchyStarredStage} identifies
\(A\) with the last ordinary stage, so the final nested and strict fields
give \(A\prec T\).  If \(q=0\), the zero branch identifies \(A\) with the
base core, and \(\mathit{coreInsideFirst}\), followed by the nested chain
and \(\mathit{terminalStage}\), gives \(A\preccurlyeq T\).  The bounds
\(1\le i<\ell-1\), together with the definitions of
\noderef{HierarchyFullTailLength} and
\noderef{HierarchyFullTailPrefix}, now give
\[
 A\prec X^-\prec X\preccurlyeq B\prec T,\qquad
 |X|=|X^-|+1.                                                  \tag{2}
\]
Every relation in (2) retains each displayed vertex and individual edge
identity by \noderef{TreeSequenceInitialSegment}.  The same stage
identities transport the assumed path-number-one equality to \(H\), since
\noderef{HierarchyPathNumber} is defined only from the last starred stage,
the exact tail entries, and their endpoint pairs.  Thus each edge after
the first tail edge has the consecutive endpoints specified by that
definition.

Apply \noderef{HierarchyCounterexamplePenultimateElementary} to the six
displayed fields of \(P.\mathit{minimumContext}\): properness,
exactly-one-uncoloredness, the lower-rung package, fewer-divider induction,
lexicographic minimality, and the large missing-set estimate.  Its exact
conclusion is that \(\noderef{TreeSequenceVertexSet}(B)\) is
\noderef{ColoredVertexSetElementary} under \(\mu\).  The first two context
fields also say that \(\mu\) is \noderef{IsProperEdgeColoring} and leaves
exactly \(e_0\) uncolored in the sense of
\noderef{IsOneEdgeUncolored}.  By the palette-surplus hypothesis choose
\[
             \theta\in\noderef{MissingColors}(G,\mu,y_{i+1}). \tag{3}
\]
The exact entries \(y_i\) and \(y_{i+1}\) are distinct by the
\(\mathit{vertex\_injective}\) field of \noderef{TreeSequence}.  Since
both lie in the elementary set \(V(B)\), its defining uniqueness property,
applied also to the given \(\alpha\)-missing witness at \(y_i\), proves
\(\alpha\ne\theta\).  It further proves that no other vertex of \(B\) is
missing \(\alpha\), and no other vertex of \(B\) is missing \(\theta\).
Consequently \(y_i\) and \(y_{i+1}\) are respectively the exact
\noderef{HierarchyFirstMissingPosition}s of \(\alpha\) and \(\theta\).
Because \(A\prec X^-\) in (2), neither color is missing on \(A\): every
vertex of \(A\) is a different earlier entry of the elementary tree
\(B\).

We record the stored-set calculation that will be used in both branches.
Unfolding \noderef{HierarchyStoredColorsAt} at the last index gives the
literal finite-set identity
\[
 D_{n,q}=D_n\setminus\noderef{TreeMissingColors}(\mu,A).       \tag{4}
\]
Thus, because \(\alpha\) is not missing on \(A\), the hypothesis
\(\alpha\notin D_{n,q}\) implies \(\alpha\notin D_n\).  If
\(\theta\notin D_{n,q}\), the same argument gives
\(\theta\notin D_n\); if \(\theta\in D_{n,q}\), (4) instead gives both
\(\theta\in D_n\) and \(\theta\notin\noderef{TreeMissingColors}(\mu,A)\).

Apply \noderef{HierarchyTailMissingColorExclusion} at the two exact first
missing vertices.  At \(y_i\) it excludes \(\alpha\) from the earlier
displayed tail, and at \(y_{i+1}\) it excludes \(\theta\), in each case
retaining only the theorem's named possibility: at divider zero the first
tail edge can be the certified SE/RE seed carrying a color in \(D_n\).
Path number one and missingness of \(\alpha\) at \(y_i\) also exclude
\(\alpha\) from the single edge between \(X^-\) and \(X\).  Hence
\(\alpha\) is absent from the exact interval from its first missing
position through the first \(\theta\)-missing position, with only that
individual seed exception still to be checked.                         \(\tag{5}\)

First suppose \(\theta\notin D_{n,q}\).  Equation (4) gives
\(\alpha,\theta\notin D_n\), so neither affected color can be the color
of the possible SE/RE seed in (5).  Thus the exception is eliminated as
an exact edge identity, not ignored.  For \(q>0\), the two colors are
disjoint from \(D_{n,q}\); for \(q=0\), they are disjoint from \(D_n\).
Together with the order in (2), the two first-missing witnesses, and the
exact unused interval (5), these are the corresponding Case 1 or Case 2
hypotheses of \noderef{ChangeLemma}.  It follows that the full
\((\alpha,\theta)\)-component through \(y_i\) equals the one through
\(y_{i+1}\).                                                        \(\tag{6}\)

The component through \(y_\ell\) differs from (6).  Indeed
\(y_i,y_{i+1},y_\ell\) are three distinct exact displayed vertices by
(2) and vertex injectivity in \noderef{TreeSequence}.  They are missing,
respectively, \(\alpha,\theta,\alpha\); after reversing the two color
arguments for the \(\alpha\)-missing vertices, the literal symmetry of
\noderef{TwoColorIncidenceGraph} and
\noderef{MissingColorIncidenceEndpoint} give incidence degree at most one
at all three nodes.  If they lay in one component,
\noderef{TwoColorComponentSpanningPath} would span it by a simple path,
while \noderef{TwoColorIncidenceDegreeAtMostTwo} says that only the two
path ends can have full degree at most one.  This is impossible.
\noderef{TwoColorComponentsDisjoint} therefore makes the terminal
component disjoint from (6).                                         \(\tag{7}\)

Let \(Q\) be that terminal component and define
\[
 \nu=\noderef{TwoColorComponentSwap}
       (G,\mu,\alpha,\theta,\operatorname{inl}y_\ell).         \tag{8}
\]
The missing endpoint and \noderef{MissingColorIncidenceEndpoint} make
\(Q\) a possibly singleton \noderef{TwoColorPathComponent}.
\noderef{TwoColorComponentSwapProper} makes \(\nu\) proper; unfolding the
swap shows that \(\mathsf{none}\) is fixed, so exactly \(e_0\) remains
uncolored.  Apply \noderef{StableChangeCaseThree} to the current ETT,
base, counterexample, the six fields of
\(P.\mathit{minimumContext}\), the two first-missing colors, (2), (5),
(7), and (8).  All vertices of \(A\) precede \(y_i\), the two affected
colors lie outside \(D_{n,q}\), and the divider-zero seed has just been
excluded using (4).  The theorem returns a full actual
\noderef{CorrespondingExtendedTashkinovTree}, maximum property, a rebuilt
base, a good hierarchy, and \noderef{SameGoodHierarchy} data.

Disjointness (7) preserves \(\theta\)-missingness at \(y_{i+1}\).  The
swap makes \(\theta\) missing at \(y_\ell\): if it was already missing,
it remains so, and otherwise properness supplies its unique incident
\(\theta\)-edge, which lies in \(Q\) and is changed to \(\alpha\).  The
two distinct witnesses make the returned terminal tree non-elementary,
so the returned hierarchy data form a
\noderef{HierarchyCounterexample}.

The returned correspondence is relative to
\(P.\mathit{corresponding}.\mathit{replacementETT}\).  Compose it with
\(P.\mathit{corresponding}\) field by field: compose the rung equalities;
at each positive ladder index compose the two ladder equalities with the
heterogeneous series presentation; and at each round-plus-one index do the
same for stored sets, connecting-edge sets, and extension kinds.  Take the
new final generating coloring, properness, and one-uncoloredness from the
returned certificate.  At every positive ladder tree, compose the two
\noderef{StableColoring} relations using \noderef{sc2}; the required tree
and protected-set equalities follow from the preceding fields and the
definition of \noderef{TashkinovStoredColors}, while properness follows
from the corresponding positive \noderef{TashkinovSeriesStep}s.  This is
an original-relative correspondence.

Compose the two same-good witnesses field by field in
\noderef{SameHierarchyStages}, the stored sets, and the Gamma pairs.  The
composite correspondence transports the lower-rung package and the large
missing-set estimate; the same-good composite transports divider count,
stages, fewer-divider induction, \noderef{HierarchyCounterexampleMeasure},
and lexicographic minimality.  With the new properness and
one-uncoloredness these are exactly all fields of
\noderef{HierarchyMinimumCounterexampleContext}.  They define a genuine
replacement presentation \(P'\) relative to the original ETT.  The given
bounds imply \(1\le i+1\le\ell-1\), so
\(i+1,P',\theta\) witnesses the required membership in
\noderef{HierarchyEligibleCommonMissingIndices}.  This completes the
\(\theta\notin D_{n,q}\) branch.

Assume now that \(\theta\in D_{n,q}\).  The \(\mathit{twoColors}\) field
of \noderef{GoodHierarchy} makes
\(\Gamma^q_\theta\) a two-element set; choose
\(\gamma_1\in\Gamma^q_\theta\).  The \(\mathit{avoidance}\) field, with
\noderef{HierarchySegmentColors}, puts \(\gamma_1\) in
\noderef{HierarchyStageMissingColors}\((H,q)\) and excludes it from every
exact displayed edge between \(A\) and the first \(\theta\)-missing
vertex \(y_{i+1}\).  If \(q>0\), this stage-missing witness lies on the
last ordinary stage \(A\).  If \(q=0\), it lies on \(T_n\), which is an
exact prefix of \(A\): in PE this is the \(\mathit{leftSegment}\) field of
the \noderef{OrderedTreeUnion} furnished by the
\(\mathit{parallel}\) field of \noderef{HierarchyBase}, and in SE/RE it
is equality by \(\mathit{nonparallel}\).  Thus in every case
\(\gamma_1\in\noderef{TreeMissingColors}(\mu,A)\), and (4) gives
\(\gamma_1\notin D_{n,q}\).  Conversely \(\alpha\) is not missing on
\(A\), so no Gamma pair can contain \(\alpha\), because every such pair
is contained in the corresponding stage-missing set.

Let \(v_\gamma\) be the first exact vertex missing \(\gamma_1\).  It lies
in the stage just described and hence in \(A\).  The order
\(v_\gamma\prec y_i\), the two missing witnesses, Gamma avoidance through
\(y_i\), and the stage-missing alternative give the relevant Case 1 or
Case 2 of \noderef{ChangeLemma} for the ordered colors
\((\gamma_1,\alpha)\).  Therefore the full component through
\(v_\gamma\) equals the one through \(y_i\).                       \(\tag{9}\)
The terminal component through \(y_\ell\) is disjoint from (9).
The vertices \(v_\gamma,y_i,y_\ell\) are three distinct exact entries:
the first is in \(A\), the second and third are at the strictly later
positions in (2), and their distinctness as graph vertices follows from
\(\mathit{vertex\_injective}\) in \noderef{TreeSequence}.  Each is a
missing endpoint for the affected colors, hence has incidence degree at
most one by \noderef{MissingColorIncidenceEndpoint}.  The spanning-path
and degree-two argument used for (7) rules out three such vertices in one
component, and \noderef{TwoColorComponentsDisjoint} gives the claimed
disjointness.                                                        \(\tag{10}\)

Swap that terminal component and define
\[
 \nu=\noderef{TwoColorComponentSwap}
       (G,\mu,\gamma_1,\alpha,\operatorname{inl}y_\ell).       \tag{11}
\]
It is proper by \noderef{TwoColorComponentSwapProper} and remains
exactly-one-uncolored because the swap fixes \(\mathsf{none}\).  Apply
\noderef{StableChangeCasesOneTwo} with ordered colors
\((\gamma_1,\alpha)\), the witnesses in \(B\), the component equality
(9), disjointness (10), and the stage-missing Cases 1/2 alternative.  It
gives
\[
 \noderef{HierarchyWeaklyStable}(H,q,\nu),\qquad
 \noderef{ModColoring}(\mathcal E_P,\nu).                    \tag{12}
\]

We next verify the first split-tail inputs at \(X\), without claiming
anything about edge values after \(X\).  Gamma avoidance fixes every
\(\gamma_1\)-candidate edge in \(X-A\).  The tail exclusion for
\(\alpha\) fixes every \(\alpha\)-candidate through \(X^-\); its possible
seed is in fact impossible because (4) already gave
\(\alpha\notin D_n\).  The remaining edge of \(X-X^-\) is incident with
the \(\alpha\)-missing vertex \(y_i\) by path number one, and therefore
cannot have value \(\alpha\) by \noderef{MissingColors}.  Hence
\[
 \nu(T.\mathit{edge}(r))=\mu(T.\mathit{edge}(r))
                       \quad(|A|\le r<|X|).                   \tag{13}
\]

For every \(v\in V(B)\), the full missing set is unchanged.  If
\(\operatorname{inl}v\) is outside the selected component, no incident
edge changes.  If it is inside, (10) makes it different from
\(v_\gamma\) and \(y_i\).  Elementarity of \(B\), applied to the missing
witnesses at those two vertices, makes both \(\gamma_1\) and \(\alpha\)
present at \(v\).  Expanding \noderef{PresentColors}, choose their
incident edge identities; properness makes each identity unique, and
both edge-nodes lie in the selected component.  The swap (11) transposes
their two values together and fixes every other color.  Thus
\[
 \noderef{MissingColors}(G,\nu,v)=
 \noderef{MissingColors}(G,\mu,v)\qquad(v\in V(B)).            \tag{14}
\]

We now extract and factor the actual certified terminal route.  The last
positive \noderef{TashkinovSeriesStep} in the
\noderef{TashkinovSeries} of \(\mathcal E_P\) has one of the following
exact forms.  In PE its closure field is a
\noderef{TashkinovClosure}, hence a
\(\mu\)-\noderef{TashkinovAugmentationSequence} from \(T_n\) to the final
ladder tree.  In SE/RE it supplies an individual edge \(f_n\), the literal
singleton connecting-edge equality, an exact
\noderef{TreeSequenceAdjoinEdge} seed \(S=T_n+f_n\), and the route from
that seed.  The \(\mathit{belowFinal}\) field of
\noderef{ExtendedTashkinovTree} makes \(T\) an exact prefix of that final
ladder tree.

Here are all source inclusions needed to restrict this route.  In PE with
\(q>0\), \(\mathit{baseStage}\) and the iterated \(\mathit{nested}\)
fields of \noderef{TashkinovHierarchy} give
\(T_n\preccurlyeq A\preccurlyeq X\).  In PE with \(q=0\), the
\(\mathit{parallel}\) field of \noderef{HierarchyBase} gives a closure
\(R_n\) and a witness
\[
              U:\noderef{OrderedTreeUnion}(T_n,R_n,A);
\]
then \(U.\mathit{leftSegment}\) gives
\(T_n\preccurlyeq A\preccurlyeq X\).  In SE/RE with \(q=0\),
\(\mathit{nonparallel}\) gives \(A=T_n\); the exact seed has length
\(|A|+1\), and \(i\ge1\) together with (2) places it inside \(X\).

Finally suppose SE/RE and \(q>0\).  The base-stage identity says that
stage zero is \(T_n\); the first nested and strict fields make stage one
an exact extension of length at least \(|T_n|+1\).  The seed has exactly
that latter length.  Both seed and stage one are exact prefixes of the
same final ladder tree: the seed by its certified closure route, and stage
one by the iterated nested fields, \(\mathit{terminalStage}\), and then
\(\mathit{belowFinal}\).  Comparing their entrywise prefixes with
\noderef{TreeSequenceInitialSegment} therefore gives
\[
 T_n+f_n\preccurlyeq H.\mathit{stage}(1)
             \preccurlyeq A\preccurlyeq X.                   \tag{15}
\]
Thus \noderef{TashkinovAugmentationSequenceRestrict} first restricts the
certified route to \(T\), and then to \(X\), in every terminal-kind case.

For completeness, factor the same finite chain rather than merely naming
a suffix.  Unfold
\noderef{TashkinovAugmentationSequence} as a reflexive-transitive chain
and induct on its constructors.  The source is a prefix of \(X\) by the
case analysis above, \(X\preccurlyeq T\) by (2), and each
\noderef{TashkinovAugmentation} increases length by exactly one and makes
its predecessor an exact prefix of its successor.  At the reflexive case
the desired member is the source when its length is \(|X|\).  At a tail
step, compare the successor length with \(|X|\): if it is smaller, apply
the induction hypothesis to the remaining chain; if it equals \(|X|\),
the successor and \(X\) are exact prefixes of the common endpoint \(T\)
of equal length and hence are equal entry by entry; it cannot exceed
\(|X|\) before equality because lengths rise one at a time from a source
of length at most \(|X|\) to \(T\) of length at least \(|X|\).  Splitting
the reflexive-transitive chain at that unique member yields both the
restricted old route to \(X\) and an old-color suffix
\[
                              X\longrightarrow T.             \tag{16}
\]

Apply \noderef{TashkinovAugmentationSequenceColoringTransport} to the old
route \(S\to X\), where \(S=T_n\) in PE and is the exact one-edge seed in
SE/RE, using \(X^-\) as the penultimate prefix.  Equation (14) and
\(X^-\preccurlyeq B\) give its pointwise missing-set premise.  Equation
(13) fixes its added edges at positions at least \(|A|\).  It remains to
check every earlier route-added edge.

If \(q>0\), the \noderef{TreeColoringInvariant} part of (12) fixes every
displayed edge of \(A\).  If \(q=0\) and the kind is SE/RE, then
\(A=T_n\) by \noderef{HierarchyBase} and the route begins at the one-edge
seed, so no route-added edge lies below that source.  Suppose \(q=0\) and
the kind is PE.  Then (12) unfolds to
\noderef{CombinedStableColoring} on \(T_n\) and the closure \(R_n\) from
the parallel base.  Applying
\noderef{CombinedStableClosurePreserved} fixes every displayed edge
identity of \(T_n\) and every displayed edge identity of \(R_n\).
For an arbitrary position \(r<|A|\), if \(r<|T_n|\), the
\(\mathit{leftSegment}\) field of the displayed
\(U:\noderef{OrderedTreeUnion}(T_n,R_n,A)\) identifies
\(A.\mathit{edge}(r)\) with \(T_n.\mathit{edge}(r)\).  If
\(|T_n|\le r\), \(U.\mathit{newEntryFromRight}\) supplies an exact
\(s<|R_n|\) with
\(A.\mathit{edge}(r)=R_n.\mathit{edge}(s)\).  The two conclusions of
\noderef{CombinedStableClosurePreserved} therefore fix every individual
displayed edge of \(A\), not merely the edges of its two vertex sets.
Together with (13), this proves the full fixed-edge premise of the
transport theorem.  It returns the exact \(\nu\)-route
\[
 \begin{cases}T_n\longrightarrow X,&\text{PE},\\
 T_n+f_n\longrightarrow X,&\text{SE/RE},\end{cases}           \tag{17}
\]
retaining the exact seed and singleton connecting-edge data.

Both affected colors remain in \noderef{TreeMissingColors} of \(X\):
their witnesses \(v_\gamma,y_i\) lie in \(X\), and their common component
(9) is disjoint from the changed component.  We next verify the two
universal bookkeeping premises of
\noderef{StableChangeCasesOneTwoSplitTail}.  Fix an arbitrary
\(\eta\in D_{n,q}=D_n\setminus\noderef{TreeMissingColors}(\mu,A)\).
The affected color \(\gamma_1\) is excluded from this set because it is
missing on \(A\), and \(\alpha\) is excluded by hypothesis.  Thus
\(\eta\) differs from both affected colors.  Unfolding the component swap
shows that the missing predicate for \(\eta\) is unchanged at every
vertex of \(T\), so its totalized first missing position is unchanged.

For the Gamma cutoff, continue with arbitrary stored \(\eta\).  If
\(\alpha\in\Gamma^q_\eta\), the avoidance field would make \(\alpha\)
stage-missing and hence missing on \(A\), contrary to the conclusion
above.  If \(\gamma_1\in\Gamma^q_\eta\), then also
\(\gamma_1\in\Gamma^q_\theta\).  Were \(\eta\ne\theta\), the
\(\mathit{disjointPairs}\) field of \noderef{GoodHierarchy} would make
these two pairs disjoint, contradicting that common member.  Hence
\(\eta=\theta\), whose first missing position is exactly \(y_{i+1}\),
the last vertex of \(X\).  This proves the cutoff for every stored
\(\eta\), not only for the displayed pair.

Equations (2), (9)--(17), the two split missing witnesses, the universal
stored-position equality, and the universal Gamma cutoff supply every
hypothesis of \noderef{StableChangeCasesOneTwoSplitTail}.  Apply it at
\(X\).  It transports the suffix (16), rather than asserting fixed values
after \(X\), and returns an actual full corresponding ETT, maximum
property, rebuilt base, hierarchy, good hierarchy, and same-good data.
The swap makes \(\gamma_1\) missing at \(y_\ell\) and preserves its stage
witness, so these outputs form a counterexample.

Construct an original-relative replacement presentation \(P_1\) exactly
as follows.  Compose the returned correspondence with
\(P.\mathit{corresponding}\) by the rung, ladder-tree, stored-set,
connecting-edge, extension-kind, and generating-coloring fields.  Compose
the stable-coloring fields at every positive ladder index by
\noderef{sc2}.  Compose \(P.\mathit{sameGood}\) with the returned
same-good witness in \noderef{SameHierarchyStages}, stored sets, and
Gamma pairs.  These composites transport the lower-rung and large-set
fields, the divider count and stages, fewer-divider induction, and
\noderef{HierarchyCounterexampleMeasure}; with the new properness and
one-uncoloredness they reconstruct every field of
\noderef{HierarchyMinimumCounterexampleContext}.  This is the required
\(P_1\).

We now perform the second swap under \(P_1\), repeating each route check
for that intermediate presentation.  Let \(H_1\), \(\mathcal B_1\),
\(T_n^1\), and \(D_n^1\) be its hierarchy, base, terminal ladder tree,
and stored set.  Its same-good data identify its last starred stage with
the same exact tree \(A\), its stored set at \(q\) with \(D_{n,q}\), and
its last Gamma pairs with the displayed old pairs.  Under its coloring
\(\nu\), \(\theta\) is still missing first at \(y_{i+1}\), while
\(\gamma_1\) is missing at its earlier stage witness and at \(y_\ell\).
The new good hierarchy puts \(\gamma_1\) in its stage-missing set and
keeps its avoidance through \(X\); the defining equality
\[
 D^1_{n,q}=D_n^1\setminus
       \noderef{TreeMissingColors}(\nu,A)                     \tag{18}
\]
from \noderef{HierarchyStoredColorsAt} shows that \(\theta\), which is
stored, is not missing on \(A\).

Apply \noderef{ChangeLemma} under \(\nu\) to the ordered colors
\((\gamma_1,\theta)\).  Stage missingness and avoidance identify the
component through the earlier \(\gamma_1\)-missing witness with the
component through \(y_{i+1}\).  The terminal component through
\(y_\ell\) is disjoint.  Indeed the stage witness, \(y_{i+1}\), and
\(y_\ell\) occupy three distinct exact positions, and vertex injectivity
in \noderef{TreeSequence} makes them distinct graph vertices; they are
three degree-at-most-one missing endpoints, contradicting
\noderef{TwoColorComponentSpanningPath} and
\noderef{TwoColorIncidenceDegreeAtMostTwo} if they belonged to one
component.  Use \noderef{TwoColorComponentsDisjoint} to obtain the stated
disjointness.                                                        \(\tag{19}\)

Swap the terminal component, call the resulting coloring \(\rho\), and
apply \noderef{StableChangeCasesOneTwo} with \(\gamma_1\) as its
stage-missing Cases 1/2 color.  It gives
\noderef{HierarchyWeaklyStable} from \(\nu\) to \(\rho\) and
\noderef{ModColoring} for the intermediate ETT.  Gamma avoidance excludes
\(\gamma_1\) on every displayed edge of \(X-A\).
\noderef{HierarchyTailMissingColorExclusion} excludes \(\theta\) through
its first missing position \(y_{i+1}\), except possibly the exact
divider-zero SE/RE seed.  If that seed carries \(\theta\in D_n^1\), the
actual \noderef{TashkinovSeriesStep} is SE or RE;
\noderef{MaximumDefectiveChoice} or
\noderef{RevisitingExtensionCertificate} makes it a
\noderef{TreeIncidentEdge} of \(T_n^1\), and the protected-edge clause of
the returned \noderef{StableColoring} fixes it.  Hence
\[
 \rho(T.\mathit{edge}(r))=\nu(T.\mathit{edge}(r))
                       \quad(|A|\le r<|X|).                   \tag{20}
\]

For each \(v\in V(B)\), split according as its vertex-node lies outside
or inside the second selected component.  Outside, no incident edge
changes.  Inside, disjointness (19) makes \(v\) different from the stage
witness and \(y_{i+1}\).  Elementarity of \(B\) makes both
\(\gamma_1\) and \(\theta\) present at \(v\); properness gives their two
unique incident edge identities, and the swap transposes both together.
Every other color is fixed.  Therefore
\[
 \noderef{MissingColors}(G,\rho,v)=
 \noderef{MissingColors}(G,\nu,v)\qquad(v\in V(B)).            \tag{21}
\]

Extract the second old-color route from the actual last certificate of
\(P_1\)'s \noderef{TashkinovSeries}.  In PE it is the closure route from
\(T_n^1\); in SE/RE it is the route from the exact seed
\(T_n^1+f_n^1\), with its literal singleton connecting-edge equality and
\noderef{TreeSequenceAdjoinEdge} certificate.  The
\(\mathit{belowFinal}\) field of the intermediate
\noderef{ExtendedTashkinovTree} places \(T\) in the final closure.
For PE and \(q>0\), \(H_1.\mathit{baseStage}\) and the iterated
\(H_1.\mathit{nested}\) fields give
\(T_n^1\preccurlyeq A\preccurlyeq X\).  For PE and \(q=0\),
the \(\mathit{parallel}\) field of \noderef{HierarchyBase}, applied to
\(\mathcal B_1\), supplies a closure \(R_n^1\) and
\[
 U_1:\noderef{OrderedTreeUnion}(T_n^1,R_n^1,A),
\]
whose \(\mathit{leftSegment}\) gives the same source inclusion.  For
SE/RE and \(q=0\), the \(\mathit{nonparallel}\) field of the same
\noderef{HierarchyBase} gives
\(A=T_n^1\), and the one-edge seed lies in \(X\) because \(i\ge1\).
For SE/RE and \(q>0\), the first \(\mathit{strict}\) and
\(\mathit{nested}\) fields of \noderef{TashkinovHierarchy} make stage
one at least one entry longer than \(T_n^1\); comparing the exact seed
and stage one as prefixes of the common final ladder tree via
\noderef{TreeSequenceInitialSegment} gives
\[
 T_n^1+f_n^1\preccurlyeq H_1.\mathit{stage}(1)
                    \preccurlyeq A\preccurlyeq X.             \tag{22}
\]
These four cases discharge the source hypotheses of
\noderef{TashkinovAugmentationSequenceRestrict}, first for \(T\) and then
for \(X\).

Again unfold the resulting
\(\nu\)-\noderef{TashkinovAugmentationSequence} and induct along its
one-entry \noderef{TashkinovAugmentation}s.  Starting from the relevant
source of length at most \(|X|\), the lengths increase by one until
\(|T|\).  The unique member of length \(|X|\) is equal to \(X\), since
both are exact prefixes of \(T\) of the same length.  Splitting the chain
at that member gives the restricted route to \(X\) and the actual suffix
\[
                              X\longrightarrow T
                     \quad\text{under }\nu.                  \tag{23}
\]
This is a second chain induction for the intermediate presentation, not
an appeal to (16).

Apply \noderef{TashkinovAugmentationSequenceColoringTransport} to the
route ending at \(X\), with penultimate prefix \(X^-\).  Equation (21)
gives its missing-set premise, and (20) fixes added edges at positions at
least \(|A|\).  We check all earlier positions under \(P_1\).  If
\(q>0\), the \noderef{TreeColoringInvariant} clause from the second weak
stability fixes them.  If \(q=0\) and the kind is SE/RE, then
\(A=T_n^1\) and the route begins at its exact one-edge seed.  If \(q=0\)
and the kind is PE, use the intermediate union \(U_1\) above.  The weak
stability is \noderef{CombinedStableColoring} on \(T_n^1\) and
\(R_n^1\); \noderef{CombinedStableClosurePreserved} fixes separately
every displayed edge of those two trees.  For arbitrary \(r<|A|\),
\(U_1.\mathit{leftSegment}\) identifies
\(A.\mathit{edge}(r)\) with an edge of \(T_n^1\) when
\(r<|T_n^1|\), while \(U_1.\mathit{newEntryFromRight}\) produces an
exact \(s<|R_n^1|\) with
\(A.\mathit{edge}(r)=R_n^1.\mathit{edge}(s)\) otherwise.  Hence every
individual edge of \(A\) is fixed from \(\nu\) to \(\rho\).  Together
with (20), this verifies the transport theorem's entire fixed-edge
premise and yields the exact \(\rho\)-route to \(X\).                \(\tag{24}\)

Both \(\gamma_1\) and \(\theta\) remain missing on \(X\), at the stage
witness and \(y_{i+1}\), because their common component is disjoint from
the changed terminal component.  Fix an arbitrary
\(\eta\in D^1_{n,q}\) for the stored-position premise.  Equation (18)
makes \(\gamma_1\notin D^1_{n,q}\).  If \(\eta\ne\theta\), then \(\eta\)
differs from both affected colors and its missing predicate is unchanged
at every vertex.  If \(\eta=\theta\), equation (21) preserves its exact
first witness \(y_{i+1}\), and elementarity says there was no earlier
witness; hence its totalized first position is again unchanged.  This
proves the required equality for every stored \(\eta\).

For the second Gamma cutoff, again quantify over arbitrary stored
\(\eta\).  Equation (18) says \(\theta\) is not missing on \(A\), while
every member of every Gamma pair is stage-missing by
\noderef{GoodHierarchy}; therefore \(\theta\) belongs to no Gamma pair.
If \(\gamma_1\in\Gamma^q_\eta\), it also lies in the copied displayed
pair \(\Gamma^q_\theta\).  The pairwise-disjoint field forces
\(\eta=\theta\), and the first \(\theta\)-missing position is the final
position of \(X\).  Thus the cutoff holds universally after the first
replacement as well.

Apply \noderef{StableChangeCasesOneTwoSplitTail} a second time, using
(18)--(24), the two split missing witnesses, the universal stored-position
check, and the universal Gamma cutoff.  It reconstructs the suffix (23)
under \(\rho\), rather than claiming full-tail edge fixation, and returns
the full corresponding ETT, maximum property, base, hierarchy, goodness,
and same-good data.  The swap makes \(\theta\) missing at \(y_\ell\) and
preserves (3) at \(y_{i+1}\), so the returned terminal tree is
non-elementary.  Compose its correspondence and same-good witness with
those of \(P_1\), field by field as above, and rebuild the transported
minimum context.  This produces a genuine original-relative replacement
presentation \(P_2\).

Finally the hypotheses give \(1\le i+1\le\ell-1\), and under \(P_2\) the
color \(\theta\) is missing at both \(y_{i+1}\) and \(y_\ell\).  Hence
\(i+1,P_2,\theta\) satisfies the defining filter in
\noderef{HierarchyEligibleCommonMissingIndices}.  This completes the
stored branch and the exhaustive case split.
\end{proof}

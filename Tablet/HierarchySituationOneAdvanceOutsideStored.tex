\begin{lemma}[Situation 7.1: advance an unstored common color]
Fix a replacement presentation \(P\) and an index
\(1\leq i<\ell-1\).  Suppose a color \(\alpha\notin D_{n,q}\) is missing,
under the coloring of \(P\), at both exact vertices \(y_i\) and \(y_\ell\).
Assume \(\noderef{HierarchyPathNumber}=1\) and the explicit
palette-surplus consequence that every vertex has a missing color under
every proper exactly-one-uncolored replacement coloring.  Then
\[
 i+1\in\noderef{HierarchyEligibleCommonMissingIndices}.
\]
Thus the paper's replacement family contains a member having a color
missing at both \(y_{i+1}\) and \(y_\ell\).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Write \(\mu=P.\mathit{coloring}\), let \(H\) be its hierarchy, let \(q\)
be the last stored index, and put
\[
 A=\noderef{HierarchyStarredStage}(H,q),\quad
 \ell=\noderef{HierarchyFullTailLength}(C),\quad
 B=T(y_{\ell-1}),\quad X=T(y_{i+1}),\quad X^-=T(y_i),         \tag{1}
\]
using \noderef{HierarchyFullTailPrefix}.  The
\noderef{SameGoodHierarchy} field of
\noderef{HierarchyReplacementPresentation} identifies the original and
current base cores, divider counts, stages, and last starred stage.  Thus
the original and current versions of all the trees in (1) are the same
exact ordered sequences, and
\[
 A\prec X^-\prec X\preccurlyeq B\prec T,\qquad |X|=|X^-|+1. \tag{2}
\]
The given path-number-one condition transports to \(H\) because
\noderef{HierarchyPathNumber} depends only on this common exact tail and
its endpoint incidences.

Apply \noderef{HierarchyCounterexamplePenultimateElementary} to
\(P.\mathit{minimumContext}\).  It makes
\(\noderef{TreeSequenceVertexSet}(B)\) a
\noderef{ColoredVertexSetElementary} set under \(\mu\); the same context
supplies \noderef{IsProperEdgeColoring} and
\noderef{IsOneEdgeUncolored}.  Choose
\[
                  \theta\in\noderef{MissingColors}(G,\mu,y_{i+1}) \tag{3}
\]
by the palette-surplus hypothesis.  The distinct vertices \(y_i,y_{i+1}\)
lie in \(B\).  Elementarity, together with the \(\alpha\)-missing witness
at \(y_i\), gives \(\theta\ne\alpha\), says \(\theta\) is missing at no
earlier vertex of \(B\), and says \(\alpha\) is missing at no vertex
other than \(y_i\) there.  Consequently these are the exact first missing
\noderef{HierarchyFirstMissingPosition}s of \(\alpha\) and \(\theta\).

Apply \noderef{HierarchyTailMissingColorExclusion} first at \(y_i\) and
then at \(y_{i+1}\).  Together with path number one, it says that neither
color occurs on the respective earlier exact displayed tail, except for
the explicit divider-zero SE/RE first seed when that individual edge has
its color in \(D_n=\noderef{TashkinovStoredColors}(\mathcal E,n)\).  In
particular \(\alpha\) is absent from the one-edge interval between its
first position and the first \(\theta\)-missing position.  This is the
paper's assertion (1), with the possible seed retained by exact identity.

We first treat the branch \(\theta\notin D_{n,q}\).  Elementarity shows
that neither affected color is missing on \(A\), because its first missing
vertex lies strictly after \(A\).  Hence every vertex of \(A\) precedes
the first \(\alpha\)-missing vertex, which precedes the first
\(\theta\)-missing vertex.  The two unstored hypotheses and the exact
interval exclusion above are precisely the external first-missing
hypotheses of \noderef{StableChangeCaseThree}.

Apply \noderef{ChangeLemma} to these same ordered colors before making the
swap.  It identifies the full component through \(y_i\) with the full
component through \(y_{i+1}\).  The terminal component through
\(y_\ell\) is different from this common component: otherwise the three
distinct vertex-nodes \(y_i,y_{i+1},y_\ell\), missing respectively
\(\alpha,\theta,\alpha\), would all have incidence degree at most one by
\noderef{MissingColorIncidenceEndpoint}.  This is impossible because
\noderef{TwoColorComponentSpanningPath} spans a proper-coloring component
by a simple path and \noderef{TwoColorIncidenceDegreeAtMostTwo} allows only
its two ends to have full degree at most one.  Therefore
\noderef{TwoColorComponentsDisjoint} makes the terminal component disjoint
from the common component through \(y_i,y_{i+1}\).                 \(\tag{3a}\)

Let \(Q\) be the \((\alpha,\theta)\)-component through \(y_\ell\), and
put
\[
 \nu=\noderef{TwoColorComponentSwap}
      (G,\mu,\alpha,\theta,\operatorname{inl}y_\ell).         \tag{4}
\]
The missing endpoint and \noderef{MissingColorIncidenceEndpoint} make
\(Q\) a possibly singleton \noderef{TwoColorPathComponent}.
\noderef{TwoColorComponentSwapProper} preserves properness, and the swap
fixes \(\mathsf{none}\), so exactly \(e_0\) remains uncolored.  Apply
\noderef{StableChangeCaseThree} with the prefix elementarity and all six
fields of \(P.\mathit{minimumContext}\).  It returns an actual
\noderef{CorrespondingExtendedTashkinovTree} on the full exact tree,
maximum property, a base, and a good hierarchy with
\noderef{SameGoodHierarchy} data.  Disjointness (3a) preserves
\(\theta\)-missingness at \(y_{i+1}\), while
the swap makes \(\theta\) missing at \(y_\ell\): if already missing it
stays so; otherwise the unique incident \(\theta\)-edge belongs to \(Q\)
and changes to \(\alpha\).  Thus the new terminal tree is non-elementary
and the returned data make a \noderef{HierarchyCounterexample}.

The returned correspondence is relative to
\(P.\mathit{corresponding}.\mathit{replacementETT}\), so compose it back
to the original ETT field by field.  Compose the rung equalities; at each
positive ladder index compose the new ladder equality, the heterogeneous
series presentation of \(P.\mathit{corresponding}\), and its old ladder
equality; at every round-plus-one index do the same for stored sets,
connecting-edge sets, and extension kinds.  Take the final generating
coloring, properness, and one-uncoloredness from the new certificate.  At
each positive ladder tree, compose the two \noderef{StableColoring}
relations by \noderef{sc2}; their trees and protected
\noderef{TashkinovStoredColors} sets agree by the preceding fields, and
their properness hypotheses come from the certified positive
\noderef{TashkinovSeriesStep}s.  This constructs a genuine original-relative
correspondence.

Compose the old and new same-good witnesses field by field in
\noderef{SameHierarchyStages}, the stored sets, and Gamma pairs.  The
composite correspondence transports the lower-rung and large-missing-set
fields; the same-good composite transports divider counts, stages,
\noderef{HierarchyCounterexampleMeasure}, fewer-divider induction, and
lexicographic minimality.  Together with properness and one-uncoloredness,
these are all fields of \noderef{HierarchyMinimumCounterexampleContext}.
They form a replacement presentation \(P'\) relative to the original ETT.
Now \(i+1,P',\theta\) directly witness the required eligible-index
membership.  This completes the \(\theta\notin D_{n,q}\) branch through
the sound external-case provider.

Assume henceforth that \(\theta\in D_{n,q}\).  The two-color and
disjoint-pairs fields of \noderef{GoodHierarchy} give a pair
\(\Gamma^q_\theta=\{\gamma_1,\gamma_2\}\); choose \(\gamma_1\).
Avoidance, using the interval in \noderef{HierarchySegmentColors}, puts
\(\gamma_1\) in
\noderef{HierarchyStageMissingColors}\((H,q)\) and excludes it from all
exact edges between \(A\) and the first \(\theta\)-missing position
\(y_{i+1}\).  Since \(\alpha\) is missing at \(y_i\), elementarity says
it is not missing on \(A\); hence it cannot be any Gamma color, all of
which are stage-missing.  Applying \noderef{ChangeLemma} to
\((\gamma_1,\alpha)\) identifies the component through the first
\(\gamma_1\)-missing stage vertex \(v_\gamma\) with the component through
\(y_i\).  Its hypotheses are the exact order, missing witnesses, Gamma
avoidance, and the tail exclusion already proved.

The component through \(y_\ell\) is disjoint from that common component.
Indeed, \(v_\gamma,y_i,y_\ell\) are three distinct missing endpoints.
By \noderef{MissingColorIncidenceEndpoint} they have incidence degree at
most one; if they lay in one component,
\noderef{TwoColorComponentSpanningPath} and
\noderef{TwoColorIncidenceDegreeAtMostTwo} would allow only two such ends.
The equal-or-disjoint assertion \noderef{TwoColorComponentsDisjoint}
therefore gives disjointness.  Swap the terminal component and call the
new coloring
\[
 \nu=\noderef{TwoColorComponentSwap}
      (G,\mu,\gamma_1,\alpha,\operatorname{inl}y_\ell).       \tag{5}
\]
It is proper and exactly-one-uncolored as in (4).  Apply
\noderef{StableChangeCasesOneTwo} first, with \(\gamma_1\) as the
stage-missing Cases 1/2 color, to obtain
\noderef{HierarchyWeaklyStable} and \noderef{ModColoring}.

We verify the data needed by the split-tail provider at
\(X=T(y_{i+1})\).  Gamma avoidance fixes every
\(\gamma_1\)-candidate edge of \(X-A\), and
\noderef{HierarchyTailMissingColorExclusion} fixes every
\(\alpha\)-candidate through \(X^-\) except the exact divider-zero seed.
The final edge \(e_{i+1}\) is incident with the \(\alpha\)-missing vertex
\(y_i\) by path number one, so \noderef{MissingColors} excludes its
\(\alpha\)-value as well.  In the seed
exception, the certified \noderef{TashkinovSeriesStep} is SE or RE;
\noderef{MaximumDefectiveChoice} or
\noderef{RevisitingExtensionCertificate} makes the exact seed a
\noderef{TreeIncidentEdge} of \(T_n\), and the protected incident-edge
clause of \noderef{StableColoring} fixes it.  Hence
\[
 \nu(T.\mathit{edge}(r))=\mu(T.\mathit{edge}(r))
                    \quad(|A|\le r<|X|).                    \tag{6}
\]

For every \(v\in V(B)\), its missing set is unchanged.  If its vertex-node
is outside the selected component, no incident edge changes.  If it is
inside, disjointness makes it different from \(v_\gamma,y_i\).
Elementarity makes both affected colors present there; expanding
\noderef{PresentColors} and using properness yields the two unique incident
edges, both in the component, and (5) transposes them together.  Thus
\[
 \noderef{MissingColors}(G,\nu,v)=
 \noderef{MissingColors}(G,\mu,v)\quad(v\in V(B)).            \tag{7}
\]

Inspect the actual terminal certificate in the \noderef{TashkinovSeries}.
Its PE \noderef{TashkinovSeriesStep} supplies a
\noderef{TashkinovClosure} and route from \(T_n\); its SE/RE constructors
supply the exact singleton connecting edge, exact
\noderef{TreeSequenceAdjoinEdge} seed, and route from that seed.  The final
prefix field of \noderef{ExtendedTashkinovTree} and
\noderef{TashkinovAugmentationSequenceRestrict} restrict the route to
\(T\) and then \(X\).  In PE the source \(T_n\) precedes \(A\); in
SE/RE at divider zero the one-edge seed is the first tail entry and lies
in \(X\) because \(i\ge1\); at positive divider the strict first
hierarchy stage already contains that seed and precedes \(A\).  These are
the exact source-prefix hypotheses needed for both restrictions.  Since every
\noderef{TashkinovAugmentation} adds exactly one entry, induction through
the same chain factors it at the unique exact member \(X\), producing the
old suffix \(X\to T\).

Let \(S\) be \(T_n\) in PE and the exact seed in SE/RE.  Apply
\noderef{TashkinovAugmentationSequenceColoringTransport} to the old route
\(S\to X\), with penultimate prefix \(X^-\).  Equation (7) gives its
pointwise missing-set premise.  Equation (6) fixes route-added edges above
\(A\).  Below \(A\), the positive-divider
\noderef{TreeColoringInvariant} clause fixes them; at divider zero PE,
\noderef{CombinedStableColoring} and
\noderef{CombinedStableClosurePreserved} fix the terminal ladder tree and
PE closure forming \(A\); at divider zero SE/RE, \(A=T_n\) and the source
is already the seed.  Thus the transport lemma supplies the exact
\(\nu\)-route through \(X\), with the old singleton seed data.

The two affected colors remain in \noderef{TreeMissingColors} of \(X\), at
\(v_\gamma\) and \(y_i\), because their common component is disjoint from
the changed one.  Every stored first position is unchanged: neither
\(\gamma_1\) nor \(\alpha\) is stored, and every other color is unaffected.
For the Gamma cutoff, \(\alpha\) is in no pair, while
\(\gamma_1\in\Gamma^q_\theta\) and the first \(\theta\)-missing position
is \(y_{i+1}\), the last vertex of \(X\).  These facts, (2), (5)--(7),
the new route to \(X\), the old suffix, and split missingness satisfy every
hypothesis of \noderef{StableChangeCasesOneTwoSplitTail}.

Apply that theorem.  It reconstructs the suffix after \(X\) by
augmentation transport, and returns an actual full corresponding ETT,
maximum property, base, hierarchy, good hierarchy, and same-good data.
The swap makes \(\gamma_1\) missing at \(y_\ell\) and preserves its stage
witness, so these outputs form a counterexample.  Applying the
field-by-field correspondence composition, same-good composition, measure
transport, and minimum-context reconstruction used in the first branch
produces an original-relative replacement presentation \(P_1\).

Under \(P_1\), the color \(\theta\) is still missing at \(y_{i+1}\),
while \(\gamma_1\) is missing both on its earlier stage and at
\(y_\ell\).  Apply \noderef{ChangeLemma} to
\((\gamma_1,\theta)\).  It identifies the stage component with the
component through \(y_{i+1}\); the terminal component is disjoint by the
same three-endpoint argument.  Swap that terminal component and call the
coloring \(\rho\).  Apply \noderef{StableChangeCasesOneTwo} with
\(\gamma_1\) as the stage-missing color.

For this second swap, Gamma avoidance excludes \(\gamma_1\) through
\(X\), and \noderef{HierarchyTailMissingColorExclusion} excludes
\(\theta\), with the exact protected seed as the sole exception.  Thus
the analogue of (6) holds only through \(X\).  Treating vertices inside
and outside the selected component gives the analogue of (7) on \(B\).
Restrict and factor the actual \(P_1\) terminal augmentation route at
\(X\), and invoke
\noderef{TashkinovAugmentationSequenceColoringTransport} with penultimate
prefix \(X^-\), giving the \(\rho\)-route to \(X\).  Both affected colors
remain missing on \(X\).  The only affected stored color is \(\theta\),
whose exact first missing position \(y_{i+1}\) is preserved by the
vertexwise equality; \(\theta\) is in no Gamma pair because it is not
stage-missing, and the cutoff for \(\gamma_1\in\Gamma^q_\theta\) is again
the last position of \(X\).

A second application of
\noderef{StableChangeCasesOneTwoSplitTail} therefore rebuilds the full
tree under \(\rho\).  It makes \(\theta\) missing at \(y_\ell\) while
preserving (3) at \(y_{i+1}\), so the output is non-elementary.  Apply the
same explicit correspondence and same-good compositions to \(P_1\) and
this output to obtain a genuine original-relative replacement presentation
\(P_2\).  Finally \(1\le i+1\le\ell-1\), and
\(i+1,P_2,\theta\) satisfy the filter defining
\noderef{HierarchyEligibleCommonMissingIndices}.  This proves the stored
branch and completes the exhaustive case split.
\end{proof}

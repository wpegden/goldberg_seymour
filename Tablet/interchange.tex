\begin{lemma}[Interchangeability at a terminal parallel extension]
Let $T$ be a positive-rung extended Tashkinov tree constructed from a
$k$-triple $G,e_0,\varphi$, with certified input coloring allowed to
be arbitrary, terminal ladder tree $T_n$, terminal generating coloring
$\varphi_n$, and terminal stored-color set $D_n$.  Assume that $T$
has maximum property and that its terminal extension is a PE.  Let
$R_n$ and $T_n\vee R_n$ be the closure and exact ordered union in a
hierarchy base under $\varphi_n$; in the Lean statement these are
respectively `base.closure' and `base.core'.

Assume, uniformly over every certified input coloring and generating
coloring, that every positive-rung ETT $T'$ of rung number $s<n$
with maximum property satisfies all six lower-rung assertions:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $V(T')$ is elementary under its generating coloring;
\item under every proper coloring leaving exactly $e_0$ uncolored and
stable on its final ladder closure, that closure is interchangeable for
two colors whenever at least one of them is missing there;
\item stability descends at each PE support to the prefix before the
support and to its first-appearance ladder stage, and stored pairs at
distinct PE slots with distinct supports and the same first-appearance
index are disjoint;
\item at a terminal PE, the stored two-color component through the
support meets the terminal ladder tree only at that support;
\item every nonminimum endpoint of a defective boundary color precedes
each later PE support or SE extension vertex allowed by its
first-appearance index; and
\item every proper terminal stable coloring leaving exactly $e_0$
uncolored is a mod-coloring of the ETT.
\end{enumerate}

Let $\sigma_n$ be proper, leave exactly $e_0$ uncolored, and be
$T_n\oplus R_n,D_n,\varphi_n$-stable.  For arbitrary colors
$\alpha,\beta\in[k]$, all four conclusions hold:
\begin{enumerate}
\item if $\alpha\in\overline\sigma_n(R_n)$, then $\alpha,\beta$
are $R_n$-interchangeable under $\sigma_n$;
\item if $\alpha\in\overline\sigma_n(T_n)$, then $\alpha,\beta$
are $T_n$-interchangeable under $\sigma_n$;
\item if $\alpha\in\overline\sigma_n(T_n\vee R_n)$ and the singleton
color set $\{\alpha\}$ is closed on $T_n\vee R_n$ under
$\sigma_n$, then $\alpha,\beta$ are
$T_n\vee R_n$-interchangeable under $\sigma_n$; and
\item if $\alpha\in\overline\sigma_n(T_n)$ and
$\beta\in\overline\sigma_n(R_n)$, then $\alpha,\beta$ are
$T_n\vee R_n$-interchangeable under $\sigma_n$.
\end{enumerate}
All trees and two-color components retain the individual identities of
parallel edges.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Write $X=V(T_n)$, $Y=V(R_n)$, and
$U=V(T_n\vee R_n)$, with vertex sets understood as in
\noderef{TreeSequenceVertexSet}.  The generating-color field of
\noderef{ExtendedTashkinovTree} identifies $\varphi_n$ with the last
coloring of the certified series.  Eliminate the last certificate in
\noderef{TashkinovSeries} using the terminal-PE hypothesis.  The PE
constructor of \noderef{TashkinovSeriesStep} supplies the exact support
$v_n$, exact connecting-edge identity $f_n$, and colors
$\gamma_n,\delta_n$.  It also supplies properness and exact
one-uncoloredness of $\varphi_n$, and the equations recording the
terminal $\gamma_n,\delta_n$-switch.  No endpoint pair is substituted
for $f_n$.

The parallel clause of \noderef{HierarchyBase} says that $R_n$ is a
$\varphi_n$-closure of the ordered prefix $T_n(v_n)$, and supplies
an \noderef{OrderedTreeUnion} certificate with
\[
                         U=X\cup Y.                       \tag{1}
\]
It also says that the missing-color set of $T_n$ under the base
coloring is its missing-color set under $\varphi_n$.
The two conjuncts of \noderef{CombinedStableColoring}, with stability
expanded as in \noderef{StableColoring}, say that
$\sigma_n$ is $(T_n,D_n,\varphi_n)$-stable and simultaneously
$(R_n,\varnothing,\varphi_n)$-stable.  Applying
\noderef{CombinedStableClosurePreserved} gives, for every displayed edge
identity $g$ of either tree,
\[
             \sigma_n(g)=\varphi_n(g),                    \tag{2}
\]
and proves that the same ordered tree $R_n$, with the same edge
identities, is a $\sigma_n$-closure of $T_n(v_n)$.

We record three preliminary facts, with their hypotheses checked at the
coloring at which they will be used.

First, $U$ is elementary under $\sigma_n$.  Indeed, retain the same
core, closure, and support position from the hierarchy base.  Equality
of missing-color sets on $T_n$ is the vertex clause of the first stable
relation.  The preceding paragraph supplies the required
$\sigma_n$-closure, and the old ordered-union certificate is unchanged.
These data form a hierarchy base under $\sigma_n$.  The first stable
relation, properness, exact one-uncoloredness, positivity, maximum
property, the terminal-PE equation, and the displayed six lower-rung
assertions are precisely the hypotheses of
\noderef{ParallelClosureUnionElementary}.  Hence
\[
                  U\text{ is elementary under }\sigma_n.   \tag{3}
\]

Second, every color missing on both $T_n$ and $R_n$ is closed on
their union under $\sigma_n$.  For the pre-step elementarity premise
of \noderef{ParallelCommonMissingClosed}, restrict the certified series
through rung $n-1$.  If $n>1$, maximum property restricts and lower
assertion (i) applies to this arbitrary-input restricted ETT; if $n=1$,
the same assertion is the elementarity conclusion of
\noderef{TechnicalTheoremBase}.  Thus that lemma gives
\[
 T_n\vee R_n\text{ is }
 \bigl(\overline\varphi_n(T_n)\cap
       \overline\varphi_n(R_n)\bigr)\text{-closed under }\varphi_n.
                                                               \tag{4}
\]
The vertex clauses of the two stable relations identify both constituent
missing-color sets under $\varphi_n$ and $\sigma_n$.  The PE constructor
of \noderef{TashkinovSeriesStep} says that $\varphi_n$ is proper and
leaves exactly $e_0$ uncolored; the same two facts for $\sigma_n$ are
hypotheses.  We may therefore apply the symmetry clause of \noderef{sc2}
separately to the two constituent stable relations.  It gives the reverse
relations saying that $\varphi_n$ is $(T_n,D_n,\sigma_n)$-stable and is
also $(R_n,\varnothing,\sigma_n)$-stable.

Now let $c$ be common missing under $\sigma_n$, and suppose that an exact
edge identity $g$ lies in the $c$-colored boundary of $U$ under
$\sigma_n$.  By \noderef{BoundaryEdges}, name its endpoints so that the
inside endpoint $p$ lies in $U$ and the other lies outside $U$.  Equation
(1) gives $p\in X$ or $p\in Y$, and \noderef{TreeIncidentEdge} makes $g$
incident with the corresponding constituent tree.  If $p\in X$, then
$c\in\overline\sigma_n(T_n)$ and the edge clause of the reverse
$T_n$-stable relation gives $\varphi_n(g)=\sigma_n(g)=c$.  If $p\in Y$,
the reverse $R_n$-stable relation gives the same equality.  Boundary
membership depends only on the exact edge endpoints, while the color
filter is \noderef{BoundaryColorEdges}; hence $g$ is a $c$-colored
boundary identity of $U$ under $\varphi_n$.  The vertex clauses also put
$c$ in both constituent missing-color sets under $\varphi_n$, so this
contradicts (4).  Consequently
\[
 c\in\overline\sigma_n(T_n)\cap\overline\sigma_n(R_n)
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 \partial_{\sigma_n,c}(U)=\varnothing.                       \tag{5}
\]
This is a statement about exact members of the boundary set in
\noderef{TreeColorSetClosed}; common missing membership has its vertex
witness by \noderef{TreeMissingColors}.

Third, we establish the following restricted terminal-tree assertion:
\[
 \begin{split}
 a\in\overline\sigma_n(T_n),\quad
 a,b\notin\{\gamma_n,\delta_n\}
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 a,b\text{ are }T_n\text{-interchangeable under }\sigma_n.
                                                               \tag{6}
 \end{split}
\]
Apply \noderef{ExtensionBase} with the six lower-rung assertions, the
given maximum property, properness, exact one-uncoloredness, and the
first stable relation.  Its terminal-PE conclusion says that the
$\gamma_n,\delta_n$-component through $v_n$ meets $X$ exactly at
$v_n$.  Thus all hypotheses of
\noderef{ParallelExtensionUndoStable} hold.  If
\[
 \tau=\sigma_n/P_{v_n}(\gamma_n,\delta_n,\sigma_n),
\]
the PE certificate gives $\gamma_n\ne\delta_n$: the rule
\noderef{ParallelExtensionColor} makes $\gamma_n$ missing at $v_n$ under
the selected coloring, while \noderef{MaximumDefectiveChoice} supplies the
exact edge identity $f_n$ incident with $v_n$ and colored $\delta_n$.
Thus \noderef{PresentColors} and \noderef{MissingColors} put the two colors
on opposite sides of the same complement.  Hence
\noderef{TwoColorComponentSwapProper},
applied to the stated properness of $\sigma_n$, proves that $\tau$ is
proper.  To verify its uncolored edge exactly, unfold
\noderef{IsOneEdgeUncolored}.  The value $\sigma_n(e_0)=\mathsf{none}$
is fixed by the definition \noderef{TwoColorComponentSwap}, so
$\tau(e_0)=\mathsf{none}$.  If $g\ne e_0$, the same hypothesis supplies
a color $d$ with $\sigma_n(g)=\mathsf{some}(d)$.  The swap either fixes
this value or, when the edge-node of $g$ is in the selected component and
$d\in\{\gamma_n,\delta_n\}$, replaces it by the other displayed color.
In every case $\tau(g)$ is still a colored value.  Thus $\tau$ leaves
exactly $e_0$ uncolored.

The actual conclusion of \noderef{ParallelExtensionUndoStable} now makes
$\tau$ stable on $T_n$ relative to the preceding stored set and preceding
generating coloring.  Since $(a,b)$ avoid the two switched colors,
\noderef{TwoColorComponentSwap} shows edge by edge that the
$(a,b)$-incidence graph, its components, and the missingness of $a$ on
$T_n$ are identical for $\tau$ and $\sigma_n$.
When $n>1$, lower assertion (ii), applied to the restricted rung
$n-1$ ETT and to $\tau$, gives interchangeability on its final
ladder closure $T_n$.  When $n=1$, use the corresponding conclusion
of \noderef{TechnicalTheoremBase}.  Transport across the identical
incidence graphs proves (6).

We now prove conclusion (i).  Let $j=m(v_n)$ be the first ladder
stage containing the support, as represented by
\noderef{ETTFirstAppearanceIndex}.  The ordered-entry equalities in
\noderef{TreeSequenceInitialSegment} identify the prefix through
$v_n$ with the corresponding prefix of $T_j$.  Apply
\noderef{ExtensionRules} to the current ETT and the full displayed
lower-rung package.  Its first conclusion says that $\sigma_n$ is
stable, with the correct earlier stored set and generating coloring,
both before $v_n$ in the sense of
\noderef{StableColoringBeforeVertex} and on $T_{j-1}$.

We next check the construction, rather than merely naming $R_n$ a
lower-rung ETT.  Each augmentation which constructs the prefix through
$v_n$ has an exact new edge identity and a missing-color witness at an
earlier vertex by \noderef{TashkinovAugmentation}.  Unfolding the vertex
and incident-edge clauses of \noderef{StableColoringBeforeVertex},
stability before $v_n$ preserves that witness and the controlled color of
that same edge identity.  Hence every original augmentation can be copied
under $\sigma_n$.  Compose this copied finite sequence with the
$\sigma_n$-closure sequence for $R_n$ obtained above; transitivity of
\noderef{TashkinovAugmentationSequence} proves that $R_n$ is a closure
of the exact seed prescribed at stage (j-1).

If $j>1$, restrict the old series through rung (j-1).  The restricted
maximum-property bounds are fields of \noderef{MaximumProperty}, and
the restriction has positive rung number below $n$.  Apply
\noderef{Td} to this restricted ETT; its lower hypotheses are obtained
by restricting the displayed universal package once more.  The stable
relation just proved makes $\sigma_n$ a \noderef{ModColoring} of that
ETT.  Its universal extension clause, applied only after the exact seed,
strict extension, and augmentation sequence have been verified, returns
a genuine \noderef{CorrespondingExtendedTashkinovTree} displayed on
$R_n$.  By
\noderef{MaximumPropertyTransport} it has maximum property.  Lower
assertion (ii), instantiated with its actual replacement input and
generating coloring, makes its final ladder closure interchangeable for
$\alpha,\beta$, since $\alpha$ is missing already on its displayed
initial segment $R_n$.  Expanding \noderef{TreeInterchangeable}, any
two path components meeting $R_n$ also meet that closure, so the
conclusion restricts to $R_n$.

If $j=1$, the composed augmentation sequence starts at the initial
one-edge tree.  Together with the original graph and numerical fields of
\noderef{KTriple}, and the stated properness and exact
one-uncoloredness, it forms the certified initial closure ending at
$R_n$.  The interchangeability clause of
\noderef{TechnicalTheoremBase} gives the same conclusion.  Thus in all
cases
\[
 \alpha\in\overline\sigma_n(R_n)
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 \alpha,\beta\text{ are }R_n\text{-interchangeable}.       \tag{7}
\]
This proves $i$, including the lower-rung closure instance used in the
paper.

Before proving (iii), we strengthen (6) to the paper's observation
\[
 \begin{split}
 a\in\overline\sigma_n(T_n),\qquad
 a\ne\delta_n,\quad b\ne\delta_n
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 a,b\text{ are }T_n\text{-interchangeable}.                 \tag{8}
 \end{split}
\]
The unconditional clause of \noderef{HkuParallelBoundaryStructure} gives
\[
 \partial_{\varphi_n,\gamma_n}(T_n)=\{f_n\}.                  \tag{8a}
\]
The same lemma's conditional clause uses the pre-step elementarity already
established for (4).  Since $\gamma_n\ne\delta_n$, if $\gamma_n$ were
missing on $T_n$ under $\varphi_n$, that clause would make the left side
of (8a) empty, contradicting the membership of the exact identity $f_n$
in its singleton right side.  Therefore
$\gamma_n\notin\overline\varphi_n(T_n)$.  The PE constructor of
\noderef{TashkinovSeriesStep} gives the exact current stored-pair equation
$S_n=\{\delta_n,\gamma_n\}$.  In the set difference defining $D_n$ in
\noderef{TashkinovStoredColors}, this puts $\gamma_n$ in the cumulative
stored-color union, and the just-proved nonmissingness prevents its
removal.  Hence $\gamma_n\in D_n$.

Use now both directions of the $T_n$-stable relation: the forward
relation is a constituent of \noderef{CombinedStableColoring}, and its
reverse was obtained from \noderef{sc2} above.  Since $\gamma_n\in D_n$,
their edge clauses apply whether a tree-incident edge is
$\gamma_n$-colored under $\varphi_n$ or under $\sigma_n$.  Exact boundary
membership is coloring-independent, so the two inclusions obtained from
the forward and reverse relations transport (8a) to
\[
 \partial_{\sigma_n,\gamma_n}(T_n)=\{f_n\}.                  \tag{8b}
\]

Now take $a$ as in (8).  The stable vertex clause makes it missing on
$T_n$ under $\varphi_n$.  Since $a\ne\delta_n$, the conditional clause of
\noderef{HkuParallelBoundaryStructure} makes its $\varphi_n$-boundary
empty.  If its $\sigma_n$-boundary contained an exact identity, the edge
clause of the reverse $T_n$-stable relation would give that identity color
$a$ under $\varphi_n$, a contradiction.  Thus the $a$-boundary under
$\sigma_n$ is empty.  Equation (8b) then gives $a\ne\gamma_n$.  If
$b\ne\gamma_n$, (8) is (6).  If $b=\gamma_n$, then (3), unfolded using
\noderef{ColoredVertexSetElementary}, makes $X\subseteq U$ elementary, the
$a$-boundary of $X$ is empty, and the $\gamma_n$-boundary contains
only $f_n$.  Properness and
\noderef{TwoColorComponentSpanningPath} then allow at most one paper
$(a,\gamma_n)$-path component to meet $X$.  For two nontrivial components,
the spanning paths would require two distinct boundary identities or give
a repeated missing color in $X$.  If one component were a singleton at a
tree vertex, both colors would be missing there; any second singleton or
any tree end of a trapped nontrivial component would repeat a missing
color, while a nontrivial component crossing $X$ cannot have both ends
outside through the sole boundary identity $f_n$.  Thus a singleton cannot
coexist with a second path component meeting $X$.  This proves (8).

We prove (iii) by contradiction, beginning with the component bookkeeping
which will also be used below.  Expanding the negation of
\noderef{TreeInterchangeable}, suppose $a$ is missing on $U$, is closed
there, and there are roots $z_1,z_2$ whose full incidence components in
\noderef{TwoColorComponent}
$P_1,P_2$ are unequal, each meets $U$, and each satisfies
\noderef{TwoColorPathComponent}.  Unpacking either path condition gives
$a\ne b$, and each component has a node with at most one neighbor.  By
\noderef{TwoColorComponentsDisjoint}, the unequal full components are
node-disjoint.

For every $P_i$ which contains an edge-node, its path certificate and
that witness give \noderef{TwoColorNontrivialPathComponent}.  Apply
\noderef{TwoColorComponentSpanningPath} to $z_i$; its simple spanning path
has support exactly $P_i$.  We verify the nature of its two ends.  By
\noderef{TwoColorIncidenceGraph}, every neighbor of an edge-node is a
vertex-node incident with that exact edge identity.  The two ends of an
edge are distinct by \noderef{LooplessMultigraph}, and incidence is the
membership relation of \noderef{Incident}; hence every colored edge-node
has exactly two distinct vertex-node neighbors.  On the other hand,
properness gives the degree bound of
\noderef{TwoColorIncidenceDegreeAtMostTwo} at every incidence node.  The
at-most-one-neighbor node supplied by
\noderef{TwoColorPathComponent} lies in a nontrivial connected component,
so it has exactly one neighbor and is a vertex-node.  Consequently the
spanning path is not closed by an extra adjacency between its two ends.
Any other extra neighbor of a path end would already occur in the spanning
support and would give either that internal node or the path end degree at
least three.  Thus both spanning-path ends have incidence degree one.  They
are vertex-nodes, since edge-nodes have degree two.

At such a vertex end $x$, at least one of $a,b$ is missing.  Indeed, if
both were present, \noderef{PresentColors} would give incident exact edge
identities of the two respective colors.  They are distinct because
$a\ne b$, and \noderef{TwoColorIncidenceGraph} would give $x$ two
neighbors, contrary to its degree one.  The two ends of every nontrivial
$P_i$ are therefore distinct vertex-nodes missing $a$ or $b$.

Only now use (3), with elementariness unfolded by
\noderef{ColoredVertexSetElementary}.  If both $P_i$ are nontrivial,
their four ends are distinct by component disjointness, and at least two
lie outside $U$: otherwise three tree ends would assign the same one of
$a,b$ to two distinct vertices of $U$.  If, after relabeling, $P_1$ has
no edge-node, its connectedness and its meeting with $U$ make it the
singleton component at some $x\in U$.  Both $a$ and $b$ are missing at
$x$, since an incident edge of either color would be an adjacent edge-node
in $P_1$.  Elementariness makes $P_2$ nontrivial and forces both ends of
its spanning path outside $U$.  The two components cannot both be
singletons, because their distinct tree vertices would repeat both
missing colors.  Thus in all cases there are at least two outside ends of
nontrivial component paths.  From each selected end, follow its spanning
path up to its first vertex in $U$; this exists because each $P_i$ meets
$U$.  If the ends belong to different components, the resulting exit
subpaths are disjoint by the preceding component disjointness.  If they
are the two ends of one component, take the two terminal portions up to
the first and last encounters with $U$.  Their inside ends are distinct:
otherwise the two distinct boundary identities at that common vertex
would both have color $b$, since the closed color $a$ has no boundary
identity, contradicting properness.  Hence these portions too are
vertex-disjoint.  Denote the resulting exit subpaths by $Q_1,Q_2$ and
their distinct outside vertex ends by $r_1,r_2$.  Each $r_i$ is one of the
nontrivial full-component ends just analyzed.

We may reduce to
\[
        a\in\overline\sigma_n(T_n)
             \cap\overline\sigma_n(R_n).                     \tag{9}
\]
Here is the reduction with the recoloring hypotheses explicit.  The
support $v_n$ lies in both constituents: it lies in the ordered prefix
and that prefix lies in its closure.  Since $k\geq\Delta(G)+1$ by
\noderef{KTriple}, properness and \noderef{MaximumDegree}, together with
the complement definition \noderef{MissingColors}, give a color
$c$ missing at $v_n$.  Thus $c$ is common missing, and is closed on
$U$ by (5).  Apply the outside-tree transposition
\noderef{OutsideTreeColorSwap} to $(a,c)$: it fixes every exact edge
identity incident with $U$ and interchanges $(a,c)$ on every other edge
identity.  We verify its coloring properties here because only the two
displayed colors, rather than every color missing on $U$, are assumed
closed.  There is no $a$- or $c$-colored boundary identity, by the
hypothesis on $a$ and by (5) for $c$.  At a vertex of $U$ all incident
values are fixed.  At a vertex outside $U$, all nonboundary incident
values undergo the same transposition, while every boundary value avoids
$(a,c)$ and is fixed.  Thus injectivity of the transposition and the
adjacent-edge clause of \noderef{IsProperEdgeColoring} prove properness
of the new coloring.  The transposition fixes the uncolored value, so
exactly $e_0$ remains uncolored by \noderef{IsOneEdgeUncolored}.  It also
fixes all incident values and hence all missing-color
sets on $U$, proving stability there for every control set.

We first show that $b$ is present at $v_n$.  If it were missing there,
then, because $v_n\in X\cap Y$, equation (5) would make $b$ closed on
$U$.  The color $a$ is missing and closed on $U$ by the present
hypothesis.  A connected $(a,b)$-component which meets $U$ could then
have no vertex outside $U$: the first exact edge identity on an incidence
walk from an inside vertex to an outside vertex would be an $a$- or
$b$-colored boundary identity.  Thus both components would be trapped in
$U$.  If both are nontrivial, their four distinct ends lie in $U$ and
repeat one of the two missing colors.  If one is a singleton, its tree
vertex misses both colors, while an end of the other component is a
distinct tree vertex missing one of them.  Either possibility contradicts
(3) by \noderef{ColoredVertexSetElementary}.
So $b$ is present at $v_n$, whereas $c$ is missing there; in particular
$c\ne b$.

On each $Q_i$, the first boundary identity is fixed and has color $b$
because $a$ is closed.  Every later vertex lies outside $U$, so every
later edge identity of this exit portion is outside $U$ and its alternating
$(a,b)$-colors become alternating $(c,b)$-colors.  At the outside end
$r_i$, the new $(c,b)$-neighbor set is exactly the old $(a,b)$-neighbor
set: nonincident identities undergo the transposition, while identities
incident with $U$ have neither color $a$ nor $c$ by their two closedness
properties and are fixed.  Hence $r_i$ still has degree one and belongs
to the full $(c,b)$-component containing the transformed $Q_i$.

Let $K$ be the full $(c,b)$-component through $v_n$.  Since $c$ is
missing and $b$ is present there, properness gives $v_n$ exactly one
$(c,b)$-neighbor.  Thus $K$ contains an exact $b$-edge and, by
\noderef{TwoColorNontrivialPathComponent}, is a nontrivial path component.  It can
contain at most one of $Q_1,Q_2$.  Otherwise it contains the three
distinct degree-one vertex-nodes $v_n,r_1,r_2$, whereas its spanning path
from \noderef{TwoColorComponentSpanningPath} has only two ends.  Retain a
transformed $Q_i$ not contained in $K$, and let $L$ be its full component.
The exact edge-node on $Q_i$ and its degree-one outside end $r_i$ verify
that $L$ also satisfies \noderef{TwoColorNontrivialPathComponent}.  It meets $U$ at
the inside end of $Q_i$.  The dichotomy in
\noderef{TwoColorComponentsDisjoint} makes $K$ and $L$ disjoint, since
otherwise they would be equal and $Q_i$ would lie in $K$.  We have
therefore produced two distinct full nontrivial $(c,b)$-path components
meeting $U$, while $c$ is missing at the common vertex
$v_n\in X\cap Y$.  Replacing the coloring and $a$ by this new coloring
and $c$ yields (9).  The stable relation with the original coloring
composes with the two constituent stable relations by \noderef{sc2}, so
(2)--(8) remain available for this replacement.  We henceforth rename it
$\sigma_n$, and rename the two constructed components $P_1,P_2$.

By (3) and \noderef{ColoredVertexSetElementary}, the witnesses for (9) in
$X$ and $Y$ must be the same vertex;
otherwise $U$ would have two vertices missing $a$.  Call it $v$.
Take $P_1$ to be the component through $v$.  By (7), every other
$(a,b)$-component meeting $R_n$ equals $P_1$, so $P_2$ is disjoint
from $R_n$.  It nevertheless meets $T_n$.  Thus $(a,b)$ are not
$T_n$-interchangeable, and (8) implies
\[
                         a=\delta_n\quad\text{or}\quad
                         b=\delta_n.                           \tag{10}
\]

Suppose first that $b=\delta_n$.  The delta-boundary clause of
\noderef{HkuParallelBoundaryStructure} says that every exact
$\delta_n$-boundary identity of $T_n$ is incident with a vertex
strictly preceding $v_n$.  The positional definition
\noderef{TreeVertexOrder}, together with the prefix definition
\noderef{TreeSequencePrefix}, puts that vertex in the ordered prefix
$T_n(v_n)$ and hence in its closure $R_n$.  Stability fixes this incidence
and color assertion.
Fix one of the nontrivial $a,\delta_n$-components meeting $T_n$.  If it
has a vertex outside $T_n$, a shortest incidence walk from a vertex in
$T_n$ to one outside it uses an $a$- or $\delta_n$-boundary identity.
An $a$-boundary identity cannot leave $U$, because $a$ is closed on $U$;
if it leaves $T_n$, its outside endpoint is therefore in $U-X\subseteq
Y$ by (1).  A $\delta_n$-boundary identity has an inside endpoint in the
prefix and hence in $Y$, by the preceding paragraph.  In either subcase
the component meets $R_n$.

It remains possible that every vertex-node of the component lies in $X$.
Apply the endpoint analysis above to this component.  Its two distinct
vertex ends each miss $a$ or $\delta_n$.  The common witness $v$ from
(9) lies in $X\cap Y$ and misses $a$.  The terminal PE switch recorded by
\noderef{TashkinovSeriesStep}, with its
\noderef{MaximumDefectiveChoice} and \noderef{ParallelExtensionColor}
fields, starts with $\gamma_n$ missing at $v_n$ and an exact incident
$\delta_n$-edge, with the two colors distinct.  By
properness these are respectively zero and one incident edges of those
colors.  The terminal \noderef{TwoColorComponentSwap} contains that exact
$\delta_n$-edge and interchanges the two colors on it, so afterward
$\delta_n$ is missing at $v_n$; terminal stability transfers this to
$\sigma_n$.  The support belongs to the ordered prefix and hence
$v_n\in X\cap Y$.  By elementariness (3), unfolded through
\noderef{ColoredVertexSetElementary}, an end which misses $a$ must
equal $v$, and an end which misses $\delta_n$ must equal $v_n$.  Thus
every end belongs to $\{v,v_n\}\subseteq Y$ (or the distinctness of the
ends already makes this internal case impossible).  In either event the
component cannot be disjoint from $R_n$.  Consequently every
nontrivial component meeting $T_n$ meets $R_n$.  In particular both
$P_1,P_2$ meet $R_n$, contradicting (7).

Suppose instead that $a=\delta_n$.  Equation (5) says that
$\delta_n$ is closed on $U$.  Both ends of the root edge $e_0$
belong to the initial two vertices of every tree-sequence, hence to
$X\cap Y$.  Since $e_0$ alone is uncolored and
$k\geq\Delta(G)+1$, each end has at least two missing colors under the
proper coloring.  Choose
\[
 c\in\overline\sigma_n(T_n)\cap\overline\sigma_n(R_n),
 \qquad c\ne\delta_n.
\]
By (5), $c$ also is closed on $U$.  Apply
\noderef{OutsideTreeColorSwap} to $(\delta_n,c)$.  Both colors are missing
and closed on $U$, so the same mixed-adjacency check used above proves
that the result is proper, leaves exactly $e_0$ uncolored, and fixes
every incident value and missing-color set on $U$.

We repeat, at this new swap, the full-component check rather than merely
reusing vertex-disjointness.  Apply the endpoint construction above afresh
to the current $P_1,P_2$, obtaining two vertex-disjoint exit portions
$Q'_1,Q'_2$ with distinct outside degree-one ends $r'_1,r'_2$.  By (3)
and \noderef{ColoredVertexSetElementary},
the witnesses that the chosen $c$ is missing on $T_n$ and on $R_n$ are
the same vertex; call it $q\in X\cap Y$.  If $b$ were missing at $q$,
then (5) would close $b$ on $U$.  The colors $\delta_n$ and $b$ would
both be missing and closed on $U$, so the boundary and four-end argument
given above would put all four distinct ends of $P_1,P_2$ in $U$ and
contradict \noderef{ColoredVertexSetElementary}.  Thus $b$ is present at
$q$, $c$ is missing
there, and $c\ne b$.

The $c,b$-component $K'$ through $q$ is therefore nontrivial and has
$q$ as a degree-one vertex, so it satisfies
\noderef{TwoColorNontrivialPathComponent}.  Each $Q'_i$ becomes a $(c,b)$-exit
portion: its boundary identity has color $b$, all later
$\delta_n$-values become $c$, and its outside end remains degree one by
the same exact-neighbor comparison.  The spanning-path argument forbids
$K'$ from containing both $r'_1,r'_2$.  Choose a transformed $Q'_i$ not
in $K'$.  Its full component $L'$ contains its exact edge-node, has the
degree-one end $r'_i$, and meets $U$, hence is another
\noderef{TwoColorNontrivialPathComponent}.  By
\noderef{TwoColorComponentsDisjoint}, $K'$ and $L'$ are distinct full
components.  The root component $K'$ meets both $X$ and $Y$ at $q$, while
$L'$ meets $U=X\cup Y$ at the inside end of its retained exit portion.  If
that inside end lies in $X$, then $K',L'$ contradict (8), because
$c\ne\delta_n$ and $b\ne\delta_n$ in this branch.  If it lies in $Y$,
then they contradict (7), since $c$ is missing on $R_n$.  Both
alternatives in (10) are impossible, proving (iii).

Conclusion (ii) now follows.  Let
$a\in\overline\sigma_n(T_n)$.  If neither $a$ nor $b$ is
$\delta_n$, use (8).  Otherwise $\delta_n$ is missing at the PE
support $v_n$, which belongs to both $T_n$ and $R_n$, and (5) makes
it closed on $U$.  Apply (iii) with $\delta_n$ as the missing closed
color and the other color as its partner.  The definition
\noderef{TreeInterchangeable} is symmetric in its two displayed colors,
and interchangeability on $U$ restricts to $T_n$.  This proves (ii).

It remains to prove (iv).  Choose
$u\in X$ missing $\alpha$ and $v\in Y$ missing $\beta$.
Suppose that roots $z_1,z_2$ witness two unequal full paper
$\alpha,\beta$-path components $C_1,C_2$ meeting $U$, possibly including
a singleton component, as in the expanded
definition \noderef{TreeInterchangeable}.  In particular,
\noderef{TwoColorPathComponent} for either root gives
$\alpha\ne\beta$.

We first locate the two missing-color witnesses.  If $u\in Y$, then
$\alpha$ is common missing, so (5) makes it closed on $U$; conclusion
(iii) would say that $\alpha,\beta$ are $U$-interchangeable, contrary to
$C_1,C_2$.  Hence $u\notin Y$.  If $v\in X$, then $\beta$ is common
missing and closed by (5).  Applying conclusion (iii) with the two colors
reversed gives $\beta,\alpha$ interchangeability.  The incidence graph,
path-component predicate, and \noderef{TreeInterchangeable} are symmetric
in their two displayed colors, so this again contradicts $C_1,C_2$.
Thus $v\notin X$, and
\[
                         u\in X-Y,\qquad v\in Y-X.             \tag{11}
\]
In particular $u\ne v$.  Elementariness (3), in the sense of
\noderef{ColoredVertexSetElementary}, together with $\beta$
missing at $v$ and $\alpha$ missing at $u$, shows respectively that
$\beta$ is present at $u$ and $\alpha$ is present at $v$.  Properness
then makes each of these present colors occur on one exact incident edge.
Consequently the full components $P_u,P_v$ through the vertex-nodes
$u,v$ each contain a genuine edge-node and have their root vertex of
degree one.  Along with $\alpha\ne\beta$, these give
\noderef{TwoColorNontrivialPathComponent}, and in particular
\noderef{TwoColorPathComponent}.

We now identify them with the counterexample components.  By (1), each
$C_i$ meets $X$ or $Y$.  Conclusion (i), applied with the common
$Y$-missing color $\beta$ and using color symmetry, permits at most one
of $C_1,C_2$ to meet $Y$.  Conclusion (ii), using $\alpha$ missing on
$X$, permits at most one to meet $X$.  Since both components meet
$X\cup Y$, no one of them can meet both constituents: the other component
would then have to meet one constituent and violate the corresponding
uniqueness.  After relabeling, $C_1$ meets $X$ and not $Y$, while $C_2$
meets $Y$ and not $X$.  Since $P_u$ is a path component meeting $X$,
conclusion (ii), applied to $P_u$ and $C_1$, identifies them.  Similarly
conclusion (i) identifies $P_v$ with $C_2$.  Thus $P_u\ne P_v$,
and \noderef{TwoColorComponentsDisjoint} makes their full node supports
disjoint.  If any vertex-node of $P_v$ lay in $X$, then $P_v$ and $P_u$
would be two path components meeting $T_n$, and conclusion (ii) would
force them equal.  Therefore the full component $P_v$ has no vertex in
$X$.

Define $\sigma'_n$ by \noderef{TwoColorComponentSwap}, swapping
$\alpha,\beta$ on the full component rooted at the vertex-node $v$.
To instantiate \noderef{OutsideKempeRecoloring}, take the singleton root
set $\{\operatorname{inl}v\}$.  The required distinct-color field is
$\alpha\ne\beta$ above.  The selected object is the full component
$P_v$, not merely a tail.  Its outside-$T_n$ field is exactly the absence
of all $X$-vertex-nodes just proved.  Finally, expanding
\noderef{TwoColorComponentSwap} says for each exact identity $f\in E$
that its value is transposed precisely when the edge-node
$\operatorname{inr}f$ belongs to $P_v$, and is fixed otherwise.  This is
word-for-word the edge-value field of
\noderef{OutsideKempeRecoloring} for the singleton root set.  Hence
$\sigma'_n$ is an outside Kempe recoloring of $T_n$.
Apply \noderef{OutsideKempeRecoloringStable} to $T_n$, with control
set $D_n$, the stated properness and one-uncoloredness, and this
outside-chain fact.  It proves that $\sigma'_n$ is proper, leaves
exactly $e_0$ uncolored, and is
$T_n,D_n,\sigma_n$-stable.  Composing with the given
$(T_n,D_n,\varphi_n)$-stability via \noderef{sc2} makes it
$(T_n,D_n,\varphi_n)$-stable.  This is exactly the stability conclusion
for which the manuscript invokes LEM-Stable; the direct
\noderef{OutsideKempeRecoloringStable} interface has exactly the hypotheses
available here.

We next preserve the part of the closure preceding $v$.  In the
augmentation sequence from $T_n(v_n)$ to $R_n$, consider an edge
adjoined before $v$.  Its color is missing at an earlier vertex.  By
(3) and \noderef{ColoredVertexSetElementary}, $\alpha$, missing at
$u\in X-Y$, is missing at no vertex of
$Y$; and $\beta$, missing at $v$, is missing at no earlier vertex
of $Y$.  Therefore none of these exact adjoined edge identities has
color $\alpha$ or $\beta$.  The swap on $P_v$ fixes every one of
them and preserves the missing-color witness at every preceding vertex.
Expanding \noderef{TashkinovAugmentation} at each step proves that the
same initial augmentation sequence is valid under $\sigma'_n$, and it
still reaches a tree containing $v$.

Let $S$ be the last tree of that copied finite sequence, so
$v\in V(S)$.  By \noderef{TashkinovClosureExists}, choose a
$\sigma'_n$-closure $R'_n$ of $S$.  Compose its augmentation sequence
with the copied sequence from $T_n(v_n)$ to $S$.  Its final closedness
is unchanged, so the definition of \noderef{TashkinovClosure} makes
$R'_n$ a closure of the original ordered prefix, and it still contains
$v$.

Construct the exact ordered union $T_n\vee R'_n$ by starting with all
entries of $T_n$ and then appending, in their $R'_n$-order, precisely
the entries whose new vertex is not already in $T_n$.  Every appended
edge attaches its new vertex to $T_n$ or to an earlier appended vertex,
because $R'_n$ is an augmentation sequence from a prefix of $T_n$.
Its new vertex and edge identity have not appeared earlier, by the
distinct-entry and attachment clauses of \noderef{TreeSequence} for
$T_n$ and $R'_n$.  Thus this is a
tree-sequence.  Directly from the construction, $T_n$ is its ordered
initial segment, its vertex set is the union, every new entry is the
same exact entry of $R'_n$, every required right-hand entry occurs, and
their relative order is retained.  These are all five fields of
\noderef{OrderedTreeUnion}.

The stable vertex clause identifies
the terminal missing-color set with that under $\varphi_n$; together
with the copied support, closure, and ordered union, this is a hierarchy
base under $\sigma'_n$.  Now all actual hypotheses of
\noderef{ParallelClosureUnionElementary} have been discharged: the
original ETT and terminal PE, positivity and maximum property, the full
arbitrary-input lower package, properness, exact one-uncoloredness,
$(T_n,D_n,\varphi_n)$-stability, and the newly constructed base.  Hence
$V(T_n\vee R'_n)$ is elementary under $\sigma'_n$.

The swap is disjoint from $T_n$, so $\alpha$ remains missing at
$u$.  At the end $v$ of $P_v$, swapping the component changes the
missing color from $\beta$ to $\alpha$.  The copied augmentation
sequence puts $v$ in $R'_n$.  Thus the two distinct vertices $u,v$
of $T_n\vee R'_n$ both miss $\alpha$ under $\sigma'_n$, contradicting
the elementarity just obtained by
\noderef{ColoredVertexSetElementary}.  Therefore two distinct components
cannot meet $U$, which is conclusion (iv).

Every use of a boundary, augmentation, component swap, or ordered union
above carries elements of the original edge type $E$; consequently no
step identifies distinct parallel edges having the same endpoints.
\end{proof}

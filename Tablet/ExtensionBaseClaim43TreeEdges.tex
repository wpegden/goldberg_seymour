\begin{lemma}[Claim 4.3(b2), tree-edge clause]
In the setting of Algorithm (A), suppose that the initial coloring
\(\sigma_n\) is proper, leaves exactly \(e_0\) uncolored, and is stable
on the terminal ladder tree relative to its terminal stored colors and
displayed coloring.  If the restoration trace satisfies
\noderef{ExtensionBaseRestorationInvariant}, then its output
\(\varrho_1\) satisfies, for every exact displayed edge identity \(f\)
of \(T_j\),
\[
             \varrho_1(f)=\sigma_n(f)=\varphi_{j-1}(f).
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Fix a displayed edge identity \(f\) of \(T_j\).  At any restoration
step, \(T_j\) is an ordered initial segment of the current \(T_t\).
The attachment clause in \noderef{TreeSequence} places both endpoint
vertices of \(f\) in \(T_j\), hence in \(T_t\).  If the edge node of
\(f\) belonged to the switched component, both endpoint vertex nodes
would belong to that same component in
\noderef{TwoColorIncidenceGraph}.  The exact-intersection clause of
\noderef{ExtensionBaseRestorationInvariant} would identify both
endpoints with the single support \(v_t\), contradicting the loopless
law in \noderef{LooplessMultigraph}.  Thus no transition changes \(f\),
and induction over the trace gives
\[
                         \varrho_1(f)=\sigma_n(f).              \tag{1}
\]

Apply \noderef{ExtensionBaseClaimTreeEdgeColors} to the stated indices,
properness, exact-one-uncolored property, and terminal stability of
\(\sigma_n\).  Its displayed-edge conclusion, which retains the exact
edge identity \(f\), gives
\[
                         \sigma_n(f)=\varphi_{j-1}(f).          \tag{2}
\]
Combining (1) and (2) proves the tree-edge part of (b2).
\end{proof}

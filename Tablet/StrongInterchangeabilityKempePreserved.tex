\begin{lemma}[Claim 4.2: strong interchangeability survives an earlier Kempe change]
Let \(s,t\) be PE slots with respective supporting vertices
\(v_s,v_t\) and connecting-color pairs
\(S_s=\{\gamma_s,\delta_s\}\) and
\(S_t=\{\gamma_t,\delta_t\}\), with these four colors equal to the
corresponding exact gamma and delta fields of the certified slots.  Let
\(\varphi\) be a proper coloring
leaving exactly \(e_0\) uncolored, and let \(P\) be an arbitrary
nontrivial \((\gamma_t,\delta_t)\)-path component in the sense of
\noderef{TwoColorNontrivialPathComponent}.  Suppose that both displayed
color pairs are strongly interchangeable at their respective trees.
Assume either that \(v_s\ne v_t\), in which case
\(S_s\cap S_t=\varnothing\), or that \(s<t\).  Then the coloring
\[
       \varphi^*=\varphi/P
\]
is again proper, leaves exactly \(e_0\) uncolored, and
\(\gamma_s,\delta_s\) remain \(T_s\)-strongly interchangeable under
\(\varphi^*\).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Let
\[
 H_t=I_G(\varphi;\gamma_t,\delta_t),\qquad
 K=\noderef{TwoColorComponent}(G,\varphi,
       \gamma_t,\delta_t,z).
\]
The path clause of \noderef{TwoColorNontrivialPathComponent}, followed by
the distinct-color clause of \noderef{TwoColorPathComponent}, gives
\(\gamma_t\ne\delta_t\).  Thus
\noderef{TwoColorComponentSwapProper}, applied to the assumed properness
of \(\varphi\), proves that
\(\varphi^*=\noderef{TwoColorComponentSwap}(
G,\varphi,\gamma_t,\delta_t,z)\) is proper.

For every edge identity \(a\), expansion of the component-swap definition
gives
\[
                 \varphi^*(a)=\mathsf{none}
       \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
                 \varphi(a)=\mathsf{none}.                    \tag{1}
\]
Indeed, if the edge-node of \(a\) is outside \(K\), the value is fixed.
If it is in \(K\), the two colored values
\(\mathsf{some}(\gamma_t)\) and
\(\mathsf{some}(\delta_t)\) are exchanged, while every other value,
including \(\mathsf{none}\), is fixed.  The two clauses of
\noderef{IsOneEdgeUncolored}, together with (1), therefore say that
\(\varphi^*\) leaves precisely the same edge \(e_0\) uncolored.

Unpack the hypothesis at slot \(s\) using
\noderef{ETTStrongInterchangeable}.  It gives
\(\gamma_s\ne\delta_s\), the exact PE-kind and supporting-vertex
equalities at slot \(s\), and an index \(r=\epsilon(s)\) such that
\(s<r\), slot \(r\) is not an RE, every slot strictly between \(s\) and
\(r\) is an RE, and the required full-component cycle assertion holds at
every vertex of \(T_s-v_s\).  We retain this exact index \(r\), including
all of its kind and minimality clauses; none of those clauses mentions the
coloring being switched.

First suppose that \(v_s\ne v_t\).  The cross-support hypothesis makes
the two finite color pairs
\(\{\gamma_s,\delta_s\}\) and
\(\{\gamma_t,\delta_t\}\) disjoint.  For each edge \(a\), inspection of
\noderef{TwoColorComponentSwap} then gives both equivalences
\[
 \varphi^*(a)=\mathsf{some}(\gamma_s)
   \Longleftrightarrow
 \varphi(a)=\mathsf{some}(\gamma_s),\qquad
 \varphi^*(a)=\mathsf{some}(\delta_s)
   \Longleftrightarrow
 \varphi(a)=\mathsf{some}(\delta_s).                           \tag{2}
\]
An edge outside \(K\) is fixed; an edge in \(K\) is either fixed or has
one member of the \(t\)-pair exchanged for the other, and disjointness
prevents either member of the \(s\)-pair from being gained or lost.
By the adjacency definition in \noderef{TwoColorIncidenceGraph}, (2)
makes the entire \((\gamma_s,\delta_s)\)-incidence graph identical before
and after the switch.  Hence every full component in
\noderef{TwoColorComponent} is identical.  In particular, each cycle,
avoidance, and containment clause supplied by the retained witness \(r\)
continues to hold under \(\varphi^*\).  This proves the assertion in the
different-support case.

We may therefore assume \(v_s=v_t\).  The alternative in the hypotheses
then yields \(s<t\).  Before comparing any edge-node supports, split on
the supplied root \(z\).  The only degenerate possibility is
\[
 z=\mathsf{inr}(f),\qquad
 \varphi(f)\ne\mathsf{some}(\gamma_t),\qquad
 \varphi(f)\ne\mathsf{some}(\delta_t).                         \tag{3}
\]
Under (3), expansion of \noderef{TwoColorIncidenceGraph} shows that the
root edge-node has empty neighbor set in \(H_t\).  Any incidence-graph
walk starting at that node is therefore constant, so its connected
component is the singleton \(\{z\}\).  Now consider any edge \(a\).  If
its edge-node is outside this singleton, the swap fixes it.  If its
edge-node is in the singleton, injectivity of the \(\mathsf{inr}\) tag
gives \(a=f\), and both color tests in the swap fail by (3), so it is
again fixed.  Thus \(\varphi^*=\varphi\) pointwise.  Properness,
exact-one-uncoloredness, and the original strong-interchangeability
hypothesis at \(s\) give the conclusion immediately in this case.

For the rest of the proof, either \(z=\mathsf{inl}(u)\) for some vertex
\(u\), or \(z=\mathsf{inr}(f)\) and \(f\) has color \(\gamma_t\) or
\(\delta_t\).  We record the consequence needed below:
\[
 \mathsf{inr}(a)\in K
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 \varphi(a)=\mathsf{some}(\gamma_t)
       \ \text{or}\
 \varphi(a)=\mathsf{some}(\delta_t).                           \tag{4}
\]
If \(\mathsf{inr}(a)=z\), then the root is an edge-node and (4) is the
nondegenerate root alternative just retained.  Otherwise, membership in
the connected component \(K\) supplies an \(H_t\)-walk from \(z\) to
\(\mathsf{inr}(a)\).  Its endpoints are distinct, so the walk has positive
length and its last step gives a neighbor of \(\mathsf{inr}(a)\).
Expanding \noderef{TwoColorIncidenceGraph}, that neighbor must be a vertex
node incident with \(a\), and the same adjacency says exactly that the
color of \(a\) is one of the two values in (4).

We next justify the tree containment used in the manuscript.  The
strong-interchangeability hypothesis at \(t\) says that slot \(t\) has PE
kind and hence is not an RE.  If \(t<r\), then \(s<t<r\), so the
minimality clause belonging to the retained first-non-RE witness \(r\)
would make slot \(t\) an RE, a contradiction.  Therefore \(r\le t\).

Here the inclusion of trees is supplied by the certified series, not just
by the definition of one step.  The structure
\noderef{ExtendedTashkinovTree} contains the series
\({\tt ett.series}\), and \noderef{TashkinovSeries} gives a certificate
\({\tt ett.series.certified}(q+1)\) at every positive index.  For each
\(q\) with \(r\le q<t\), unpack this certificate.  Its index is positive,
so it cannot use the initial constructor of
\noderef{TashkinovSeriesStep}; each of its SE, PE, and RE constructors has
an \({\tt orderedExtension}\) field saying that the stored tree at slot
\(q\) is an ordered initial segment of the stored tree at slot \(q+1\).
Starting with the reflexive initial segment at \(r\), compose these fields
through \(q=r,\ldots,t-1\).  Composition is obtained directly from
\noderef{TreeSequenceInitialSegment}: compose the length inequalities and
then the entrywise vertex and edge equalities.  Thus \(T_r\) is an ordered
initial segment of \(T_t\).  Finally, every member of
\noderef{TreeSequenceVertexSet} is the value of the corresponding stored
vertex map; the composed vertex equalities therefore give
\[
                         V(T_r)\subseteq V(T_t).                \tag{5}
\]

Fix \(x\in V(T_s)\setminus\{v_s\}\), and write
\[
 C_x=\noderef{TwoColorComponent}(G,\varphi,
           \gamma_s,\delta_s,\mathsf{inl}(x)).
\]
The retained witness \(r\) says that \(C_x\) is a full component cycle,
that \(\mathsf{inl}(v_s)\notin C_x\), and that every vertex-node of
\(C_x\) belongs to \(V(T_r)\).  By (5), every such vertex lies in
\(V(T_t)\); since \(v_s=v_t\), the component \(C_x\) avoids \(v_t\).

We claim that a vertex-node of \(K\) which belongs to \(V(T_t)\) must be
\(\mathsf{inl}(v_t)\).  Otherwise let
\(y\in V(T_t)-\{v_t\}\) with \(\mathsf{inl}(y)\in K\).
Strong interchangeability at \(t\) makes the
\((\gamma_t,\delta_t)\)-component through \(y\) a full component cycle.
Because \(y\in K\), connected-component equality identifies this
component with \(K\).  On the other hand,
\noderef{TwoColorNontrivialPathComponent} supplies, through its path
clause, a node of \(K\) whose neighbor
set is subsingleton.  The full-support equality in
\noderef{TwoColorComponentCycle} places that node on the simple closed
walk from \noderef{TwoColorCycle}; at an occurrence of that node, the two
directions around the simple cycle give two distinct incidence-graph
neighbors.  This contradicts subsingletonness.  The claim follows.
Together with the preceding paragraph, it proves
\[
 \{\text{vertex-nodes of }C_x\}\cap
 \{\text{vertex-nodes of }K\}=\varnothing.                    \tag{6}
\]

We now prove edge-node disjointness, using (4) before invoking an endpoint.
Every edge-node \(\mathsf{inr}(a)\) in \(C_x\) lies on the full cycle
witness.  A neighboring step of that cycle and
\noderef{TwoColorIncidenceGraph} show that
\(\varphi(a)\) is \(\mathsf{some}(\gamma_s)\) or
\(\mathsf{some}(\delta_s)\).  Suppose that the same edge-node also lies
in \(K\).  Then (4) also puts its color in the \(t\)-pair.  Choose either
endpoint \(y\) of \(a\), whose incidence is expressed by
\noderef{Incident} for the endpoint pair stored by
\noderef{LooplessMultigraph}.  Incidence and the \(s\)-pair color make
\(\mathsf{inl}(y)\) adjacent to \(\mathsf{inr}(a)\) in the
\((\gamma_s,\delta_s)\)-incidence graph; incidence and (4) make the same
two nodes adjacent in \(H_t\).  Since a connected component contains
every neighbor of each of its nodes, \(\mathsf{inl}(y)\) belongs both to
\(C_x\) and to \(K\), contrary to (6).  Hence
\[
 \{\text{edge-nodes of }C_x\}\cap
 \{\text{edge-nodes of }K\}=\varnothing.                      \tag{7}
\]

It remains to verify equality of the full component after the switch, not
merely persistence of one closed walk.  By (7), every edge-node on the old
cycle is outside \(K\), so \noderef{TwoColorComponentSwap} fixes its exact
color.  Every adjacency of the old cycle therefore remains an adjacency
under \(\varphi^*\).  The old cycle is connected and its support is all of
\(C_x\), so every node of \(C_x\) belongs to the new component through
\(x\).

Conversely, the old support is closed under every new
\((\gamma_s,\delta_s)\)-adjacency.  For a vertex-node
\(\mathsf{inl}(y)\in C_x\), let \(\mathsf{inr}(a)\) be a neighbor under
\(\varphi^*\).  If \(\mathsf{inr}(a)\in K\), then (4), together with the
incidence of \(y\) and \(a\), makes \(\mathsf{inl}(y)\) a member of
\(K\), contradicting (6).  Thus \(\mathsf{inr}(a)\notin K\), the switch
fixes \(a\), and the same adjacency existed under \(\varphi\); hence
\(\mathsf{inr}(a)\in C_x\).  For an edge-node
\(\mathsf{inr}(a)\in C_x\), (7) again says that it is outside \(K\), so
its color is fixed; each of its new adjacent vertex-nodes was already an
old adjacent vertex-node and therefore lies in \(C_x\).  Induction along
any new incidence-graph walk starting at \(x\) now keeps every node of the
walk inside \(C_x\).  This proves the reverse inclusion and hence the
exact equality
\[
 \noderef{TwoColorComponent}(G,\varphi^*,
       \gamma_s,\delta_s,\mathsf{inl}(x))=C_x.                 \tag{8}
\]

The old simple closed walk is still a two-color cycle under
\(\varphi^*\), and (8) says that its support is exactly the new full
component.  Its avoidance of \(v_s\) and its vertexwise containment in
\(V(T_r)=V(T_{\epsilon(s)})\) are properties of that unchanged support.
Thus all component clauses required by
\noderef{ETTStrongInterchangeable} hold under \(\varphi^*\) for this
arbitrary \(x\).  The same is true for every
\(x\in V(T_s)-\{v_s\}\), while the exact PE kind, supporting vertex, and
least-non-RE witness \(r\) were retained unchanged.  This proves strong
interchangeability at \(s\); together with properness and
exact-one-uncoloredness established above, it completes the proof.
\end{proof}

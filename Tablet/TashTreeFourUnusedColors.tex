\begin{lemma}[Four unused missing colors]
Let \((\varphi,T)\) be a minimum bad pair and let \(T_q\) be the prefix
through \(y_q\), where \(1\le q<|E(T)|\).  Then at least four colors are
missing on \(V(T_q)\) and unused on the colored tree-edge identities
\(e_2,\ldots,e_q\); equivalently,
\[
  |\mathcal F(T_q,e,\varphi)|\ge4.
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The prefix is the tree-sequence \noderef{TreeSequencePrefix}, and it is
elementary by \noderef{TashTreeProperPrefixElementary}.  We count its
missing-color incidences.

By \noderef{KTriple}, \(k\ge\Delta(G)+1\), the coloring is proper, and the
exact edge \(e=e_1\) is its unique uncolored edge.  The attachment and
first-edge fields of \noderef{TreeSequence} say that \(e\) has endpoints
\(y_0,y_1\).  These vertices are distinct, and at either one at most
\(d_G(y_i)-1\le\Delta(G)-1\) colored edges are incident: the incident edge
identity \(e\) is uncolored.  Properness makes all colors present at a
vertex occur on distinct incident edges.  Hence each of \(y_0,y_1\) misses
at least
\(k-(\Delta(G)-1)\ge2\) colors.  Every other vertex has at most
\(d_G(v)\le\Delta(G)\) present colors and therefore misses at least one.

There are \(q+1\) vertices in the prefix, with distinctness supplied by
\noderef{TreeSequence}.  The total number of missing-color incidences is
therefore at least
\[
  2+2+(q-1)=q+3.
\]
Elementariness, in the precise pairwise-disjoint sense of
\noderef{ColoredVertexSetElementary}, makes the colors in all these
incidences distinct.  Thus \noderef{TreeMissingColors} contains at least
\(q+3\) colors.  The exact colored tree edges of this prefix are only
\(e_2,\ldots,e_q\), a list of \(q-1\) identities, so
\noderef{TreeUsedColors} contains at most \(q-1\) colors.  Removing those
used colors leaves at least
\((q+3)-(q-1)=4\) colors in \noderef{TreeFreeColors}, as required.
\end{proof}

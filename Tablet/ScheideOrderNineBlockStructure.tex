\begin{helper}[The two balanced blocks at order nine]
Let $(T,e,\varphi,N)$ be a balanced normal globally maximum triple in a
finite loopless multigraph $G$, with $t(G)=9$ and $h(G)=5$.  Then there is
a balanced normal globally maximum triple $(S,e',\psi,M)$ with
$S.\mathsf{length}=8$ and $M.\mathsf{height}=5$, and there are two
distinct colors $\gamma_0,\gamma_1$, such that, writing the positions of
$S$ as
\[
 (y_0,a_0,y_1,a_1,y_2,a_2,y_3,a_3,y_4,
   f_{0,0},u_{0,0},f_{0,1},u_{0,1},
   f_{1,0},u_{1,0},f_{1,1},u_{1,1}),
\]
the following hold for $i,j\in\{0,1\}$:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $f_{i,j}$ has exact endpoint pair $\{y_q,u_{i,j}\}$ for some
      $q\in\{0,1,2,3,4\}$;
\item $\psi(f_{i,j})=\gamma_i$;
\item $\gamma_i$ is missing at some $y_q$ with $0\leq q<5$;
\item the colored base identities $a_1,a_2,a_3$ have colors different
      from both $\gamma_0$ and $\gamma_1$.
\end{enumerate}
All displayed edges are individual edge identities; parallel identities
are not identified.
\end{helper}
\begin{proof}
Because the triple is balanced and has height five, its eight displayed
edge positions split after the five-vertex normal trunk into two
equal-colored pairs.  Call their colors initially $\gamma_0$ and
$\gamma_1$.  The \noderef{TashkinovTree} field supplies a missing witness
for $\gamma_0$ among the five earlier trunk vertices and a missing witness
for $\gamma_1$ among the first seven vertices.  The equality
$t(G)=9$, the global maximality field, and the injectivity in
\noderef{TreeSequence} give exactly nine distinct displayed vertices.

Choose the first trunk vertex missing $\gamma_0$.  If it is not the
first displayed vertex, apply \noderef{ScheideTrunkRotationPreserves}.
The exact formulas in \noderef{TrunkRotation} move that witness to the
first position, preserve every post-trunk vertex and edge identity, and
alter only the two normal trunk colors on the closing cycle.  To check
that the missing color really survives, use the elementary clause of
\noderef{ScheideGlobalMaximumOrder}: a color has at most one missing
witness in the displayed vertex set.  If $\gamma_0$ is the first normal
color, its witness was already the first vertex; if it is the second
normal color, its witness is the second vertex and the rotation changes
only the adjacent uncolored and first-normal-color identities; otherwise
no identity whose color is $\gamma_0$ changes.  Thus after rotation
$\gamma_0$ is missing at $y_0$, while both tail pairs and balancedness
are preserved.

The first identity of the first tail pair crosses from the trunk to its
new vertex by the attachment clause of \noderef{TreeSequence}.  Apply
\noderef{ScheideHeightFiveGammaEdges}.  It produces three distinct exact
roles: a $\gamma_0$-identity from $y_1$ to an outside-trunk vertex $v_1$,
a $\gamma_0$-identity from $y_4$ to an outside-trunk vertex $v_2$, and a
$\gamma_0$-identity with endpoint pair $\{y_2,y_3\}$.  Both identities
of the first balanced pair attach to the trunk.  Indeed, the second
cannot attach to the first new vertex, since the two distinct identities
would then be adjacent in the sense of \noderef{AdjacentEdges}, have the
same color, and contradict \noderef{IsProperEdgeColoring}.  Neither can
attach at $y_0$, where the color is missing, nor at $y_2$ or $y_3$, where
the internal identity already gives the unique proper $\gamma_0$
incidence.  Hence their attachment vertices are $y_1$ and $y_4$, one
each.  Properness identifies them with the two crossing identities just
obtained, preserving their individual edge identities.  Interchanging
the two complete edge--new-vertex entries if necessary preserves every
\noderef{TreeSequence} and \noderef{TashkinovTree} condition.  We may
therefore label them
\[
 f_{0,0}=y_1v_1,qquad f_{0,1}=y_4v_2.
\]

Now apply \noderef{ScheideHeightFiveOutsideLinks}.  It gives two distinct
parallel identities on the exact endpoint pair $\{v_1,v_2\}$, one of
each normal trunk color.  Together with the alternating normal closing
path and the three $\gamma_0$-identities above, these identities form
three matchings on the first seven vertices.  Each matching leaves only
its displayed missing vertex unmatched.  If $\gamma_1$ were either
normal trunk color or $\gamma_0$, neither of its two distinct tree
identities could attach to an earlier vertex: every nonmissing earlier
vertex already has a unique incident identity of that color, while the
missing vertex has none.  This contradicts the attachment field of
\noderef{TreeSequence}.  Thus $\gamma_1$ differs from both trunk colors
and from $\gamma_0$.

The elementary and closed clauses of
\noderef{ScheideGlobalMaximumOrder} next force an identity of each of the
two trunk colors and $\gamma_0$ between the last two vertices.  For any
one of these colors, elementarity makes it present at each last vertex.
Choose its incident identity at the first last vertex by
\noderef{PresentColors}.  Its other endpoint cannot lie outside the
tree, because the color is missing at its unmatched earlier vertex and
closedness forbids a boundary identity of a tree-missing color.  It
cannot be any of the first seven vertices: at the unique missing vertex
there is no such incidence, and at every other one properness already
fixes the displayed matching identity.  Looplessness excludes the first
last vertex itself.  The other endpoint is therefore the second last
vertex.  Applying this argument to the three pairwise distinct colors
gives three separately named parallel edge identities.

The witness for $\gamma_1$ lies either on the trunk or among
$v_1,v_2$.  In the latter case replace the displayed order by
\[
 (y_0,e,y_1,y_1v_1,v_1,v_1v_2,v_2,v_2y_4,y_4,
   a_1,y_2,a_3,y_3,f_{1,0},u_{1,0},f_{1,1},u_{1,1}),
\]
where the middle identity $v_1v_2$ has the second trunk color.  The
endpoint equalities show successively that every new vertex attaches to
an earlier vertex.  The edge identities are distinct by the old edge
injectivity, by their different endpoint pairs, or by their different
colors.  The missing witnesses are, successively, the old
$\gamma_0$-witness for the two crossing identities, the old second
trunk-color witness for $v_1v_2$, the old first-trunk-color witness for
$a_1,a_3$, and the assumed $\gamma_1$-witness for the last pair.
Consequently this is again a Tashkinov tree.  It has the same nine
vertices and the same proper coloring with the same unique uncolored
identity, so it is globally maximum.  Its first five vertices lie on the
simple alternating path through
$y_1,v_1,v_2,y_4,y_0$, hence it is normal of height five; its two tail
pairs have equal colors, hence it is balanced.  Relabeling colors and
positions now puts the $\gamma_1$-witness on the trunk while retaining
all conclusions above.  If the witness was already on the trunk, no
replacement is needed.

It remains to prove that the last balanced pair attaches to the trunk.
First some $\gamma_1$-identity joins the trunk to the last two vertices.
Otherwise properness and the attachment clauses force the two
$\gamma_1$ tree identities to match $\{v_1,v_2\}$ with the last two
vertices.  Together with the two parallel identities of the second
trunk color on $\{v_1,v_2\}$ and on the last pair, they form a complete
alternating cycle in \noderef{TwoColorIncidenceGraph}.  Exact endpoint
equalities, properness, and
\noderef{TwoColorIncidenceDegreeAtMostTwo} show that no incidence-graph
edge leaves this cycle, so it is the full
\noderef{TwoColorComponent}.  Switch it using
\noderef{TwoColorComponentSwap}.  By
\noderef{TwoColorComponentSwapProper} the new coloring is proper; it
fixes the trunk and the unique uncolored identity, and it changes both
last tree identities to the second trunk color, which is missing on the
trunk.  Thus the same ordered sequence is again a globally maximum
Tashkinov tree.

In that switched coloring, the $\gamma_0$--second-trunk-color component
through the two normal missing vertices is forced, identity by identity,
through seven multigraph vertices: the first crossing identity, one
switched last-pair identity, the $\gamma_0$ identity between the last
vertices, the other switched identity, the second crossing identity,
and the normal closing identity.  At the two ends missingness gives
incidence degree one; at each internal vertex properness gives exactly
the two displayed incidences; and each edge-node has exactly its two
loopless endpoints.  Hence this is the full component and every simple
path between its ends has twelve incidence steps.  But
\noderef{ScheideKempePathHeightBound} supplies such a path with
$\mathsf{length}/2+1\leq h(G)=5$, whereas here that quantity is seven, a
contradiction.

Choose a trunk vertex missing $\gamma_1$ and temporarily rotate it to
the first position with \noderef{ScheideTrunkRotationPreserves}.  Since
$\gamma_1$ differs from both trunk colors, this changes neither its
identities nor its missingness.  Apply
\noderef{ScheideHeightFiveGammaEdges} and then
\noderef{ScheideHeightFiveOutsideLinks}.  The first result gives exactly
two $\gamma_1$ crossings from the trunk and one internal trunk identity;
the second joins the outside ends by an identity of each temporary trunk
color.  At least one outside end is a last vertex by the preceding
paragraph.  An identity of the same trunk color already joins the two
last vertices, so properness forces the other outside end to be the
other last vertex.  Undoing the temporary rotation leaves these
$\gamma_1$ identities fixed.  Properness then identifies them with the
two identities of the last balanced pair.  Thus all four block
identities attach to the five base vertices, both block colors have base
missing witnesses, and the colors are distinct.

Finally, the three colored nonfirst base identities lie on the normal
closing path, so each has one of the two trunk colors.  The first block
color differs from both trunk colors by its missing incidence and the
proper crossing identities above, and the second does so by the matching
argument.  Therefore every such base color avoids both block colors.
These are exactly the four asserted properties.
\end{proof}

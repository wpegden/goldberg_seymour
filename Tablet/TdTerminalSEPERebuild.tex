\begin{helper}[Terminal SE and PE reconstruction]
Let (T) be a positive-rung extended Tashkinov tree generated by
\[
 \mathcal T=\{(T_i,\varphi_{i-1},S_{i-1},F_{i-1},\Theta_{i-1}):
                   1\leq i\leq n+1\},
\]
and suppose that (T) has the maximum property.  Keep the graph (G),
palette size (k), and distinguished uncolored edge (e_0) fixed, but
allow the certified input coloring to vary arbitrarily.  Assume explicitly
the following six assertions for every positive-rung ETT (T') on these
fixed data, with arbitrary certified input coloring \(\varphi'_0\), maximum
property, and rung number (s<n), writing \(\varphi'_s\) for its generating
coloring and \(\mathcal T'\) for its certified series:
\begin{enumerate}
\item (V(T')) is elementary with respect to \(\varphi'_s\).
\item Under every proper \((T'_{s+1},D'_s,\varphi'_s)\)-stable coloring
leaving precisely (e_0) uncolored, (T'_{s+1}) is interchangeable for
any pair of colors of which at least one is missing on (T'_{s+1}).
\item For each PE slot (a\leq s) with supporting vertex (u_a) and
(m(u_a)=b), every proper \((T'_a,D'_a,\varphi'_a)\)-stable coloring
leaving precisely (e_0) uncolored is
\((T'(u_a)-u_a,D'_{b-1},\varphi'_{b-1})\)-stable.  When (b>1), it is
also \((T'_{b-1},D'_{b-1},\varphi'_{b-1})\)-stable.  Moreover, the stored
color pairs at two distinct PE slots with distinct supporting vertices
and the same first-appearance index are disjoint.
\item If the terminal slot is PE with supporting vertex (u_s) and
stored colors \(\gamma'_s,\delta'_s\), then under every proper
\((T'_s,D'_s,\varphi'_s)\)-stable coloring leaving precisely (e_0)
uncolored, the \((\gamma'_s,\delta'_s)\)-component through (u_s)
contains exactly the one vertex (u_s) of (T'_s).
\item If \(\tau\) is any proper \((T'_s,D'_s,\varphi'_s)\)-stable
coloring leaving precisely (e_0) uncolored, (d) is defective on
(T'_s) under \(\tau\), and (x) is a nonminimum vertex of
\(I[\partial_{\tau,d}(T'_s)]\), then
(x\preceq_{T'}u_a) for every PE supporting vertex or SE extension
vertex (u_a) with (m(x)\leq a\leq s).
\item Every proper \((T'_s,D'_s,\varphi'_s)\)-stable coloring leaving
precisely (e_0) uncolored is a \(\varphi'_s\bmod T'_s\) coloring.
\end{enumerate}

Assume in addition that the terminal extension kind is SE or PE.  Then every proper \((T_n,D_n,\varphi_n)\)-stable coloring \(\sigma_n\)
leaving precisely (e_0) uncolored is a
\(\varphi_n\bmod T_n\) coloring in the sense of
\(\noderef{ModColoring}\).  In particular, the conclusion is universal
over every eligible ordered tree-sequence strictly extending (T_n),
and not merely over one selected closure.
\end{helper}
\begin{proof}
Write (n) for the rung number and put

\[
 T_n=\mathcal T.\mathit{tree}(n),\qquad
 \varphi_{n-1}=\mathcal T.\mathit{coloring}(n-1),\qquad
 \varphi_n=\mathcal T.\mathit{coloring}(n).
\]

Fix a proper coloring \(\sigma_n\) leaving exactly \(e_0\) uncolored in
the sense of \noderef{IsOneEdgeUncolored}
which is \((T_n,D_n,\varphi_n)\)-stable.  The last positive certificate
in \noderef{TashkinovSeries} is, by hypothesis, either the SE or the PE
constructor of \noderef{TashkinovSeriesStep}.  In both cases it contains
a \noderef{MaximumDefectiveChoice}; denote its selected coloring, support
vertex, outside endpoint, exact selected edge, and selected color by

\[
                   (\pi,v,u,f,\delta).                       \tag{1}
\]

Thus \(\pi\) is proper, leaves exactly \(e_0\) uncolored, and is
\((T_n,D_{n-1},\varphi_{n-1})\)-stable.  The same definition gives
\(v\in V(T_n)\), \(u\notin V(T_n)\),
\(G.\mathit{ends}(f)=\{v,u\}\), \(\pi(f)=\delta\), and the full
quantified maximality of \(v\) among defective vertices arising under
all such stable colorings.  Since the chosen edge is a
\noderef{DefectiveEdge}, its color equation and
\noderef{BoundaryColorEdges} show that \(\delta\) is a
\noderef{DefectiveColor} and that

\[
                  f\in\partial_{\pi,\delta}(V(T_n)).          \tag{2}
\]

Apply \noderef{TdReplacementPrefixExact}.  We obtain a proper coloring
\(\sigma_{n-1}\), leaving exactly \(e_0\) uncolored, such that

\[
 \sigma_{n-1}\text{ is }(T_n,D_{n-1},\varphi_{n-1})
 \text{-stable}.                                             \tag{3}
\]

If \(n=1\), the same result supplies an actual zero-rung replacement
series whose final tree and coloring are exactly \(T_n\) and
\(\sigma_{n-1}\).  If \(n>1\), it supplies an actual maximum
rung-\(n-1\) restriction of the original ETT, with displayed tree
\(T_n\), generating coloring \(\varphi_{n-1}\), and all retained data
equal to the original data.  It also supplies a corresponding maximum
replacement-prefix ETT, with exact displayed and final series tree
\(T_n\), exact final coloring \(\sigma_{n-1}\), and pointwise equality
of the retained ladder trees, raw stored sets, connecting-edge sets, and
kinds.  Every positive replacement coloring is stable relative to its
original counterpart.  We call this common zero- or positive-rung object
the replacement prefix.

We next prove the stronger relation

\[
 \sigma_{n-1}\text{ is }
 (T_n,D_{n-1}\cup\{\delta\},\pi)\text{-stable}.               \tag{4}
\]

This is the first point at which the two terminal kinds require different
arguments.

Suppose first that the last kind is SE.  The SE constructor says
\(\varphi_n=\pi\), and
\noderef{TdReplacementPrefixExact} says
\(\sigma_{n-1}=\sigma_n\).  The first inclusion in
\noderef{HkuMissingStoredMonotone} gives

\[
 \overline\varphi_{n-1}(T_n)\cup D_{n-1}
 \subseteq \overline\pi(T_n)\cup D_n.                        \tag{5}
\]

The same constructor gives \(S_n=\{\delta\}\).  Expanding
\noderef{TashkinovStoredColors}, either \(\delta\) is missing on
\(T_n\) under \(\pi\), or it survives in \(D_n\).  Hence

\[
 D_{n-1}\cup\{\delta\}
 \subseteq\overline\pi(T_n)\cup D_n.                         \tag{6}
\]

The terminal stability hypothesis identifies the missing sets of
\(\sigma_n\) and \(\pi\) at every vertex of \(T_n\), by the vertex
clause of \noderef{StableColoring}.  For an edge incident with \(T_n\),
if its value under either coloring lies in
\(\overline\pi(T_n)\cup D_{n-1}\cup\{\delta\}\), (5)--(6) put that
value in the control set for the given terminal stability.  The symmetric
edge criterion \noderef{sc1} therefore makes the two edge values equal.
A second application of \noderef{sc1}, with the smaller displayed
control set, proves (4).

Now suppose that the last kind is PE, and write \(\gamma\) for its
recorded gamma color.  Current assertion (iv), in exactly the form
\noderef{ExtensionBase}, applies to the assumed lower-rung package and
gives

\[
 P_v(\gamma,\delta,\sigma_n)\cap V(T_n)=\{v\}.               \tag{7}
\]

Apply \noderef{ParallelExtensionUndoStable} using (7).  Its inverse
switch is exactly the PE formula in
\noderef{TdReplacementPrefixExact}, so its recovered coloring is our
\(\sigma_{n-1}\):

\[
 \sigma_{n-1}=\sigma_n/P_v(\gamma,\delta,\sigma_n).           \tag{8}
\]

The cited undo lemma also identifies the old witnesses \(\pi\) and the
old second-stable coloring and proves successively, with the enlarged
control set, stability relative to each of them.  In particular it proves
(4), as well as (3), with all switches performed on exact edge identities.

We now use (4) in both branches.  Properness and the exact
one-uncolored-edge condition hold for both \(\pi\) and
\(\sigma_{n-1}\).  Since \(\delta\) belongs to the enlarged control
set, \noderef{sc1} applied to (4) gives, for every boundary edge identity
\(a\),

\[
 \pi(a)=\delta\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \sigma_{n-1}(a)=\delta.
\]

Consequently

\[
 \partial_{\pi,\delta}(V(T_n))
 =\partial_{\sigma_{n-1},\delta}(V(T_n))                    \tag{9}
\]

as sets of exact edge identities.  Equations (2) and (9) retain the
same defective edge \(f\), the same incidence with \(v\), the same
outside endpoint \(u\), the same endpoint equation, and the equation
\(\sigma_{n-1}(f)=\delta\).  Because the complete boundary set is
equal, its two distinct witnesses supplied by
\noderef{DefectiveColor} remain distinct witnesses under
\(\sigma_{n-1}\).  Hence \(\delta\) is defective and \(v\) is a
\noderef{DefectiveVertex} under \(\sigma_{n-1}\).

The stable-coloring classes used by the old and new choices are identical.
Indeed, (3), symmetry in \noderef{sc2}, and transitivity in
\noderef{sc2} show that a proper coloring leaving exactly \(e_0\)
uncolored is \((T_n,D_{n-1},\sigma_{n-1})\)-stable if and only if it is
\((T_n,D_{n-1},\varphi_{n-1})\)-stable.  Apply the quantified maximality
field of the old \noderef{MaximumDefectiveChoice} to a coloring and a
defective vertex in this common class.  It gives equality with \(v\) or
precedes \(v\) in the tree order, exactly the maximality field required
for a new choice.  Reflexivity from \noderef{sc2} supplies the selected
stability of \(\sigma_{n-1}\) relative to itself.  Together with (9)
and the unchanged structural endpoint data, this constructs a new
\noderef{MaximumDefectiveChoice}

\[
 \mathcal M^*=(\sigma_{n-1},v,u,f,\delta)                    \tag{10}
\]

for the replacement prefix.

The replacement prefix ends at the exact tree \(T_n\).  In the zero-rung
case this is the initial closure; in the positive-rung case it is the
last closure in its certified prefix.  Thus \(V(T_n)\) is closed under
\(\sigma_{n-1}\), by \noderef{TashkinovSeriesStep} and
\noderef{ColoredVertexSetClosed}.  On the other hand, the two distinct
members of the defective boundary set in (9) have the same color
\(\delta\).  They violate the boundary-color uniqueness clause of
\noderef{ColoredVertexSetStronglyClosed}.  Therefore

\[
 V(T_n)\text{ is closed but not strongly closed under }
 \sigma_{n-1}.                                                \tag{11}
\]

We record next the exact identification of every earlier PE slot.  This is
vacuous when \(n=1\).  When \(n>1\), fix a positive retained slot \(a<n\)
whose original, equivalently replacement, kind is PE.  Denote the old and
replacement PE data by

\[
 (v_a,u_a,f_a,\delta_a,\gamma_a),\qquad
 (v_a^*,u_a^*,f_a^*,\delta_a^*,\gamma_a^*).                  \tag{12}
\]

The prefix correspondence and the PE constructor in
\noderef{TashkinovSeriesStep} give equality of the singleton exact-edge
sets and equality of the unordered stored pairs.  Thus
\(f_a^*=f_a\).  The two endpoint equations from
\noderef{MaximumDefectiveChoice}, together with membership of both
supports in the common ladder tree and exclusion of both outside endpoints,
orient the common endpoint pair and give

\[
                         v_a^*=v_a,\qquad u_a^*=u_a.           \tag{13}
\]

For the old PE, \noderef{ParallelExtensionColor} makes \(\gamma_a\)
missing at \(v_a\) under its selected coloring.  The second-stable
relation preserves that missingness by \noderef{StableColoring}; it also
fixes the exact edge \(f_a\) with color \(\delta_a\), because that edge
is incident with the ladder tree and \(\delta_a\) is in the enlarged
control set.  The two colors are distinct: \(\gamma_a\) is absent at
\(v_a\), while \(f_a\) makes \(\delta_a\) present there, using
\noderef{MissingColors} and \noderef{PresentColors}.  Properness from
\noderef{IsProperEdgeColoring} makes \(f_a\) the unique incident
\(\delta_a\)-edge.  After the PE switch, the definition
\noderef{TwoColorComponentSwap} therefore makes \(\gamma_a\) present
and \(\delta_a\) missing at \(v_a\).  The identical argument in the
replacement PE constructor makes \(\gamma_a^*\) present and
\(\delta_a^*\) missing at \(v_a^*\) after its switch.

The positive-stage stability field of
\noderef{CorrespondingExtendedTashkinovTree}, evaluated at the common
vertex in (13), identifies the two post-switch missing sets.  We have now
verified every hypothesis of
\noderef{TdEarlierPEWitnessIdentification}: common singleton edge, common
unordered stored pair, common post-switch missing set, each delta missing,
and each gamma present.  It follows that

\[
 f_a^*=f_a,\quad v_a^*=v_a,\quad u_a^*=u_a,\quad
 \delta_a^*=\delta_a,\quad\gamma_a^*=\gamma_a.               \tag{14}
\]

We now verify the branch-specific fields following the new choice (10).
In SE, let \(\tau\) be any proper coloring leaving exactly \(e_0\)
uncolored and stable relative to
\((T_n,D_{n-1}\cup\{\delta\},\sigma_{n-1})\).  Relation (4) and
transitivity in \noderef{sc2} make \(\tau\) stable relative to
\((T_n,D_{n-1}\cup\{\delta\},\pi)\).  The old SE universal field in
\noderef{TashkinovSeriesStep} therefore gives

\[
 \overline\tau(u)\cap\overline\tau(T_n)=\varnothing.
\]

Thus the universal disjointness field is transported with its arbitrary
coloring \(\tau\), rather than merely asserted to persist.

In PE, the old constructor supplies an actual coloring \(\rho\) witnessing
failure of that disjointness: it is proper, leaves exactly \(e_0\)
uncolored, is
\((T_n,D_{n-1}\cup\{\delta\},\pi)\)-stable, and has a specific color
in \(\overline\rho(u)\cap\overline\rho(T_n)\).  By symmetry of (4) in
\noderef{sc2}, \(\pi\) is stable relative to \(\sigma_{n-1}\) for the
same enlarged set; transitivity in \noderef{sc2} then makes \(\rho\)
stable relative to \(\sigma_{n-1}\).  The same coloring and the same
color in the same two missing sets therefore witness the replacement
existential failure field.

Continue in the PE branch.  The vertex clause of (4) transports the old
gamma-rule missingness, so

\[
                         \gamma\in\overline{\sigma}_{n-1}(v). \tag{15}
\]

We verify the history clause of \noderef{ParallelExtensionColor}, not
just its first conjunct.  If the old gamma rule chooses a latest earlier
PE slot \(h\) with support \(v\) and \(\gamma=\delta_h\), then (14)
gives the same replacement support and delta at \(h\).  If a later
replacement PE slot through \(n-1\) had support \(v\), applying (14) at
that slot would give a later old PE slot with support \(v\), contrary to
the old latestness assertion.  Hence \(h\) is latest in the replacement
history as well.  If instead the old gamma rule says that no earlier PE
slot has support \(v\), a hypothetical replacement slot with that support
would again contradict (14).  For \(n=1\) the latter alternative follows
directly because a positive earlier index bounded by zero cannot exist.
Together with (15), these are exactly the two alternatives in
\noderef{ParallelExtensionColor}.

For the replacement PE constructor take its second-stable coloring also
to be \(\sigma_{n-1}\).  It is proper and leaves exactly \(e_0\)
uncolored, and its enlarged stability relative to the selected coloring
is reflexivity in \noderef{sc2}.  We construct its
\noderef{TreeExit} explicitly.  By (9), the exact edge \(f\) has color
\(\delta\) and is incident with \(v\) under \(\sigma_{n-1}\); by
(15), no incident edge has color \(\gamma\).  The colors are distinct,
since the same vertex cannot both miss \(\gamma\) and have the
\(\gamma\)-colored edge \(f\) if \(\gamma=\delta\),
and properness plus \noderef{AdjacentEdges} makes \(f\) the only incident
\(\delta\)-edge.  Thus \(v\) has degree one in the graph
\noderef{TwoColorIncidenceGraph} for
\((\sigma_{n-1};\gamma,\delta)\), and its component is nontrivial.

The swap in (8) interchanges \(\gamma\) and \(\delta\) on precisely this
component and does not change its incidence support, by
\noderef{TwoColorComponentSwap}.  Hence (7) says that this component
under \(\sigma_{n-1}\) meets \(V(T_n)\) exactly at \(v\).  Apply
\noderef{TwoColorComponentSpanningPath}.  The supplied simple spanning
path has \(v\) as an endpoint, since \(v\) has degree one.  Its other
endpoint \(x\) is a graph-vertex node: a colored edge-node has its two
distinct endpoint vertices as neighbors by the loopless law in
\noderef{LooplessMultigraph}, and an edge-node endpoint would
force either a third neighbor at an internal path node or a second neighbor
at \(v\), contrary to \noderef{TwoColorIncidenceDegreeAtMostTwo}.  The
one-vertex intersection gives \(x\notin V(T_n)\).  The same degree bound
shows that \(x\) has no neighbor beyond its path neighbor; otherwise that
neighbor already lies on the spanning path and again creates degree three
internally or degree two at \(v\).  Thus one of \(\gamma,\delta\) is
missing at \(x\), by \noderef{MissingColors} and
\noderef{PresentColors}.  Reversing the spanning path gives a nontrivial
\(T_n\)-exit path from \(x\) to \(v\) in the sense of
\noderef{TreeExitPath}, and hence the required \noderef{TreeExit}.

Finally, (8) can be inverted exactly.  The first switch only exchanges
the two colors on one full component; its incidence support is therefore
the same after the switch.  Expanding \noderef{TwoColorComponentSwap}
edge by edge, a second switch sends \(\gamma\) back to \(\delta\), sends
\(\delta\) back to \(\gamma\), and fixes every other colored or uncolored
value.  Extensionality of partial colorings gives

\[
 \sigma_n=\sigma_{n-1}/P_v(\gamma,\delta,\sigma_{n-1}),       \tag{16}
\]

which is the exact current-coloring equation required by the replacement
PE constructor.  In SE the corresponding equation is simply
\(\sigma_n=\sigma_{n-1}\).

It remains to verify the priority field: no revisiting extension is
available for the replacement prefix.  When \(n=1\), a hypothetical
\noderef{RevisitingExtensionCertificate} at current index zero would
contain a positive earlier PE index at most zero, an immediate
contradiction.  When \(n>1\), apply
\noderef{P2971_T1_PositivePrefixNoRevisiting} with original ETT \(T\),
the original rung-\(n-1\) prefix and corresponding replacement prefix
from \noderef{TdReplacementPrefixExact}, and coloring
\(\sigma_{n-1}\).  The prefix package supplies the exact retained
ladder-tree, coloring, raw stored-set, connecting-edge, kind, and computed
stored-color maps, both maximum witnesses, and the replacement final
tree \(T_n\) and final coloring \(\sigma_{n-1}\).
Relation (3) supplies the required preterminal stability, and the prefix
package supplies properness and unique uncoloredness.  The assumed six
lower-rung assertions include precisely assertions (ii) and (iv) required
by the helper.  Finally the original terminal SE or PE constructor of
\noderef{TashkinovSeriesStep} supplies the original no-revisiting
premise.  Thus the helper excludes a revisiting certificate at the
replacement prefix's final index, giving the required priority field.

We finish by proving the universal clause of \noderef{ModColoring}.  Let
\(T^*\) be an arbitrary eligible ordered tree-sequence with
\(T_n\prec T^*\).  In PE, eligibility is a
\(\sigma_n\)-\noderef{TashkinovAugmentationSequence} from \(T_n\) to
\(T^*\).  In SE, it supplies an exact edge \(f^*\), an exact seed
\(A=T_n+f^*\) in the sense of \noderef{TreeSequenceAdjoinEdge}, and an
augmentation sequence from \(A\) to \(T^*\), with
\(F_n=\{f^*\}\).  The old SE constructor gives \(F_n=\{f\}\), so the
singleton equality identifies \(f^*=f\).

Apply \noderef{TashkinovClosureExists} to \(T^*\) under \(\sigma_n\),
and call the resulting closure \(C\).  By \noderef{TashkinovClosure}, it
supplies an augmentation sequence from \(T^*\) to \(C\), and
\(V(C)\) is closed.  Compose the reflexive-transitive routes in
\noderef{TashkinovAugmentationSequence}.  We obtain

\[
 \begin{array}{ll}
 T_n\longrightarrow T^*\longrightarrow C,&\text{in PE},\\
 A=T_n+f\longrightarrow T^*\longrightarrow C,&\text{in SE}.
 \end{array}                                                  \tag{21}
\]

Every one-step \noderef{TashkinovAugmentation} preserves its source as
an ordered initial segment.  Thus
\(T_n\prec T^*\preccurlyeq C\); (21) is the exact closed-extension
field required in the appropriate constructor.

Append one terminal certificate to the replacement prefix.  In both
branches use the choice \(\mathcal M^*\) from (10), the closed and
not-strongly-closed facts (11), and the no-RE result above.  In SE use the
transported universal disjointness field, set the next coloring to
\(\sigma_n=\sigma_{n-1}\), set the raw stored set, connecting-edge set,
kind, chosen vertex, outside vertex, and delta field to

\[
 \{\delta\},\quad\{f\},\quad\mathrm{SE},\quad v,\quad u,
 \quad\delta,
\]

set gamma to absent, use the exact adjoined seed \(A\), and use the SE
route in (21).  In PE use the transported existential failure witness,
the verified history-sensitive gamma rule, the reflexive second-stable
coloring, the constructed exit, and equation (16).  Set the corresponding
fields to

\[
 \{\delta,\gamma\},\quad\{f\},\quad\mathrm{PE},\quad v,
 \quad u,\quad\delta,\quad\gamma,
\]

and use the PE route in (21).  Properness and exact one-edge
uncoloredness of the preceding coloring come from the prefix package;
those of the current coloring are the hypotheses on \(\sigma_n\).  Thus
every field, including strictness and ordered extension, of the selected
constructor in \noderef{TashkinovSeriesStep} has been verified.

Appending this certificate gives a \noderef{TashkinovSeries} of rung
\(n\), with final series tree exactly \(C\) and final coloring exactly
\(\sigma_n\).  Present the originally arbitrary \(T^*\) as the displayed
tree.  The relation \(T_n\preccurlyeq T^*\), its strict length inequality,
the relation \(T^*\preccurlyeq C\), and the exact final-color equation
verify respectively the above-penultimate, strict, below-final, and
generating-color fields of \noderef{ExtendedTashkinovTree}.

Finally we check the complete correspondence record.  Use the replacement
input and all prefix data from \noderef{TdReplacementPrefixExact}.  The
prefix keeps every ladder tree through \(T_n\), every earlier raw stored
set, every earlier exact connecting-edge set, and every earlier kind.
The appended constructor uses the old terminal singleton edge and the old
terminal raw stored set and kind, so these equalities also hold at index
\(n\).  All prefix colorings are proper and leave exactly \(e_0\)
uncolored; every positive prefix stage has the stability supplied by the
prefix correspondence.  At the new terminal stage, properness and unique
uncoloredness are the hypotheses on \(\sigma_n\), and its required
\((T_n,D_n,\varphi_n)\)-stability is precisely the terminal hypothesis
with which we began.  Rung equality is literal, the series used by the
displayed ETT is the appended series itself, and its final generating
color is \(\sigma_n\).  These checks give every field of
\noderef{CorrespondingExtendedTashkinovTree} for \(T^*\).

Since \(T^*\) was an arbitrary eligible strict extension, and since the
initial properness and exact one-uncolored-edge conditions on \(\sigma_n\)
were also retained, the definition \noderef{ModColoring} now gives the
claim.
\end{proof}

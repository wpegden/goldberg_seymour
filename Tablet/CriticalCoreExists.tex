\begin{lemma}
Every finite multigraph $G$ has an edge restriction $H=G|F$ such that
$\chi'(H)=\chi'(G)$ and $H$ is critical.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Consider all subsets $F\subseteq E(G)$ for which
$\chi'(G|F)=\chi'(G)$.  This finite family is nonempty because it contains
$E(G)$.  Choose a member $F$ of minimum cardinality and put $H=G|F$.
The chosen equality gives $\chi'(H)=\chi'(G)$.

Let $e$ be an edge identity of $H$.  Deleting $e$ gives, up to the canonical
identification of retained edge identities, the restriction
$G|(F\setminus\{e\})$.  By \noderef{ChromaticIndexRestrict}, its chromatic
index is at most $\chi'(H)$.  Equality would put
$F\setminus\{e\}$ in the chosen family, contradicting its strictly smaller
cardinality.  Thus $\chi'(H-e)<\chi'(H)$ for every edge $e$ of $H$, which is
exactly criticality according to \noderef{Critical}.
\end{proof}

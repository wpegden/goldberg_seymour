\begin{lemma}[At most one stored color survives per series round]
For every certified Tashkinov series and every data index \(i\),
\[
 |D_i|\leq i.
\]
Here \(D_i\) is the finite set \noderef{TashkinovStoredColors}; in
particular, \(D_0=\varnothing\).  The natural number on the right is the
value of the zero-based Lean index.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Write
\[
 U_m=\bigcup_{0\leq h\leq m}S_h,
 \qquad M_\psi(T)=\overline\psi(T).
\]
Thus \noderef{TreeMissingColors} says that \(M_\psi(T)\) is the union of
the vertexwise missing-color sets over \(V(T)\), and
\noderef{TashkinovStoredColors} gives
\[
 D_0=\varnothing,
 \qquad D_m=U_m\setminus M_{\varphi_m}(T_m)\quad(m>0).            \tag{1}
\]
We induct on the natural value of the data index.  Equation (1) proves
the assertion at index zero.

Fix a positive index \(n\), and put \(a=n-1\).  The certificate field of
\noderef{TashkinovSeries} at slot \(n=a+1\) supplies exactly one of the
three noninitial constructors in \noderef{TashkinovSeriesStep}.  Before
considering those constructors, we record the tree bookkeeping which is
available only when \(n>1\).  In that case \(n-1\) is itself a positive
slot.  Unpack the series certificate at that preceding slot.  It cannot
be the initial constructor, and each of its SE, PE, and RE constructors
has an ordered-extension field saying that its input tree \(T_{n-1}\)
is an ordered initial segment of its output tree \(T_n\).  Hence, by the
entrywise relation \noderef{TreeSequenceInitialSegment}, every vertex of
\(T_{n-1}\) is a vertex of \(T_n\).  The union formula in
\noderef{TreeMissingColors} then gives, for every coloring \(\psi\),
\[
 M_\psi(T_{n-1})\subseteq M_\psi(T_n)\qquad(n>1).                \tag{2}
\]
Notice that (2) comes from the certificate at slot \(n-1\), not from the
current certificate at slot \(n\).

We now prove, constructor by constructor, that at most one color survives
in addition to \(D_{n-1}\).

Suppose first that the current constructor is RE.  Its available
\noderef{RevisitingExtensionCertificate} contains an earlier index
\(h\) with \(0<h\leq n-1\) and \(\Theta_h=\mathrm{PE}\).  In particular,
an RE is impossible when \(n=1\), since then it would require
\(0<h\leq0\).  At general \(n\), unpack the
\noderef{TashkinovSeries} certificate at \(h\).  Positivity excludes its
initial constructor, and the recorded PE kind excludes its SE and RE
constructors.  Its PE constructor and the current RE constructor in
\noderef{TashkinovSeriesStep} therefore give, for the same ordered pair of
colors,
\[
 S_h=\{\delta_h,\gamma_h\}=S_n,
 \qquad \varphi_n=\varphi_{n-1}.                                \tag{3}
\]
Thus \(S_n\subseteq U_{n-1}\), so \(U_n=U_{n-1}\).  We have already
seen that this case forces \(n>1\), and (2), with
\(\psi=\varphi_{n-1}\), shows
\[
\begin{aligned}
 D_n
 &=U_{n-1}\setminus M_{\varphi_{n-1}}(T_n)\\
 &\subseteq U_{n-1}\setminus M_{\varphi_{n-1}}(T_{n-1})
  =D_{n-1}.                                                      \tag{4}
\end{aligned}
\]

Suppose next that the current constructor is SE, and denote its selected
coloring and defective color by \(\pi\) and \(\delta_n\).  The exact
fields in \noderef{TashkinovSeriesStep} say
\(\varphi_n=\pi\) and \(S_n=\{\delta_n\}\).  The selected coloring in
\noderef{MaximumDefectiveChoice} is stable relative to
\(\varphi_{n-1}\) on \(T_n\).  The vertex clause of
\noderef{StableColoring}, followed by the union in
\noderef{TreeMissingColors}, yields
\[
 M_{\varphi_n}(T_n)=M_\pi(T_n)=M_{\varphi_{n-1}}(T_n).          \tag{5}
\]
When \(n=1\), the initial constructor in
\noderef{TashkinovSeriesStep} gives \(S_0=\varnothing\), while (1) gives
\(D_0=\varnothing\).  Consequently
\[
 D_1=(S_0\cup S_1)\setminus M_{\varphi_1}(T_1)
     \subseteq\{\delta_1\}=D_0\cup\{\delta_1\}.                \tag{6}
\]
When \(n>1\), an element of \(D_n\) which is not \(\delta_n\) belongs
to \(U_{n-1}\) and, by (5), is not in
\(M_{\varphi_{n-1}}(T_n)\).  Inclusion (2) says it is then not in
\(M_{\varphi_{n-1}}(T_{n-1})\), so (1) puts it in \(D_{n-1}\).
Together with the exceptional color this proves
\[
 D_n\subseteq D_{n-1}\cup\{\delta_n\}                           \tag{7}
\]
in every SE.

It remains to treat a PE.  Use the notation from its constructor in
\noderef{TashkinovSeriesStep}: \(\pi\) is the coloring selected by the
maximum-defective choice, \(\rho\) is the second stable coloring,
\(v_n\) is the support, \(f_n\) is the connecting edge,
\(\delta_n\) is its selected color, and \(\gamma_n\) is the parallel
color.  The constructor gives
\[
 S_n=\{\delta_n,\gamma_n\},
 \qquad
 \varphi_n=\rho/K,                                              \tag{8}
\]
where \(K\) is the \((\gamma_n,\delta_n)\)-component through the
vertex-node \(v_n\).  We prove directly the missing-color propagation
needed below.

First, the vertex clause of the stability belonging to
\noderef{MaximumDefectiveChoice}, and then the vertex clause of the second
\noderef{StableColoring} relation recorded by the PE constructor, give
for every \(x\in V(T_n)\)
\[
 \overline{\varphi_{n-1}}(x)=\overline\pi(x)=\overline\rho(x).  \tag{9}
\]
The exact connecting data in \noderef{MaximumDefectiveChoice} say that
\(v_n\in V(T_n)\), that \(v_n\) is incident with the edge identity
\(f_n\), and that \(\pi(f_n)=\delta_n\).  On the other hand,
\noderef{ParallelExtensionColor} says that \(\gamma_n\) is missing at
\(v_n\) under \(\pi\).  By (9), \(\gamma_n\) is also missing there under
\(\rho\).  For the edge color, the edge clause of the second
\noderef{StableColoring} relation applies to \(f_n\): the incidence just
recorded is incidence with \(T_n\), in the sense of
\noderef{Incident}, and \(\pi(f_n)=\delta_n\) lies in that relation's
control set \(D_{n-1}\cup\{\delta_n\}\).  Hence
\[
 \rho(f_n)=\pi(f_n)=\delta_n.                                  \tag{10}
\]
By \noderef{PresentColors} and \noderef{MissingColors}, (9)--(10) say
that \(v_n\) has an incident \(\delta_n\)-edge and no incident
\(\gamma_n\)-edge under \(\rho\); in particular
\(\gamma_n\ne\delta_n\).

We next identify the whole component \(K\), rather than merely the exit
path.  The PE constructor supplies a \noderef{TreeExit} at \(v_n\).
Unpack it as an outside vertex \(u\) and a walk \(P\) from the
vertex-node \(u\) to the vertex-node \(v_n\) in
\(I_G(\rho;\gamma_n,\delta_n)\).  By
\noderef{TreeExitPath}, \(P\) is a simple path of length at least two,
\(u\notin V(T_n)\), \(v_n\in V(T_n)\), one of
\(\gamma_n,\delta_n\) is missing at \(u\), and the only vertex-node of
\(P\) belonging to \(T_n\) is \(v_n\).

The vertex-node \(v_n\) has degree exactly one in this incidence graph.
Indeed, the adjacency rule in \noderef{TwoColorIncidenceGraph}, together
with (10), makes the edge-node \(f_n\) a neighbor.  There is no neighbor
coming from a \(\gamma_n\)-edge because \(\gamma_n\) is missing at
\(v_n\), by \noderef{PresentColors} and \noderef{MissingColors}.  There
cannot be a second \(\delta_n\)-edge at \(v_n\): it would be distinct
from \(f_n\), the two identities would be adjacent by
\noderef{Incident} and \noderef{AdjacentEdges}, and assigning both the
color \(\delta_n\) would contradict the properness condition
\noderef{IsProperEdgeColoring} for \(\rho\).  Thus \(f_n\) is the unique
neighbor of \(v_n\).

The vertex-node \(u\) also has degree exactly one.  Since \(P\) has
positive length, its first step supplies an edge-node \(g\) adjacent to
\(u\).  By \noderef{TwoColorIncidenceGraph}, the edge identity \(g\) is
incident with \(u\) and has one of the two colors.  At least one of those
colors is missing at \(u\) by \noderef{TreeExitPath}; the incident edge
\(g\) shows, through \noderef{PresentColors} and
\noderef{MissingColors}, that its own color is not missing.  Since the
two colors are distinct, the other color is the missing one and supplies
no neighbor at \(u\).  Finally, two distinct incident edges of the color
of \(g\) would be adjacent by \noderef{Incident} and
\noderef{AdjacentEdges} and would violate
\noderef{IsProperEdgeColoring}.  Hence the edge-node \(g\) is the unique
neighbor of \(u\).

Every internal node of the simple path \(P\) has its two distinct path
neighbors.  The bound \noderef{TwoColorIncidenceDegreeAtMostTwo} says
that it has no third neighbor, while the preceding two paragraphs say
that neither endpoint has a neighbor other than its unique path neighbor.
It follows that the support of \(P\) is the whole component \(K\).  To
check this conclusion explicitly, every node of \(P\) is connected along
\(P\) to \(v_n\), so it belongs to the connected-component support
\noderef{TwoColorComponent}.  If a node of \(K\) were outside the support
of \(P\), take a connecting walk from that node to \(v_n\), and at its
first entry into the support of \(P\) obtain a path node with a neighbor
outside the path.  At an internal path node this would be a third
neighbor, and at either endpoint it would be a second neighbor.  Both
possibilities contradict the degree conclusions above.  Therefore
\[
 K=\operatorname{supp}(P),
 \quad\text{and the only vertex-node of }K\text{ in }T_n
 \text{ is }v_n.                                                \tag{11}
\]

Now apply the component swap in (8), using its edgewise definition
\noderef{TwoColorComponentSwap}.  If \(x\in V(T_n)\) and \(x\ne v_n\),
then (11) places the vertex-node \(x\) outside \(K\).  No edge-node in
\(K\) can be incident with \(x\): such an incidence, for an edge having
one of the two component colors, would be an adjacency in
\noderef{TwoColorIncidenceGraph} and would put \(x\) in the same
component.  Thus the swap changes no edge incident with \(x\), and
\[
 \overline{\varphi_n}(x)=\overline\rho(x)\qquad
 (x\in V(T_n),\ x\ne v_n).                                    \tag{12}
\]
At \(v_n\), its unique two-color neighbor is \(f_n\), which has color
\(\delta_n\) by (10).  The swap changes that edge to
\(\gamma_n\).  There was no incident \(\gamma_n\)-edge, and properness
excluded any other incident \(\delta_n\)-edge; all colors outside the
pair are left fixed by \noderef{TwoColorComponentSwap}.  Expanding
\noderef{PresentColors} and \noderef{MissingColors} therefore gives the
exact equality
\[
 \overline{\varphi_n}(v_n)
   =\bigl(\overline\rho(v_n)\setminus\{\gamma_n\}\bigr)
       \cup\{\delta_n\}.                                      \tag{13}
\]
Equations (9), (12), and (13), followed by the vertex union in
\noderef{TreeMissingColors}, prove directly that
\[
 \bigl(M_{\varphi_{n-1}}(T_n)\setminus\{\gamma_n\}\bigr)
       \cup\{\delta_n\}
 \subseteq M_{\varphi_n}(T_n).                                 \tag{14}
\]
Indeed, \(\delta_n\) is missing at \(v_n\) after the swap.  For any old
missing color other than \(\gamma_n\), choose a tree vertex where it was
missing; (9) transports that fact to \(\rho\), and either (12) or (13)
preserves it after the swap.  This establishes (14) from the PE data
themselves.

When \(n=1\), the initial-constructor equality \(S_0=\varnothing\) and
(14) give
\[
 D_1=\{\delta_1,\gamma_1\}\setminus M_{\varphi_1}(T_1)
     \subseteq\{\gamma_1\}=D_0\cup\{\gamma_1\},                \tag{15}
\]
because \(\delta_1\) belongs to the set subtracted.  When \(n>1\), take
\(c\in D_n\).  By (1) and (8),
\(c\in U_{n-1}\cup\{\delta_n,\gamma_n\}\) and
\(c\notin M_{\varphi_n}(T_n)\).  Inclusion (14) first gives
\(c\ne\delta_n\).  If \(c=\gamma_n\), it is the allowed exceptional
color.  Otherwise \(c\in U_{n-1}\), and (14) also shows that
\(c\notin M_{\varphi_{n-1}}(T_n)\).  By (2), it is not missing on
\(T_{n-1}\) under that coloring either, so (1) gives
\(c\in D_{n-1}\).  Hence every PE satisfies
\[
 D_n\subseteq D_{n-1}\cup\{\gamma_n\}.                         \tag{16}
\]

The RE inclusion (4), the SE inclusion (7), and the PE inclusion (16)
show in every case that
\[
 |D_n|\leq |D_{n-1}|+1.
\]
Here we used only monotonicity of finite-set cardinality and the elementary
bound \(|A\cup\{x\}|\leq |A|+1\).  The induction hypothesis gives
\(|D_{n-1}|\leq n-1\), whence \(|D_n|\leq n\).  This proves the claim at
every allowed finite index.
\end{proof}

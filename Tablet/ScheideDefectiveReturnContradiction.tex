\begin{helper}[Last-return data contradict global maximality]
For every defective color $\delta$, no
\noderef{ScheideDefectiveReturnData} for $\delta$ exists.
\end{helper}
\begin{proof}
Suppose that a return datum $R$ exists.  Put
$X=\noderef{TreeSequenceVertexSet}(T)$,
$i=\gammaIndex(\delta)$, $j=R.j$,
$\gamma=\gamma_i$, and $\eta=\gamma_j$.  Let
$\varphi_2$ be the first final coloring, obtained from the
$\alpha _0,\gamma$ outside swap followed by the component switch through
$v^0_\delta$, and let $\psi_2$ be the second final coloring from
\noderef{ScheideDefectiveSecondTerminalComponent}.

Choose the partial Tashkinov trees $S_\gamma,S_\eta$ from
\noderef{ScheideDefectiveDeletedTreePair}, and choose
$f_2=z_2u_2$ from \noderef{ScheideDefectiveThirdBoundaryEdge}.  Thus
\[
 V(S_\gamma)=(X\setminus U_\gamma)\cup\{R.u_1\},qquad
 V(S_\eta)=(X\setminus U_\eta)\cup\{u^0_\delta\},             \tag{1}
\]
$u_2\notin X$, $u_2\ne R.u_1,u^0_\delta$, and $f_2$ has color
$\delta$ in both final colorings.

Append $(f_2,u_2)$ to both sequences.  In the first sequence, $z_2$ is
an earlier vertex because it belongs to
$X\setminus U_\gamma$; in the second it belongs to
$X\setminus U_\eta$.  The endpoint $u_2$ is new in both by (1) and the
exclusions just listed.  The identity $f_2$ is new: it is distinct from
the already appended $R.f_1,f^0_\delta$, and the unused-defective-color
conclusion used in \noderef{ScheideDefectiveDeletedTreePair} excludes it
from every retained displayed edge.  Therefore
\noderef{TreeSequenceBoundaryEdgeAppendExists} produces exact
tree-sequences
\[
 \widehat T_\gamma,\qquad \widehat T_\eta
\]
with vertex sets
\[
 V(\widehat T_\gamma)
   =(X\setminus U_\gamma)\cup\{R.u_1,u_2\},qquad
 V(\widehat T_\eta)
   =(X\setminus U_\eta)\cup\{u^0_\delta,u_2\}.                \tag{2}
\]
These equalities are the vertex-set clause of
\noderef{TreeSequenceAdjoinEdge} applied to (1).

We check the new Tashkinov clauses rather than infer them from
cardinality.  For the first recoloring,
\noderef{ScheideDefectiveTerminalComponent} identifies the exact switched
component.  Together with \noderef{TwoColorComponentSwapMissingBehavior}
it says that $\delta$ is missing at the retained base vertex
$v^0_\delta$.  For the second recoloring,
\noderef{ScheideDefectiveSecondTerminalComponent} says that $\delta$ is
missing at the retained base vertex $R.v_1$.  In both cases $f_2$ has
color $\delta$, so the corresponding retained vertex is an earlier
missing witness.  Every old Tashkinov clause comes from the two partial
trees.  Hence
\[
 \noderef{TashkinovTree}(G,e_0,\varphi_2,\widehat T_\gamma),
 \qquad
 \noderef{TashkinovTree}(G,e_0,\psi_2,\widehat T_\eta).       \tag{3}
\]

Both trees in (2) have exactly $|X|$ vertices: each deletes the two
vertices of one block and appends two distinct vertices outside $X$.
We now rebuild their global maximality.  For $\varphi_2$, the first
outside swap is proper and leaves exactly $e_0$ uncolored by
\noderef{ScheideDefectiveTerminalComponent}; its two component colors
are distinct by the return datum, so
\noderef{TwoColorComponentSwapProper} gives properness after the switch,
and \noderef{TwoColorComponentSwap} preserves the exact colored versus
uncolored status of every identity.  The corresponding facts for
$\psi_2$ are stated directly in
\noderef{ScheideDefectiveSecondTerminalComponent}.  In each case combine
these coloring-dependent facts with the unchanged criticality,
chromatic-index, and maximum-degree fields of the original
\noderef{KTriple}.  This gives a $K$-triple for the new coloring.
Equations (2)--(3) and the original equality
$|X|=\noderef{TashkinovOrder}(G)$ then satisfy every clause of
\noderef{GlobalMaximumTriple}.  Thus both new triples are globally
maximum.  Applying \noderef{ScheideGlobalMaximumOrder} to them makes
the two sets in (2) elementary and closed in their respective colorings.

It remains to contradict the simultaneous global maximality.  The exact
position equations in \noderef{ScheideReducedConfiguration} and the
attachment law in \noderef{TreeSequence} identify the endpoints of the
first edge $e_0$ as the distinct first two base vertices $y_0,y_1$.
Apply \noderef{ScheideGlobalMaximumMissingCounts} to the original global
maximum triple at each endpoint.  Each missing-color set has at least
two members.  The original set $X$ is elementary by
\noderef{ScheideGlobalMaximumOrder}, so the two sets are disjoint and
their union has at least four colors.  Choose a color $\beta$ in this
union outside $\{\alpha _0,\gamma,\eta\}$, and let
$y_\beta\in\{y_0,y_1\}$ miss it.  The defective color $\delta$ is
represented by a member of \noderef{DefectiveColors}, so it has a
boundary identity of $X$; because $X$ is closed, \noderef{MissingColors}
and \noderef{PresentColors} make $\delta$ present at every vertex of
$X$.  Thus
\[
 \beta\notin\{\alpha _0,\gamma,\eta,\delta\}.                \tag{4}
\]

Every operation producing either final coloring transposes two colors
from the set on the right of (4).  Hence it fixes every exact
$\beta$-edge and creates no new one; $\beta$ remains missing at the
retained base vertex $y_\beta$ in both new maximum trees.
Elementariness of the first new tree and the distinct vertices
$y_\beta,u_2$ imply that $\beta$ is present at $u_2$.  By
\noderef{PresentColors}, choose its exact incident $\beta$-edge $b$.
Closedness of the first new tree, using the missing witness $y_\beta$,
forces the other endpoint of $b$ to lie in its vertex set.  That endpoint
cannot lie in $X$: since $u_2\notin X$, this would make $b$ a
$\beta$-colored boundary identity of the original closed set $X$, where
$\beta$ is missing.  From the first equality in (2), and
\noderef{LooplessMultigraph},
the other endpoint is therefore exactly $R.u_1$:
\[
 \operatorname{ends}(b)=\{u_2,R.u_1\}.                       \tag{5}
\]

Equation (4) shows that the same identity $b$ has color $\beta$ under
$\psi_2$.  The vertex $R.u_1$ is absent from the second set in (2): it
lies outside $X$, it differs from $u_2$ by the third-edge helper, and it
differs from $u^0_\delta$ because the distinct proper
$\delta$-edges $R.f_1,f^0_\delta$ could not share their outside endpoint.
They are distinct because equality of their endpoint pairs from
\noderef{ScheideDefectiveReturnData} and
\noderef{ScheideDefectiveSetup} would identify the distinct tree
vertices $R.v_1,v^0_\delta$ (or a tree vertex with an outside vertex).
Thus (5) makes $b$ a boundary edge of the second new maximum tree, with
the new endpoint $R.u_1$.  Append $(b,R.u_1)$.  The earlier endpoint is
$u_2$, the exact identity is new because its color differs from the two
appended $\delta$-edges and its two endpoints lie outside $X$, and the
retained vertex $y_\beta$ is an earlier missing witness.  Hence the
result is a Tashkinov tree for the same $K$-triple.

This last tree has $|X|+1$ vertices by the second equality in (2),
contradicting $|X|=\noderef{TashkinovOrder}(G)$.  Therefore the assumed
return datum cannot exist.
\end{proof}

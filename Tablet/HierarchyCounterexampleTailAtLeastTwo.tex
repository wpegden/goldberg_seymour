\begin{lemma}[The tail of a minimum hierarchy counterexample has at least two additions]
Fix a finite loopless multigraph with its individual edge identities, a
palette of $k$ colors, and a distinguished edge $e_0$.  Let
$(T,\varphi_n)$ be a positive-rung extended Tashkinov tree constructed
from an arbitrary certified input coloring, let $\mathcal B$ be a
hierarchy base, and let $C$ be a hierarchy counterexample over that base.
Write $H=C.\mathit{hierarchy}$, let $q$ be its divider count, and put
\[
 A=\noderef{HierarchyStarredStage}(H,q).
\]
Assume that $\varphi_n$ is proper and leaves exactly $e_0$ uncolored.

Assume, uniformly for every positive-rung maximum ETT on the same graph,
palette, and distinguished edge, with arbitrary certified input and
generating colorings and with rung number smaller than that of $T$, all
six lower-rung assertions of the technical theorem: elementarity,
terminal interchangeability, the unconditional PE stability-before-support
assertion, the predecessor-ladder stability assertion conditional on
first-appearance index greater than one, and the unconditional stored-pair
disjointness assertion, the terminal PE component assertion, the defective-endpoint
order assertion, and the mod-coloring assertion.  Assume also that every
same-rung maximum ETT with a good hierarchy having fewer than $q$
dividers is elementary.

Assume that $C$ is lexicographically minimal among hierarchy
counterexamples whose ETTs have the same rung number as $T$ and whose
hierarchies have exactly $q$ dividers, allowing the input coloring,
generating coloring, ETT, base, and hierarchy to vary.  Thus no such
same-rung $C'$ has
\[
 \noderef{HierarchyCounterexampleLess}
   (\noderef{HierarchyCounterexampleMeasure}(C'),
    \noderef{HierarchyCounterexampleMeasure}(C)).
\]
Put $\ell=|T|-|A|$ and let
\[
 T(y_{\ell-1})=
 T\big|_{\min\{|A|+(\ell-1),|T|\}}
\]
be the exact ordered prefix supplied by \noderef{TreeSequencePrefix}.
Assume the large-branch estimate
\[
 2n+11\leq |\overline\varphi_n(T_n)|,
\]
where $n$ is the rung number and $T_n$ is the terminal ladder tree.
Then
\[
                         2\leq |T|-|A|.
\]
Here $|T|-|A|$ is the number of exact edge/vertex additions above the
last starred stage.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Apply \noderef{HierarchyCounterexamplePenultimateElementary} to the
properness, unique-uncoloredness, complete lower-rung package,
fewer-divider induction hypothesis, rung-local lexicographic minimality,
and large-branch estimate.  With the notation in the statement, its
conclusion is exactly
\[
 V(T(y_{\ell-1}))\text{ is elementary under }\varphi_n.       \tag{6.6}
\]

We first record $A\preccurlyeq T$.  If $q>0$, the positive-index clause
of \noderef{HierarchyStarredStage} identifies $A$ with
$H.\mathit{stage}(q)$; the nested and terminal-stage fields of
\noderef{TashkinovHierarchy} then give the ordered inclusion and in fact
$|A|<|T|$.  If $q=0$, the zero-index clause identifies $A$ with the
base core, and the `coreInsideFirst' and terminal-stage fields give the
same weak ordered inclusion.

Suppose $\ell=0$.  The positive-divider case is impossible by the strict
length inequality just noted, so $q=0$.  Since $A\preccurlyeq T$ and
$|T|-|A|=0$, their lengths agree.  The vertex and edge clauses of
\noderef{TreeSequenceInitialSegment} identify $A=T$ as complete ordered
tree-sequences.  Truncated subtraction also gives $\ell-1=0$, and hence
the prefix in (6.6) has length
$\min\{|A|,|T|\}=|T|$.  The full-length case of
\noderef{TreeSequencePrefix} identifies it with $T$.  Thus (6.6) makes
$V(T)$ elementary under $\varphi_n$, contradicting the `notElementary'
field of \noderef{HierarchyCounterexample}.  Consequently
\[
                              1\leq\ell.                      \tag{1}
\]

Assume for contradiction that $\ell<2$.  Arithmetic with (1) gives
$\ell=1$.  If $q=0$, apply
\noderef{HierarchyCounterexampleOneAdditionZeroDividerImpossible} to
the same ETT, base, counterexample, properness, unique-uncoloredness,
lower-rung package, fewer-divider hypothesis, minimality, and large
estimate, together with $\ell=1$, (6.6), and $q=0$.  Its conclusion is
the required contradiction.

If $q>0$, apply
\noderef{HierarchyCounterexampleOneAdditionPositiveDividerImpossible}
to those same ambient hypotheses, $\ell=1$, (6.6), and $q>0$.  This
again gives a contradiction.  The two divider cases are exhaustive, so
$\ell<2$ is impossible.  Therefore
$2\leq\ell=|T|-|A|$, as claimed.
\end{proof}

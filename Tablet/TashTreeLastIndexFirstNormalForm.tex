\begin{helper}[First normal form in the last-index case]
Let \((\varphi,T)\) be a minimum bad pair with
\(2<\rho(T)=|E(T)|=n\).  There are a coloring \(\varphi_1\), a color
\(\beta\), and an index \(j\le n-2\) such that
\((\varphi_1,T)\) is a minimum bad pair, \(\beta\) is missing at both
\(y_j,y_n\), and \(\beta\) is not used on any colored displayed tree
edge.
\end{helper}
\begin{proof}
The inequality gives \(n\ge3\), so the prefixes through \(y_{n-1}\) and
\(y_{n-2}\) are defined by \noderef{TreeSequencePrefix}.  The first is
elementary by \noderef{TashTreeProperPrefixElementary}, whereas the full
vertex set is not elementary by \noderef{TashTreeMinimumBadPair} and
\noderef{TashTreeBadPair}.  Expanding
\noderef{ColoredVertexSetElementary}, choose a color \(\beta\) missing
at \(y_n\) and at \(y_j\) for some \(j<n\).

By \noderef{TashTreeFourUnusedColors}, the prefix through \(y_{n-2}\)
has four colors in \noderef{TreeFreeColors}.  The definitions
\noderef{TreeMissingColors} and \noderef{TreeUsedColors} say that each
has a missing witness in that prefix and is unused on its displayed
colored edges.  The two remaining exact identities \(e_{n-1},e_n\) use
at most two colors.  Hence at least two of the four colors are unused on
all displayed colored edges of \(T\).  Choose one, \(\alpha\ne\beta\),
and a missing witness \(y_m\) with \(m\le n-2\).

First suppose \(j=n-1\).  The color \(\beta\) is unused through
\(y_{n-1}\).  Indeed, an occurrence on any relevant earlier tree edge
would have, by the Tashkinov condition contained in
\noderef{TashTreeBadPair}, an earlier missing-\(\beta\) witness distinct
from \(y_{n-1}\), contradicting elementariness of the prefix.  It cannot
color \(e_n\), because that edge is incident with the
missing-\(\beta\) vertex \(y_n\).  Apply
\noderef{TashTreeControlledInterchange} to \(\alpha,\beta\) at
\(y_m,y_{n-1}\).  Its component is a path with those two controlled
ends.  The vertex \(y_n\), which also misses \(\beta\), cannot lie on
that path as a third end: \noderef{MissingColorIncidenceEndpoint} gives
its vertex-node degree at most one, while
\noderef{TwoColorComponentSpanningPath} says that the component support
is a simple path already having the two distinct controlled ends.
Thus \(y_n\) is outside the component, and the outside-vertex clause of
\noderef{TwoColorComponentSwapMissingBehavior} shows that the swap
leaves the missing-\(\beta\) status at \(y_n\), moves the other occurrence to
\(y_m\), fixes the tree-edge values in question, and returns a minimum
bad pair.  Taking \(j=m\) gives the conclusion.

Now suppose \(j\le n-2\).  If \(\beta\notin
\noderef{TreeUsedColors}(\varphi,T)\), take \(\varphi_1=\varphi\).
Otherwise let \(Q\) be the \((\alpha,\beta)\)-component rooted at
\(y_n\).  If \(Q\) meets the prefix through \(y_{n-2}\), then
\noderef{TashTreeLastIndexIntersectionShortening} applies: both colors
are missing on that prefix, \(y_n\) is outside it by vertex injectivity,
and its length is exactly \(n-2\).  This gives a contradiction.

Thus \(Q\) avoids every vertex of the prefix.  We claim that it also
avoids every displayed exact tree-edge node.  The edges through
\(e_{n-2}\) have both ends in the prefix, and \(e_{n-1}\) has its earlier
end there.  Finally, \(\rho(T)=n\) says that the final edge is not the
consecutive edge \(y_{n-1}y_n\); its attachment witness from
\noderef{TreeSequence} is therefore in the prefix through \(y_{n-2}\).
If any of these edge-nodes belonged to \(Q\), its affected color and
incidence with the displayed prefix endpoint would put that endpoint in
the same component, a contradiction.  Here an off-pair or uncolored
edge-node has no incidence-graph adjacency at all; since \(Q\) is rooted
at a vertex-node, such an isolated edge-node cannot belong to \(Q\).

The colors \(\alpha,\beta\) are unused through the retained prefix:
this is clear for \(\alpha\), and for \(\beta\) an occurrence would give
an earlier missing witness and contradict prefix elementariness exactly
as above.  Apply \noderef{TashTreeDisjointComponentNormalization} with
that prefix cutoff and the fixed missing witnesses \(y_m,y_j\).  It
returns the explicit local component-swap coloring, proves it is again a
minimum bad pair, and makes \(\alpha\) missing at both \(y_m,y_n\).
Every displayed exact edge-node avoids \(Q\), so
\noderef{TwoColorComponentSwap} fixes every tree-edge value.  Since
\(\alpha\) was unused on the original tree, it remains outside
\noderef{TreeUsedColors} after the swap.  Rename this newly repeated
color \(\alpha\) as the output \(\beta\), and take \(j=m\).  This uses
only the local swap; no global transposition of color names is needed.
\end{proof}

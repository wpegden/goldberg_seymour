\begin{lemma}[A minimum bad tree is not a path]
If \((\varphi,T)\) is a minimum bad pair, then
\(\rho(T)>2\).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Write \(T=(y_0,e_1,y_1,\ldots,e_n,y_n)\) and \(q=\rho(T)\).  Unfolding
\noderef{TashTreeMinimumBadPair} and then \noderef{TashTreeBadPair}
shows that \(T\) is a non-elementary Tashkinov tree for the \(k\)-triple
\((G,e_0,\varphi)\), and that no bad pair under any coloring has rank
below \(T\).

First \(n\ne1\).  If \(n=1\), then
\noderef{TreeSequenceVertexSet} is exactly \(\{y_0,y_1\}\).  Its failure
of \noderef{ColoredVertexSetElementary} supplies a color \(\eta\) missing
at both distinct vertices.  Define a new coloring \(\psi\) by giving
\(e_1=e_0\) color \(\eta\) and leaving every other exact edge unchanged.
The endpoints of \(e_0\) are \(y_0,y_1\) by
\noderef{TreeSequence}.  Because \(\eta\) is missing at both, no edge
adjacent to \(e_0\) has color \(\eta\); hence the definition
\noderef{IsProperEdgeColoring} shows that \(\psi\) is proper.  The clause
\noderef{IsOneEdgeUncolored} for the original \(k\)-triple says that
every other edge was already colored, so \(\psi\) is full in the sense of
\noderef{IsFullEdgeColoring}.  Thus \(\psi\) witnesses
\noderef{HasKEdgeColoring} with \(k\) colors.  By the leastness in
\noderef{ChromaticIndex}, \(\chi'(G)\le k\), contradicting the equation
\(\chi'(G)=k+1\) in \noderef{KTriple}.  Since
\noderef{TreeSequence} also gives \(n\ge1\), we have \(n\ge2\).

Suppose for contradiction that \(q\le2\).  The bounds in
\noderef{TashTreePathIndex} give \(1\le q\).  We first dispose explicitly
of \(q=2\).  By \noderef{TashTreeSuffixPath}, every exact edge
\(e_h\), \(3\le h\le n\), joins \(y_{h-1}\) to \(y_h\).  The attachment
field of \noderef{TreeSequence} says that \(e_2\) joins \(y_2\) to
exactly one of \(y_0,y_1\).

If that earlier endpoint is \(y_1\), the original display is already a
path in order.  Then \(1\) is a suffix-path start, so leastness in
\noderef{TashTreePathIndex} gives \(\rho(T)=1\), immediately
contradicting \(q=2\).

It remains that the earlier endpoint is \(y_0\).  Define a new displayed
sequence
\[
 S=(y_1,e_1,y_0,e_2,y_2,e_3,y_3,\ldots,e_n,y_n);
\]
that is, exchange only the first two vertex entries and retain every exact
edge identity and every later vertex entry.  We verify that this is a
tree-sequence, rather than treating the exchange as notation.
Vertex and edge injectivity are inherited because only two distinct
vertices were permuted.  The first edge remains the exact edge \(e_0\);
its endpoint pair is unchanged because an unordered pair is symmetric.
The chosen orientation makes \(e_2\) join the new second vertex \(y_0\)
to the new vertex \(y_2\), and the suffix equations above give the
successive attachments for every \(e_h\), \(h\ge3\).  Hence all fields of
\noderef{TreeSequence} hold, and \(S\) is a path in displayed order.

The reordered \(S\) remains a Tashkinov tree.  For \(e_2\), its old
witness under \noderef{TashkinovTree} was one of \(y_0,y_1\), and both
still precede \(e_2\).  For \(e_h\), \(h\ge3\), an old witness among the
first two vertices is merely exchanged and still precedes \(e_h\), while
every other witness retains its position.  Its vertex set is exactly that
of \(T\), and the coloring and \(k\)-triple are unchanged, so its
non-elementariness is unchanged.  Thus \noderef{TashTreeBadPair} holds
for \((\varphi,S)\).  The in-order path gives \(\rho(S)=1\), whereas
\(\rho(T)=2\), so \noderef{TashTreeRankLess} makes \(S\) smaller than
\(T\), contradicting the universal clause of
\noderef{TashTreeMinimumBadPair}.  Therefore \(q\ne2\).  Under our
assumption \(q\le2\), it follows that \(q=1\), and
\noderef{TashTreeSuffixPath} now says that the original display \(T\) is
itself a path in order.

Let \(P=T_{n-1}\) be the prefix from \noderef{TreeSequencePrefix}.
It is elementary by \noderef{TashTreeProperPrefixElementary}, whereas
\(T\) is not.  Expanding \noderef{ColoredVertexSetElementary}, a color
\(\beta\) is therefore missing at the new vertex \(y_n\) and at some
\(y_i\) with \(i<n\): if a repeated missing color had both witnesses in
\(P\), it would already violate elementariness there.

Assume \(i<n-1\).  There is a color \(\gamma\) missing at \(y_{i+1}\).
Indeed, the properness and degree inequality in \noderef{KTriple}, together
with \noderef{PresentColors}, give one incident edge for every present
color in the sense of \noderef{Incident}.  Two edges chosen for the same
color would be adjacent by \noderef{AdjacentEdges} and would contradict
\noderef{IsProperEdgeColoring}.  Thus \noderef{Degree} and
\noderef{MaximumDegree} give
\[
 |\varphi(y_{i+1})|\le d_G(y_{i+1})\le\Delta(G)<k.
\]
The complement definition \noderef{MissingColors} then supplies
\(\gamma\).

Both \(\beta\) and \(\gamma\) are unused on
\(e_2,\ldots,e_{i+1}\).  For \(\gamma\), if such an edge used it, the
Tashkinov condition \noderef{TashkinovTree} would give an earlier
missing-\(\gamma\) vertex, distinct from \(y_{i+1}\), contradicting
elementariness of the proper prefix \(T_{i+1}\) supplied by
\noderef{TashTreeProperPrefixElementary}.  For \(\beta\), an occurrence
on one of \(e_2,\ldots,e_i\) similarly supplies an earlier witness
distinct from \(y_i\).  The only remaining edge \(e_{i+1}\) joins
\(y_i\) to \(y_{i+1}\), because \(T\) is an in-order path; it cannot have
color \(\beta\), since \(\beta\) is missing at its incident vertex
\(y_i\).  Thus the hypotheses of
\noderef{TashTreeControlledInterchange} hold for the colors
\(\beta,\gamma\) at \(y_i,y_{i+1}\).

After that interchange, \(\beta\) is missing at \(y_{i+1}\) and the pair
is again minimum bad.  It is still missing at \(y_n\).  To justify this
last assertion, the controlled component has the two distinct vertex ends
\(y_i,y_{i+1}\).  Since \(y_n\) also misses \(\beta\),
\noderef{MissingColorIncidenceEndpoint} makes it a vertex of degree at
most one in the same two-color incidence graph.  If it belonged to that
component, the spanning path from
\noderef{TwoColorComponentSpanningPath} would have three distinct
degree-at-most-one vertex-nodes, impossible for a path.  Thus \(y_n\) is
outside the swapped component, and all its incident edge colors, including
its missing-\(\beta\) status, are fixed.

The sequence itself has not changed.  We may repeat this argument with
\(i+1\) in place of \(i\); each application returns a minimum bad pair,
so \noderef{TashTreeProperPrefixElementary} remains applicable at the next
stage.  The integer \(n-1-i\) decreases by one, so after finitely many
steps we have a minimum bad pair, which we again denote
\((\varphi,T)\), in which \(\beta\) is missing at both
\(y_{n-1}\) and \(y_n\).

Because \(q=1\), the last exact edge \(e_n\) joins these two vertices.
It has a colored value \(\delta\): \(n\ge2\), edge injectivity in
\noderef{TreeSequence} gives \(e_n\ne e_1=e_0\), and
\noderef{IsOneEdgeUncolored} colors every edge other than \(e_0\).
Recolor only \(e_n\) with \(\beta\), obtaining \(\varphi^*\).  This is
proper because both endpoints previously missed \(\beta\); all pairs of
edges not involving \(e_n\) are unchanged, and no edge adjacent to
\(e_n\) can have color \(\beta\).  The uniquely uncolored edge remains
\(e_0\), since a colored edge was replaced by another colored value.
Thus all clauses of the \(k\)-triple remain true.

The old Tashkinov witness for the \(\delta\)-colored \(e_n\) is a vertex
\(y_j\) with \(j<n\).  It cannot be \(y_{n-1}\), because \(e_n\) was an
incident \(\delta\)-edge there, whereas a missing color is absent from all
incident edges by \noderef{PresentColors} and \noderef{MissingColors}.
Hence \(j\le n-2\).  Properness also implies that \(e_n\) was the only
incident \(\delta\)-edge at \(y_{n-1}\); after removing its old color,
\(\delta\) is missing there.  No exact edge or missing-color witness in
the prefix \(T_{n-1}\) was otherwise changed.  Therefore that prefix is a
Tashkinov tree under \(\varphi^*\), and it is non-elementary because the
distinct vertices \(y_j,y_{n-1}\) both miss \(\delta\).
By \noderef{TashTreeBadPair}, \((\varphi^*,T_{n-1})\) is bad.

Both \(T\) and \(T_{n-1}\) are in-order paths, so both path indices are
one, while the latter has one fewer exact edge.  Thus
\noderef{TashTreeRankLess} makes this new bad pair strictly smaller than
the current minimum bad pair.  This contradicts
\noderef{TashTreeMinimumBadPair}.  The assumption \(q\le2\) is false, and
therefore \(\rho(T)>2\).
\end{proof}

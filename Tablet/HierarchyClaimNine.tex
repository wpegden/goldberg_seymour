\begin{lemma}[Situation 7.1, Claim 9]
Fix a minimum hierarchy-counterexample context and suppose
\[
 \noderef{HierarchyPathNumber}(C.\mathit{hierarchy})=1.
\]
Assume explicitly the paper's ambient palette-surplus consequence: for
every proper replacement coloring leaving exactly \(e_0\) uncolored,
every graph vertex has a missing color.  Then the finite set
\noderef{HierarchyEligibleCommonMissingIndices} is nonempty.  Equivalently,
for some \(1\leq i\leq\ell-1\), there are a full replacement presentation
in the paper's same-rung minimum-counterexample family \(\mathcal K\) and
a color missing both at the exact tail vertex \(y_i\) and at the final
vertex \(y_\ell\).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
We first construct the original member of \(\mathcal K\), rather than
assuming it.  In \noderef{CorrespondingExtendedTashkinovTree}, take the
replacement input and series to be the original input and series, the
replacement ETT to be the original ETT, and every agreement proof to be
reflexivity.  Its properness, one-uncoloredness, and stability fields are
the corresponding fields of the supplied
\noderef{HierarchyMinimumCounterexampleContext} and the reflexive
stability relation.  Reuse the original maximum property, base,
\noderef{HierarchyCounterexample}, good hierarchy, and minimum context;
\noderef{SameGoodHierarchy} is reflexive.  This constructs a
\noderef{HierarchyReplacementPresentation} \(P_0\) with coloring
\(\varphi_n\), on the same exact edge-valued tree \(T\).

Apply \noderef{HierarchyCounterexampleTerminalCommonMissing} to the fields
of the minimum context.  It gives a color \(\alpha\) missing both somewhere
on
\[
 B=\noderef{HierarchyFullTailPrefix}(C,\ell-1)
\]
and at the final displayed vertex \(y_\ell\).  The same context and
\noderef{HierarchyCounterexampleTailAtLeastTwo} give \(2\leq\ell\).
If \(\alpha\), or any other color, is missing both at some exact
\(y_i\), \(1\leq i\leq\ell-1\), and at \(y_\ell\), then
\(i,P_0\), and that color satisfy the filter defining
\noderef{HierarchyEligibleCommonMissingIndices}, and we are done.
Hence assume the finite intersections are empty for every such \(i\).

The exact prefix \(B\) consists of the last starred stage \(A\), followed
by \(y_1,\ldots,y_{\ell-1}\).  This follows entry by entry from
\noderef{HierarchyFullTailPrefix}, the hierarchy's nested exact prefixes,
and the equality \(|A|+\ell=|T|\).  Since \(\alpha\) is missing somewhere
on \(B\), but its missingness at \(y_\ell\) and the assumed empty
intersections exclude every \(y_i\) with \(1\leq i\leq\ell-1\), it is
missing on \(A\).

We normalize the zero-divider PE exception exactly as in the manuscript.
If the divider count is positive, \noderef{HierarchyStarredStage}
identifies \(A\) with \(T_{n,q}\), so \(\alpha\) already satisfies the
positive normalization.  If the divider count is zero and the terminal
kind is SE or RE, \noderef{HierarchyBase} identifies \(A=T_n\), giving the
zero-divider normalization.  If the kind is PE and \(\alpha\) is missing
on \(T_n\), the same conclusion holds.  In the remaining case apply
\noderef{HierarchyClaimNinePENormalization}.  If its eligible-index
alternative holds we are done.  Otherwise replace \(P_0,\alpha\) by its
new presentation \(P,\alpha'\).  The theorem gives explicitly that
\(\alpha'\) is missing on \(T_n\), on \(A\), and at \(y_\ell\), and that
the no-common condition remains true.  Thus in every case we now have a
replacement presentation \(P\), a color still denoted \(\alpha\), and
the exact positive- or zero-divider normalization required in Cases 1/2.

We also transport the Situation 7.1 hypothesis to this current
presentation; it is not inherited merely by notation.  The field
\(P.\mathit{sameGood}\) in
\noderef{HierarchyReplacementPresentation}, unfolded through
\noderef{SameGoodHierarchy}, supplies
\noderef{SameHierarchyStages} between the original hierarchy
\(C.\mathit{hierarchy}\) and
\(H=P.\mathit{counterexample}.\mathit{hierarchy}\).  Hence their divider
counts agree.  At divider count zero the same-stages base-core equality,
and at positive divider count the transported equality of the last
ordinary stage, show from the two defining branches of
\noderef{HierarchyStarredStage} that the two last starred stages are the
same exact ordered tree.  The definition of
\noderef{HierarchyPathNumber} depends only on the common terminal tree
\(T\), that last starred stage, and the endpoint equations for the exact
displayed edge identities.  Its two candidate finite sets are therefore
equal, including the empty-set fallback.  Consequently
\[
 \noderef{HierarchyPathNumber}(H)
 =\noderef{HierarchyPathNumber}(C.\mathit{hierarchy})=1.       \tag{7.1a}
\]

The equality
\(\noderef{HierarchyPathNumber}(H)=1\) says, by its defining filtered
minimum, that every exact displayed tail edge from the second tail
position onward joins consecutive displayed vertices.  The first edge
is the certified PE augmentation or the individual SE/RE seed, and all
subsequent edges are exact Tashkinov augmentations.  Thus the manuscript
case split can be made on individual edge identities.

Consider the finite set of tail positions \(t\) at or above \(A\) whose
exact edge has color \(\alpha\) under the coloring of \(P\).  If it is
empty, its defining universal negation is precisely the hypothesis of
\noderef{HierarchyClaimNineUnusedTailColor}; that node produces a
replacement presentation with a color common-missing at
\(y_{\ell-1},y_\ell\), and hence an eligible index.  Its explicit
current-presentation path-number hypothesis is (7.1a).

Otherwise choose the least such position.  Because tail positions are
exactly
\[
 |A|,\ |A|+1,\ldots,|A|+\ell-1,
\]
there are a unique subscript \(j\) with \(1\leq j\leq\ell\) and an exact
index \(e_j\) satisfying
\[
 e_j.\mathit{val}=|A|+j-1,\qquad
 P.\mathit{coloring}(T.\mathit{edge}(e_j))=\alpha.
\]
Leastness gives the universal exclusion at all earlier exact positions.
If \(j\geq2\), apply
\noderef{HierarchyClaimNineFirstUseAfterSeed}; its output is the desired
eligible index \(j-1\), with (7.1a) supplying its Situation 7.1 premise.
If \(j=1\), apply
\noderef{HierarchyClaimNineFirstUseSeed}; it constructs the manuscript
closed color \(\gamma\), keeps the exact first edge fixed, and reduces to
one of the preceding two branches; again its path-number premise is
(7.1a).  Its conclusion is nonemptiness of the eligible-index set.  These
cases exhaust the finite tail positions and prove Claim 9.
\end{proof}

\begin{helper}[Two-constituent path contradiction]
Let $A,B,U$ be tree-sequences with $U$ an ordered union of $A$ and $B$
under a proper coloring $\sigma$.  Suppose $A$ and $B$ are elementary and
$B$ is closed.  Let distinct vertices $s,w$ lie in both constituents, let
$\delta$ be missing at $s$, let $\gamma$ be missing nowhere on $A$, and
suppose $w$ has a missing color.  Assume that any two colors missing on $A$
and avoiding $\{\gamma,\delta\}$ are $A$-interchangeable, and that every
pair with a color missing on $B$ is $B$-interchangeable.  Then $U$ is
elementary under $\sigma$.
\end{helper}
\begin{proof}
Assume that $U$ is not elementary.  Expanding
\noderef{ColoredVertexSetElementary}, choose distinct vertices $u,v$ in
$V(U)$ and a color $\alpha$ with
\[
 \alpha\in\noderef{MissingColors}(G,\sigma,u)
 \cap\noderef{MissingColors}(G,\sigma,v).                       \tag{1}
\]
The exact vertex-set equality in \noderef{OrderedTreeUnion} says
$V(U)=V(A)\cup V(B)$.  Elementarity of each constituent rules out both
vertices lying in $A$ and rules out both lying in $B$.  After interchanging
their names, therefore,
\[
 u\in V(A)-V(B),\qquad v\in V(B)-V(A).
\]
Since $s,w$ lie in both constituents and $s\ne w$, these memberships give
all pairwise distinctness relations among $u,v,w,s$.

Because $s,v\in V(B)$ are distinct,
$\delta\in\noderef{MissingColors}(G,\sigma,s)$ and (1), elementarity of
$B$ implies $\alpha\ne\delta$.  The definition
\noderef{TreeMissingColors} turns $u\in V(A)$ and (1) into
$\alpha\in\overline\sigma(A)$; since gamma is not in that tree-missing
set, $\alpha\ne\gamma$.  Choose
$\beta\in\noderef{MissingColors}(G,\sigma,w)$ from the assumed nonempty
set.  Elementarity of $B$ at the distinct vertices $w,s$ gives
$\beta\ne\delta$.  Membership $w\in V(A)$ and
\noderef{TreeMissingColors} similarly give $\beta\ne\gamma$.
Finally, $u\ne w$, (1), and elementarity of $A$ give
$\alpha\ne\beta$.

The witnesses $u,w\in V(A)$ and \noderef{TreeMissingColors} show that
$\alpha,\beta\in\overline\sigma(A)$; the four inequalities just proved
therefore make them $A$-interchangeable by the first hypothesis.  Likewise
$v,w\in V(B)$ witness
$\alpha,\beta\in\overline\sigma(B)$, so the second hypothesis makes them
$B$-interchangeable.  We do not need to assert that either full incidence
component stays inside $B$.

We now justify the incidence degrees.  At $v$, (1) and
\noderef{MissingColors} say that no edge incident with $v$ is colored
$\alpha$.  Since $\beta$ is missing at the distinct vertex $w$ and $B$ is
elementary, $\beta$ is not missing at $v$; the complementary definitions
\noderef{MissingColors} and \noderef{PresentColors} supply an exact edge
$b_v$ incident with $v$ and colored $\beta$.  If a distinct edge identity
$b'_v$ had the same properties, the shared endpoint would make
$b_v,b'_v$ adjacent by \noderef{AdjacentEdges}, contradicting
\noderef{IsProperEdgeColoring}.  The adjacency rule in
\noderef{TwoColorIncidenceGraph} says that the neighbors of the vertex-node
$v$ are exactly the incident alpha- or beta-colored edge identities.
Thus $v$ has degree exactly one in this incidence graph.

At $w$, beta is missing.  Since alpha is missing at the distinct vertex
$v$ of $B$, elementarity of $B$ makes alpha present at $w$; the same
\noderef{AdjacentEdges}, \noderef{IsProperEdgeColoring}, and
\noderef{TwoColorIncidenceGraph} argument gives degree exactly one at
$w$.  Finally, alpha is missing at $u$, while beta is missing at the
distinct vertex $w$ of the elementary set $A$; hence beta is present at
$u$, and the identical exact-edge argument gives degree exactly one at
$u$.

Let $K_x$ denote the support in \noderef{TwoColorComponent} through the
vertex-node $x$.  Since $\alpha\ne\beta$ and $K_v$ contains the degree-one
node $v$, it satisfies \noderef{TwoColorPathComponent}; the same is true
of $K_w$.  Both meet $B$, so $B$-interchangeability and
\noderef{TreeInterchangeable} give $K_v=K_w$.  Applying the same argument
inside $A$ to $K_u$ and $K_w$ gives $K_u=K_w$.  Thus the three distinct
vertex-nodes $u,v,w$ lie in one full connected component $K$, and each has
degree one in the full incidence graph.

By \noderef{TwoColorComponentSpanningPath}, properness supplies a simple
path whose node set is exactly $K$.  Each of $u,v,w$ must be an end of
that path: an internal path node has two distinct path neighbors, whereas
each of these nodes has only one neighbor in the entire incidence graph.
A simple path has at most two ends, contradicting the distinctness of
$u,v,w$.  Therefore no two distinct vertices of $U$ have a common member
of their \noderef{MissingColors} sets.  By
\noderef{ColoredVertexSetElementary}, this is exactly the required
elementarity of $V(U)$.
\end{proof}

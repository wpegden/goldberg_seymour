\begin{lemma}[Step 1, Subcase 1.2: the later end is inside the starred predecessor]
Use the same finite loopless multigraph, distinguished edge, color set,
positive-rung certified ETT $T$ with generating coloring $\varphi$, maximum
property, properness, and exact one-uncoloredness as in Subcase 1.1.
Assume the complete six-part lower-rung package.  Let $H$ be a good
hierarchy over a hierarchy base, with $d<q_0$ dividers; assume the
same-rung fewer-than-$q_0$ elementarity hypothesis for arbitrary certified
inputs and generating colorings, and the bound
$|\overline\varphi(T_n)|\geq2n+11$.  Put
$A=H.\mathit{starred}(d)$.  Assume separately the terminal certificate
\[
 T\text{ is }
 \left(\bigcup_{\eta\in D^H_{n,d+1}}\Gamma^d_\eta\right)^-
 \text{-closed under }\varphi,
\]
and assume that $V(T)$ is elementary under $\varphi$.

Let $\alpha\ne\beta$, where $\alpha$ is missing and singleton-closed on
$T$ and $\beta$ is missing on $T$.  Let $P_1,P_2$ be exact exit paths
for $\alpha,\beta$, with disjoint incidence-walk supports, distinct
outside ends $a_1,a_2$, distinct tree ends $b_1,b_2$, and
$b_1\prec_T b_2$.  Retain exact $\beta$-colored boundary-edge identities
$f_j$ on $P_j$ incident with $b_j$.  Suppose $b_2\in V(A)$.

Assume that a color $\theta$ has already been selected with
$\theta\ne\alpha,\beta$, that $\theta$ is missing and singleton-closed on
$A$, and that it is singleton-closed on $T$.  Finally assume the explicit
no-smaller-presentation consequence of the chosen global minimum: for
every coloring $\sigma$ stable from $\varphi$ on the final ladder tree,
every arbitrary replacement input, exact corresponding same-rung ETT
$T'$ under $\sigma$, properness, exact one-uncoloredness, maximum property,
hierarchy base, and good hierarchy $K$ with fewer than $d$ dividers, if
the replacement satisfies its own large bound and the separate terminal
minus-closed certificate, then no two disjoint exact exit legs with
distinct outside ends can witness a missing singleton-closed first color.
Then the displayed Subcase 1.2 data are contradictory.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Exchange $\alpha$ and $\theta$ on every full component outside $T$, and
call the result $\nu$.  The colors are distinct by hypothesis, and both
are singleton-closed on $T$, so this is an
\noderef{OutsideKempeRecoloring}.  The theorem
\noderef{OutsideKempeRecoloringStable} proves that $\nu$ is proper,
leaves exactly $e_0$ uncolored, fixes every exact edge incident with every
displayed stage, preserves the missing set at every such vertex, and is
stable for every required control set.  Applying
\noderef{HierarchyLEMStable} with the lower package yields a
\noderef{CorrespondingExtendedTashkinovTree} under $\nu$, maximum
property, the identical ordered stages and individual connecting-edge
identities, the same stored sets and Gamma pairs, all five
\noderef{GoodHierarchy} fields, and the transported terminal
\noderef{TreeColorSetMinusClosed} certificate.  Since the recoloring is
outside $T_n$, it preserves the stated large bound.

Every external $\alpha$-edge of $P_1,P_2$ lies in a selected component
and becomes a $\theta$-edge.  Their $\beta$-edges are fixed because
$\beta$ differs from both exchanged colors.  Thus the same exact
edge-node walks are $(A,\nu,\{\theta,\beta\})$-exit paths: their tree ends
belong to $A$; the original \noderef{TreeExitPath} condition says that
they have no other vertex even in the larger tree $T$; their outside
ends still miss one displayed color; and their supports and outside
endpoints remain disjoint.  This verifies the two-leg data directly.  We
make no assertion that the two transformed walks lie in distinct full
$(\theta,\beta)$ components.

Suppose first that $d>0$.  Truncate the transported hierarchy at the exact
ordered tree $A$.  The construction in
\noderef{GoodHierarchyPrefixRepackage} gives a corresponding same-rung
maximum ETT, the same certified input data and generating coloring $\nu$,
a hierarchy with $d-1$ dividers, all five good-hierarchy fields, and the
old last internal minus-closed field as its separate terminal certificate.
The stable recoloring fixes the final ladder tree, so the large estimate
is unchanged.  The color $\theta$ is missing and singleton-closed on $A$,
and the two exact exit legs just checked have distinct outside ends and
disjoint supports.  Every premise of the explicit no-smaller-presentation
hypothesis is therefore satisfied, while $d-1<d$.  This is a
contradiction.

It remains to treat $d=0$.  Then $A$ is the actual hierarchy core.  We
verify the zero-divider endpoint rather than manufacturing a zero-rung
ETT.  Assume first that the terminal extension kind is not PE.  By
\noderef{HierarchyBase}, $A=T_n$.  Choose a vertex $x\in V(T_n)$ and a
color $c$ missing at $x$.  Such a color exists because the initial
\noderef{KTriple} gives $k\geq\Delta(G)+1$, whereas properness,
\noderef{Degree}, and \noderef{MaximumDegree} give at most
$d_G(x)\leq\Delta(G)$ present colors.  In the RE case the certified
terminal constructor in \noderef{TashkinovSeriesStep} identifies the
generating coloring with the preceding one; in the SE case its
\noderef{MaximumDefectiveChoice} stability, reversed with \noderef{sc2},
transports missingness and closedness.  The same argument closes every
color missing on $T_n$ under $\nu$, and in particular closes $c$.

If $\theta=c$, retain $\nu$; otherwise exchange $\theta,c$ on the full
components outside $T_n$.  By
\noderef{OutsideKempeRecoloringStable} the resulting coloring $\rho$ is
proper, exactly one-uncolored, and has the terminal stability required
below.  The two walks become disjoint $(c,\beta)$ exit legs.  At $x$, the
color $c$ is missing.  If $\beta$ were also missing there, both colors
would be closed and either exit leg would exhibit a forbidden boundary
edge, so $\beta$ is present at $x$.  Hence the full $(c,\beta)$ component
through $x$ is a nontrivial path component with degree-one end $x$.
The two outside leg ends also have degree one.  By
\noderef{TwoColorIncidenceDegreeAtMostTwo} and
\noderef{TwoColorComponentSpanningPath}, one full path component cannot
contain all three degree-one vertex-nodes.  Therefore the component
through $x$ and the component containing at least one leg are distinct
path components meeting $T_n$.

If the rung is greater than one, \noderef{CertifiedETTPredecessor}
supplies the actual positive-rung predecessor, with the exact old ladder
tree, generating coloring, certificates, and restricted maximum property.
The RE equality or the SE selected-stability field, composed with the
outside stability by \noderef{sc2}, supplies the stable-coloring premise
of lower assertion (ii).  That assertion makes $c,\beta$
interchangeable on $T_n$, contradicting the two distinct path components.
If the rung is one, the same properness, exact one-uncoloredness, and
stable-coloring data are precisely the hypotheses of
\noderef{TechnicalTheoremBase}, which gives the same contradiction.  No
zero-rung ETT occurs in either branch.

Finally suppose that the terminal extension is PE.  Let $x$ be its actual
support.  The parallel field of \noderef{HierarchyBase} identifies the
closure $R_n$, puts $x$ in both $T_n$ and $R_n$, and identifies
$A=T_n\vee R_n$.  Choose $c$ missing at $x$ by the same degree count.
When the rung is greater than one,
\noderef{CertifiedETTPredecessor} supplies the actual predecessor and
lower assertion (i) supplies its elementarity; when the rung is one,
\noderef{TechnicalTheoremBase} supplies the corresponding initial-tuple
elementarity.  Therefore
\noderef{ParallelCommonMissingClosed} makes $c$ closed on the exact core
$A$.  The color $\beta$ cannot be missing at $x$, since the same lemma
would close it on $A$ and an exit leg would again give a forbidden
boundary identity.

Retain $\nu$ if $\theta=c$ and otherwise exchange $\theta,c$ on all full
components outside $A$.  Apply
\noderef{OutsideKempeRecoloringStable} separately to $T_n$ with its stored
control set and to $R_n$ with empty control.  Their two stability
conclusions are exactly \noderef{CombinedStableColoring}.  As above, the
component through $x$ and a component containing one of the transformed
legs are distinct nontrivial path components meeting $A$.  Conclusion
(iii) of \noderef{interchange}, applied to this proper, one-uncolored,
combined-stable coloring, makes $c,\beta$ interchangeable on $A$, a final
contradiction.  This is the manuscript's PE base endpoint and completes
Subcase 1.2.
\end{proof}

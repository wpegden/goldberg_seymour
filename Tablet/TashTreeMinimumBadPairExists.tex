\begin{lemma}[Existence of a minimum bad pair]
Let \((G,e,\varphi)\) be a \(k\)-triple and let \(T\) be a Tashkinov
tree for it.  If \(V(T)\) is not \(\varphi\)-elementary, then there are a
partial coloring \(\psi\) and a Tashkinov tree \(S\) for which
\((\psi,S)\) is a minimum bad pair.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The hypotheses say exactly that \((\varphi,T)\) is a bad pair in the sense
of \noderef{TashTreeBadPair}, so the class of bad pairs is nonempty.  Map
each bad pair \((\theta,R)\) to the pair of natural numbers
\[
  (\rho(R),|E(R)|),
\]
using \noderef{TashTreePathIndex}.  The lexicographic order on
\(\mathbb N\times\mathbb N\) is well founded: first choose the least first
coordinate occurring among bad pairs, and among the bad pairs with that
first coordinate choose the least second coordinate.  Both choices exist
by well ordering of \(\mathbb N\).

Choose a bad pair \((\psi,S)\) attaining the resulting two coordinates.
If a bad pair \((\psi',S')\) had smaller rank according to
\noderef{TashTreeRankLess}, then either its first coordinate would be below
the first chosen minimum, or its first coordinate would agree and its
second coordinate would be below the second chosen minimum.  Both are
impossible by construction.  Thus \((\psi,S)\) satisfies precisely the
minimality clause of \noderef{TashTreeMinimumBadPair}.
\end{proof}

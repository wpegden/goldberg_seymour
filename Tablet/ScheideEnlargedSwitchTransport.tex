\begin{helper}[Transport through the two switches]
Let $X=\noderef{ScheideEnlargedVertexSet}(R)$ and suppose that $X$ is not
strongly closed.  Then there are a color $\alpha$, a defective index
$d=(\delta,h_\delta)$, an edge $f$, and vertices $w,u$ with the following
properties.  Put
\[
 \varphi_d=\noderef{TwoColorComponentSwap}
 (G,\varphi,\gamma(d),\delta,\operatorname{inl}v^0_d),
\]
\[
 Q=\noderef{TwoColorComponent}
 (G,\varphi_d,\alpha_0,\alpha,\operatorname{inl}u),\qquad
 \psi=\noderef{TwoColorComponentSwap}
 (G,\varphi_d,\alpha_0,\alpha,\operatorname{inl}u).
\]
Then $\alpha\ne\alpha_0$, $G.\mathop{\rm ends}(f)=s(w,u)$,
$\varphi_d(f)=\alpha$, $\operatorname{inr}f\in Q$, and $Q$ contains no
vertex-node from $V(T)$.  Furthermore
\[
 (T_d,e_0,\psi),\qquad T_d=(R d).\mathit{replacementTree},
\]
is a globally maximum triple; $\alpha_0$ is missing at $y_0=C.y(0)$
under $\varphi_d$; $y_0,w\in V(T_d)$; $u\notin V(T_d)$; and $f$ is not
an edge identity of $T_d$.
\end{helper}
\begin{proof}
Put
\[
 X_0=\noderef{TreeSequenceVertexSet}(T),\qquad
 X=\noderef{ScheideEnlargedVertexSet}(R),\qquad y_0=C.y(0).
\]
Apply \noderef{ScheideEnlargedBoundarySelection}.  It supplies
$\alpha,d,f,w,u$ with $\alpha\ne\alpha_0$, the stated endpoint equation,
$\varphi(f)=\alpha$, membership of $\operatorname{inr}f$ in the original
component through $u$, avoidance of all vertex-nodes of $X_0$ by that
component, and $w=(R d).u_1$ or $w=(R d).u_2$.  Write
$\delta=d.1$, $r=D.\gammaIndex(d)$, $\gamma=C.\gamma(r)$, and
$T_d=(R d).\mathit{replacementTree}$.

We first record the background facts that will separate the four switch
colors.  By \noderef{ScheideReducedConfiguration}, $C$ contains the
inherited \noderef{ScheideStructuralConfiguration}; its final field is a
\noderef{GlobalMaximumTriple}, whose \noderef{KTriple} clause says that
$\varphi$ is proper in the sense of \noderef{IsProperEdgeColoring}.
The structural base-position equation and
\noderef{TreeSequenceVertexSet} put $y_0$ in $X_0$, and
\noderef{ScheideDefectiveSetup} gives
\[
 \alpha_0\in\noderef{MissingColors}(G,\varphi,y_0).          \tag{1}
\]
The lemma \noderef{ScheideGlobalMaximumOrder} says that $X_0$ is closed.

According to which of $(R d).u_1,(R d).u_2$ equals $w$,
\noderef{ScheideReplacementData} says that the corresponding one of
$(R d).f_1,(R d).f_2$ is a $\delta$-edge incident with $w$, with its other
endpoint in $X_0$.  The second clause of
\noderef{ScheideSymmetricDefectiveInternal} supplies a $\gamma$-edge
internal to $\{(R d).u_1,(R d).u_2\}$.  Its two ends are distinct by the
loopless law in \noderef{LooplessMultigraph}, so it is incident with $w$.
The endpoint equation for $f$, together with $w\in X$ and $u\notin X$,
makes $f$ a member of \noderef{BoundaryEdges}$(G,X)$.  The two comparison
edges have both endpoints in $X$, so neither is the identity $f$.
Thus they are distinct edges adjacent to $f$ at $w$ in the sense of
\noderef{AdjacentEdges}.  Their three colors and
\noderef{IsProperEdgeColoring} give
\[
 \alpha\ne\delta,\qquad \alpha\ne\gamma.                  \tag{2}
\]

The setup gives $\alpha_0\ne\gamma$ because $\gamma$ is an indexed block
color.  Also $d$ certifies
$\delta\in\noderef{DefectiveColors}(G,\varphi,X_0)$.  Unfolding that
definition and \noderef{BoundaryColorEdges} supplies a $\delta$-colored
identity in \noderef{BoundaryEdges}$(G,X_0)$.  If $\delta$ were missing at
$y_0$, closedness as defined by \noderef{ColoredVertexSetClosed} would
forbid this identity.  Hence $\delta$ is not missing there; the
complementary definitions \noderef{MissingColors} and
\noderef{PresentColors} say that it is present.  Since (1) says that
$\alpha_0$ is missing, we obtain
\[
 \alpha_0\ne\gamma,\qquad \alpha_0\ne\delta.               \tag{3}
\]

Let
\[
 \varphi_d=\noderef{TwoColorComponentSwap}
   (G,\varphi,\gamma,\delta,\operatorname{inl}v^0_d).
\]
For every edge identity $q$, (2)--(3) and the three defining branches of
this swap give the two equivalences
\[
 \varphi_d(q)=\alpha_0\iff\varphi(q)=\alpha_0,
 \qquad
 \varphi_d(q)=\alpha\iff\varphi(q)=\alpha.                 \tag{4}
\]
Indeed, the reverse implications are fixed branches, while an output
$\alpha_0$ or $\alpha$ in the forward implications cannot have arisen
from the only switched inputs $\gamma,\delta$.  By
\noderef{TwoColorIncidenceGraph}, all labeled
$(\alpha_0,\alpha)$ incidences are therefore identical for $\varphi$ and
$\varphi_d$.  The definition \noderef{TwoColorComponent} consequently
identifies the full components through $u$.  Thus the displayed $Q$ under
$\varphi_d$ contains $\operatorname{inr}f$, avoids every vertex-node of
$X_0$, and (4) gives $\varphi_d(f)=\alpha$.

We next prove that no edge identity of $T_d$ belongs to $Q$.  The exact
edge-set equation in \noderef{ScheideReplacementData} says that every
such identity is either a retained identity of $T$ or one of
$(R d).f_1,(R d).f_2$.  Every old tree edge is incident with a vertex in
$X_0$ by the attachment clause of \noderef{TreeSequence}.  If its
edge-node belonged to $Q$, the adjacency criterion in
\noderef{TwoColorIncidenceGraph}, unfolded through \noderef{Incident},
would put that vertex-node in $Q$, contradicting avoidance.

For each $i\in\{1,2\}$, \noderef{ScheideReplacementData} records an
endpoint $v_i\in X_0$, the endpoint equation
$G.\mathop{\rm ends}(f_i)=s(v_i,u_i)$, the old color
$\varphi(f_i)=\delta$, and the exact inequality $f_i\ne f^0_d$.
Consequently $f_i$ is incident with the retained vertex $v_i$ in the
sense of \noderef{Incident}.  The fixed-edge clause of
\noderef{ScheideDefectiveSwitchEffect}, applied separately to $f_1$ and
$f_2$, now gives
\[
 \varphi_d(f_i)=\varphi(f_i)=\delta\qquad(i=1,2).           \tag{5}
\]
By (2)--(3), a $\delta$-colored edge-node is absent from the support of
the $(\alpha_0,\alpha)$ graph, so neither appended identity lies in $Q$.
This proves the claim for every edge of $T_d$.  Since
$\operatorname{inr}f\in Q$, it also proves that $f$ is not an edge
identity of $T_d$.

The theorem \noderef{ScheideReplacementMaximum} says that
$(T_d,e_0,\varphi_d)$ is globally maximum.  We verify explicitly that the
second switch preserves enough earlier witnesses for its Tashkinov tree.
Put $b=C.p-1+2r$ and $n=T.length$.  The position clauses of
\noderef{ScheideReplacementData} say that $T_d$ has length $n$, retains
positions below $b$, shifts later old positions down by two, and places
$f_1,f_2$ in its last two edge positions.

For a retained noninitial base edge, the original
\noderef{TashkinovTree} condition gives an earlier missing witness, which
is again a base vertex.  The base-edge avoidance clause of
\noderef{ScheideStructuralConfiguration} says that its color differs from
every block color, in particular from $\gamma$.  For a retained block
edge, the structural configuration gives its indexed block color and a
base vertex missing that color; injectivity of the block-color list and
the fact that this is not the deleted block again make the color different
from $\gamma$.  The exact replacement position equations keep each chosen
base witness earlier than the retained or shifted edge.

Every retained old tree edge is incident with $X_0$ and differs from
$f^0_d$, whose endpoint from \noderef{ScheideDefectiveSetup} lies outside
$X_0$.  Hence the fixed-edge clause of
\noderef{ScheideDefectiveSwitchEffect} preserves its color.  The same
node preserves the missing set at its base witness unless that witness is
$v^0_d$; at $v^0_d$ its erase-and-insert formula removes only $\gamma$,
which differs from every retained witness color just considered.  Every
base vertex lies in the retained set because the exact base and block
positions are disjoint by vertex injectivity in \noderef{TreeSequence}.
Thus every retained noninitial edge has the required earlier missing
witness under $\varphi_d$ at a vertex of $X_0$.

For the final two edges, equation (5) gives their color $\delta$, and the
missing-set formula in \noderef{ScheideDefectiveSwitchEffect} inserts
$\delta$ at $v^0_d$.  We locate that witness before both edges.  The
definition \noderef{ScheideDefectiveSetup} records the full simple
$(\alpha_0,\delta)$ incidence path from $y_0$ to an outside vertex, the
occurrence of $v^0_d$ on it, and the fact that no later vertex-node lies
in $X_0$.  Reversing the displayed order of the two colors does not change
\noderef{TwoColorIncidenceGraph}; with starting missing color $\alpha_0$
and defective color $\delta$, these data give the predicate
\noderef{ScheideExitVertex} for $v^0_d$.  Unfolding
\noderef{ScheideExitVertices} therefore puts $v^0_d$ in the finite exit
set.  The image conclusion of \noderef{ScheideStructuralExitsInBase}
supplies $a:\operatorname{Fin}C.p$ with $v^0_d=C.y(a)$.

The base-position equation identifies this as the old tree vertex at
position $a$.  The deleted vertices occupy positions $C.p+2r$ and
$C.p+2r+1$, so vertex injectivity in \noderef{TreeSequence} shows that
$v^0_d$ is retained.  The retained-position equation in
\noderef{ScheideReplacementData} places it at position $a$ of $T_d$.
Since $r:\operatorname{Fin}C.s$, one has $C.s\ge1$; the structural length
equation $n+1=C.p+2C.s$ gives
\[
 a\le C.p-1\le n-2<n-1.
\]
The last two edge positions are $n-2,n-1$, so the retained occurrence of
$v^0_d$ is an earlier missing-$\delta$ witness for each appended edge.

All witnesses just constructed lie in $X_0$, whose vertex-nodes $Q$
avoids.  If an edge-node of $Q$ were incident with such a witness, the
definition of \noderef{TwoColorIncidenceGraph} and
\noderef{Incident} would put the witness vertex-node in the same
\noderef{TwoColorComponent}, a contradiction.  The second switch
therefore fixes every incident edge value and, by the definition
\noderef{MissingColors}, preserves the witness's missing set.  It also
fixes every tree-edge color because no edge identity of $T_d$ lies in
$Q$.  The same witnesses verify all clauses of
\noderef{TashkinovTree} for $T_d$ under $\psi$.

The theorem \noderef{ScheideReplacementMaximum} supplies properness of
$\varphi_d$.
Since $\alpha\ne\alpha_0$,
\noderef{TwoColorComponentSwapProper} makes $\psi$ proper, again in the
sense of \noderef{IsProperEdgeColoring}.  The defining branches of
\noderef{TwoColorComponentSwap} fix the uncolored value and map every
colored value to a colored value.  Hence the assertion from
\noderef{ScheideReplacementMaximum} that exactly $e_0$ is uncolored is
preserved in the precise sense of \noderef{IsOneEdgeUncolored}.  The
criticality, chromatic-index, and maximum-degree clauses of
\noderef{KTriple} depend only on $G$ and $k$, so they are copied from the
replacement triple.  Thus $(G,e_0,\psi)$ is a $k$-triple.  The tree and
its vertex set have not changed, so the maximum-cardinality clause from
\noderef{ScheideReplacementMaximum}, together with the Tashkinov
verification, establishes all three clauses of
\noderef{GlobalMaximumTriple} for $(T_d,e_0,\psi)$.

It remains to check the remaining displayed package.  The vertex $y_0$
is retained in $T_d$: its base position is zero, whereas the deleted block
positions are at least $C.p\ge2$.  Under the first switch,
\noderef{ScheideDefectiveSwitchEffect} either fixes its missing set or,
if $y_0=v^0_d$, erases only $\gamma\ne\alpha_0$ and inserts $\delta$.
Together with (1), this gives
\[
 \alpha_0\in\noderef{MissingColors}(G,\varphi_d,y_0).
\]
The replacement vertex equation places the selected
$w=(R d).u_1$ or $(R d).u_2$ in $V(T_d)$ and gives
\[
 V(T_d)\subseteq X_0\cup\{(R d).u_1,(R d).u_2\}
             \subseteq\noderef{ScheideEnlargedVertexSet}(R)=X.
\]
Since $u\notin X$, it is not in $V(T_d)$.  Along with (4), membership and
avoidance of $Q$, the global-maximum conclusion, and the already proved
freshness of $f$, these are exactly all asserted transport properties.
\end{proof}

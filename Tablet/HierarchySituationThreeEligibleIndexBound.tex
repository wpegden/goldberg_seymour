\begin{lemma}[Situation 7.3: maximum-index normalization]
Fix a rung-local minimum hierarchy counterexample \(C\), put
\[
 r=\noderef{HierarchyPathNumber}(C.\mathit{hierarchy}),\qquad
 \ell=\noderef{HierarchyFullTailLength}(C),
\]
and assume \(2\le r<\ell\).  Assume also that every vertex has a missing
color under every proper coloring that leaves exactly \(e_0\) uncolored.
Then every \(i\) in
\(\noderef{HierarchyEligibleCommonMissingIndices}\) satisfies \(i<r\).
Thus the maximum eligible common-missing index, when the finite set is
nonempty, is strictly before the path part of the tail.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Write \(I\) for the eligible-index set.  Suppose \(i\in I\) and
\(r\le i\).  The nonempty finite set
\[
                   I_{\ge r}=\{t\in I:r\le t\}
\]
has a largest member \(m\).  Unfolding
\noderef{HierarchyEligibleCommonMissingIndices} gives an actual
\noderef{HierarchyReplacementPresentation} \(P\) and a color \(\alpha\)
missing under \(\mu=P.\mathit{coloring}\) both at the exact displayed
vertex \(y_m\) and at the terminal vertex \(y_\ell\).  Hence
\[
                         r\le m\le\ell-1.                     \tag{1}
\]
The \noderef{SameGoodHierarchy} field of \(P\) identifies all ordered
stages, divider positions, stored sets, and Gamma pairs with those of
\(C\).  In particular, the replacement last starred stage is the same
exact tree \(A\), and its path number and full-tail prefixes agree with
those defining \(I\).

Suppose first that \(m<\ell-1\).  Put
\[
 X^-=T(y_m),\quad X=T(y_{m+1}),\quad
 B=T(y_{\ell-1}),\quad
 D=\noderef{HierarchyStoredColorsAt}(P.H,q),
\]
where the displayed trees are the corresponding
\noderef{HierarchyFullTailPrefix}es and \(q\) is the last stored index.
The hierarchy nesting and the prefix definitions give
\[
 A\prec X^-\prec X\preccurlyeq B\prec T,\qquad
 |X|=|X^-|+1.                                                 \tag{2}
\]
Every relation in (2) is entrywise in the sense of
\noderef{TreeSequenceInitialSegment}.  Since \(r\le m\), the candidate
clause in \noderef{HierarchyPathNumber} says that the edge from \(y_m\)
to \(y_{m+1}\), and every later tail edge, has the consecutive endpoint
pair prescribed by the paper.

Apply \noderef{HierarchyCounterexamplePenultimateElementary} to the six
fields of \(P.\mathit{minimumContext}\): properness,
exactly-one-uncoloredness, the complete lower-rung package,
fewer-divider induction, lexicographic minimality, and the \(2n+11\)
estimate.  Its conclusion is
\[
            V(B)\text{ is elementary under }\mu.              \tag{3}
\]
Choose \(\theta\in\noderef{MissingColors}(G,\mu,y_{m+1})\) by the
palette-surplus hypothesis.  Vertex injectivity in
\noderef{TreeSequence}, the \(\alpha\)-missing witness at \(y_m\), and
(3) give \(\alpha\ne\theta\).  They also say that \(y_m,y_{m+1}\) are
the respective first missing positions after \(A\), so neither affected
color is missing on \(A\).

Unfolding \noderef{HierarchyStoredColorsAt} at the last index gives
\[
 D=D_n\setminus\noderef{TreeMissingColors}(\mu,A).             \tag{4}
\]
The exact first-position conclusion of
\noderef{HierarchyTailMissingColorExclusion} excludes each affected
color from its preceding tail interval.  It retains one named exception:
at divider zero an SE/RE first seed can carry a color in \(D_n\).

If \(\alpha\notin D\), (4) gives \(\alpha\notin D_n\).  When also
\(\theta\notin D\), the same holds for \(\theta\), and the seed exception
is eliminated for both colors.  The exact unused interval and first
witnesses satisfy the relevant case of \noderef{ChangeLemma}.  It
identifies the component through \(y_m\) with the component through
\(y_{m+1}\).  That component is different from the component through
\(y_\ell\): equality would put the three distinct missing endpoints
\(y_m,y_{m+1},y_\ell\) in one two-color component.  Properness and
\noderef{MissingColorIncidenceEndpoint} give incidence degree at most
one at all three, contradicting
\noderef{TwoColorComponentSpanningPath} and
\noderef{TwoColorIncidenceDegreeAtMostTwo}.  Hence
\noderef{TwoColorComponentsDisjoint} separates the two components.
Switch the terminal component by \noderef{TwoColorComponentSwap}.
It is a possibly singleton \noderef{TwoColorPathComponent}, is proper by
\noderef{TwoColorComponentSwapProper}, and fixes the uncolored value.

If \(\theta\in D\), choose
\(\gamma\in P.\mathit{good}.\Gamma^q_\theta\) using the cardinality-two
field of \noderef{GoodHierarchy}.  Avoidance and
\noderef{HierarchyStageMissingColors} put a first
\(\gamma\)-missing witness in \(A\) and exclude \(\gamma\) from the exact
segment through \(y_{m+1}\).  Apply \noderef{ChangeLemma} first to
\((\gamma,\alpha)\), separate and switch the terminal component, then
apply it to \((\gamma,\theta)\) and perform the second terminal switch.
If the divider count is zero and the final kind is SE/RE, (4) excludes
the unstored color from the exact seed, while the terminal-seed clause of
\noderef{StableChangeCasesOneTwo} fixes the seed when the stored color is
its color.  Thus the sole seed exception is checked in both stored
branches.  The case \(\alpha\in D\) is symmetric: first use one color of
\(\Gamma^q_\alpha\) to move the terminal missing color off \(\alpha\),
then use the preceding \(\theta\)-change.  This is precisely the
two-switch alternative in the paper's proof of Claim \(p-1\).

We verify the replacement after each switch.  Let \(\psi'\) be a current
switched coloring and \(Q\) its switched terminal component.  For a
vertex \(v\in V(B)\) outside \(Q\), any affected incident edge would
have an edge-node adjacent to \(v\) in
\noderef{TwoColorIncidenceGraph}, so every incident edge is fixed.  If
\(v\in Q\), separation from the first-witness component and
elementarity (3) say that neither affected color is missing at \(v\).
Properness supplies exactly one incident edge of each affected color;
both edge-nodes lie in \(Q\), and the switch exchanges these two while
fixing every other incident color.  Consequently
\[
 \noderef{MissingColors}(G,\psi',v)
     =\noderef{MissingColors}(G,\psi,v)\qquad(v\in V(B)).      \tag{5}
\]
This is the required pointwise outside-versus-internal-vertex argument;
it does not infer vertex-disjointness from component separation.  It
also preserves every stored first missing position, including the
totalized fallback, and verifies the Gamma cutoff at \(X\).

The route is obtained from an actual certificate.  Inspect
\[
 \mathcal E_P.\mathit{series}.\mathit{certified}
       (\operatorname{Fin.last}\mathcal E_P.\mathit{rounds})
\]
in \noderef{TashkinovSeries}, and use
\noderef{ExtendedTashkinovTree}.\mathit{belowFinal} to put \(X\) below
its output.  In PE, retain the terminal-ladder closure route and use
\noderef{HierarchyBase}.parallel for the exact core and closure.  In
SE/RE, retain the individual connecting edge, singleton connecting set,
literal \noderef{TreeSequenceAdjoinEdge} seed, and route from that seed.
At positive divider count
\noderef{HierarchyNonparallelFirstEdge} puts the seed below \(A\); at
divider zero the seed itself is the route source.  Restrict the old route
at \(X\) with \noderef{TashkinovAugmentationSequenceRestrict}, transport
its exact steps using the edge equalities and (5), and concatenate them
in \noderef{TashkinovAugmentationSequence}.  The old suffix
\(X\longrightarrow T\) transports under the component swap because both
affected colors are missing on \(X\).  These are exactly the route,
missing-set, first-position, and cutoff premises of
\noderef{StableChangeCasesOneTwoSplitTail}, or of
\noderef{StableChangeCaseThree} in the Case 3 alternative, separately
for PE, divider-zero SE/RE, and positive-divider SE/RE.

The cited stable-change theorem returns an actual corresponding
same-rung ETT on \(T\), maximum property, rebuilt base, hierarchy,
goodness, and same-good data.  The final switched color \(\theta\) is
missing at both \(y_{m+1}\) and \(y_\ell\).  Package the output as a
\noderef{HierarchyReplacementPresentation} \(P'\).  Its complete
minimum context is built field by field: properness is the component-swap
theorem; the uncolored value is fixed; correspondence transports the
lower-rung package and large estimate; same-good equality transports the
fewer-divider field; and equality of stages, first positions, and
terminal tree identifies \noderef{HierarchyCounterexampleMeasure},
transporting lexicographic minimality.  Thus
\(m+1,P',\theta\) witnesses \(m+1\in I\), contradicting maximality of
\(m\).

It remains that \(m=\ell-1\).  Set
\(B=T(y_{\ell-1})\), \(X^-=T(y_{\ell-2})\), and let
\(e_\ell=T.\mathit{edge}(|T|-1)\) be the individual last edge.  Since
\(r\le\ell-1\), the path-number candidate clause gives
\[
             G.\mathit{ends}(e_\ell)=\{y_{\ell-1},y_\ell\}.   \tag{6}
\]
Restrict the actual terminal-series route first to \(T\) and then to
\(B\).  Induction on the finite augmentation sequence, using the fact
that each \noderef{TashkinovAugmentation} raises length by one, identifies
its last step with \(B\prec T\).  Unfolding that step gives a color
\(\beta\) such that
\[
 \mu(e_\ell)=\operatorname{some}\beta,\qquad
 \beta\in\noderef{TreeMissingColors}(\mu,B).                  \tag{7}
\]
Choose a displayed \(z\in V(B)\) missing \(\beta\).  Equations (6)--(7)
and properness imply \(z\ne y_{\ell-1}\), since \(e_\ell\) is a
\(\beta\)-edge incident there.  The exact one-edge relation
\(X^-\prec B\) therefore puts \(z\) in \(X^-\).

Define \(\sigma(e_\ell)=\operatorname{some}\alpha\) and
\(\sigma(e)=\mu(e)\) otherwise.  Common missingness of \(\alpha\) at the
two endpoints in (6) proves \noderef{IsProperEdgeColoring} by checking
adjacent edge pairs.  The edge \(e_\ell\ne e_0\): its displayed index is
positive because \(2\le\ell\), and edge injectivity plus the first-edge
field of \noderef{TreeSequence} identifies only index zero with \(e_0\).
Thus \noderef{IsOneEdgeUncolored} is preserved.  Neither endpoint in
(6) lies in \(X^-\), so every displayed edge of \(B\) and every missing
set on \(X^-\) is fixed.                                      \(\tag{8}\)

In particular \(\sigma\) is a \noderef{StableColoring} on \(T_n\).
Apply \noderef{Td} to the replacement ETT, its maximum property and
positive rung, and the lower-rung field of
\(P.\mathit{minimumContext}\).  It yields a
\noderef{ModColoring}.  Transport the old PE route, or the route from the
retained exact SE/RE seed, to \(B\) with
\noderef{TashkinovAugmentationSequenceColoringTransport}, using (8) for
both its edge and missing-set premises.  The mod-coloring clause returns
an actual \noderef{CorrespondingExtendedTashkinovTree} on \(B\), and
\noderef{MaximumPropertyTransport} gives its maximum property.

Apply \noderef{GoodHierarchyTerminalPrefixRepackage} to the old hierarchy
and \(B\), retaining its shortened stages, divider positions, core,
closure, and Gamma pairs.  Rebuild the base for the new corresponding
ETT.  In SE/RE, correspondence identifies the terminal ladder tree and
the old nonparallel core.  In PE, the two terminal certificates identify
the exact singleton connecting edge and support vertex; (8) fixes the
old closure route and boundary colors, while the coloring-free
\noderef{OrderedTreeUnion} retains the core.  These verify every field
of \noderef{HierarchyBase}.

Copy the shortened stages and divider positions to obtain a
\noderef{TashkinovHierarchy} ending at \(B\).  For goodness, (8)
identifies stored sets, stage-missing sets, and first missing positions.
The color \(\beta\) has the unchanged witness \(z\in X^-\); for
\(\alpha\), either an earlier first witness is unchanged or the old last
witness and new totalized fallback have the same position.  Hence all
\noderef{HierarchySegmentColors} sets agree.  The two-color, avoidance,
disjoint-pair, refresh, minus-closed, and PE parallel-base fields copy,
since all their stages lie in \(X^-\).  This constructs a genuine
\noderef{GoodHierarchy}.

The vertex \(z\) still misses \(\beta\), and recoloring the unique
incident \(\beta\)-edge \(e_\ell\) makes \(y_{\ell-1}\) miss \(\beta\).
Thus the new terminal tree \(B\) is not elementary and we have a genuine
hierarchy counterexample \(C'\).  Correspondence gives equal rung and the
copied hierarchy gives equal divider count.  Since \(r<\ell\), all
consecutive-edge equations beginning at \(r\) remain in \(B\), so \(r\)
is a path-number candidate there.  A smaller candidate would also be a
candidate for the old \(T\), contradicting minimality of \(r\).
Therefore the new path number equals \(r\), while the terminal excess
above the last ordinary hierarchy stage is smaller by one.  Hence
\[
 \noderef{HierarchyCounterexampleMeasure}(C')
   <\noderef{HierarchyCounterexampleMeasure}(P.\mathit{counterexample}),
\]
contradicting the lexicographic-minimality field of
\(P.\mathit{minimumContext}\).  Both possibilities for \(m\) are
impossible, so every \(i\in I\) satisfies \(i<r\).
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Stable change, Cases 1 and 2, with a transported split tail]
Fix a finite loopless multigraph with its individual edge type, a palette
of \(k\) colors, a distinguished edge \(e_0\), an input coloring, an
ordered tree-sequence \(T\), a generating coloring \(\varphi_n\), a
positive-rung ETT \(\mathcal E\) on \(T\), a hierarchy base, and a
\noderef{HierarchyCounterexample} \(C\).  Assume the properness,
exactly-one-uncoloredness, complete lower-rung six-pack, fewer-divider
induction, rung-local lexicographic minimality, and \(2n+11\) estimate
recorded in a
\noderef{HierarchyMinimumCounterexampleContext}.

Write \(H=C.\mathit{hierarchy}\), let \(q\) be its last stored index, and
put
\[
 A=\noderef{HierarchyStarredStage}(H,q),\quad
 B=\noderef{HierarchyFullTailPrefix}
       (C,\noderef{HierarchyFullTailLength}(C)-1).
\]
Let \(\alpha\ne\beta\) be missing on \(B\).  Let \(z\) certify a possibly
singleton \noderef{TwoColorPathComponent} \(Q\) under \(\varphi_n\), put
\[
 \sigma_n=\noderef{TwoColorComponentSwap}
      (G,\varphi_n,\alpha,\beta,z),
\]
and assume that \(Q\) is disjoint from the full component through the
first displayed vertex of \(T\) missing \(\alpha\).  Assume the explicit
Cases 1/2 alternative: either \(q>0\) and \(\alpha\) is missing on
\(T_{n,q}\), or \(q=0\) and either \(\alpha\) is missing on \(T_n\), or
\(\alpha\) is missing on \(A\) and both \(\alpha,\beta\) lie outside
\(D_n=\noderef{TashkinovStoredColors}(\mathcal E,n)\).

Let \(X\) be an exact split prefix satisfying
\[
             A\prec X\preccurlyeq B
\]
in the sense of \noderef{TreeSequenceInitialSegment}.  Assume the
following transport data.
\begin{enumerate}
\item Every individual displayed edge of \(T\) at a position \(r\) with
\(|A|\le r<|X|\) keeps its value under \(\sigma_n\).
\item There is a verified \(\sigma_n\)-augmentation route through \(X\):
if the last extension kind is PE, it is a
\noderef{TashkinovAugmentationSequence} \(T_n\longrightarrow X\); if the
kind is SE or RE, there are the exact edge \(f_n\), exact seed
\(T_n+f_n\), singleton equality \(F_n=\{f_n\}\), and a
\(\sigma_n\)-augmentation sequence from that seed to \(X\).
\item There is an original-color augmentation suffix
\(X\longrightarrow T\), and both \(\alpha\) and \(\beta\) are missing on
\(X\) under \(\sigma_n\).
\item For every vertex \(v\in V(B)\),
\(\overline\sigma_n(v)=\overline\varphi_n(v)\).
\item For every \(\eta\in D_{n,q}=\noderef{HierarchyStoredColorsAt}(H,q)\),
the totalized first missing position on \(T\) is the same under the two
colorings.
\item If either affected color belongs to the copied last Gamma pair
\(C.\mathit{good}.\Gamma^q_\eta\), then the old first missing position of
\(\eta\) is at most \(|X|\).
\end{enumerate}

Then \(\sigma_n\) is \noderef{HierarchyWeaklyStable} at \(A\) and is a
\noderef{ModColoring} of \(\mathcal E\).  Moreover there exist an actual
\noderef{CorrespondingExtendedTashkinovTree} certificate on the same full
ordered tree \(T\), maximum property for its replacement ETT, a hierarchy
base for that ETT, a full hierarchy \(K\), and a
\noderef{GoodHierarchy} \(g_K\), such that
\[
                 \noderef{SameGoodHierarchy}(C.\mathit{good},g_K).
\]
Thus the conclusion constructs, rather than assumes, the full-tree ETT,
maximum property, base, hierarchy, goodness, and same-good data; it makes
no assertion that all affected tail-edge values are fixed.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Let \(T_n\) be the terminal ladder tree and retain the notation
\(H,q,A,B,Q\) from the statement.  First derive the fact (6.6) which is
not a field of the minimum context.  Apply
\noderef{HierarchyCounterexamplePenultimateElementary}, in order, to
\(G,k,e_0\), the input coloring, \(T\), \(\varphi_n\), the present ETT,
base, and \noderef{HierarchyCounterexample} \(C\), followed by exactly
\[
 \mathit{ctx.proper},\quad \mathit{ctx.oneEdgeUncolored},\quad
 \mathit{ctx.lowerRung},\quad \mathit{ctx.fewerDividers},\quad
 \mathit{ctx.lexicographicMinimality},\quad
 \mathit{ctx.largeMissingSet}.
\]
The six displayed arguments are respectively the properness,
exactly-one-uncoloredness, complete lower-rung six-pack, fewer-divider
induction, rung-local lexicographic minimality, and \(2n+11\) projections
of the stated \noderef{HierarchyMinimumCounterexampleContext}.  By the
definitions of \noderef{HierarchyFullTailLength} and
\noderef{HierarchyFullTailPrefix}, the exact conclusion of the cited
lemma is
\[
 h_B:\quad
 V(B)\text{ is elementary under }\varphi_n.                      \tag{0}
\]
Thus \(h_B\) is derived from those six projections; it is not itself a
context projection.

Now apply \noderef{StableChangeCasesOneTwo} to the same
\(G,k,e_0\), input coloring, \(T\), \(\varphi_n\), ETT, base, and
counterexample; pass, in its displayed order, the six context projections
above with the newly derived fact \(h_B\) between
\(\mathit{ctx.lexicographicMinimality}\) and
\(\mathit{ctx.largeMissingSet}\); and then pass \(\alpha\ne\beta\), the
two supplied colors missing on \(B\), the path certificate for \(Q\), the
defining component-swap equality for \(\sigma_n\), the Cases 1/2
alternative, and the component disjointness.  We obtain
\[
\begin{array}{ll}
\text{(S1)}&\noderef{HierarchyWeaklyStable}(H,q,\sigma_n),\\
\text{(S2)}&\noderef{ModColoring}(\mathcal E,\sigma_n),\\
\text{(S3)}&A=T_n\text{ or }A\text{ has an actual maximum ETT
 presentation under }\sigma_n,\\
\text{(S4)}&\text{when }q>0,\text{ actual objects }
 \mathit{inputA},\mathcal E_A,\mathcal B_A,H_A,g_A,\mathit{mpA}
 \text{ on }A.
\end{array}                                                       \tag{1}
\]
Here \(\mathcal E_A\) is the returned ETT under \(\sigma_n\),
\(\mathit{mpA}\) is its own maximum witness, \(\mathcal B_A\) is its
base, \(H_A\) is its hierarchy, and \(g_A\) is its good-hierarchy
witness.  Retain every further conjunct of (S4): if \(\kappa_A\) and
\(\kappa\) are the terminal extension kinds of \(\mathcal E_A\) and
\(\mathcal E\), respectively, then
\[
 \kappa_A=\kappa,\qquad H_A.\mathit{dividerCount}+1=q;             \tag{1a}
\]
the core and closure of \(\mathcal B_A\) equal those of the original
base; every stage of \(H_A\) equals the corresponding retained stage of
\(H\); and, at every retained index, its
\noderef{HierarchyStoredColorsAt} set and each defined Gamma pair of
\(g_A\) equal the corresponding set and pair of \(C.\mathit{good}\).
Finally retain the separate last output of (S4): the required
minus-closedness of \(T_{n,q}\) under \(\sigma_n\).  Thus all the prefix
objects, \(\mathit{mpA}\), the kind and divider equalities, and the
core, closure, stage, stored-set, Gamma, and last minus-closed equalities
are fixed witnesses returned by the cited lemma.

We next construct the exact full-tree route.  The original suffix is a
\(\varphi_n\)-augmentation sequence from \(X\) to \(T\), while both
affected colors are missing on \(X\) under the displayed component swap.
Therefore
\noderef{TashkinovAugmentationSequenceComponentSwap}, applied with the
same root \(z\), gives the same exact ordered sequence and individual
new-edge identities as a \(\sigma_n\)-augmentation sequence
\[
                         X\longrightarrow T.                 \tag{2}
\]
This application is precisely why no assertion about the colors of the
suffix edges is needed.

If the final kind is PE, concatenate the supplied
\(\sigma_n\)-route \(T_n\longrightarrow X\) with (2).  Transitivity of the
reflexive-transitive relation in
\noderef{TashkinovAugmentationSequence} gives
\[
                         T_n\longrightarrow T.               \tag{3P}
\]
If the final kind is SE or RE, retain the supplied individual identity
\(f_n\), the literal equality \(F_n=\{f_n\}\), and the supplied
\noderef{TreeSequenceAdjoinEdge} certificate for the exact seed.  Concatenate
its route \(T_n+f_n\longrightarrow X\) with (2), obtaining
\[
                         T_n+f_n\longrightarrow T.           \tag{3N}
\]
Thus (3P) and (3N) have exactly the two shapes required by the universal
clause of \noderef{ModColoring}; no seed is reconstructed from endpoint
data, and parallel edges remain distinguished.

The `abovePenultimate' and `strictAbovePenultimate' fields in the
definition \noderef{ExtendedTashkinovTree} say, for this old ETT, exactly
that \(T_n\preccurlyeq T\) and \(|T_n|<|T|\).
Instantiate (S2) at the actual tree \(T\) and
the relevant route (3P) or (3N).  It returns a nonempty
\noderef{CorrespondingExtendedTashkinovTree}; choose one and call it
\(\mathit{corrMod}\).  Applying \noderef{MaximumPropertyTransport} to
(S2), \(\mathit{corrMod}\), and the maximum-property field of
\noderef{HierarchyCounterexample} \(C\) gives a maximum witness
\(\mathit{mpMod}\) for
\(\mathit{corrMod}.\mathit{replacementETT}\).  Retain this pair for the
SE/RE branch.                                                        \(\tag{4}\)

It remains to build the base, hierarchy, and goodness on this actual
replacement ETT.  First suppose the final kind is PE.  Unfolding the
weak stability in (S1) gives the required
\noderef{CombinedStableColoring} on \(T_n\) and the PE closure, and (3P)
is the required full augmentation route.  Apply
\noderef{HierarchySplitter} to the original ETT, the maximum and
positive-rung fields of \(C\), the context's lower-rung projection, the
original base and hierarchy \(H\), and the present PE-kind equality.
Its old-coloring properness and exactly-one-uncolored premises are
\(\mathit{ctx.proper}\) and \(\mathit{ctx.oneEdgeUncolored}\); its
new-coloring versions are the first two fields of (S2); and its final
two premises are the combined stability from (S1) and route (3P).  Name
the objects returned together by this one application
\[
 \mathit{corrSplit},\qquad \mathit{mpSplit},\qquad
 \mathit{baseSplit},\qquad K.                                      \tag{4a}
\]
Thus \(\mathit{corrSplit}\) is the final corresponding certificate in
the PE branch, \(\mathit{mpSplit}\) is the maximum witness for its own
replacement ETT, and \(\mathit{baseSplit},K\) are the base and hierarchy
on that same replacement.  The splitter identifies their core and PE
closure with those of the original base and identifies every stage and
divider position of \(K\) with that of \(H\).  We make no identification
between \(\mathit{corrMod}\) and \(\mathit{corrSplit}\).

If the final kind is SE or RE, use exactly
\(\mathit{corrMod},\mathit{mpMod}\) from (4).
The fields of \noderef{CorrespondingExtendedTashkinovTree} identify the
rung, every positive ladder tree, every raw stored set, the exact
connecting-edge set, and the extension kind.  Define the replacement
base core to be the replacement terminal ladder tree.  The old
\noderef{HierarchyBase}.nonparallel clause identifies the old core with
\(T_n\), so this is the same exact ordered tree after the ladder-tree
identification.  Copy the numerical support position and unused closure;
the terminal missing-color clause is reflexive under \(\sigma_n\), the PE
implication is vacuous, and the non-PE clause is the defining core
equality.  Copy the divider count, stages, and positions of \(H\).
All fields of \noderef{TashkinovHierarchy} are coloring-free exact-prefix,
strict-length, and exact-entry assertions, so they construct the required
hierarchy \(K\) on the replacement ETT.

In PE set \(\mathit{corrT}=\mathit{corrSplit}\),
\(\mathit{mpT}=\mathit{mpSplit}\), and
\(\mathit{baseT}=\mathit{baseSplit}\).  In SE/RE set
\(\mathit{corrT}=\mathit{corrMod}\),
\(\mathit{mpT}=\mathit{mpMod}\), and let \(\mathit{baseT}\) be the base
just rebuilt.  We now compare the stored sets for this branch-coherent
tuple.  Correspondence identifies the raw stored arrays.  By
\noderef{TashkinovStoredColors}, the computed \(D_n\) is that common
bounded union with the terminal-ladder missing colors removed.  Weak
stability in (S1) identifies the missing set at each vertex of \(T_n\),
hence identifies the two computed copies of \(D_n\).
Every starred stage is an exact prefix of \(A\), and
\(A\preccurlyeq X\preccurlyeq B\).  For a vertex of a starred stage,
unfold \noderef{TreeSequenceVertexSet}, carry its displayed index through
these exact \noderef{TreeSequenceInitialSegment}s, and apply the stated
vertexwise equality on \(B\).  Thus the two colorings have equal
tree-missing sets on every starred stage.  Unfolding
\noderef{HierarchyStoredColorsAt} gives, at every index \(j\),
\[
                         D^K_{n,j}=D^H_{n,j}.                 \tag{5}
\]
The identical index argument on \(T_n\) for \(j=0\), and on the retained
positive hierarchy stages for \(j>0\), gives equality of the two
\noderef{HierarchyStageMissingColors} sets as well.             \(\tag{6}\)

Define the new Gamma pair by copying
\(C.\mathit{good}.\Gamma^j_\eta\), with the canonical index casts from
the equal divider counts.  Equations (5)--(6) immediately transport the
two-color cardinality and pairwise-disjointness fields of
\noderef{GoodHierarchy}.

We first verify the PE parallel-base field when \(q>0\).  The terminal
kind equality \(\kappa_A=\kappa\) in (1a), together with the present PE
assumption, proves the antecedent of \(g_A.\mathit{parallelBase}\).
Consequently that field supplies both
\[
 \neg\noderef{TreeColorSetMinusClosed}
   (G,\sigma_n,\mathcal B_A.\mathit{core},\Gamma_A^0)              \tag{7a}
\]
and
\[
 4\leq\bigl|(\overline\sigma_n(T_n^A)\cap
       \overline\sigma_n(\mathcal B_A.\mathit{closure}))
       -\Gamma_A^0\bigr|,                                         \tag{7b}
\]
where \(T_n^A\) is the terminal ladder tree of \(\mathcal E_A\), and
\(\Gamma_A^0\) is the union of the zeroth \(g_A\)-pairs over the zeroth
stored set of \(H_A\).

Every object in (7a)--(7b) is exactly its counterpart for the full PE
hierarchy \(K\).  The core and closure equalities retained in (S4)
identify \(\mathcal B_A\)'s core and closure with those of the original
base, and the output of \noderef{HierarchySplitter} identifies
\(\mathit{baseSplit}\)'s core and closure with those same two ordered
trees.  Next, the base-stage equation of \(H_A\), the retained equality
of its stage zero with stage zero of \(H\), the splitter's equality of
stage zero of \(K\) with that same stage of \(H\), and the base-stage
equation of \(K\) identify \(T_n^A\) with the terminal ladder tree of
\(\mathit{corrSplit}.\mathit{replacementETT}\).  Finally, the retained
zeroth stored-set and Gamma equalities of (S4), equation (5), and the
copied definition of the new Gamma pairs identify \(\Gamma_A^0\) with
the zeroth reserved union for \(K\).  Rewriting (7a) and (7b) through
these exact equalities proves respectively the non-minus-closed and
cardinality-at-least-four clauses.  Both assertions already use
\(\sigma_n\), so no coloring transport is hidden here.

Suppose \(q>0\).  Use the actual prefix good hierarchy \(g_A\) in (S4)
for every unique-refresh and minus-closed clause strictly before the last
divider.  Its exact retained stage, stored-set, and Gamma equalities
identify these clauses with those just defined for \(K\).  The
unique-refresh clause at the last divider transports directly from
\(C.\mathit{good}\): all vertices in the relevant stage difference lie
in \(A\preccurlyeq B\), so the vertexwise equality on \(B\), equations
(5)--(6), and the copied Gamma pairs transport the old unique witness and
its uniqueness.  The remaining last minus-closed clause is exactly the
separately retained certificate in (S4).  When \(q=0\), there are no
unique-refresh or minus-closed fields, and in the SE/RE case the
parallel-base implication is vacuous.

For completeness, consider the PE parallel-base field when \(q=0\).
Here \noderef{HierarchyStarredStage} identifies \(A\) with the base core,
while the parallel field of \noderef{HierarchyBase} supplies an
\noderef{OrderedTreeUnion} certificate for that core and puts
\(T_n\preccurlyeq A\).  Since \(A\prec X\), the supplied PE route
\(T_n\to X\), whose \noderef{TashkinovAugmentation} steps increase length
by one, has an exact intermediate stage \(A\).  Split it there and unpack
the first \(\sigma_n\)-augmentation \(A\to Y\).  It supplies the exact
new edge \(g=Y.\mathit{edge}(|A|)\), its \(\sigma_n\)-color \(c\), and a
\(\sigma_n\)-missing witness for \(c\) on \(A\).  The exact prefixes
\(Y\preccurlyeq X\preccurlyeq B\preccurlyeq T\) identify this edge with
\(T.\mathit{edge}(|A|)\).  The first transport assumption fixes this
individual edge because \(|A|<|X|\), so its \(\varphi_n\)-color is also
\(c\).  Vertexwise equality on \(B\), restricted along
\(A\preccurlyeq B\), turns the same witness into a
\(\varphi_n\)-missing witness.

The attachment field of \noderef{TreeSequence} says that \(g\) joins the
new displayed vertex of \(Y\) to an earlier displayed vertex.  Exact
prefix equality puts the earlier endpoint in \(V(A)\), while vertex
injectivity puts the new endpoint outside \(V(A)\).  Hence \(g\) is a
literal \noderef{BoundaryEdges} member of \(V(A)\), and its displayed
color makes it a \noderef{BoundaryColorEdges} member of color \(c\)
under \(\sigma_n\).

Suppose \(c\) lay in the stage-zero Gamma union.  Choose
\(\eta\in\noderef{HierarchyStoredColorsAt}(H,0)\) whose old Gamma pair
contains \(c\).  Membership in this stored set excludes \(\eta\) from
the colors missing on \(A\).  Along the exact prefix
\(A\preccurlyeq T\), its old first missing position is therefore
strictly after \(|A|\), or is the fallback \(|T|\), which is also
strictly after \(|A|\) because \(A\prec X\preccurlyeq T\).  Since
\(q=0\), the terminal-stage field of \noderef{TashkinovHierarchy}
identifies the next hierarchy stage with \(T\).  Thus the exact position
\(|A|\) and the old value \(\varphi_n(g)=c\) place \(c\) in
\noderef{HierarchySegmentColors}(H,0,\eta)\), contradicting old
\noderef{GoodHierarchy}.avoidance.  Hence \(c\) is unreserved.  The
missing-color witness and exact colored boundary edge now show directly
from \noderef{TreeColorSetMinusClosed} that the core is not minus-closed
under \(\sigma_n\).
The combined stability in (S1) consists of two
\noderef{StableColoring} relations.  Their vertexwise clauses identify
missing-color sets on \(T_n\) and on the PE closure; together with (5)
and the copied Gamma pairs, they identify the finite-set difference in
the old cardinality-four clause with the corresponding new difference.
Thus that bound transports.  This proves both parts of the parallel-base
field without asserting equality of all boundary-edge colors.

We next verify avoidance at every retained index below the last.  Suppose
\(q>0\), and fix \(j<q\), a color \(\eta\) in the retained stored set at
\(j\), and a copied color \(c\in g_A.\Gamma^j_\eta\).  Let
\[
 U=H_A.\mathit{stage}(j+1)=K.\mathit{stage}(j+1)                 \tag{8}
\]
be the common next stage, where the equality is obtained through the two
retained stage arrays.  The same stage equations give exact ordered
prefixes \(U\preccurlyeq A\preccurlyeq T\), and hence \(|U|\leq|A|\).
Apply \noderef{HierarchyFirstMissingPositionPrefixCutoff} to the coloring
\(\sigma_n\), the exact prefix \(A\preccurlyeq T\), the color \(\eta\),
and cutoff \(|U|\).  It gives the exact numeric equality
\[
 \min\{\noderef{HierarchyFirstMissingPosition}(\sigma_n,A,\eta),|U|\}
 =
 \min\{\noderef{HierarchyFirstMissingPosition}(\sigma_n,T,\eta),|U|\}.
                                                                    \tag{9}
\]
The starred stage at \(j\), the next stage \(U\), and every exact edge
of \(U\) are the same for \(H_A\) and \(K\).  Equation (9) identifies
the remaining upper range bound in
\noderef{HierarchySegmentColors}; both segment predicates use the same
coloring \(\sigma_n\).  Their
\noderef{HierarchyStageMissingColors} sets are equal as well: for
\(j>0\) this is the retained equality of the corresponding stage, while
for \(j=0\) the base-stage equations of \(H_A\) and \(K\), together with
their common retained stage zero, identify the two terminal ladder
trees.  The avoidance field of \(g_A\) therefore transfers pointwise:
the copied color \(c\) is missing on the common stage and occurs on no
exact edge in the full hierarchy's segment.  This proves avoidance at
every \(j<q\), including the fallback case covered by the cited cutoff
lemma.

Only avoidance at the last stored index remains.  Fix
\(\eta\in D^H_{n,q}\) and
\(c\in C.\mathit{good}.\Gamma^q_\eta\).  Old avoidance says that \(c\)
is stage-missing and that no exact edge position \(r\) satisfies
\[
 |A|\le r<
 \min\{\noderef{HierarchyFirstMissingPosition}
          (\varphi_n,T,\eta),|T|\},
 \qquad \varphi_n(T.\mathit{edge}(r))=c.                    \tag{7}
\]
The stage-missing part transports by (6), and the endpoint in (7) is
unchanged by the stored-first-position hypothesis.

If \(c\notin\{\alpha,\beta\}\), the definition of
\noderef{TwoColorComponentSwap} fixes every edge whose old or new value
is \(c\).  A new segment witness under \(\sigma_n\) would therefore be
the identical old witness forbidden by (7).  If
\(c\in\{\alpha,\beta\}\), the Gamma-cutoff premise says that the common
first missing position of \(\eta\) is at most \(|X|\).  Every candidate
\(r\) for the new segment then satisfies
\(|A|\le r<|X|\), where the explicit fixed-prefix premise gives
\(\sigma_n(T.\mathit{edge}(r))=\varphi_n(T.\mathit{edge}(r))\).
Again a new witness is an old witness forbidden by (7).  This checks the
last avoidance clause pointwise.  It neither equates the full segment
color sets nor fixes any affected edge after \(X\).

All fields of a \noderef{GoodHierarchy} \(g_K\) on \(K\) have now been
constructed.  The replacement base has the same core and PE closure,
the hierarchy has the same divider count, ordered stages, and positions,
and (5) together with the definition of \(g_K\) gives equal stored sets
and Gamma pairs at every index.  These are exactly the clauses of
\noderef{SameGoodHierarchy}(C.\mathit{good},g_K).  Combining this with
(S1)--(S2), take the final existential tuple in PE to be
\[
 (\mathit{corrSplit},\mathit{mpSplit},\mathit{baseSplit},K,g_K),
\]
and in SE/RE to be
\[
 (\mathit{corrMod},\mathit{mpMod},\mathit{baseT},K,g_K).
\]
Each maximum witness belongs to the replacement ETT in its own tuple.
This proves the stated conclusion.
\end{proof}

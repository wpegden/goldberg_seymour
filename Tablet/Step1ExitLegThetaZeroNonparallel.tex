\begin{lemma}[The zero-divider non-PE predecessor color]
Let $P$ be a Step 1 exit-leg presentation whose good hierarchy has zero
dividers, and suppose that the terminal certified extension kind is not
PE.  Then there is a color $\theta$, different from the two exit-leg
colors, which is missing and singleton-closed on the last starred stage
and singleton-closed on the terminal tree of $P$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Write $X$ for the terminal ladder tree and $A$ for the last starred
stage.  The embedded ETT and its terminal series are fields of
\noderef{Step1ExitLegPresentation}.  The `generatingColoring' field of
\noderef{ExtendedTashkinovTree} identifies the presentation coloring
$\rho$ with the last series coloring, and its `roundsPositive' field
says that the final series index is positive.  Thus the final
certificate supplied by \noderef{TashkinovSeries} cannot be the initial
constructor of \noderef{TashkinovSeriesStep}.  Its three possibilities
are SE, PE, and RE; the hypothesis excluding PE leaves precisely SE and
RE.

In the RE case, `currentColoring' identifies the last coloring with the
preceding coloring $\pi$, and `previousClosed' says that $V(X)$ is
closed under $\pi$.  Since $\rho=\pi$, every color missing on $X$ under
$\rho$ has no $\rho$-colored boundary identity: this is exactly the
content of \noderef{ColoredVertexSetClosed} and
\noderef{BoundaryColorEdges}.  By \noderef{TreeColorSetClosed}, its
singleton is therefore closed on $X$.

In the SE case, again let $\pi$ be the preceding coloring and $\rho$ the
last coloring.  The SE constructor contains a
\noderef{MaximumDefectiveChoice}; its `selectedProper',
`selectedOneUncolored', and `selectedStable' fields state that $\rho$ is
proper, leaves exactly $e_0$ uncolored, and is stable from $\pi$ to
$\rho$ on $X$.  The SE constructor itself supplies the corresponding
properties of $\pi$.  Symmetry in \noderef{sc2} therefore gives
stability from $\rho$ back to $\pi$.  By
\noderef{StableColoring}, the two colorings have identical missing-color
sets at every vertex of $X$.  Now let $c$ be missing on $X$ under
$\rho$, and suppose that a boundary identity $g$ of $X$ has
$\rho(g)=c$.  Reverse stability fixes $g$, because its $\rho$-color $c$
is tree-missing; hence $\pi(g)=c$.  The common vertexwise missing sets
also make $c$ missing on $X$ under $\pi$.  This contradicts the
`previousClosed' field after unfolding
\noderef{ColoredVertexSetClosed} and
\noderef{BoundaryColorEdges}.  Thus the $c$-colored boundary is empty,
and \noderef{TreeColorSetClosed} makes $\{c\}$ closed on $X$.  We have
proved in both RE and SE cases that every presentation color missing on
$X$ is singleton-closed there.

By \noderef{HierarchyStoredColorsCardinality}, the internal stored set
at index zero has size at most the rung number.  The `twoColors' and
`disjointPairs' fields of \noderef{GoodHierarchy} therefore bound the
union of its Gamma pairs by twice the rung number.  The `rounds_eq' and
`large' fields in \noderef{Step1ExitLegPresentation} turn this into a
bound of $2n$ and a lower bound $2n+11$ for the colors missing on $X$.
Hence after deleting that union and the two exit-leg colors at least
nine colors remain.  Choose one and call it $\theta$.

The `nonparallel' field of \noderef{HierarchyBase} identifies its core
with $X$, and at divider count zero
\noderef{HierarchyStarredStage} identifies $A$ with this core.  Hence
$\theta$ is missing and closed on $A$.  The hierarchy inclusions put
$X$ inside the terminal tree: this is the chain recorded by
\noderef{TashkinovHierarchy}.  Exact initial-segment containment retains
all vertices, so the union description in \noderef{TreeMissingColors}
shows that $\theta$ is missing there as well and gives
$\overline\rho(A)\subseteq\overline\rho(P.T)$.  Consequently,
subtracting the latter, larger missing set from the common raw stored set
shows directly from
\noderef{HierarchyTerminalStoredColors} and
\noderef{HierarchyStoredColorsAt} that the terminal stored set is a
subset of the internal set at index zero.  Thus $\theta$ avoids the
terminal Gamma union.  The terminal minus-closure field of the
presentation in \noderef{Step1ExitLegPresentation}, unfolded with
\noderef{TreeColorSetMinusClosed} and then
\noderef{TreeColorSetClosed}, now says that the singleton $\{\theta\}$
is closed on the terminal tree.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Claim 4.1(a5): strong interchangeability]
Let \(n\) be a terminal PE round and \(L\) the active set.  Assume the
explicit lower-rung assertion (ii) needed here: for every \(t\in L\) with
\(t<n\), the colors \(\gamma_t,\delta_t\) are interchangeable on
\(T_{t+1}\) under \(\varphi_t\).  Let \(\sigma_n\) be a proper coloring
leaving precisely \(e_0\) uncolored and
\((T_n,D_n,\varphi_n)\)-stable.  Then for every \(t\in L\setminus\{n\}\),
\(\gamma_t\) and \(\delta_t\) are \(T_t\)-strongly interchangeable under
\(\sigma_n\), in the sense of \noderef{ETTStrongInterchangeable}.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Fix an index \(t\in L\) with \(t<n\), and write \(v_t\) for its certified
support.  Unfolding \noderef{ExtensionBaseActiveIndices} gives the exact
PE-kind equation at the positive slot \(t\), while the hypotheses give
the exact chosen-vertex, gamma, and delta equations there.  By the
certificate field of \noderef{TashkinovSeries}, the witness at that slot
is one of the four constructors of \noderef{TashkinovSeriesStep}; its
kind equation excludes the initial, SE, and RE constructors.  We may
therefore unpack its PE constructor.  Denote its preceding coloring by
\(\varphi^-\), its maximum-defective selected coloring by \(\pi\), its
second stable coloring by \(\rho\), its pre-switch tree by \(T_t\), and
its switched coloring by \(\varphi_t\).  The constructor equations
identify its chosen support and colors with
\(v_t,\gamma_t,\delta_t\), and say exactly that
\[
 \varphi_t=
 \operatorname{swap}_{K_{v_t}(\gamma_t,\delta_t;\rho)}(\rho).
 \tag{1}
\]

We first transfer the closedness in the PE certificate all the way to
\(\rho\).  Its \(\varphi^-\)-closedness is the condition of
\noderef{ColoredVertexSetClosed} on \(V(T_t)\).  The
\noderef{MaximumDefectiveChoice} says that \(\pi\) is proper, leaves
exactly \(e_0\) uncolored, and is
\((T_t,D_{t-1},\varphi^-)\)-stable.  The preceding coloring has the same
two coloring properties by the PE certificate.  Hence symmetry in
\noderef{sc2} makes \(\varphi^-\) stable relative to \(\pi\).  If a
boundary edge had \(\pi\)-color \(c\) missing at a tree vertex, the
vertex clause of \noderef{StableColoring} would make \(c\) missing there
under \(\varphi^-\), while reverse stability would fix the exact boundary
edge because its \(\pi\)-color lies among the tree-missing colors.  This
would contradict \(\varphi^-\)-closedness.  Thus \(T_t\) is closed under
\(\pi\).  Apply the identical argument to the PE field saying that
\(\rho\) is
\((T_t,D_{t-1}\cup\{\delta_t\},\pi)\)-stable.  Symmetry from
\noderef{sc2} and the missing-set equality in
\noderef{StableColoring} show that every hypothetical
\(\rho\)-colored boundary obstruction would be the same obstruction
under \(\pi\).  Consequently
\[
                 V(T_t)\text{ is closed under }\rho .            \tag{2}
\]

The selected exact connecting edge \(f_t\) in
\noderef{MaximumDefectiveChoice} is a boundary identity, is incident
with \(v_t\), has its other endpoint outside \(T_t\), and has
\(\pi(f_t)=\delta_t\).  Since \(\delta_t\) belongs to the enlarged
control set, forward stability fixes this identity and gives
\(\rho(f_t)=\delta_t\).  Now fix any \(x\in V(T_t)\).  If
\(\delta_t\) were missing at \(x\) under \(\rho\), the same boundary
edge \(f_t\) would violate (2).  Hence \(\delta_t\) is not missing at
\(x\).  By the complementary definitions
\noderef{MissingColors} and \noderef{PresentColors}, there is an exact
edge identity \(g_x\) incident with \(x\) such that
\[
                         \rho(g_x)=\delta_t .                    \tag{3}
\]
This conclusion holds at every tree vertex, not merely at the support.

The \noderef{ParallelExtensionColor} field says that \(\gamma_t\) is
missing at \(v_t\) under \(\pi\), and the vertex clause of second
stability transfers this to \(\rho\).  On the other hand, (3), or the
specific connecting edge \(f_t\), makes \(\delta_t\) present there.
Thus \(\gamma_t\ne\delta_t\).  Unpack the PE's
\noderef{TreeExit} witness as a walk \(P\) from an outside vertex \(u\)
to \(v_t\).  The clauses of \noderef{TreeExitPath} say that \(P\) is a
simple path of length at least two, that one of the two colors is missing
at \(u\), and that its only vertex-node in \(T_t\) is \(v_t\).  At
\(v_t\), the missingness of \(\gamma_t\), together with the incident
path edge, gives incidence-graph degree one.  At \(u\), the path edge
and its missing-color clause likewise give degree one.  Every other node
of \(P\) has its two path neighbors, and every incidence-graph degree is
at most two by \noderef{TwoColorIncidenceDegreeAtMostTwo}.  If a node of
the component were outside \(P\), take a shortest route from it to
\(P\).  The first attachment would give a third neighbor at an internal
node of \(P\), or a second neighbor at one of its degree-one endpoints.
Both are impossible.  Hence \(P\) exhausts the full component through
\(v_t\).  In particular it is the nontrivial path component from
\noderef{TwoColorNontrivialPathComponent}, and it contains no other tree vertex.
Equation (1) and \noderef{TwoColorComponentSwap} interchange the two
colors on this entire component and nowhere else.  Its support therefore
remains the same full path component under \(\varphi_t\), still meeting
\(T_t\) only at \(v_t\).

Now fix \(v\in V(T_t)\setminus\{v_t\}\).  Choose the identity \(g_v\)
from (3).  Were its edge-node in the root component before the switch,
incidence at \(v\) in \noderef{TwoColorIncidenceGraph} would put the
vertex-node \(v\) in that component, contrary to the just-proved
unique-tree-vertex property.  Thus the swap fixes \(g_v\), and
\[
                       \varphi_t(g_v)=\delta_t .                 \tag{4}
\]
The component \(C_v\) through \(v\) under \(\varphi_t\) is therefore
nonisolated: it contains the genuine edge-node of \(g_v\).  The lower-rung
hypothesis \noderef{TreeInterchangeable} applies on the certified next
tree, which contains \(T_t\) by the ordered-extension field in
\noderef{TashkinovSeriesStep} and
\noderef{TreeSequenceInitialSegment}.  If \(C_v\) satisfied
\noderef{TwoColorPathComponent}, interchangeability would identify it
with the already exhibited path component through \(v_t\), since both
meet that next tree.  It would then put \(v\) in the root component,
again contradicting its unique-tree-vertex property.  Thus \(C_v\) is
not a path component.

Here is the required cycle certificate, including the formerly missing
isolated-component case.  The colors are distinct and (4) supplies an
edge-node in \(C_v\).  Therefore, by the definition of
\noderef{TwoColorPathComponent}, failure of the path alternative says
that no node of \(C_v\) has a subsingleton neighbor set.  The degree bound
\noderef{TwoColorIncidenceDegreeAtMostTwo}, now applied to the proper
coloring \(\varphi_t\), makes every node of \(C_v\) have exactly two
neighbors.  Take the simple spanning path supplied by
\noderef{TwoColorComponentSpanningPath}; its support is exactly \(C_v\).
Each endpoint has one neighbor along the path and hence one further
neighbor in \(C_v\).  The further neighbor cannot be an internal path
node, because that node would then have its two path neighbors and a third
neighbor.  It is consequently the other endpoint.  Closing the spanning
path with this edge gives a simple closed walk in
\noderef{TwoColorCycle}, and its support is still exactly \(C_v\).
This is precisely \noderef{TwoColorComponentCycle}.  Moreover
\(v_t\notin C_v\): otherwise the connected-component definition
\noderef{TwoColorComponent} would identify \(C_v\) with the root
component and put \(v\) in the latter.  Denote the resulting full cycle
by \(O_v\).

We next prove that every vertex-node of \(O_v\) lies in
\(T_{\epsilon(t)}\).  Since the terminal slot \(n\) is a PE and
\(t<n\), the finite set of later non-RE slots is nonempty.  Let
\(\epsilon(t)\) be its least member.  Then
\[
 t<\epsilon(t)\leq n,\qquad
 \Theta_{\epsilon(t)}\ne\mathrm{RE},                            \tag{5}
\]
and minimality says that every strictly intermediate slot is an RE.
The RE current-coloring equalities in
\noderef{TashkinovSeriesStep}, composed over this finite interval, give
\(\varphi_t=\varphi_{\epsilon(t)-1}\).  Its ordered-extension fields,
composed using \noderef{TreeSequenceInitialSegment}, also put every
vertex of \(T_t\), in particular \(v\), in
\(T_{\epsilon(t)}\).

Suppose some vertex-node \(w\) of \(O_v\) were outside
\(T_{\epsilon(t)}\).  Regard the same exact cycle as a cycle under
\(\varphi_{\epsilon(t)-1}\), using the preceding coloring equality.
Starting at \(v\), traverse the simple cycle toward \(w\), and stop at
the first outside vertex.  Let \(f\) be the exact edge-node immediately
before that vertex, let \(a\) be its preceding inside endpoint, and let
\(b\) be that outside endpoint.  By \noderef{BoundaryEdges},
\(f\in\partial(T_{\epsilon(t)})\), with the exact endpoint equation
\(G.\operatorname{ends}(f)=\{a,b\}\).  Reverse the initial cycle segment
from the edge-node \(f\) back to \(v\).  It is a simple path on \(O_v\);
every one of its vertex-nodes is in \(T_{\epsilon(t)}\), by the choice of
the first outside vertex.

These data fill every field of
\noderef{RevisitingExtensionCertificate} at the current index
\(\epsilon(t)-1\): take the earlier index to be the positive slot \(t\),
whose positivity and bound follow from \(t<\epsilon(t)\); its PE-kind,
gamma, and delta equations are the exact equations unpacked above; take
the cycle to be \(O_v\), which meets its earlier tree at \(v\) and leaves
the current tree at \(w\); take the boundary identity and endpoints to be
\(f,a,b\); and take the return vertex and return path to be \(v\) and the
reversed initial segment.  The segment is simple, lies on the cycle, and
all its vertex-nodes are current-tree vertices, exactly as the three
return-path fields require.  Now inspect the certified constructor at
slot \(\epsilon(t)\).  It is not initial by (5), and its non-RE equation
excludes the RE constructor.  Each remaining SE or PE constructor has the
`noRevisiting' field, contradicting the inhabited certificate just
constructed.  This is the witness-carrying form of RE priority.  The
supposition was therefore false, and
\[
  \operatorname{Vert}(O_v)\subseteq V(T_{\epsilon(t)}).          \tag{6}
\]

It remains to transport the full component to \(\sigma_n\).  The PE
constructor gives the exact stored pair
\(S_t=\{\delta_t,\gamma_t\}\).  For either color \(c\) in this pair,
at stage \(t\) either \(c\) is tree-missing, or it is not tree-missing
and the set-difference definition \noderef{TashkinovStoredColors} puts it
in \(D_t\).  Thus both colors lie in
\(\overline\varphi_t(T_t)\cup D_t\).  Iterating the two inclusions of
\noderef{HkuMissingStoredMonotone} carries both colors first to the
control set at \(T_{\epsilon(t)}\), and then to
\(\overline\varphi_n(T_n)\cup D_n\).

Every exact edge-node \(e\) of \(O_v\) has both endpoint vertex-nodes in
the same full component.  By (6) both endpoints lie in
\(T_{\epsilon(t)}\), so \(e\) belongs to the exact set
\noderef{InternalEdges}.  Apply the internal-edge conclusion of
\noderef{SameColorSeriesPreserved} from \(\epsilon(t)\) through \(n\):
the exact \(\gamma_t\)- or \(\delta_t\)-color of every edge of \(O_v\)
is unchanged from \(\varphi_{\epsilon(t)-1}=\varphi_t\) to
\(\varphi_n\).  The terminal PE certificate makes \(\varphi_n\) proper
and leaves exactly \(e_0\) uncolored.  Since each vertex of the cycle has
one incident cycle edge of each of the two distinct colors, properness
in the exact-edge sense of \noderef{IsProperEdgeColoring} allows no
additional \(\gamma_t\)- or \(\delta_t\)-edge at such a vertex.  Hence
the support of \(O_v\), not merely its displayed edges, is the full
two-color component under \(\varphi_n\).

Each cycle edge is incident with the terminal tree, and its
\(\varphi_n\)-color belongs to the terminal control set just obtained.
Forward terminal stability \noderef{StableColoring} therefore fixes all
of these exact identities from \(\varphi_n\) to \(\sigma_n\).  For the
reverse inclusion of full components, package the proper, one-uncolored
colorings \(\varphi_n,\sigma_n\) and apply symmetry in \noderef{sc2} to
the assumed terminal stability.  Their vertexwise missing sets agree, so
the two colors also lie in the control set when \(\sigma_n\) is used as
reference.  If an edge \(h\) incident with a cycle vertex had
\(\sigma_n(h)\in\{\gamma_t,\delta_t\}\), reverse stability would give
\(\varphi_n(h)=\sigma_n(h)\).  Properness of \(\varphi_n\), together
with the already fixed cycle edge of that same color at the same vertex,
forces \(h\) to be that exact cycle-edge identity.  Thus no path in the
\(\sigma_n\) incidence graph can leave the support of \(O_v\).  Since
the fixed cycle edges keep that support connected, the full component
under \(\sigma_n\) is exactly \(O_v\).  It is a cycle, avoids \(v_t\),
and satisfies (6).

Finally, the active-set and chosen-vertex equations give the PE-kind and
exact-support clauses of \noderef{ETTStrongInterchangeable}; distinctness
was proved above; (5) supplies the least non-RE witness and all
intermediate RE clauses; and the construction for each
\(v\in V(T_t)\setminus\{v_t\}\) supplies the required full cycle,
avoidance, and containment clauses under \(\sigma_n\).  This proves
Claim 4.1(a5).
\end{proof}

\begin{helper}[Terminal parallel preprocessing]
Let $T$ be a positive-rung maximum ETT whose terminal step is parallel,
and assume the induction context from condition (5.1): lower-rung
elementarity and interchangeability, stability descent and disjoint stored
pairs, the terminal-component intersection assertion, the defective-endpoint
order assertion, and the mod-coloring assertion, together with current-rung
assertions (iii)--(vi).  Let $T_n$ be its terminal
ladder tree, let $\sigma_n$ be a proper
$(T_n,D_n,\varphi_n)$-stable coloring leaving exactly $e_0$ uncolored, and
let $J_n$ be the support-prefix closure in a parallel hierarchy base.
Then there are the terminal support $v_n$, a vertex $w\ne v_n$ in both
$T_n$ and $J_n$, and terminal colors
$\gamma_n,\delta_n$ with their exact selected-field identities such that
$v_n\in V(T_n)\cap V(J_n)$, $\delta_n$ is missing at $v_n$ under
$\sigma_n$, $\gamma_n$ is missing nowhere on $T_n$ under $\sigma_n$,
$w$ has at least one missing color, and $V(T_n)$ is elementary under
$\sigma_n$.
\end{helper}
\begin{proof}
Put $n=r(T)$ and eliminate the last certified step using
\noderef{TashkinovSeries} and the assumption that its kind is PE.  The PE
constructor of \noderef{TashkinovSeriesStep} supplies a selected coloring
$\pi_{n-1}$, a second stable coloring $\pi'_{n-1}$, an exact connecting edge
$f_n$, the support $v_n$, and colors $\gamma_n,\delta_n$.  Its field
equalities are precisely the three selected-field identities in the
conclusion, and it states
\[
 \varphi_n=\pi'_{n-1}/P_{v_n}(\gamma_n,\delta_n,\pi'_{n-1}).
\]
The selected defective-edge data make $f_n$ a $\delta_n$-colored boundary
edge incident with $v_n$, while \noderef{ParallelExtensionColor} makes
$\gamma_n$ missing at $v_n$ before the switch.  The two stable relations
preserve these vertex and controlled-edge facts, so
$\gamma_n\ne\delta_n$.

If $n>1$, \noderef{CertifiedETTRestriction} at rung $n-1$ gives an actual
positive lower-rung maximum ETT whose displayed tree is exactly $T_n$ and
whose generating coloring is $\varphi_{n-1}$.  Lower assertion (i) therefore
makes $V(T_n)$ elementary under $\varphi_{n-1}$.  If $n=1$, the elementarity
clause of \noderef{TechnicalTheoremBase} gives the same fact for the initial
tuple.  The stable vertex clauses transport elementarity successively to
$\pi_{n-1}$ and $\pi'_{n-1}$.

The terminal PE constructor also says that $V(T_n)$ is closed under
$\varphi_{n-1}$.  If a color missing on $T_n$ under $\pi'_{n-1}$ appeared on
a boundary edge, the vertex clauses would make it missing under the two
preceding colorings, and the controlled incident-edge clauses of
\noderef{StableColoring}, composed by \noderef{sc2}, would preserve that
edge's color.  This contradicts \noderef{ColoredVertexSetClosed}.  Thus
$T_n$ is closed under $\pi'_{n-1}$, and the boundary edge $f_n$ proves that
$\delta_n$ is missing nowhere on $T_n$ before the switch.

Apply \noderef{ExtensionBase} to the post-switch generating coloring.  Its
terminal component conclusion says that the
$(\gamma_n,\delta_n)$-component through $v_n$ meets $T_n$ only at $v_n$.
The definition \noderef{TwoColorComponentSwap} preserves the underlying
two-color incidence graph, so the same singleton-intersection statement
holds before the switch.  Consequently every edge incident with a vertex of
$T_n-v_n$ is fixed.  At $v_n$, properness makes $f_n$ the unique incident
$\delta_n$-edge; the switch changes it to $\gamma_n$ and changes the missing
color from $\gamma_n$ to $\delta_n$.  Pre-switch elementarity and the absence
of a pre-switch missing $\delta_n$ show that this creates no repeated missing
color.  Hence $V(T_n)$ is elementary under $\varphi_n$, and the assumed
stable vertex clauses transport both this elementarity and the missing
$\delta_n$ at $v_n$ to $\sigma_n$.

The selected edge is defective in the sense of
\noderef{MaximumDefectiveChoice}.  By the definitions
\noderef{DefectiveEdge} and \noderef{DefectiveColor}, there is a second
$\delta_n$-boundary edge.  It cannot lie in the switched component: otherwise
its inside endpoint would be $v_n$ by the singleton intersection, contrary
to properness in \noderef{IsProperEdgeColoring} and the uniqueness of the
incident $\delta_n$-edge $f_n$.
The switch therefore fixes this second edge.  The terminal boundary-order
conclusion of \noderef{HkuParallelBoundaryStructure} supplies its inside
endpoint $w\prec_{T_n}v_n$.  In particular $w\ne v_n$ and
$w\in V(T_n(v_n))$ by \noderef{TreeSequencePrefix}.

The PE clause of \noderef{HierarchyBase} identifies its support position
with $v_n$ and makes $J_n$ a \noderef{TashkinovClosure} of the corresponding
prefix.  The prefix contains both its terminal displayed vertex and every
earlier vertex by
\noderef{TreeSequencePrefix}, while every augmentation preserves earlier
vertices by \noderef{TashkinovAugmentationSequence}, so
$v_n,w\in V(J_n)$; their positions also put both vertices in
$V(T_n)$.  Finally, the $k$-triple clause of \noderef{KTriple} gives
$\Delta(G)+1\le k$, and \noderef{MaximumDegree} bounds the number of colors
present at $w$ in the sense of \noderef{PresentColors} by its degree.
Therefore not all $k$ colors are present,
so the complementary definition \noderef{MissingColors} gives at least one
missing color at $w$.

It remains to exclude $\gamma_n$ from the tree-missing set after the switch.
The first boundary conclusion of \noderef{HkuParallelBoundaryStructure}
says that the $\gamma_n$-boundary under $\varphi_n$ is the nonempty singleton
$\{f_n\}$.  Its conditional conclusion, together with pre-step
elementarity, would make that boundary empty if $\gamma_n$ were missing on
$T_n$.  Thus it is not tree-missing under $\varphi_n$, and the stable vertex
clause gives the same conclusion under $\sigma_n$.  All claimed properties
now hold for the extracted $v_n,\gamma_n,\delta_n$.
\end{proof}

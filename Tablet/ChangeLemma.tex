\begin{lemma}[Change lemma]
Fix a finite loopless multigraph with its exact edge set, a palette of
$k$ colors, and a distinguished edge $e_0$.  Let $(T,\varphi_n)$ be a
positive-rung extended Tashkinov tree.  Let $\mathcal B$ be a hierarchy
base and let
\[
 T_n=T_{n,0}\subset T_{n,1}\subset\cdots\subset
 T_{n,q}\subset T_{n,q+1}=T
\]
be a good hierarchy under $\varphi_n$, where $q$ is its last divider
index and
\[
 A=T_{n,q}^{*}=\noderef{HierarchyStarredStage}(H,q).
\]
Assume that $T$ has maximum property, that $V(T)$ is not elementary,
that $\varphi_n$ is proper and leaves exactly $e_0$ uncolored, and that
the rung number is positive.  These data form a
\noderef{HierarchyCounterexample} $C$.

Assume the six lower-rung assertions of the technical theorem, uniformly
for every positive-rung maximum ETT on the same graph, palette, and
distinguished edge, with arbitrary certified input and generating
colorings and with rung number smaller than that of $T$: lower-rung
elementarity, terminal interchangeability, the two PE stability and
stored-pair assertions, the terminal PE component assertion, the
defective-endpoint order assertion, and the mod-coloring assertion.
Assume also the same-rung induction hypothesis that every maximum ETT
with a good hierarchy having fewer than $q$ dividers is elementary.
Finally assume the large-color estimate
\[
 2n+11\leq |\overline\varphi_n(T_n)|.
\]

Put $\ell=|T|-|A|$, the number of exact tail additions above $A$, and let
\[
 T(y_{\ell-1})=T\big|_{\min\{|A|+(\ell-1),|T|\}}
\]
be the exact ordered prefix supplied by \noderef{TreeSequencePrefix}.
Assume (6.6): $V(T(y_{\ell-1}))$ is elementary under $\varphi_n$.
Assume moreover that $C$ is lexicographically minimal among all
\noderef{HierarchyCounterexample}s on the fixed graph, palette, and
$e_0$ which have exactly $q$ dividers, allowing their certified input
colorings, generating colorings, bases, ETTs, and good hierarchies to
vary.  Explicitly, no such counterexample $C'$ satisfies
\[
 \noderef{HierarchyCounterexampleLess}
  \bigl(\noderef{HierarchyCounterexampleMeasure}(C'),
        \noderef{HierarchyCounterexampleMeasure}(C)\bigr).
\]

Let $\alpha\ne\beta$ be colors missing on $T(y_{\ell-1})$.  Write
\[
 i_c=\noderef{HierarchyFirstMissingPosition}(\varphi_n,T,c),
 \qquad v_c=T.\mathit{vertex}(i_c).
\]
If at least one of $\alpha,\beta$ is not missing on $A$, assume both
\[
 v_\alpha\prec v_\beta                                      \tag{a}
\]
and the exact edge-identity exclusion
\[
 \alpha\notin
 \left\{c:\exists t\in\operatorname{Fin}|T|,
       |A|\leq t<i_\beta,
       \ \varphi_n(T.\mathit{edge}(t))=c\right\}.             \tag{b}
\]
Thus (b) is precisely
$\alpha\notin\varphi_n\langle T(v_\beta)-A\rangle$; it is not
an endpoint-only abbreviation.

Then the full two-color components through $v_\alpha$ and $v_\beta$
are equal in each of the following cases.
\begin{enumerate}
\item $q\geq1$, and either
$\alpha\in\overline\varphi_n(T_{n,q})$ or
$\{\alpha,\beta\}\cap D_{n,q}=\varnothing$.
\item $q=0$, and either
$\alpha\in\overline\varphi_n(T_n)$ or
$\{\alpha,\beta\}\cap D_n=\varnothing$.
\item $\alpha\in\overline\varphi_n(A)$ and the singleton
$\{\alpha\}$ is closed on $A$ under $\varphi_n$.
\end{enumerate}
In case 3 the colors are also $A$-interchangeable: at most one paper
$(\alpha,\beta)$-path component meets $A$, with an isolated vertex
component counted as a path.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Using the packaged hierarchy field of \noderef{HierarchyCounterexample}, put
$H=C.\mathit{hierarchy}$; let $q$ be its last divider index, put
$A=\noderef{HierarchyStarredStage}(H,q)$, and abbreviate the two
first-missing vertices by $a=v_\alpha$ and $b=v_\beta$.  We first record
why these four symbols really yield an exhaustive ordered-prefix split.

The nested and terminal fields of \noderef{TashkinovHierarchy}, together
with \noderef{HierarchyStarredStage}, make $A$ an exact ordered initial
segment of $T$.  At divider index zero this is the `coreInsideFirst'
field followed by the stage chain; at a positive divider index it is the
exact prefix supplied by the corresponding `dividerStage' field followed
by the remaining stage chain.  Thus the entrywise relation
\noderef{TreeSequenceInitialSegment} holds from $A$ to $T$.

We shall use two immediate consequences.  First, if a color $c$ is
missing on $A$, choose a vertex entry of $A$ missing $c$.  Its image is a
candidate in the definition \noderef{HierarchyFirstMissingPosition} on
$T$.  The least candidate $i_c$ occurs no later than that entry, so the
initial-segment relation puts $v_c=T.\mathit{vertex}(i_c)$ in
$\noderef{TreeSequenceVertexSet}(A)$.  Hence
\[
 c\in\overline\varphi_n(A)\quad\Longrightarrow\quad v_c\in V(A).
                                                               \tag{1}
\]
Second, suppose $a\prec_T b$ and $b\in V(A)$.  Expanding
\noderef{TreeVertexOrder}, let $i_a<i_b$ be their unique displayed
indices; uniqueness is the vertex-injectivity field of
\noderef{TreeSequence}.
Writing $b$ as an entry of $A$ and using the entrywise initial-segment
equality shows $i_b\leq |A|$.  Therefore $i_a<|A|+1$, and the entry of
$A$ at index $i_a$ equals $a$.  Thus
\[
              a\prec_T b,\ b\in V(A)\quad\Longrightarrow\quad a\in V(A).
                                                               \tag{2}
\]

Consider either alternative 1 or alternative 2 in the statement.  If
$a,b\in V(A)$, apply \noderef{ChangeLemmaBothInside}; if
$a\in V(A)$ and $b\notin V(A)$, apply
\noderef{ChangeLemmaInsideOutside}; and if $a,b\notin V(A)$, apply
\noderef{ChangeLemmaBothOutside}.  All three applications use verbatim
the properness, exact one-uncolored edge, complete lower-rung package,
fewer-divider induction, full-tail (6.6) prefix, $2n+11$ estimate,
lexicographic minimality, conditional order and exact unused-edge
hypothesis, and the displayed location hypothesis.

There is no fourth case.  Indeed, if $a\notin V(A)$ and $b\in V(A)$,
then (1), contrapositively, says that $\alpha$ is not missing on $A$.
The antecedent of the conditional hypothesis (a)--(b) therefore holds,
and its first conclusion gives $a\prec_T b$.  Equation (2) then gives
$a\in V(A)$, a contradiction.  This completes alternatives 1 and 2.

Finally assume alternative 3.  Its missingness hypothesis and (1) give
$a\in V(A)$.  If $b\in V(A)$, the third conclusion of
\noderef{ChangeLemmaBothInside} gives both equality of the full
components and $A$-interchangeability.  If $b\notin V(A)$, the third
conclusion of \noderef{ChangeLemmaInsideOutside} gives the same two
claims.  In each child, interchangeability quantifies over the paper's
full path components and includes an isolated vertex component.  These
are exactly the final conjunct of the unchanged statement.
\end{proof}

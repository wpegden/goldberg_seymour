\begin{lemma}[Certified reordered trees in Situation 7.2]
Fix the graph, palette, distinguished edge, ETT, hierarchy
counterexample, minimum context, and a
\noderef{HierarchySituationTwoTailData} \(D\).  Then there is a
\noderef{HierarchySituationTwoReorderedTrees} datum
\(R=(T^-,T^*)\) with the following additional properties.  Put
\[
 n=\mathcal E.\mathit{rounds},\quad
 T_n=\mathcal E.\mathit{series}.\mathit{tree}(n),\quad
 B=\noderef{HierarchyFullTailPrefix}(C,\ell-2).
\]
If \(B=T_n\), then the terminal extension kind is PE.  Each of \(T^-\)
and \(T^*\) has the exact terminal construction route appropriate to
that kind: in the PE case it is obtained from \(T_n\) by a
\noderef{TashkinovAugmentationSequence} under \(\varphi_n\); in the SE
or RE case there are an individual edge \(f_n\), the equality
\(F_n=\{f_n\}\), the exact seed \(T_n+f_n\), and an augmentation
sequence from that seed.  Finally, each reordered tree carries an actual
\noderef{CorrespondingExtendedTashkinovTree} generated by
\(\varphi_n\), and both replacement ETTs have
\noderef{MaximumProperty}.  For each replacement ETT there is moreover
an actual \noderef{HierarchyBase} under \(\varphi_n\), having the same
ordered core and closure as the original base and the same numerical
support position.  On that base there is an actual
\noderef{TashkinovHierarchy} having the original divider count, all
original stages through \(T_{n,q}\), and the same numerical divider
positions; only its terminal stage is replaced by \(T^-\), respectively
\(T^*\).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
By the definition of a \noderef{HierarchyCounterexample}, the supplied
object \(C\), rather than the minimum-context object, has the three fields
that we shall use at the outset:
\[
 H:=C.\mathit{hierarchy},\qquad 0<n:=C.\mathit{positive},
 \qquad C.\mathit{maximum}:\noderef{MaximumProperty}(\mathcal E).
                                                               \tag{1}
\]
Put \(q=H.\mathit{dividerCount}\),
\(A=\noderef{HierarchyStarredStage}(H,\operatorname{Fin.last}q)\), and
\(m=|T|\).  If \(q=0\), then \(A\) is the core of the
\noderef{HierarchyBase}; its \texttt{coreInsideFirst} field and the stage
chain of \noderef{TashkinovHierarchy} put \(A\) below \(T\).  If \(q>0\),
then \(A=H.\mathit{stage}(q)\), and the nested-stage and terminal-stage
fields give the same conclusion.  Composing the entrywise relations in
\noderef{TreeSequenceInitialSegment} therefore gives
\(A\preccurlyeq T\), so \(|A|\leq m\).

Let \(\ell=\noderef{HierarchyFullTailLength}(C)=m-|A|\).  The field
\texttt{tailAtLeastTwo} of \(D\) gives \(2\leq\ell\); hence the preceding
length inequality yields
\[
 |A|+\ell=m,\qquad
 |B|=|A|+(\ell-2)=m-2,\qquad
 |B^+|=|A|+(\ell-1)=m-1,                                  \tag{2}
\]
where \(B=\noderef{HierarchyFullTailPrefix}(C,\ell-2)\) and
\(B^+=\noderef{HierarchyFullTailPrefix}(C,\ell-1)\).  Indeed, after
unfolding these two definitions, the minima in the two defining
\noderef{TreeSequencePrefix}s have respectively the first arguments
\(m-2\) and \(m-1\).  Their vertex and edge maps are the restrictions of
those of \(T\).  Thus direct comparison of the restricted indices gives
the exact ordered relations
\[
             B\preccurlyeq B^+\preccurlyeq T,               \tag{3}
\]
and each relation in (3) preserves every displayed vertex and individual
edge identity, as required by \noderef{TreeSequenceInitialSegment}.

We next construct the reordered displays and verify all four append
relations.  The position equations in the
\noderef{HierarchySituationTwoTailData} \(D\) say that its last-edge,
penultimate-vertex, and terminal-vertex indices have numerical values
\(m-1,m-1,m\), respectively.  Since \(m=|A|+\ell\geq3\), there is also
the index \(r\) of numerical value \(m-2\).  Write
\[
 e_{p-1}=T.\mathit{edge}(r),\quad
 e_p=T.\mathit{edge}(D.\mathit{lastEdgeIndex}),\quad
 y_{p-1}=T.\mathit{vertex}(D.\mathit{penultimateVertexIndex}),\quad
 y_p=T.\mathit{vertex}(D.\mathit{terminalVertexIndex}).       \tag{4}
\]

Apply the attachment field of \noderef{TreeSequence} to the last edge
\(e_p\).  It supplies an index \(i<m\) such that
\[
       G.\mathit{ends}(e_p)=\{y_p,T.\mathit{vertex}(i)\}.     \tag{5}
\]
If \(T.\mathit{vertex}(i)=y_{p-1}\), then (5) would make \(e_p\)
incident with \(y_{p-1}\) by \noderef{Incident}, contrary to the
non-incidence field of \(D\).
Consequently \(i\ne m-1\), and \(i<m\) gives \(i<m-1\).  The old endpoint
in (5) is therefore a vertex of \(B\).  Vertex injectivity in
\noderef{TreeSequence} also shows that \(y_p\), whose old index is \(m\),
is not a vertex of \(B\).  Likewise, the attachment field for
\(e_{p-1}\) supplies an old endpoint at an index below \(m-1\), hence in
\(B\), while its new endpoint is \(y_{p-1}\).

Define \(T^-\) by copying the ordered vertices and edges of \(B\) and
then displaying the pair \((e_p,y_p)\).  Define \(T^*\) by copying
\(T^-\) and then displaying \((e_{p-1},y_{p-1})\).  These are
tree-sequences: positivity and the first-edge equation are inherited from
\(B\); attachment for each copied edge is inherited from \(T\); attachment
for the two new entries is given by (5) and by the preceding attachment
for \(e_{p-1}\).  For injectivity, the copied entries are injective, the
new vertices have old indices \(m\) and \(m-1\), and the new edges have old
indices \(m-1\) and \(m-2\); the injectivity fields of
\noderef{TreeSequence} separate each of these from all earlier copied
entries and from one another.

We now unfold \noderef{TreeSequenceAdjoinEdge}, rather than infer an
append equation merely from the identity of its last edge.
\begin{itemize}
\item For \(B+(e_{p-1},y_{p-1})=B^+\), equation (2) gives
\(|B^+|=|B|+1\); (3) gives the exact initial segment \(B\preccurlyeq B^+\);
the last edge of \(B^+\) is literally the restriction of \(T\) at index
\(m-2\), hence is \(e_{p-1}\); and its last vertex is literally the
restriction of \(T\) at index \(m-1\), hence is \(y_{p-1}\).
\item For \(B^++(e_p,y_p)=T\), equation (2) gives
\(|T|=|B^+|+1\); (3) gives \(B^+\preccurlyeq T\); the individual last
edge is \(T.\mathit{edge}(m-1)=e_p\); and the literal fresh last vertex is
\(T.\mathit{vertex}(m)=y_p\).
\item For \(B+(e_p,y_p)=T^-\), the definition of \(T^-\) gives
\(|T^-|=|B|+1\), equality of every vertex and edge through \(B\), and
individual last edge \(e_p\); separately, its fresh last vertex was
defined to be the literal vertex \(y_p\).
\item For \(T^-+(e_{p-1},y_{p-1})=T^*\), the definition of \(T^*\)
gives \(|T^*|=|T^-|+1\), equality of every retained entry, and individual
last edge \(e_{p-1}\); separately, its fresh last vertex is literally
\(y_{p-1}\).
\end{itemize}
Thus every append has the unit-length, exact ordered-prefix, and
individual-last-edge clauses of \noderef{TreeSequenceAdjoinEdge}, and
each asserted fresh vertex has been identified independently from those
three clauses.  Reading the four explicit entry maps through
\noderef{TreeSequenceVertexSet} and \noderef{TreeSequenceEdgeSet} gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
 V(T^-)&=V(T)\setminus\{y_{p-1}\},&
 E(T^-)&=E(T)\setminus\{e_{p-1}\},\\
 V(T^*)&=V(T),& E(T^*)&=E(T).
\end{aligned}                                                 \tag{6}
\]
The reverse inclusions in the first line use vertex and edge injectivity
to see that the deleted entries occur nowhere else.  We have now filled
every field of \noderef{HierarchySituationTwoReorderedTrees}; denote the
result by \(R=(T^-,T^*)\).

We turn to the augmentation routes.  Let
\(Z=\mathcal E.\mathit{series}.\mathit{tree}(n+1)\), the output tree of
the terminal certified step.  By (1), the certificate at the terminal
index cannot be the initial constructor.  The certification field of
\noderef{TashkinovSeries}, the three positive constructors in
\noderef{TashkinovSeriesStep}, and the generating-coloring equation in
\noderef{ExtendedTashkinovTree} give exactly the following alternatives:
\begin{itemize}
\item if \(\Theta_n=PE\), then
\(\noderef{TashkinovClosure}(\varphi_n,T_n,Z)\), whose first field is a
\(\varphi_n\)-augmentation sequence \(T_n\longrightarrow Z\);
\item if \(\Theta_n=SE\) or \(RE\), there are an individual edge \(f_n\)
and a tree-sequence \(S\) such that
\[
 F_n=\{f_n\},\qquad
 \noderef{TreeSequenceAdjoinEdge}(T_n,f_n,S),\qquad
 \noderef{TashkinovClosure}(\varphi_n,S,Z).                  \tag{7}
\]
\end{itemize}
In every case the \texttt{belowFinal} field of
\noderef{ExtendedTashkinovTree} is the exact relation \(T\preccurlyeq Z\).

We first put the appropriate source below \(B\).  In the PE case, unfold
the \texttt{parallel} field of \noderef{HierarchyBase}.  It supplies an
object
\[
 U:\noderef{OrderedTreeUnion}(T_n,\mathcal B.\mathit{closure},
                              \mathcal B.\mathit{core}).
\]
The \texttt{leftSegment} field of \(U\) is exactly
\(T_n\preccurlyeq\mathcal B.\mathit{core}\).  If \(q=0\), this core is
\(A\).  If \(q>0\), \texttt{coreInsideFirst} followed by the nested fields
of \noderef{TashkinovHierarchy} puts the core below
\(H.\mathit{stage}(q)=A\).  Finally, \(A\preccurlyeq B\) follows by
comparing their entrywise prefixes of \(T\), since
\(|A|\leq|B|=|A|+\ell-2\).  Transitivity of the three explicit
\noderef{TreeSequenceInitialSegment} relations yields
\[
                         T_n\preccurlyeq B.                   \tag{8}
\]

Suppose next that the kind is SE or RE.  The \texttt{nonparallel} field
of \noderef{HierarchyBase} identifies its core with \(T_n\).  The same
hierarchy and full-tail prefix chain therefore again gives
\(T_n\preccurlyeq B\).  Assume for the moment that \(B\ne T_n\).  If
their lengths were equal, the length and entry equations of
\noderef{TreeSequenceInitialSegment} would identify the two ordered
tree-sequences; hence
\[
                         |T_n|<|B|.                           \tag{9}
\]
Apply \noderef{HierarchyNonparallelFirstEdge} to the kind alternative
and the equality \(F_n=\{f_n\}\) in (7).  It says that the entry of the
first hierarchy stage at edge position \(|T_n|\) is the individual edge
\(f_n\).  Both that stage and \(B\) are exact prefixes of \(T\), and (9)
makes this position exist in \(B\); the entrywise edge equations of
\noderef{TreeSequenceInitialSegment} therefore give
\[
                    B.\mathit{edge}(|T_n|)=f_n.              \tag{10}
\]

Unfold the adjoin relation in (7).  It gives \(|S|=|T_n|+1\), the exact
prefix \(T_n\preccurlyeq S\), and last edge \(f_n\).  These are also the
length, prefix, and last-edge data for the first \(|T_n|+1\) entries of
\(B\), by (8)--(10).  The last vertices agree as well, and this is a
separate endpoint argument.  The attachment field of \(S\) writes the
ends of \(f_n\) as its new last vertex together with a vertex of \(T_n\).
The attachment field of \(B\) at (10) writes the same endpoint pair as
the new vertex of \(B\) at position \(|T_n|+1\) together with a vertex of
\(T_n\).  In each pair the new vertex is outside \(V(T_n)\), by vertex
injectivity and the exact prefix, whereas the other endpoint lies in
\(V(T_n)\).  Equality of the two endpoint pairs consequently identifies
the two unique outside endpoints.  We have proved equality of every
vertex and edge through length \(|S|\), and hence
\[
                         S\preccurlyeq B.                     \tag{11}
\]

It remains to exclude the non-PE boundary \(B=T_n\).  Suppose, toward a
contradiction, that this equality holds and that the terminal kind is SE
or RE.  The same application of
\noderef{HierarchyNonparallelFirstEdge}, now compared with the exact
prefix \(B^+\preccurlyeq T\), identifies \(f_n\) with the first edge
after \(B=T_n\), namely the individual edge \(e_{p-1}\).  Both \(S\) in
(7) and \(B^+\) are unit extensions of \(B\), have \(B\) as exact initial
segment, and have this same individual last edge.  Their fresh last
vertices agree by the endpoint-and-injectivity argument used above.
Thus all ordered entries agree and
\[
                              S=B^+.                          \tag{12}
\]

The closure in (7) gives a \(\varphi_n\)-augmentation sequence from \(S\)
to \(Z\).  Before applying
\noderef{TashkinovAugmentationSequenceRestrict}, note both of its exact
prefix premises: by (12) and (3), \(S=B^+\preccurlyeq T\), and by the
\texttt{belowFinal} field, \(T\preccurlyeq Z\).  The restriction lemma
therefore gives a route \(S\longrightarrow T\).  Its endpoints differ in
length by one.  Since a \noderef{TashkinovAugmentation} increases length
by exactly one, induction on the finite
\noderef{TashkinovAugmentationSequence} shows that this route is the
single augmentation \(B^+=S\longrightarrow T\).  The new individual edge
is \(e_p\), and \(D\) gives \(\varphi_n(e_p)=\theta\); hence the
missing-color clause of that augmentation says
\(\theta\in\noderef{TreeMissingColors}(\varphi_n,B^+)\).  A witnessing
vertex cannot be the sole new vertex \(y_{p-1}\), because \(D\) says that
\(\theta\) is present there.  The explicit vertex entries of \(B^+\) thus
put the witness in \(B=T_n\), so
\[
                 \theta\in\overline\varphi_n(T_n).           \tag{13}
\]

In the RE constructor, \texttt{previousClosed} and
\texttt{currentColoring}, followed by the generating-coloring equation of
\noderef{ExtendedTashkinovTree}, say directly that \(V(T_n)\) is
\noderef{ColoredVertexSetClosed} under \(\varphi_n\).  In the SE
constructor, \texttt{previousClosed} gives closedness under the preceding
coloring, while the selected-coloring field of
\noderef{MaximumDefectiveChoice} and \texttt{currentColoring} identify
\(\varphi_n\) as a proper, exactly-one-uncolored
\noderef{StableColoring} of \(T_n\) relative to that preceding coloring.
If a boundary edge had \(\varphi_n\)-color missing on \(T_n\), the
vertex-missing-set clause of stability would make that color missing under
the preceding coloring as well, and the reverse edge criterion in
\noderef{sc1} would give the same color to that exact boundary edge under
the preceding coloring.  This contradicts \texttt{previousClosed}.  Hence
\(V(T_n)\) is closed under \(\varphi_n\) in SE too.

Finally, (5) has one endpoint of \(e_p\) in \(B=T_n\) and its other
endpoint \(y_p\) outside \(B\), so the definition of
\noderef{BoundaryEdges} makes \(e_p\) a boundary edge of \(V(T_n)\).
Its color is \(\theta\) by \(D\), while (13) says that this color is
tree-missing.  This contradicts the just-proved closedness in both SE and
RE.  Since (1) excludes the initial constructor and
\noderef{TashkinovSeriesStep} has only the PE, SE, and RE positive
constructors, we have proved
\[
              B=T_n\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\Theta_n=PE.    \tag{14}
\]

We can now restrict the old route in every case.  Let \(S_0=T_n\) in PE,
and let \(S_0=S\) from (7) in SE or RE.  In PE, (8) gives
\(S_0\preccurlyeq B\).  In SE or RE, (14) gives \(B\ne T_n\), so (11)
gives \(S_0\preccurlyeq B\).  Also the relevant closure in every case
gives a \(\varphi_n\)-augmentation sequence \(S_0\longrightarrow Z\).
We list both prefix premises before each use of
\noderef{TashkinovAugmentationSequenceRestrict}:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \(S_0\preccurlyeq T\) is the composite
\(S_0\preccurlyeq B\preccurlyeq T\), and
\(T\preccurlyeq Z\) is \texttt{belowFinal}.  Restriction gives
\(S_0\longrightarrow T\).
\item \(S_0\preccurlyeq B\) was just proved, and
\(B\preccurlyeq T\) is (3).  Restricting the route from item 1 gives
\(S_0\longrightarrow B\).
\item \(S_0\preccurlyeq B^+\) is the composite through \(B\), and
\(B^+\preccurlyeq T\) is (3).  Restricting the route from item 1 gives
\(S_0\longrightarrow B^+\).
\end{enumerate}
Thus no restriction is inferred from a vertex-set inclusion; every source
and target relation above is an exact
\noderef{TreeSequenceInitialSegment}.

The route to \(B^+\) has a final augmentation whose predecessor has
length \(|B^+|-1=|B|\).  That predecessor and \(B\) are both exact initial
segments of \(B^+\) with the same length, so the entry equations in
\noderef{TreeSequenceInitialSegment} identify them.  Hence
\[
                         B\longrightarrow B^+                \tag{15}
\]
is one \(\varphi_n\)-Tashkinov augmentation.  Applying the same argument
to the route ending at \(T\), whose final predecessor has length
\(|T|-1=|B^+|\), gives
\[
                         B^+\longrightarrow T.               \tag{16}
\]
These arguments are also valid when the route to \(B\) is empty.

Let \(c\) be the color supplied by the missing-color clause of (15).
Then \(\varphi_n(e_{p-1})=c\) and \(c\) has a missing witness in \(B\).
The clause of (16), together with
\(D.\mathit{thetaOnLastEdge}\), says that \(\theta\) has a missing
witness in \(B^+\).  As in the derivation of (13),
\(D.\mathit{thetaPresentPenultimate}\) excludes the sole vertex
\(y_{p-1}\) of \(B^+-B\); hence
\[
                    \theta\in\overline\varphi_n(B).          \tag{17}
\]
The exact relation \(B+(e_p,y_p)=T^-\), the edge-color equation for
\(e_p\), and (17) now verify all clauses of
\noderef{TashkinovAugmentation} for \(B\longrightarrow T^-\).  The old
witness for \(c\) in \(B\) remains a vertex of \(T^-\), so
\(c\in\overline\varphi_n(T^-)\); together with the exact relation
\(T^-+(e_{p-1},y_{p-1})=T^*\), this verifies
\(T^-\longrightarrow T^*\).  Composing these two one-step augmentations
with the route \(S_0\longrightarrow B\) gives
\[
 S_0\longrightarrow T^-,\qquad S_0\longrightarrow T^*.       \tag{18}
\]
For PE, \(S_0=T_n\).  For SE or RE, (7) retains the same individual
\(f_n\), exact equality \(F_n=\{f_n\}\), and exact adjoined seed
\(S_0=T_n+f_n\).  Thus (18) is precisely each route alternative asserted
in the statement.

It remains to produce the corresponding ETTs and their maximum property.
Here the roles of \(C\) and the minimum context must again be kept
separate.  The fields \texttt{proper}, \texttt{oneEdgeUncolored}, and
\texttt{lowerRung} of
\noderef{HierarchyMinimumCounterexampleContext} supply respectively
properness of \(\varphi_n\), its unique uncolored edge \(e_0\), and the
lower-rung package required by \noderef{Td}.  The generating-coloring
field of \noderef{ExtendedTashkinovTree} identifies \(\varphi_n\) with
the terminal series coloring.  After this rewrite, reflexivity in
\noderef{StableColoring}, with the actual
\noderef{TashkinovStoredColors}, gives the needed terminal stability.
Apply \noderef{Td} with the positivity \(C.\mathit{positive}\) from
(1), the maximum-property argument \(C.\mathit{maximum}\) from (1), and
the package \(\mathit{ctx.lowerRung}\).  Its conclusion is
\[
                     \noderef{ModColoring}(\mathcal E,\varphi_n). \tag{19}
\]

Both reordered trees are exact strict extensions of \(T_n\).  In PE this
follows from \(T_n\preccurlyeq B\prec T^-\preccurlyeq T^*\); in SE or RE
it follows already from the unit extension
\(T_n\prec S_0\preccurlyeq B\prec T^-\preccurlyeq T^*\).  Together with
the corresponding alternatives in (18), these are all premises of the
universal clause in (19).  That clause returns an actual
\noderef{CorrespondingExtendedTashkinovTree} certificate
\(\mathcal C^-\) on \(T^-\) and an actual certificate \(\mathcal C^*\)
on \(T^*\).

Finally apply \noderef{MaximumPropertyTransport} to
\(\mathcal C^-\), using (19) and, explicitly, the base maximum-property
field \(C.\mathit{maximum}\).  Apply it a second time to
\(\mathcal C^*\), again using (19) and the same explicit argument
\(C.\mathit{maximum}\).  Denote the resulting maximum-property
certificates by \(M^-\) and \(M^*\).

We finally construct hierarchy bases for the two replacement ETTs.  This
requires more than the raw correspondence fields, because
\noderef{CorrespondingExtendedTashkinovTree} does not assert equality of
the chosen PE vertices.  We prove that equality from the certified
terminal steps.  Since \(n>0\), write the terminal index as \(i+1\),
where \(i=n-1\).  The certification field of
\noderef{TashkinovSeries} gives an old and a replacement
\noderef{TashkinovSeriesStep} at this index.  Correspondence identifies
their terminal ladder trees, their extension kinds, and their exact
connecting-edge sets.

If the common terminal kind is PE, the kind equations in the four
constructors of \noderef{TashkinovSeriesStep} rule out the initial, SE,
and RE constructors for both steps.  Thus both are PE constructors.  Let
\((v,f,u)\) and \((v',f',u')\) be the inside vertex, connecting edge,
and outside endpoint in their respective
\noderef{MaximumDefectiveChoice}s.  The PE connecting-set equations and
correspondence give
\(\{f'\}=F_n'=F_n=\{f\}\), so \(f'=f\) as an individual edge identity.
The choice fields give
\[
 v,v'\in V(T_n),\qquad u,u'\notin V(T_n),
 \qquad
 G[f]=\{v,u\}=\{v',u'\}.                                  \tag{20}
\]
Here the two copies of \(T_n\) have first been identified by the exact
ladder-tree agreement.  Equality of the two unordered endpoint pairs
either matches inside to inside or inside to outside.  The latter is
impossible by the membership and nonmembership statements in (20).
Consequently \(v'=v\).  The two PE constructor fields identifying the
recorded chosen vertices with \(v'\) and \(v\) now give
\[
 \mathcal T'.\mathit{chosenVertex}(n)
   =\mathcal T.\mathit{chosenVertex}(n).                    \tag{21}
\]
This is the same field-level inside/outside endpoint argument as in the
PE part of the hierarchy-splitting construction; it does not infer a
chosen vertex merely from equality of endpoint pairs.

For either replacement certificate \(\mathcal C'\), transport the old
support position across the equality of its terminal ladder tree with
\(T_n\), retaining its numerical value.  Define a
\noderef{HierarchyBase} \(\mathcal B'\) with the original ordered core
and closure.  Its terminal missing-color equality is reflexive because
both the old and replacement displayed generating colorings are the
same coloring \(\varphi_n\).  If the common kind is PE, (21) and the
transported support position prove the chosen-vertex clause.  The old
PE base clause then supplies the same \(\varphi_n\)-closure of the same
ordered prefix and the same
\noderef{OrderedTreeUnion}; exact equality of \(T_n\) transports both
claims.  If the common kind is not PE, the old nonparallel base clause
identifies the old core with \(T_n\), and kind agreement plus ladder-tree
agreement identifies that same core with the replacement terminal
ladder tree.  This verifies the nonparallel clause.  Thus in every case
\[
 \mathcal B'.\mathit{core}=\mathcal B.\mathit{core},\quad
 \mathcal B'.\mathit{closure}=\mathcal B.\mathit{closure},\quad
 \mathcal B'.\mathit{supportPosition}
   =\mathcal B.\mathit{supportPosition}\text{ numerically}. \tag{22}
\]

It remains to put the old nonterminal stages over these bases.  For
\(X=T^-\) and then for \(X=T^*\), define a
\noderef{TashkinovHierarchy} \(H_X\) with divider count \(q\), with
\(H_X(j)=H(j)\) for \(0\leq j\leq q\), with terminal stage \(X\), and
with every divider position transported at the same numerical value.
The base-stage equation follows from terminal ladder-tree agreement.
All nestedness and strictness clauses below the last stage, all divider
prefix equations, and the core-inside-first clause are literally the old
ones, using (22).  For the last nestedness clause, if \(q=0\), (8) puts
the common stage \(T_n\) below \(B\); if \(q>0\), the original stage
\(H(q)=A\) is an exact prefix of \(B\).  The two append equations put
\(B\preccurlyeq T^-\preccurlyeq T^*\), so the last nestedness clause
holds for each \(X\).  It is strict because \(|A|\leq m-2\),
\(|T^-|=m-1\), and \(|T^*|=m\).  Every old divider position occurs no
later than its retained stage, hence no later than \(A\); it therefore
casts into both new terminal trees with the same numerical value, and
its divider-prefix equation is unchanged.

Applying this construction first to \(\mathcal C^-\) and then to
\(\mathcal C^*\) gives the two bases and two copied hierarchies asserted
in the statement, together with \(M^-\) and \(M^*\).  No goodness claim
has been made: whether terminal avoidance survives is exactly the
case-dependent issue handled by the consumers of this certificate.
\end{proof}

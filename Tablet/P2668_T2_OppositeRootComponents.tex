\begin{helper}[Opposite rooted components of two interchangeable constituents]
Let $G$ be a finite loopless multigraph, $\varphi$ a proper partial
edge-coloring, and $X,Y,U$ tree-sequences with $U$ an ordered union of
$X$ and $Y$. Suppose that $\alpha,\beta$ are interchangeable on each
of $X$ and $Y$, but not on $U$. Let $u\in V(X)$ miss $\alpha$ and
$v\in V(Y)$ miss $\beta$. Then $\alpha\ne\beta$, $u\notin V(Y)$,
$v\notin V(X)$, the full $\alpha,\beta$ component rooted at $v$
avoids $V(X)$, and the full component rooted at $u$ avoids $V(Y)$.
Path components here include isolated vertices, and edges retain their
individual identities even when their endpoints coincide.
\end{helper}
\begin{proof}
If $\alpha=\beta$, there are no path components in the sense of
\noderef{TwoColorPathComponent}, whose definition requires distinct
colors. Thus \noderef{TreeInterchangeable} would hold vacuously on $U$,
a contradiction. Hence $\alpha\ne\beta$.

Write $C_u,C_v$ for the full rooted components in
\noderef{TwoColorComponent}. Each root belongs to its own component.
By \noderef{MissingColorIncidenceEndpoint}, the degree of $u$ in the
two-color incidence graph is at most one. Applying the same result with
the colors reversed gives this bound at $v$: the adjacency relation of
\noderef{TwoColorIncidenceGraph} is unchanged by reversing the colors.
A finite neighbor set of cardinality at most one is subsingleton.
Consequently both rooted components satisfy
\noderef{TwoColorPathComponent}, including when a root is isolated.

Suppose $C_u=C_v$. Every path component meeting $U$ meets $X$ or $Y$,
because the vertex-set clause of \noderef{OrderedTreeUnion} says
$V(U)=V(X)\cup V(Y)$. If it meets $X$, interchangeability on $X$,
applied also to the root $u$, identifies it with $C_u$. If it meets
$Y$, interchangeability on $Y$, applied also to the root $v$,
identifies it with $C_v=C_u$. Thus every two path components meeting
$U$ are equal. By \noderef{TreeInterchangeable} this contradicts the
hypothesis on $U$, so $C_u\ne C_v$.

If $C_v$ met $X$, interchangeability on $X$ would identify $C_v$ with
$C_u$, since $C_u$ meets $X$ at $u$. Hence $C_v$ avoids $V(X)$.
Likewise, if $C_u$ met $Y$, interchangeability on $Y$ would identify it
with $C_v$, so $C_u$ avoids $V(Y)$. Membership of each root in its own
component now gives $v\notin V(X)$ and $u\notin V(Y)$ respectively.
These arguments use full connected components, not selected subpaths,
so neither isolated roots nor parallel edge identities are discarded.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Situation 7.2, Subcase 3.2]
Every \noderef{HierarchySituationTwoCaseThreeTwoData} is impossible.
Equivalently, normalized Case 3 cannot occur when the last-edge color
\(\theta=\varphi_n(e_p)\) is missing at \(y_{p-1}\).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Write \(H=C.\mathit{hierarchy}\), let \(q\) be its last stored index, and
put
\[
 A=\noderef{HierarchyStarredStage}(H,q),\qquad
 \ell=\noderef{HierarchyFullTailLength}(C),
\]
\[
 B=\noderef{HierarchyFullTailPrefix}(C,\ell-2),\qquad
 B^+=\noderef{HierarchyFullTailPrefix}(C,\ell-1),\qquad
 D_{n,q}=\noderef{HierarchyStoredColorsAt}(H,q).              \tag{1}
\]

We first derive all of the exact prefix bookkeeping.  The base-core,
nested-stage, strict-growth, and terminal-stage fields of
\noderef{TashkinovHierarchy}, together with the two branches of
\noderef{HierarchyStarredStage}, give an entrywise exact initial segment
\(A\preccurlyeq T\).  Thus \(|A|\leq |T|\), and the definition of
\noderef{HierarchyFullTailLength} gives
\(|A|+\ell=|T|\).  Since the common-tail datum has \(2\leq\ell\), the
minima in the two definitions of \noderef{HierarchyFullTailPrefix} do not
truncate.  Consequently
\[
 |B|=|A|+\ell-2=|T|-2,
 \qquad |B^+|=|A|+\ell-1=|T|-1.                              \tag{2}
\]
Both prefixes copy the vertices and individual edges of \(T\) entry by
entry in the sense of \noderef{TreeSequenceInitialSegment}.  Comparing
those entries, and using \(|A|\leq|B|<|B^+|<|T|\), gives
\[
                    A\preccurlyeq B\prec B^+\prec T.          \tag{3}
\]

Let \(s,a,b\) be the last-edge, penultimate-vertex, and terminal-vertex
indices in the supplied common-tail datum.  Their position equations and
(2) say
\[
 s=|B^+|,qquad a=|B^+|,qquad b=|T|.
\]
Hence the last vertex of \(B^+\) is exactly \(y_{p-1}=T.\mathit{vertex}(a)\),
the last vertex of \(T\) is \(y_p=T.\mathit{vertex}(b)\), and the unique
edge added in \(B^+\prec T\) is the individual identity
\(e_p=T.\mathit{edge}(s)\).  The attachment field of
\noderef{TreeSequence}, applied at the index \(s\), says that this very
edge is incident with its new vertex \(y_p\).  Vertex injectivity in the
same definition shows that \(y_{p-1}\notin V(B)\), because every vertex
index of \(B\) is at most \(|B|=a-1\).  It also shows
\(y_{p-1}\ne y_p\).

Apply \noderef{HierarchyCounterexamplePenultimateElementary} to the
properness, exact one-uncoloredness, complete lower-rung package,
fewer-divider induction, rung-local lexicographic minimality, and
large-missing-set fields of the supplied
\noderef{HierarchyMinimumCounterexampleContext}.  In view of (1)--(2),
its exact conclusion is
\[
             V(B^+)\text{ is elementary under }\varphi_n.     \tag{4}
\]

We record the normalized condition (20).  The datum supplies a vertex of
\(B\) missing \(\alpha\), also supplies \(\alpha\) missing at \(y_p\),
and says, on every exact displayed edge identity,
\[
       \varphi_n(T.\mathit{edge}(t))\ne\alpha
                         \quad(|A|\leq t<|T|).                 \tag{5}
\]
It also supplies one of the following three alternatives:
\[
\begin{array}{ll}
q>0:&\alpha\notin D_{n,q}\cup\Gamma^q,\\
q=0:&\alpha\notin D_n\cup\Gamma^0,\\
&\alpha\in D_{n,q}\text{ and }
  \Gamma^q_\alpha\cap\varphi_n\langle T-A\rangle=\varnothing,
\end{array}                                                    \tag{6}
\]
where \(D_n=\noderef{TashkinovStoredColors}(\mathcal E,n)\).  These are
exactly the alternatives in
\noderef{HierarchySituationTwoCaseThreeNormalizedData}, including the
raw \(D_n\) exclusion when \(q=0\).

The Subcase 3.2 field gives
\(\theta\in\noderef{MissingColors}(G,\varphi_n,y_{p-1})\).  If a vertex
of \(B\) also missed \(\theta\), it and \(y_{p-1}\) would be distinct
vertices of the elementary set (4) with intersecting missing sets,
contrary to \noderef{ColoredVertexSetElementary}.  Thus no vertex of
\(B\), and hence none of \(A\), misses \(\theta\).  Since
\(y_{p-1}\) does miss it, the least-candidate definition in
\noderef{HierarchyFirstMissingPosition} gives
\[
 i_\theta=a=|B^+|.                                           \tag{7}
\]
Finally \(\alpha\ne\theta\).  Indeed, \(e_p\) is incident with \(y_p\),
\(\varphi_n(e_p)=\theta\), while \(\alpha\) is missing at \(y_p\).
Expanding \noderef{MissingColors} says that no incident edge has color
\(\alpha\), so the displayed edge forces \(\theta\ne\alpha\).

We shall repeatedly use the following pointwise argument, and we state it
fully so that no component-disjointness claim is mistaken for
vertex-disjointness from \(B^+\).  Let a proper coloring \(\psi\) have an
elementary \(B^+\), let \(c\ne d\) have first missing witnesses
\(v_c,v_d\in V(B^+)\) in one common \((c,d)\)-component \(K\), and let
\(Q\) be a component disjoint from \(K\).  Put
\(\psi'=\psi/Q\).  Fix \(u\in V(B^+)\).  If \(u\notin Q\), no affected
edge incident with \(u\) can have its edge-node in \(Q\), since that
edge-node is adjacent to the vertex-node \(u\) in
\noderef{TwoColorIncidenceGraph}; hence every incident edge is fixed by
\noderef{TwoColorComponentSwap}.  If \(u\in Q\) and \(u\) missed \(c\),
then \(u\) and \(v_c\) would be distinct vertices of the elementary set
missing the same color: they are distinct because \(u\in Q\),
\(v_c\in K\), and \(Q\cap K=\varnothing\).  This contradicts
\noderef{ColoredVertexSetElementary}; the identical argument with
\(v_d\) shows that \(u\) does not miss \(d\).  By
\noderef{MissingColors}, both colors are therefore present at \(u\), and
properness gives exactly one incident edge of each color.  Both edge-nodes
belong to \(Q\), so the swap exchanges these two edges and fixes every
other incident color.  In either case
\[
             \overline{\psi'}(u)=\overline\psi(u).             \tag{8}
\]
This proof permits \(Q\) to meet \(B^+\) at internal vertices; it never
claims that \(Q\) avoids the prefix.

We also record how the component at \(y_p\) supplies the new terminal
missing color.  If \(d\) is missing at \(y_p\) before a \((c,d)\)-swap
rooted there, then afterward \(c\) is missing there.  If an incident
\(c\)-edge exists, its edge-node is adjacent to the root, hence belongs
to the rooted component and is changed to \(d\); no incident \(d\)-edge
can create a new \(c\)-edge.  If no incident \(c\)-edge exists, both
colors were missing and the rooted component is the isolated vertex
allowed by \noderef{TwoColorPathComponent}, so the conclusion remains
true.  This follows directly from \noderef{MissingColors},
\noderef{TwoColorIncidenceGraph}, and
\noderef{TwoColorComponentSwap}.

In every use of \noderef{ChangeLemma} below, component separation is
proved as follows.  The change lemma identifies the components through
the two first witnesses.  If those witnesses coincide, that vertex misses
both colors and its component is isolated.  Otherwise, equality with the
component through \(y_p\) would put three distinct missing endpoints in
one finite spanning path.  Properness and
\noderef{TwoColorIncidenceDegreeAtMostTwo} give degree at most one at all
three endpoints, contradicting \noderef{TwoColorComponentSpanningPath}.
Then \noderef{TwoColorComponentsDisjoint} gives disjointness, and the
spanning-path theorem supplies the possibly singleton
\noderef{TwoColorPathComponent} certificate needed by the stable-change
theorems.

\begin{lemma}[Normalizing Situation 7.2 Case 3]
Fix a minimum hierarchy counterexample and a raw
\noderef{HierarchySituationTwoCaseThreeData} \(D\).  Thus
\(p(T)=\ell\ge2\), and its color \(\alpha\) is missing at the terminal
vertex \(y_p\) and at an explicitly displayed vertex
\(v\prec y_{p-1}\).  Then there is a
\noderef{HierarchyReplacementPresentation} \(P\) on the same exact
terminal tree \(T\) for which Case 3 has a
\noderef{HierarchySituationTwoCaseThreeNormalizedData}.  Equivalently,
after passing to an actual corresponding same-rung maximum ETT and
transporting the complete minimum context when necessary, condition (20)
may be assumed.  The conclusion is independent of whether the last-edge
color \(\theta\) is missing at \(y_{p-1}\).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Write \(H=C.\mathit{hierarchy}\), let \(q\) denote its divider count and
also use \(q\) for the last finite divider index, and put
\[
 A=T_{n,q}^{*}=\noderef{HierarchyStarredStage}(H,q),\qquad
 \ell=\noderef{HierarchyFullTailLength}(C).
\]
For \(0\le i\le\ell\), write
\(T(y_i)=\noderef{HierarchyFullTailPrefix}(C,i)\).  In particular set
\[
 B=T(y_{p-2})=T(y_{\ell-2}),
 \qquad B^+=T(y_{p-1})=T(y_{\ell-1}).                           \tag{1}
\]
The common-tail data give \(\ell\ge2\).  Hence all indices in (1) are
defined, \(A\preccurlyeq B\prec B^+\prec T\), and \(T-B\) consists of
precisely the last two individual edges.  The position equations in
\noderef{HierarchySituationTwoCommonTailData} identify those last two
positions with the displayed penultimate and terminal additions; no count
of endpoint pairs is being substituted for a count of edge identities.

First derive the prefix-elementarity premise needed for counting.  Apply
\noderef{HierarchyCounterexamplePenultimateElementary} using properness,
exact one-uncoloredness, the six lower-rung assertions, fewer-divider
induction, lexicographic minimality, and the \(2n+11\) estimate, all of
which are fields of
\noderef{HierarchyMinimumCounterexampleContext}.  We obtain
\[
                       V(B^+)\text{ is elementary}.            \tag{2}
\]

We now apply \noderef{NineN} at the exact index \(i=\ell-2<\ell\).
Use the notation of that lemma:
\[
 X=(\overline\varphi_n(B)\setminus M_0)\setminus U_{\ell-2},
 \quad D=D_{n,q},\quad
 \Gamma^q=\bigcup_{\eta\in D}\Gamma^q_\eta,                  \tag{3}
\]
where \(M_0\) is the set of colors missing on
\(T_{n,0}^{*}-V(T_n)\), and \(U_{\ell-2}\) is the set of colors on the
exact prefix-tail edges from \(A\) through \(B\).  The hypotheses of
\noderef{NineN} are (2) and the large-set field of the context.  Its first
conclusion gives
\[
                         |X|\ge2n+11.                           \tag{4}
\]

We split according to the size of
\(X\setminus(\Gamma^q\cup D)\).

Suppose first that
\[
                 |X\setminus(\Gamma^q\cup D)|\ge5.             \tag{5}
\]
Passing from the edge interval ending at \(B\) to the full tail adds
exactly the two final edge identities.  Their two colors delete at most
two members of the set in (5).  Consequently at least three colors remain.
Choose one and call it \(\beta\).  Directly from (3) and the choice,
\[
 \beta\in\overline\varphi_n(B),
 \quad \beta\notin M_0,
 \quad \beta\notin\varphi_n\langle T-A\rangle,
 \quad \beta\notin D_{n,q}\cup\Gamma^q.                       \tag{6}
\]
When \(q>0\), (6) is the first alternative required in (21).

At \(q=0\), the required statement is stronger: it excludes \(\beta\)
from the raw \(D_n\), not only from \(D_{n,0}\).  We verify it rather than
silently identifying the two sets.  By
\noderef{HierarchyStoredColorsAt},
\[
                  D_{n,0}=D_n\setminus\overline\varphi_n(A).   \tag{7}
\]
If \(\beta\in D_n\), then \(\beta\notin D_{n,0}\) from (6) and (7)
would force \(\beta\in\overline\varphi_n(A)\).  A raw stored color is
not missing on \(T_n\), by the defining subtraction in
\noderef{TashkinovStoredColors}.  Since at divider zero \(A\) is the
hierarchy core, every remaining vertex of \(A\) lies in
\(T_{n,0}^{*}-V(T_n)\).  Thus \(\beta\in M_0), contradicting (6).
Therefore
\[
                 \beta\notin D_n\cup\Gamma^0                 \tag{8}
\]
when \(q=0\).  This is precisely the raw-set upgrade at paper line 4210.

Now suppose instead that
\[
                 |X\setminus(\Gamma^q\cup D)|\le4.             \tag{9}
\]
The second conclusion of \noderef{NineN} supplies an injectively indexed
family \((\eta_h)_{h\in\operatorname{Fin}7}\) such that, for every \(h\),
\[
 \eta_h\in D\cap\overline\varphi_n(B),
 \qquad
 (\Gamma^q_{\eta_h}\cup\{\eta_h\})\cap U_{\ell-2}=\varnothing.\tag{10}
\]
These seven three-color blocks are pairwise disjoint.  Indeed, injectivity
makes their stored colors distinct; the `disjointPairs' field of
\noderef{GoodHierarchy} separates their Gamma pairs; and
\noderef{GoodHierarchyReservedDisjoint} says that the full Gamma union is
disjoint from \(D\).  Each of the two final edge colors can therefore meet
at most one block.  Since there are seven blocks, choose \(h\) whose entire
block meets neither final edge color, and put \(\beta=\eta_h\).  Combining
this choice with (10) gives
\[
 \beta\in D_{n,q}\cap\overline\varphi_n(B),
 \qquad
 (\Gamma^q_\beta\cup\{\beta\})
       \cap\varphi_n\langle T-A\rangle=\varnothing.             \tag{11}
\]

Thus in both size branches we have selected a color \(\beta\) satisfying
\[
 \beta\in\overline\varphi_n(B)
       \setminus\varphi_n\langle T-A\rangle,                   \tag{12}
\]
together with exactly one of: the positive-divider exclusion in (6), the
divider-zero raw exclusion (8), or the stored-color alternative (11).
This is condition (21), with every exact edge of the full tail accounted
for.

We next turn (12) into a normalized Case 3 presentation.  If
\(\beta\in\overline\varphi_n(y_p)\), take the identity replacement
presentation.  Concretely, reuse the original input, series, ETT, base,
hierarchy, good-hierarchy certificate, counterexample, and all fields of
the minimum context.  The identity equalities fill every field of
\noderef{CorrespondingExtendedTashkinovTree}, so this is an actual
\noderef{HierarchyReplacementPresentation}, not a bare assertion that no
recoloring occurred.  Choose from (12) a vertex of \(B\) missing
\(\beta\).  Its displayed index is strictly before \(y_{p-1}\) by (1),
while \(\beta\) is also missing at \(y_p\).  Equations (6), (8), or (11),
together with (12), fill the inherited raw Case 3 fields and all fields of
\noderef{HierarchySituationTwoCaseThreeNormalizedData}.  This proves the
theorem in this branch.

Assume henceforth that \(\beta\notin\overline\varphi_n(y_p)\).  The raw
Case 3 hypothesis gives
\(\alpha\in\overline\varphi_n(y_p)\), so \(\alpha\ne\beta\).  Let
\[
 Q=P_{y_p}(\alpha,\beta,\varphi_n),
 \qquad \sigma_n=\varphi_n/Q.                                 \tag{13}
\]
Properness from the context and
\noderef{TwoColorComponentSpanningPath} allow us to traverse \(Q\) as a
finite simple alternating path starting at \(y_p\).

If \(Q\) has no vertex in \(B^+\), apply
\noderef{HierarchySituationTwoOutsidePenultimateReconstruction}.  Its
candidate hypotheses are exactly (12) and the appropriate one of (6),
(8), and (11), and its terminal-present hypothesis is the present branch
assumption.  The cited lemma returns an actual replacement presentation
on \(T\) and normalized datum with color \(\beta\).  Notice that this use
treats \(Q\) as outside \(B^+\) only; since \(y_p\in Q\cap V(T)\), it does
not invoke \noderef{HierarchyLEMStable} on the full \(T\).

It remains to suppose that \(Q\) meets \(B^+\).  Let \(u\) be the first
vertex of \(B^+\) encountered while traversing the path from \(y_p\).  If
\(u=y_{p-1}\), form the exact tree-sequence
\[
                      T'=B^+\cup Q[u,y_p];                     \tag{14a}
\]
otherwise form
\[
                      T'=B\cup Q[u,y_p].                       \tag{14b}
\]
In either formula the union means: retain the indicated ordered prefix
entry by entry, then append in traversal order the individual path-edge
identities and new path vertices from \(u\) to \(y_p\).  Firstness of \(u\)
and simplicity of the path supplied by
\noderef{TwoColorComponentSpanningPath} show that every vertex after
\(u\) is outside \(B^+\), and in particular is new relative to the retained
prefix.  The attachment and endpoint fields of \noderef{TreeSequence}
therefore make each displayed path edge, with its individual identity, an
exact one-edge extension.  Both \(\alpha\) and \(\beta\) belong to
\(\overline\varphi_n(B)\): for \(\alpha\), the raw displayed witness has
index strictly below the index of \(y_{p-1}\), hence lies in the exact
prefix \(B\); for \(\beta\), this is (12).  These fixed witnesses remain
in every intermediate extension.  Since every edge of the alternating
segment has color \(\alpha\) or \(\beta\), each append is a
\noderef{TashkinovAugmentation} under \(\varphi_n\).  Thus, once the
correct terminal-series seed has reached the retained prefix, these
appends form a finite \noderef{TashkinovAugmentationSequence} to the exact
tree \(T'\).

We now verify that seed condition, including the non-PE corner.  Let
\(T_n\) be the terminal ladder tree.  The certified terminal member of
\noderef{TashkinovSeries}, expanded using
\noderef{TashkinovSeriesStep}, is noninitial because the ETT is positive.

If its kind is PE, its \noderef{TashkinovClosure} contains an exact
augmentation sequence from \(T_n\) to the final ladder closure.  The
relations \(T_n\preccurlyeq A\preccurlyeq B\preccurlyeq B^+\preccurlyeq
T\), together with the final-prefix field of
\noderef{ExtendedTashkinovTree}, allow
\noderef{TashkinovAugmentationSequenceRestrict} to stop this sequence at
the retained prefix in (14a) or (14b).  Concatenating the path appends
above gives the PE eligibility route \(T_n\longrightarrow T'\).

Suppose instead that the terminal kind is SE or RE.  Its constructor
records an individual edge \(f_n\), the exact singleton connecting-set
equality \(F_n=\{f_n\}\), an exact
\noderef{TreeSequenceAdjoinEdge} seed \(S=T_n+f_n\), and a closure route
from \(S\).  We split according to whether \(B=T_n\).

First assume \(B\ne T_n\).  Since \(T_n\preccurlyeq B\), the length
inequality is then strict.  The lemma
\noderef{HierarchyNonparallelFirstEdge} says that the first exact edge of
the first hierarchy stage after \(T_n\) is \(f_n\).  If \(q>0\), that
first stage is an exact prefix of \(A\preccurlyeq B\); if \(q=0\), it is
the terminal stage \(T\), and strictness of \(T_n\preccurlyeq B\) still
puts its first new entry in \(B\).  Hence the edge of \(B\) at position
\(|T_n|\) is the individual edge \(f_n\).

There is also no ambiguity in the new endpoint.  In the SE constructor,
the exact singleton equation identifies \(f_n\) with the edge in its
\noderef{MaximumDefectiveChoice}; that definition records one endpoint
inside \(T_n\) and its other endpoint outside \(T_n\).  In the RE
constructor the same inside/outside endpoint data are fields of
\noderef{RevisitingExtensionCertificate}.  The attachment equation for
the first new entry of \(B\), together with vertex injectivity in
\noderef{TreeSequence}, rules out using the already displayed inside
endpoint as the new vertex.  Thus its new vertex is exactly the recorded
outside endpoint, just as in the adjoin-edge certificate for \(S\).
Equality of the ordered entries through length \(|T_n|+1\) now gives
\(S\preccurlyeq B\).  Restrict the old closure route first to the retained
prefix of (14), and append the path segment.  This is precisely the
SE/RE eligibility route \(S\longrightarrow T'\), retaining the exact edge
identity \(f_n\).

It remains within the non-PE case to assume \(B=T_n\).  Then
\(|B^+|=|T_n|+1\).  If \(u=y_{p-1}\), the preceding first-edge and
inside/outside endpoint argument identifies all entries of \(B^+\) with
the adjoin-edge seed, so
\[
                              B^+=S.                            \tag{14c}
\]
The path appends after \(u\) therefore again give the required SE/RE
route from the exact seed.

We show that the alternative \(u\ne y_{p-1}\) is impossible.  The only
vertex of \(B^+-B\) is \(y_{p-1}\), by (1) and the exact
\noderef{TreeSequenceInitialSegment} relations.  Hence \(u\in V(B)=V(T_n)\).
Let \(w\) be the path vertex immediately preceding \(u\) as the path is
traversed from \(y_p\), and let \(g\) be their individual path edge.
Firstness of \(u\) gives \(w\notin V(B^+)\), while the path endpoint
equation gives \(G[g]=\{u,w\}\).  Thus
\(g\in\noderef{BoundaryEdges}(G,V(T_n))\), and its color is one of
\(\alpha,\beta\).  Both colors are missing on \(B=T_n\), as proved above.

For a terminal RE constructor, its `currentColoring' field identifies
\(\varphi_n\) with the preceding coloring; after this rewrite its
`previousClosed' field is exactly
\noderef{ColoredVertexSetClosed}\((G,\varphi_n,V(T_n))\), contradicting
the boundary edge \(g\).  For a terminal SE constructor, write \(\pi\)
for the preceding coloring and \(\rho\) for the selected coloring in its
\noderef{MaximumDefectiveChoice}.  The constructor gives
\(\varphi_n=\rho\), says that \(T_n\) is closed under \(\pi\), and says
that \(\rho\) is \(\noderef{StableColoring}(T_n,D_{n-1},\pi)\).  Its two
colorings are proper and leave exactly \(e_0\) uncolored.  Stability makes
the color \(c=\varphi_n(g)\) missing under \(\pi\) at the same witness in
\(T_n\).  Applying the reverse edge criterion in \noderef{sc1} to the
incident boundary edge \(g\), using that \(\rho(g)=c\) and that \(c\) is
in the controlled missing set, gives \(\rho(g)=\pi(g)\).  The closedness
of \(T_n\) under \(\pi\) is again contradicted.  These RE and SE
contradictions exhaust the non-PE constructors.  Therefore every possible
shorter-tree construction has the exact PE route from \(T_n\), or the
exact SE/RE route from \(T_n+f_n\).

Apply \noderef{Td} to the original ETT with \(\sigma=\varphi_n\).
Properness and exact one-uncoloredness are fields of
\noderef{HierarchyMinimumCounterexampleContext}; the two clauses of
\noderef{StableColoring} are reflexive, and the complete six-part
lower-rung package is the context's `lowerRung' field.  Hence
\(\noderef{ModColoring}(\mathcal E,\varphi_n)\).  The eligible route just
constructed is in the appropriate universal branch of that definition,
so it yields an actual
\noderef{CorrespondingExtendedTashkinovTree} certificate \(\mathcal C'\)
on \(T'\).  Applying \noderef{MaximumPropertyTransport} to the mod-coloring,
\(\mathcal C'\), and the old maximum field gives maximum property \(M'\)
for the replacement ETT.

We next build the replacement \noderef{HierarchyBase} field by field.
The certificate \(\mathcal C'\) identifies the rung, the exact terminal
ladder tree \(T_n\), the extension kind, the exact connecting-edge set,
and the stored sets, but it does not assert equality of internal PE
witnesses.  If the common terminal kind is PE, inspect separately the old
and replacement PE constructors in \noderef{TashkinovSeriesStep}.  Write
\((v,f,z)\) and \((v',f',z')\) for their inside vertex, connecting edge,
and outside endpoint in the two \noderef{MaximumDefectiveChoice} records.
The two constructor singleton equations and connecting-set agreement give
\[
                  \{f'\}=F'_n=F_n=\{f\},\qquad f'=f.          \tag{P1}
\]
Their endpoint fields give
\[
 G[f]=\{v,z\}=\{v',z'\},\qquad
 v,v'\in V(T_n),\quad z,z'\notin V(T_n).                     \tag{P2}
\]
The crossed orientation of the unordered endpoint pairs would equate an
inside vertex with an outside vertex.  It is impossible, so \(v'=v\).
The two PE `currentVertex' equations therefore identify the replacement
chosen vertex with the old chosen vertex.

Transport the old support position across the exact equality of the two
copies of \(T_n\), and take the replacement core and closure to be the old
core and closure.  Support positivity is unchanged.  The terminal
missing-set equation is reflexive because both the hierarchy coloring and
the replacement ETT generating coloring are \(\varphi_n\).  In the PE
case, (P1)--(P2) and the old base's PE field give the required chosen-vertex
equation at that support position; its closure is still the same
\(\varphi_n\)-closure of the same ordered prefix, and its exact ordered
union is unchanged.  In the non-PE case, kind agreement makes the
replacement kind non-PE and the old nonparallel field identifies the
chosen core with the common \(T_n\); the unused closure has no condition.
This supplies every field of a base \(\mathcal B'\).

Define a \noderef{TashkinovHierarchy} \(H'\) on \(\mathcal B'\) by keeping
the old divider count, every old nonterminal stage and every old numerical
divider position, and replacing only the terminal stage \(T\) by \(T'\).
For \(q>0\), the last ordinary stage is \(A\), which is an exact prefix of
the retained \(B\); for \(q=0\), the core is an exact prefix of \(B\).
This proves final nesting and strictness.  All earlier nesting and
strictness fields are unchanged.  Each divider lies in an old ordinary
stage contained in \(A\preccurlyeq B\), so exact prefix equality through
\(B\) preserves every divider-stage equation.  The base-stage,
terminal-stage, divider positivity and increasingness, and core-inside-first
fields now follow term by term.  Thus \(H'\) has the old stages and
dividers except for its displayed terminal tree.

The individual stored arrays of the two ETTs agree by \(\mathcal C'\),
while the terminal ladder tree and its coloring are literally the same.
Expanding \noderef{TashkinovStoredColors} therefore identifies the two
computed terminal sets \(D_n\).  The base core and every nonterminal stage
also agreed exactly.  Expanding \noderef{HierarchyStoredColorsAt} now gives
the old computed
\(D_{n,j}\) at every \(j\).  Expanding
\noderef{HierarchyStageMissingColors} gives the old stage-missing set at
every \(j\), since that definition uses \(T_n\) at zero and the unchanged
ordinary stage at a positive index.  Give \(H'\) the old Gamma pairs.
Pair cardinality and pairwise disjointness now copy.  Unique refresh uses
only the unchanged ordinary and starred stages, so it copies as well.  The
PE-base clause uses the same core and closure under the same coloring, and
every minus-closed clause uses an unchanged nonterminal ordinary stage;
these clauses also copy.  It remains to verify avoidance, including all
lower divider indices.

Fix \(j<q\) and a stored color \(\eta\).  The next ordinary stage
\(T_{n,j+1}\), and every displayed edge and vertex in it, are exactly the
same in \(H\) and \(H'\) and lie in \(A\preccurlyeq B\).  Unfold
\noderef{HierarchyFirstMissingPosition}.  If \(\eta\) has a first missing
vertex inside that next stage, the identical preceding vertices show that
the least position is the same in both terminal displays.  If it has none
there, both first positions, whether attained later or replaced by the
terminal fallback, truncate to the common length of that next stage.
Consequently
\[
 \min\{i_\eta,|T_{n,j+1}|\}
   =\min\{i'_\eta,|T_{n,j+1}|\}.                              \tag{17}
\]
The starred stage, next stage, exact edge identities, and coloring are all
unchanged.  Unfolding \noderef{HierarchySegmentColors} and using (17)
therefore proves equality of the old and new \((j,\eta)\) segment-color
sets.  Old avoidance supplies avoidance for every \(j<q\).

At \(j=q\), let \(R\) be the prefix retained in (14a) or (14b).  If a
stored color has its old first missing position in \(R\), equality of all
preceding displayed vertices makes its new first missing position the same.
If it has no old missing position in \(R\), every retained edge lies before
the old cutoff, and hence was controlled by old avoidance; whichever first
missing position the appended path creates, the retained edges before the
new cutoff are a subset of these controlled edges.  Thus old avoidance
controls every retained edge that enters the new terminal segment-color
set.  All newly appended edges have color \(\alpha\) or \(\beta\).  We
check those colors in the two shorter-counterexample branches below; this
is the only remaining avoidance issue.

We also compute the decrease in path number directly.  The old common-tail
datum says
\[
          \noderef{HierarchyPathNumber}(H)=\ell.              \tag{18}
\]
For (14a), \(|B^+|=|A|+\ell-1\), and at least one path edge is appended
from \(u=y_{p-1}\) to the distinct vertex \(y_p\).  Hence
\(1\leq\ell-1\leq |T'|-|A|\).  Every exact edge position
\(r\geq|A|+\ell-1=|B^+|\) is an appended path edge and has endpoint pair
\(\{T'.\mathit{vertex}(r),T'.\mathit{vertex}(r+1)\}\).
Thus \(\ell-1\) belongs to the candidate set in the definition of
\noderef{HierarchyPathNumber}(H')\).

For (14b), \(|B|=|A|+\ell-2\), and the nonempty segment from \(u\) to
\(y_p\) appends at least one edge, so again
\(1\leq\ell-1\leq |T'|-|A|\).  The threshold
\(|A|+\ell-1=|B|+1\) deliberately skips the first appended edge.  Every
edge at or beyond it is a subsequent edge of the same simple path and has
the required consecutive endpoints; if only one edge was appended, the
universal condition is vacuous.  Therefore \(\ell-1\) is a candidate in
this case as well.  The candidate set is nonempty, and its minimum gives
\[
 \noderef{HierarchyPathNumber}(H')\leq\ell-1<\ell
    =\noderef{HierarchyPathNumber}(H).                        \tag{19}
\]

The candidate \(\beta\) is outside \(\Gamma^q\) in every alternative.
This is explicit in (6) and (8).  In (11), it follows from
\(\beta\in D_{n,q}\) and the disjointness conclusion of
\noderef{GoodHierarchyReservedDisjoint}.  We now consider the three
possible positions of the original \(\alpha\).

If \(\alpha\notin\Gamma^q\), copy all old hierarchy stages and Gamma
pairs to \(T'\).  For the terminal avoidance clause, every retained edge
before the new cutoff was already controlled by the old clause, and every
new edge has color \(\alpha\) or \(\beta\), both outside \(\Gamma^q\).
Together with (17) and all copied fields checked above, this gives a
\noderef{GoodHierarchy} on \(H'\) with the same divider count.
The two old \(\alpha\)-missing vertices, the
earlier one in \(B\) and \(y_p\) on the appended path, both lie in \(T'\),
so \(T'\) violates the pairwise-disjointness condition in
\noderef{ColoredVertexSetElementary}.  Rung agreement supplies positivity,
and \(H'\), the good witness just constructed, and \(M'\) supply the
hierarchy, goodness, and maximum fields.  These are every field of a
genuine \noderef{HierarchyCounterexample} \(C'\), with the same rung and
divider count as \(C\).

Suppose next that
\(\alpha\in\Gamma^q\cap\varphi_n\langle T-A\rangle\).  The last edge is
not colored \(\alpha\): the edge is incident with \(y_p\), whereas
\(\alpha\in\noderef{MissingColors}(G,\varphi_n,y_p)\).  Hence an old
\(\alpha\)-colored tail edge has position at most \(|T|-2=|B|\).  Choose
\(\eta\in D_{n,q}\) with \(\alpha\in\Gamma^q_\eta\), and let \(r\) be
such an edge position.  Unfolding the terminal clause of
\noderef{HierarchySegmentColors}, old avoidance and
\(\varphi_n(T.\mathit{edge}(r))=\alpha\) force the first
\(\eta\)-missing position to be at most \(r\); otherwise that exact edge
would belong to the forbidden segment-color set.  Thus \(\eta\) already
has its first missing vertex in \(B\).  Since \(T'\) and \(T\) agree
entrywise through \(B\), unfolding
\noderef{HierarchyFirstMissingPosition} shows that this least position is
unchanged in \(T'\).

All new \(\alpha\)-edges have positions at least \(|B|\) in (14b), and at
least \(|B^+|\) in (14a), so they occur at or after the just-established
cutoff and do not enter the \(\eta\)-segment.  For every stored
\(\xi\ne\eta\), pairwise disjointness in \noderef{GoodHierarchy} gives
\(\alpha\notin\Gamma^q_\xi\); and \(\beta\notin\Gamma^q\) was proved
above.  Hence no newly appended edge violates any terminal avoidance
clause.  Together with (17), this supplies the complete good hierarchy on
\(H'\).  The two retained \(\alpha\)-missing witnesses again make \(T'\)
non-elementary by \noderef{ColoredVertexSetElementary}.  Together with
the same positivity, \(H'\), goodness, and \(M'\), this branch also fills
every field of a genuine counterexample \(C'\) with the same rung and
divider count.

In each of these first two subbranches, (19) strictly decreases the first
coordinate of
\noderef{HierarchyCounterexampleMeasure}.  Therefore
\[
 \noderef{HierarchyCounterexampleLess}
   (\noderef{HierarchyCounterexampleMeasure}(C'),
    \noderef{HierarchyCounterexampleMeasure}(C))
\]
holds without inspecting the second coordinate.  This contradicts the
lexicographic-minimality field of the supplied minimum context.  Thus both
subbranches are eliminated.

The only remaining case is
\[
          \alpha\in\Gamma^q
             \setminus\varphi_n\langle T-A\rangle.             \tag{22}
\]
Choose \(\eta\in D_{n,q}\) with
\(\alpha\in\Gamma^q_\eta\).  The avoidance field of
\noderef{GoodHierarchy} puts \(\alpha\) in the corresponding
\noderef{HierarchyStageMissingColors}: on the last ordinary stage when
\(q>0\), and on \(T_n\) when \(q=0\).  In particular \(\alpha\) is missing
on \(A\): at a positive divider that stage is \(A\), while at divider zero
the missing witness in \(T_n\preccurlyeq A\) remains a witness in \(A\).
Both \(\alpha\) and \(\beta\) are therefore missing on \(B^+\).

We verify the conditional order premise of \noderef{ChangeLemma}.  If
\(\beta\) is also missing on \(A\), the premise's antecedent is false,
because both colors are missing there.  Otherwise the first
\(\alpha\)-missing vertex lies in \(A\), while the first
\(\beta\)-missing vertex lies after \(A\) and no later than its witness in
\(B\).  Thus the former precedes the latter.  Equation (22) says that
\(\alpha\) is unused on every exact edge after \(A\), so in particular it
is unused up to that first \(\beta\)-missing vertex.  This is precisely the
required order-and-unused implication.  The lower-rung, fewer-divider,
same-rung minimality, prefix-elementarity, and large-set inputs are the
corresponding fields of the minimum context and (2).  Finally, the
stage-missing conclusion above supplies the positive-divider case of the
lemma, or its \(T_n\)-missing divider-zero case.  Consequently
\noderef{ChangeLemma} gives
\[
 P_{v_\alpha}(\alpha,\beta,\varphi_n)
   =P_{v_\beta}(\alpha,\beta,\varphi_n),                     \tag{23}
\]
where \(v_\alpha\) and \(v_\beta\) are the respective first missing
vertices.  Both belong to \(B^+\).

The component in (23) is not \(Q\).  If
\(v_\alpha=v_\beta\), that vertex misses both colors and hence is an
isolated vertex of the \noderef{TwoColorIncidenceGraph}, whereas \(Q\) also
contains the distinct terminal vertex \(y_p\).  If they are distinct,
the common component in (23) contains one vertex missing \(\alpha\) and
one missing \(\beta\); equality with \(Q\) would add the third distinct
degree-at-most-one vertex \(y_p\), which misses \(\alpha\).  Properness,
\noderef{TwoColorIncidenceDegreeAtMostTwo}, and
\noderef{TwoColorComponentSpanningPath} make every such component a
finite path, which has only two endpoints.  Both cases are impossible.
The dichotomy in \noderef{TwoColorComponentsDisjoint} therefore makes
\(Q\) disjoint from the component in (23).

Now let \(x\in V(B^+)\).  Elementarity (2) says that if \(x\) misses
\(\alpha\), then \(x=v_\alpha\), and if it misses \(\beta\), then
\(x=v_\beta\).  Either equality would put \(x\) in the component from
(23), which is disjoint from \(Q\).  Hence a vertex of
\(Q\cap V(B^+)\) misses neither color.
The local injectivity clause of \noderef{IsProperEdgeColoring} gives it
exactly one incident edge of each affected color; their edge-nodes are in
the same component \(Q\), so the pointwise definition of
\noderef{TwoColorComponentSwap} exchanges both.  At a vertex of \(B^+\)
outside \(Q\), every incident affected-color edge also lies outside \(Q\),
and is unchanged.  Edges of all other colors are always unchanged.
Therefore
\[
 \overline{\sigma_n}(x)=\overline{\varphi_n}(x)
          \qquad(x\in V(B^+)).                                \tag{24}
\]

Equations (12) and (22) say that neither \(\alpha\) nor \(\beta\) colors
an exact full-tail edge.  The definition of
\noderef{TwoColorComponentSwap} therefore fixes every individual displayed
edge at or above \(A\), including the two final edges.  The first missing
position of every color in \(D_{n,q}\) is also fixed.  Indeed,
\noderef{GoodHierarchyReservedDisjoint} makes \(\alpha\notin D_{n,q}\),
because \(\alpha\in\Gamma^q\).  If a stored color is \(\beta\), its
missing witness lies in \(B\) by (12), and (24) preserves every candidate
through \(B^+\); if it is neither affected color, all of its incident-edge
colors are unchanged everywhere.  Unfolding
\noderef{HierarchyFirstMissingPosition}, including the no-candidate
fallback, gives equality of the two least positions.  These facts verify,
respectively, the full-tail edge, penultimate missing-set, and
stored-first-position hypotheses of
\noderef{StableChangeCasesOneTwoFullTail}.  Its Cases 1/2 premise is
supplied with \(\alpha\) in the stage-missing slot, as just obtained from
(22); no such premise is imposed on the candidate \(\beta\).  The cited
theorem returns a weakly stable \noderef{ModColoring}, an actual
\noderef{CorrespondingExtendedTashkinovTree} certificate \(\mathcal C^T\)
on the same exact \(T\), maximum property \(M^T\) for its replacement ETT,
a replacement \noderef{HierarchyBase} \(\mathcal B^T\), a full hierarchy
\(K^T\), a \noderef{GoodHierarchy} witness \(g^T\), and
\[
                 \noderef{SameGoodHierarchy}(C.\mathit{good},g^T). \tag{25}
\]

We now construct every remaining replacement field.  First, \(\beta\)
becomes missing at \(y_p\).  Before the swap, \(\alpha\) is missing there
and \(\beta\) is present.  Properness gives a unique incident
\(\beta\)-edge; its edge-node is joined to the root \(y_p\) in the
\noderef{TwoColorIncidenceGraph} and hence belongs to \(Q\).  The swap changes it
to \(\alpha\), while the old missingness of \(\alpha\) excludes any other
affected incident edge that could become \(\beta\).  Thus
\(\beta\in\overline{\sigma_n}(y_p)\).  Its old missing witness in \(B\)
remains missing by (24).  That witness and \(y_p\) are distinct because
\(B\prec B^+\prec T\).  They share the missing color \(\beta\), so the
terminal vertex set is not elementary by
\noderef{ColoredVertexSetElementary}.  Together with the positivity
transported by rung agreement, \(K^T,g^T\), and \(M^T\), this fills all
fields of a replacement \noderef{HierarchyCounterexample} \(C^T\).

Construct its \noderef{HierarchyMinimumCounterexampleContext} as follows.
The properness and exact-one-uncolored fields are the first clauses of the
returned mod-coloring.  The rung equality in \(\mathcal C^T\) rewrites the
strict rung bound in every universally quantified instance of the old

For `fewerDividers', the same rung equality and the divider-count equality
in (25) turn a hierarchy with fewer dividers than \(K^T\) into one with
fewer dividers than \(H\); the old universal field then proves its
elementarity.

For lexicographic minimality, (25) gives equality of every ordinary stage,
divider position, computed stored set, and indexed Gamma pair, and gives

therefore identifies the first coordinates of
\(\noderef{HierarchyCounterexampleMeasure}(C^T)\) and

instance of the replacement-stability clause in \(\mathcal C^T\), unfolded
through \noderef{StableColoring}, identifies the old and new missing-color
sets on the common ladder tree \(T_n\); rung agreement then transports the
old \(2n+11\) inequality.  These are all five context fields, including
all six assertions inside `lowerRung'.

The coloring \(\sigma_n\), certificate \(\mathcal C^T\), maximum property
\(M^T\), base \(\mathcal B^T\), counterexample \(C^T\), same-good witness
(25), and the context just constructed fill every field of
\noderef{HierarchyReplacementPresentation}.

It remains to fill the normalized datum with color \(\beta\).  Retain the
same exact last-edge, penultimate-vertex, and terminal-vertex indices.
Choose an exact displayed index for the vertex of \(B\) missing \(\beta\);
because \(|B|=|T|-2\), that index is strictly below the stored index of
\(y_{p-1}\), and use it as the new earlier-witness index.  Equation (25),
the fixed terminal tree, and the
definitions of \noderef{HierarchyFullTailLength} and

The same equality (24) gives \(\beta\in\overline{\sigma_n}(B)\), and the

Therefore the appropriate alternative among (6), (8), and (11) transports
term by term.  These facts fill all fields of

Every branch that survives the shorter-\(T'\) contradiction has now

Subcases 3.1 and 3.2.
\end{proof}

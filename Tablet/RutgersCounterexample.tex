\begin{definition}
Let \(\mathcal P=(\sigma,U,\ldots)\) be a
\noderef{RutgersClosedPresentation} relative to a fixed original ETT.
A \emph{Rutgers counterexample on \(\mathcal P\)} consists of distinct
colors \(\alpha,\beta\), with
\(\alpha\in\overline\sigma(U)\) and
\(\beta\notin\overline\sigma(U)\), and three exact incidence-graph walks
\[
       Q_i:a_i\longrightarrow b_i\qquad (i=1,2,3).
\]
Each \(Q_i\) is a
\(\noderef{TreeExitPath}(U,\sigma,\alpha,\beta,a_i,b_i,Q_i)\).
Their three incidence-graph supports are pairwise disjoint.  Their
outside ends \(a_1,a_2,a_3\) are pairwise distinct, their inside ends
\(b_1,b_2,b_3\) are pairwise distinct, and the latter have been labeled
in the strict tree order
\[
                         b_1\prec_U b_2\prec_U b_3
\]
of \noderef{TreeVertexOrder}.

Finally the data retain exact edge identities \(f_1,f_2,f_3\).  For each
\(i\), the edge \(f_i\) belongs to the \(\beta\)-colored boundary-edge
set of \(V(U)\), is incident with \(b_i\), and its edge node belongs to
the support of the corresponding walk \(Q_i\).  Thus these fields record
the individual \(\beta\)-colored boundary edges used by the three exits,
not merely their endpoint pairs.
\end{definition}

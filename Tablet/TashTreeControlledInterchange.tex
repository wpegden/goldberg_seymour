\begin{lemma}[Controlled interchange for a minimum bad pair]
Let \((\varphi,T)\) be a minimum bad pair, with
\(T=(y_0,e_1,y_1,\ldots,e_n,y_n)\).  Suppose \(i<j<n\), the distinct
colors \(\alpha,\beta\) are missing at \(y_i,y_j\), respectively, and
neither color is used on \(e_2,\ldots,e_j\).  Let \(Q\) be the
\((\alpha,\beta)\)-component containing \(y_j\), and obtain
\(\varphi'\) by interchanging the two colors on \(Q\).  Then:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \(Q\) is a path component containing \(y_i\), with \(y_i,y_j\) as
its two vertex ends;
\item \(\beta\) is missing at \(y_i\) and \(\alpha\) at \(y_j\) under
\(\varphi'\);
\item every edge \(e_2,\ldots,e_j\) keeps its exact color, and every old
Tashkinov witness for every later edge gives a (possibly exchanged)
earlier witness for that edge after the interchange; and
\item \((\varphi',T)\) is again a minimum bad pair.
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Unfold \noderef{TashTreeMinimumBadPair}.  Its first conjunct, unfolded
with \noderef{TashTreeBadPair}, gives a \(k\)-triple, the Tashkinov
condition for \(T\), and non-elementariness of \(V(T)\); its second
conjunct excludes every smaller bad pair for every coloring of the fixed
exact edge \(e_0\).  In particular, \noderef{KTriple} supplies properness
of \(\varphi\) and the fact that \(e_0=e_1\) is uniquely uncolored.

Let \(Q\) be the component in \noderef{TwoColorComponent}.  Since
\(y_j\) misses \(\beta\), \noderef{MissingColorIncidenceEndpoint} says
that its vertex-node has at most one neighbor in the incidence graph
\noderef{TwoColorIncidenceGraph}.  It belongs to \(Q\) by definition.
Together with \(\alpha\ne\beta\), this is exactly the leaf condition in
\noderef{TwoColorPathComponent}, so \(Q\) is a path component (including
the possible isolated case).  More explicitly,
\noderef{TwoColorComponentSpanningPath} supplies a simple path whose
support is all of \(Q\), and \(y_j\) is one of its ends.  Likewise, since
\(y_i\) misses \(\alpha\), \noderef{MissingColorIncidenceEndpoint} says
that if \(y_i\) belongs to \(Q\), then it is an end.  The vertices \(y_i\)
and \(y_j\) are distinct by the vertex-injectivity field of
\noderef{TreeSequence}.  Hence, if \(y_i\in Q\), it is the other end of
the spanning path.

We prove that \(y_i\in Q\) by contradiction.  Suppose it is not the other
end.  The preceding paragraph then shows \(y_i\notin Q\).  Let
\(\psi\) be the exact component swap defined in
\noderef{TwoColorComponentSwap}.  It is proper by
\noderef{TwoColorComponentSwapProper}.  At \(y_j\), there is at most one
incident \(\alpha\)- or \(\beta\)-edge.  If there is one, it is colored
\(\alpha\), because \(\beta\) is missing, and the swap changes it to
\(\beta\); if there is none, both colors remain missing.  Thus \(\alpha\)
is missing at \(y_j\) under \(\psi\).  Since \(y_i\notin Q\), no
\(\alpha\)- or \(\beta\)-edge incident with \(y_i\) belongs to \(Q\), so
\(\alpha\) remains missing there.

Let \(P\) be the prefix through \(y_j\), the object supplied by
\noderef{TreeSequencePrefix}; it is a proper prefix because \(j<n\).
We check directly that \((\psi,P)\) is bad.  For its \(k\)-triple, the
graph, \(k\), criticality, chromatic-index equation, and degree inequality
are unchanged.  The swap preserves properness as just noted.  By
\noderef{TwoColorComponentSwap}, it maps colored values to colored values
and fixes every uncolored value.  Hence the exact clause
\noderef{IsOneEdgeUncolored} remains true with the same \(e_0\).

The prefix is also a Tashkinov tree.  Indeed, \(e_1=e_0\) is uncolored.
For every one of \(e_2,\ldots,e_j\), the two hypotheses
\noderef{TreeColorUnusedThrough} say that its old color is neither
\(\alpha\) nor \(\beta\).  Its exact color is therefore fixed by the
swap.  Its old witness, supplied by \noderef{TashkinovTree}, misses that
same color at an earlier prefix vertex; colors outside
\(\{\alpha,\beta\}\) and their missing statuses are untouched by the
swap.  Thus every displayed colored edge of \(P\) retains its old
Tashkinov witness.  Finally, the two distinct vertices \(y_i,y_j\) of
\(P\) both miss \(\alpha\) under \(\psi\), so
\noderef{ColoredVertexSetElementary} fails for
\noderef{TreeSequenceVertexSet} of \(P\).  These facts are precisely the
three clauses of \noderef{TashTreeBadPair}; hence \((\psi,P)\) is bad.

For completeness, it is smaller than \(T\) for the actual registered
rank.  Put \(m=|E(P)|=j\) and \(q=\rho(T)\).  By
\noderef{TashTreePathIndex}, \(\rho(P)\le m\).  If \(m<q\), the first
rank coordinate decreases.  If \(q\le m\), the suffix-path equations for
\(T\), as expressed by \noderef{TashTreeSuffixPath}, restrict entry by
entry to \(P\), so \(\rho(P)\le q\).  A strict inequality decreases the
first coordinate, while equality together with \(m<n\) decreases the
second.  Thus \noderef{TashTreeRankLess} holds for \(P,T\).  This smaller
bad pair contradicts the universal clause of
\noderef{TashTreeMinimumBadPair}.  The counterfactual is impossible, and
\(Q\) therefore contains \(y_i\), with \(y_i,y_j\) its two ends.  The
two applications of \noderef{MissingColorIncidenceEndpoint} also give
the two asserted subsingleton neighbor sets.

Now set \(\varphi'=\varphi/Q\).  Because \(Q\) is a nontrivial path from
\(y_i\) to \(y_j\), its unique incident two-color edge at \(y_i\) is
colored \(\beta\) and becomes \(\alpha\); hence \(\beta\) is missing at
\(y_i\).  Its unique such edge at \(y_j\) is colored \(\alpha\) and
becomes \(\beta\); hence \(\alpha\) is missing at \(y_j\).  The unused
hypotheses also show directly, exact identity by exact identity, that
every \(e_2,\ldots,e_j\) lies outside \(Q\) and retains its value.

We next verify the pointwise later-witness assertion.  Fix a displayed
edge \(e_h\) after this prefix and an old witness \(y_r\), with \(r<h\),
for its old color \(c\).
\begin{itemize}
\item If \(c\notin\{\alpha,\beta\}\), neither the edge value nor the
missing status of \(c\) changes, so the same witness works.
\item Suppose the edge is outside \(Q\) and \(c=\alpha\).  If its old
witness is outside \(Q\), that witness remains valid.  If it lies in
\(Q\), then its missing \(\alpha\) makes it an end by
\noderef{MissingColorIncidenceEndpoint}; the spanning-path description
forces it to be \(y_i\) or \(y_j\).  The only endpoint which loses missing
\(\alpha\) is \(y_i\), and in that event \(y_j\), which also precedes
\(e_h\), is the new missing-\(\alpha\) witness.  The case \(c=\beta\)
is symmetric: a lost witness at \(y_j\) is replaced by the earlier
\(y_i\).
\item If the edge-node itself lies in \(Q\), an old \(\alpha\)-edge
becomes a \(\beta\)-edge and uses the new missing-\(\beta\) witness
\(y_i\); an old \(\beta\)-edge becomes an \(\alpha\)-edge and uses the
new missing-\(\alpha\) witness \(y_j\).
\end{itemize}
Since \(i<j\) and \(h>j\), every replacement vertex is earlier than the
edge.  These alternatives are exactly the quantified witness alternatives
in the statement, and they prove via \noderef{TashkinovTree} that \(T\)
remains Tashkinov under \(\varphi'\).

It remains to package minimum badness.  Properness follows from
\noderef{TwoColorComponentSwapProper}, while
\noderef{TwoColorComponentSwap} and
\noderef{IsOneEdgeUncolored} show that the same exact \(e_0\) remains
uniquely uncolored; every other \(k\)-triple clause is unchanged.  Outside
\(Q\), missing sets do not change.  At an internal vertex-node of the
spanning path, properness gives one incident edge of each affected color
both before and after the swap, so neither is missing.  At the two ends,
the missing statuses of \(\alpha,\beta\) are exchanged between
\(y_i,y_j\), as proved above.  Consequently, for each of the two affected
colors, the number of vertices of \(T\) missing it is unchanged; all other
missing-color incidences are fixed.  Since \(V(T)\) was non-elementary,
it remains so.  Thus \noderef{TashTreeBadPair} holds for
\((\varphi',T)\).

The displayed sequence is literally the same \(T\), so its path index and
both coordinates of its rank are unchanged.  The universal minimality
clause for \(T\) in the original
\noderef{TashTreeMinimumBadPair} quantifies over every coloring, and hence
still excludes every smaller bad pair.  Combining that clause with the
badness just proved gives
\( \operatorname{TashTreeMinimumBadPair}(G,k,e_0,\varphi',T)\), completing
all four assertions.
\end{proof}

\begin{helper}[Replacement excursion and final-departure order]
Let $\beta,v,z,P_\delta$ be any replacement-coloring data with $v\in Z$,
with $\beta$ missing at $v$ under both colorings, free for $T_\delta$,
distinct from $\delta$ and $\gamma$, and with $P_\delta$ a full simple
$(\gamma,\beta)$ path from $v$ to $z$ outside $X_\delta$.  There is a
$\beta$-colored edge $h$ under $\varphi_\delta$ with exact ends
$u^1_\gamma,u^2_\gamma$.  In one of its two orientations,
$P_\delta$ contains the five consecutive nodes
\[
 f^1_\gamma,u^1_\gamma,h,u^2_\gamma,f^2_\gamma.
\]
This excursion occurs strictly before the last $X_\delta$-vertex; that
last vertex is $v^0_\delta$, the immediately following edge is
$f^0_\delta$, and all later vertex-nodes lie outside $X_\delta$.
Moreover neither $u^1_\delta$ nor $u^2_\delta$ is a Scheide exit of the
replacement tree.  The assertion retains exact finite indices for the
five consecutive nodes and the final vertex-edge pair.
\end{helper}
\begin{proof}
Put $r=\gammaIndex(\delta)$, $\gamma=\gamma(r)$,
$X_\delta=V(T_\delta)$, and
$Z=V(T)\setminus\{u^1_\gamma,u^2_\gamma\}$.  We first fix the
replacement maximum certificate which is used throughout the proof.
The statement of \noderef{ScheideReplacementMaximum} gives all three
facts
\[
 \varphi_\delta\text{ is proper},\qquad
 \varphi_\delta^{-1}(\varnothing)=\{e_0\},\qquad
 (T_\delta,e_0,\varphi_\delta)\text{ is globally maximum}.       \tag{1}
\]
In particular, every later invocation of
\noderef{ScheideDefectiveChainBoundaryEdges},
\noderef{ScheideGlobalMaximumMissingCounts},
\noderef{ScheideGlobalMaximumOrder}, or
\noderef{ScheideExitNoFree} below is made for this same triple and the
same proper coloring $\varphi_\delta$.  From
\noderef{ScheideReplacementData} we also have the exact set identity
\[
                 X_\delta=Z\cup\{u^1_\delta,u^2_\delta\}.        \tag{2}
\]
Thus the assumed $v\in Z$ belongs to $X_\delta$.

We next construct and order the deleted-block excursion.  The color
$\beta$ is missing under the original coloring at $v\in Z$ and is
different from $\gamma$.  Hence
\noderef{ScheideStructuralInternalPair} supplies an individual edge
identity $h$ such that
\[
 \varphi(h)=\beta,
 \qquad G.\operatorname{ends}(h)=
        \{u^1_\gamma,u^2_\gamma\}.                              \tag{3}
\]
This edge is incident with $V(T)$.  It is not $f^0_\delta$, because the
latter has original color $\delta$ by
\noderef{ScheideDefectiveSetup}, whereas $\beta\ne\delta$ by hypothesis.
The edge-fixing clause of \noderef{ScheideDefectiveSwitchEffect}
therefore gives
\[
                         \varphi_\delta(h)=\beta.                \tag{4}
\]

The statement of
\noderef{ScheideSymmetricReplacementBoundaryConfiguration} says that
$\gamma$ is defective for $X_\delta$ under $\varphi_\delta$ and that
$f^1_\gamma,f^2_\gamma$ are $\gamma$-colored edges from $Z$ to the
complement of $X_\delta$.  By the definitions
\noderef{BoundaryEdges} and \noderef{BoundaryColorEdges}, both belong to
$\partial_{\varphi_\delta,\gamma}(X_\delta)$.  Apply
\noderef{ScheideDefectiveChainBoundaryEdges} with the globally maximum
triple in (1), defective color $\gamma$, free color $\beta$, starting
vertex $v$, and the supplied full path $P_\delta$.  It follows that the
two individual edge-nodes $f^1_\gamma,f^2_\gamma$ occur on $P_\delta$.
The endpoint equations in \noderef{ScheideStructuralConfiguration},
together with (3), give the four incidence adjacencies
\[
 f^1_\gamma-u^1_\gamma-h-u^2_\gamma-f^2_\gamma.                \tag{5}
\]
Every node in (5) lies in the same two-color component as
$f^1_\gamma$.  Since \noderef{ScheideDefectiveChainPath} says that the
support of $P_\delta$ is that entire component, all five nodes occur on
the path.

We now prove that adjacent named nodes in (5) occur consecutively; mere
component membership is not enough.  By the definition
\noderef{MissingColors}, no $\beta$-colored edge is incident with the
starting vertex $v$.  The path is nontrivial because its first vertex is
in $X_\delta$ and its last vertex is outside $X_\delta$, as recorded by
\noderef{ScheideDefectiveChainPath}.  Consequently the first step of the
path gives an incident $\gamma$-edge at $v$.  Properness in (1), unpacked
using \noderef{IsProperEdgeColoring}, makes this edge identity the unique
$\gamma$-edge incident with $v$.  Hence the vertex-node $v$ has exactly
one neighbor in its full $(\gamma,\beta)$ component, namely the node at
path index one.

Let two component nodes occurring at indices $i<j$ on $P_\delta$ be
adjacent in the incidence graph.  If $i=0$, the preceding uniqueness at
$v$ forces $j=1$.  If $i>0$ and $j>i+1$, then the predecessor and
successor of the node at index $i$, together with the node at index $j$,
are three neighbors of the index-$i$ node.  They are distinct because
the path is simple, again by
\noderef{ScheideDefectiveChainPath}.  This contradicts
\noderef{TwoColorIncidenceDegreeAtMostTwo}, applied using the properness
in (1).  Therefore every adjacent pair of component nodes that occurs on
the full path occurs at consecutive indices, in one direction or the
other.

Apply this conclusion to the four adjacencies in (5).  The edge-nodes
$f^1_\gamma$ and $h$ are distinct, as are $h$ and $f^2_\gamma$, because
their $\varphi_\delta$-colors are respectively $\gamma$ and $\beta$.
Also $u^1_\gamma\ne u^2_\gamma$, since otherwise (3) would make $h$ a
loop, contrary to \noderef{LooplessMultigraph}.  If the directions of
the four consecutive steps changed at an intermediate node, the simple
path would repeat respectively $f^1_\gamma$, one of the two block
vertices, or $h$.  Thus the directions are uniform.  We obtain indices
$i_0,\ldots,i_4$ satisfying the four asserted successor equalities and
having the five displayed values in (5), or having all five values in
the reverse order.

We next prove, in detail, that neither appended vertex
$u^1_\delta,u^2_\delta$ is a replacement exit.  First we establish the
following claim.

\emph{Claim.}  If a color $c$ is used on $T_\delta$, then some vertex of
$Z$ misses $c$ under $\varphi_\delta$.

Indeed, by \noderef{TreeUsedColors}, $c$ colors a nonfirst displayed
edge of $T_\delta$.  Write $n$ for the common length of $T$ and
$T_\delta$, and write $b=p-1+2r$ for the first edge position of the
deleted block.  The exact positional clauses of
\noderef{ScheideReplacementData} give three exhaustive cases.

If the displayed position $j$ satisfies $1\le j<p-1$, use the
\noderef{TashkinovTree} clause contained in the globally maximum
certificate (1).  It gives an earlier replacement vertex, at an index at
most $j$, which misses $c$.  The retained-vertex clause of
\noderef{ScheideReplacementData} identifies that position with the same
old position.  Since it is below $p$, it is a base vertex $y_a$ by
\noderef{ScheideStructuralConfiguration}.  Such a base vertex lies in
$Z$: its position $a<p$ differs from the deleted block-vertex positions
$p+2r$ and $p+2r+1$, and vertex-position injectivity is part of
\noderef{TreeSequence}.  This is the required witness.

Suppose next that $p-1\le j<b$.  Division of $j-(p-1)$ by two writes the
corresponding retained old edge as one of the two edges in a unique block
$q<r$.  If instead $b\le j$ and $j+2<n$, division of
$j+2-(p-1)$ by two writes it as an edge in a unique block $q>r$.
In either subcase, \noderef{ScheideStructuralConfiguration} says that
the old edge color is $\gamma(q)$ and supplies a base vertex $y_a$
missing $\gamma(q)$ under $\varphi$.  The block colors are injectively
indexed, so $\gamma(q)\ne\gamma(r)$.  The edge is incident with
$V(T)$ and is not $f^0_\delta$: alternatively, the latter conclusion
also follows because \noderef{ScheideGlobalMaximumUnused} makes the
defective color $\delta$ unused on $T$, whereas
$\varphi(f^0_\delta)=\delta$.  Thus
\noderef{ScheideDefectiveSwitchEffect} fixes its color.  That same lemma
fixes the missing set at $y_a$ unless $y_a=v^0_\delta$; in the exceptional
case its exact erase-and-insert formula still preserves
$\gamma(q)$ because $\gamma(q)\ne\gamma(r)$.  Hence $c=\gamma(q)$ is
missing under $\varphi_\delta$ at the base vertex $y_a\in Z$.

The only remaining positions are $n-2,n-1$.  By
\noderef{ScheideReplacementData} their edge identities are
$f^1_\delta,f^2_\delta$.  Each is incident with the corresponding
vertex of $U_\delta$, so
\noderef{ScheideReplacementPairFixed} preserves its original color
$\delta$.  The exact missing-set formula in
\noderef{ScheideDefectiveSwitchEffect} says that $\delta$ is missing at
$v^0_\delta$ under $\varphi_\delta$.  Moreover the full path, last-tree-
vertex, defective-color, and missing-color fields of
\noderef{ScheideDefectiveSetup}, when substituted into the definition
\noderef{ScheideExitVertex}, make $v^0_\delta$ an exit of the original
reduced configuration.  Hence
\noderef{ScheideStructuralExitsInBase} therefore writes it as a base
vertex.  The preceding position argument puts that base vertex in $Z$.
This proves the claim in every case.

Now fix $u\in\{u^1_\delta,u^2_\delta\}$.  Equation (2) puts $u$ in
$X_\delta$, while \noderef{ScheideReplacementData} puts it outside
$V(T)$ and hence outside $Z$.  Apply
\noderef{ScheideGlobalMaximumMissingCounts} to the globally maximum
replacement triple (1): whether or not $u$ is incident with $e_0$, the
stated lower bound shows that $u$ misses at least one color $c$.  If $c$
were used on $T_\delta$, the claim would give $y\in Z$ missing $c$.
The distinct vertices $y,u$ both lie in $X_\delta$, contradicting the
elementariness supplied by \noderef{ScheideGlobalMaximumOrder} for the
same triple (1), interpreted through
\noderef{ColoredVertexSetElementary}.  Therefore $c$ is unused.  By
\noderef{TreeFreeColors}, it is free for $T_\delta$.  Finally
\noderef{ScheideExitNoFree}, again applied to the replacement triple in
(1), says that an exit misses no free color.  Hence
\[
 u^1_\delta,u^2_\delta\notin
 \noderef{ScheideExitVertices}(T_\delta,\varphi_\delta).        \tag{6}
\]

It remains to identify the final departure.  Among the finitely many
positions of $P_\delta$, choose the largest index $q_v$ whose node is a
vertex-node $w\in X_\delta$.  The set is nonempty because its index zero
node is $v$, and $q_v$ is not the terminal index because the terminal
vertex $z$ is outside $X_\delta$.  By the bipartite adjacency definition
in \noderef{TwoColorIncidenceGraph}, the node at
$q_f=q_v+1$ is an edge-node, say $f$, and the following node is a
vertex-node $x$.  Maximality of $q_v$ gives $x\notin X_\delta$.
The two walk adjacencies make $f$ incident with both $w$ and $x$.
Simplicity gives $w\ne x$, and the unordered endpoint map in
\noderef{LooplessMultigraph} therefore gives
$G.\operatorname{ends}(f)=\{w,x\}$.  Thus $f$ is a boundary identity of
$X_\delta$ in the sense of \noderef{BoundaryEdges}.

The incidence-graph definition says that $f$ has color $\gamma$ or
$\beta$.  The second alternative is impossible: $\beta$ is missing at
$v\in X_\delta$, while the closedness supplied by
\noderef{ScheideGlobalMaximumOrder} for (1), interpreted through
\noderef{ColoredVertexSetClosed}, forbids a $\beta$-colored boundary
edge.  Therefore
\[
                         \varphi_\delta(f)=\gamma.              \tag{7}
\]
The data $v,\beta,\gamma,z,P_\delta,q_v$ now satisfy each clause of
\noderef{ScheideExitVertex}: $v\in X_\delta$, $\beta$ is missing there,
$\gamma$ is defective by the replacement boundary configuration, the
path is the supplied full path, and the maximal definition of $q_v$
says that all later vertex-nodes lie outside $X_\delta$.  Thus $w$ is a
replacement exit.  By (2) and (6), it cannot be either vertex of
$U_\delta$; hence $w\in Z$.

Now (7), the exact endpoints $\{w,x\}$, $w\in Z$, and
$x\notin X_\delta$ meet the precise hypothesis of
\noderef{ScheideSymmetricReplacementBoundaryConfiguration}.  Its
exact classification gives
\[
                    f\in\{f^0_\delta,f^1_\gamma,f^2_\gamma\}.  \tag{8}
\]
The five-node segment (5) returns to $Z$ after its final displayed block
edge.  More explicitly, in the forward orientation the node after
$f^2_\gamma$ must be the base endpoint of that edge: by
\noderef{ScheideStructuralConfiguration} its other endpoint is
$u^2_\gamma$, which is the immediately preceding node, and simplicity
forbids returning to it.  That base endpoint lies in $Z$ by the position
argument above.  In the reverse orientation the identical argument
after $f^1_\gamma$ gives its base endpoint in $Z$.  Therefore a vertex of
$X_\delta$ occurs at index $i_4+1$, so maximality of $q_v$ yields
$i_4<q_v$.  Since $q_f=q_v+1$, simplicity of $P_\delta$ now forbids its
edge-node $f$ from repeating either already displayed block-edge
identity.  Equation (8) forces
\[
                              f=f^0_\delta.                     \tag{9}
\]

Finally, \noderef{ScheideDefectiveSetup} gives
$G.\operatorname{ends}(f^0_\delta)=
\{v^0_\delta,u^0_\delta\}$ with $v^0_\delta\in V(T)$ and
$u^0_\delta\notin V(T)$.  The exact classification in
\noderef{ScheideSymmetricReplacementBoundaryConfiguration}, applied to
$f^0_\delta$ itself, also presents this same edge with one endpoint in
$Z$ and the other outside $X_\delta$.  Comparing the two exact unordered
endpoint pairs identifies the endpoint in $Z$ as $v^0_\delta$ and the
outside endpoint as $u^0_\delta$.  Since $w\in X_\delta$ is the inside
endpoint of (9), $w=v^0_\delta$.

The constructed indices therefore satisfy all four successor
equalities, $i_4<q_v$, and $q_f=q_v+1$; their path values are the five
nodes in (5), in one of its two orientations, followed at the last
departure by $v^0_\delta,f^0_\delta$.  By the maximal definition of
$q_v$, every later vertex-node lies outside $X_\delta$.  Equation (6)
supplies the two final non-exit clauses, completing every assertion.
\end{proof}

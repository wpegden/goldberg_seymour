\begin{lemma}[Change lemma, mixed location: final starred stage closed]
Fix a finite loopless multigraph with its exact edge set, a palette of
$k$ colors, and a distinguished edge $e_0$.  Let $(T,\varphi_n)$ be a
positive-rung extended Tashkinov tree.  Let $\mathcal B$ be a hierarchy
base and let
\[
 T_n=T_{n,0}\subset T_{n,1}\subset\cdots\subset
 T_{n,q}\subset T_{n,q+1}=T
\]
be a good hierarchy under $\varphi_n$, where $q$ is its last divider
index and
\[
 A=T_{n,q}^{*}=\noderef{HierarchyStarredStage}(H,q).
\]
Assume that $T$ has maximum property, that $V(T)$ is not elementary,
that $\varphi_n$ is proper and leaves exactly $e_0$ uncolored, and that
the rung number is positive.  These data form a
\noderef{HierarchyCounterexample} $C$.

Assume the six lower-rung assertions of the technical theorem, uniformly
for every positive-rung maximum ETT on the same graph, palette, and
distinguished edge, with arbitrary certified input and generating
colorings and with rung number smaller than that of $T$: lower-rung
elementarity, terminal interchangeability, the two PE stability and
stored-pair assertions, the terminal PE component assertion, the
defective-endpoint order assertion, and the mod-coloring assertion.
Assume also the same-rung induction hypothesis that every maximum ETT
with a good hierarchy having fewer than $q$ dividers is elementary.
Finally assume the large-color estimate
\[
 2n+11\leq |\overline\varphi_n(T_n)|.
\]

Put $\ell=|T|-|A|$, the number of exact tail additions above $A$, and let
\[
 T(y_{\ell-1})=T\big|_{\min\{|A|+(\ell-1),|T|\}}
\]
be the exact ordered prefix supplied by \noderef{TreeSequencePrefix}.
Assume (6.6): $V(T(y_{\ell-1}))$ is elementary under $\varphi_n$.
Assume moreover that $C$ is lexicographically minimal among all
\noderef{HierarchyCounterexample}s on the fixed graph, palette, and
$e_0$ which have exactly $q$ dividers, allowing their certified input
colorings, generating colorings, bases, ETTs, and good hierarchies to
vary.  Explicitly, no such counterexample $C'$ satisfies
\[
 \noderef{HierarchyCounterexampleLess}
  \bigl(\noderef{HierarchyCounterexampleMeasure}(C'),
        \noderef{HierarchyCounterexampleMeasure}(C)\bigr).
\]

Let $\alpha\ne\beta$ be colors missing on $T(y_{\ell-1})$.  Write
\[
 i_c=\noderef{HierarchyFirstMissingPosition}(\varphi_n,T,c),
 \qquad v_c=T.\mathit{vertex}(i_c).
\]
If at least one of $\alpha,\beta$ is not missing on $A$, assume both
\[
 v_\alpha\prec v_\beta                                      \tag{a}
\]
and the exact edge-identity exclusion
\[
 \alpha\notin
 \left\{c:\exists t\in\operatorname{Fin}|T|,
       |A|\leq t<i_\beta,
       \ \varphi_n(T.\mathit{edge}(t))=c\right\}.             \tag{b}
\]
Thus (b) is precisely
$\alpha\notin\varphi_n\langle T(v_\beta)-A\rangle$; it is not
an endpoint-only abbreviation.


Assume in addition that the first $\alpha$-missing vertex lies in the
last starred stage and the first $\beta$-missing vertex lies outside it:
\[
        v_\alpha\in V(A),\qquad v_\beta\notin V(A).
\]
Assume that $\alpha$ is missing on $A$ and that the singleton
$\{\alpha\}$ is closed on $A$ under $\varphi_n$.  Then
\[
 \noderef{TwoColorComponent}(G,\varphi_n,\alpha,\beta,
       \operatorname{inl}v_\alpha)
 =
 \noderef{TwoColorComponent}(G,\varphi_n,\alpha,\beta,
       \operatorname{inl}v_\beta).
\]
Moreover, $\alpha$ and $\beta$ are $A$-interchangeable: among all paper
$(\alpha,\beta)$-path components, including isolated vertex components,
at most one meets $A$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Write $H=C.\mathit{hierarchy}$, put
$q=H.\mathit{dividerCount}$, and retain the notation $A$, $\ell$,
$T(y_{\ell-1})$, $i_\alpha,i_\beta,v_\alpha,v_\beta$ from the
statement.  Write
\[
 K_x=\noderef{TwoColorComponent}
       (G,\varphi_n,\alpha,\beta,\operatorname{inl}x).
\]
Thus $K_x$ is the full component in the incidence graph and contains the
individual edge nodes of the multigraph.

We first record the prefix facts used below.  The nestedness and terminal
fields of \noderef{TashkinovHierarchy}, together with the two cases in
\noderef{HierarchyStarredStage}, make $A$ an exact ordered initial
segment of $T$.  If $\ell=0$, then $|A|=|T|$ and
\noderef{TreeSequenceInitialSegment} identifies $A$ with $T$ entry by
entry, including every exact edge identity.  The prefix
$T(y_{\ell-1})$ from \noderef{TreeSequencePrefix} would then also be
$T$.  Its assumed elementarity would contradict the
\texttt{notElementary} field of \noderef{HierarchyCounterexample}.  Hence
$\ell\geq1$.  It follows that $A$, and every ordinary or starred
hierarchy stage at or below $q$, is an ordered initial segment of
$T(y_{\ell-1})$.  Restricting
\noderef{ColoredVertexSetElementary} makes each of these stages
elementary.

The least-candidate definition
\noderef{HierarchyFirstMissingPosition}, applied to the two colors
missing on $T(y_{\ell-1})$, says that $v_\alpha$ misses $\alpha$ and
$v_\beta$ misses $\beta$.  Their stated locations make them distinct.
Elementarity of the prefix then says that $v_\alpha$ does not miss
$\beta$ and $v_\beta$ does not miss $\alpha$.  By
\noderef{MissingColors} and \noderef{PresentColors}, each is incident
with an edge of the other color.  Properness therefore makes $K_{v_\alpha}$
and $K_{v_\beta}$ nontrivial path components in the sense of
\noderef{TwoColorNontrivialPathComponent}; this also explains why the
singleton-inclusive convention in \noderef{TreeInterchangeable} causes
no endpoint exception here.

We next prove interchangeability on $A$.  If $q>0$, then
$A=T_{n,q}$ by \noderef{HierarchyStarredStage}.  The last-stage instance
of \noderef{step1} applies: the statement supplies maximum property,
the six lower-rung assertions, the fewer-divider hypothesis, the
large-color bound, elementarity of the full-tail prefix, and the
missing and singleton-closed color $\alpha$.

Suppose $q=0$.  The positive terminal certificate in
\noderef{TashkinovSeries} is, by the constructors of
\noderef{TashkinovSeriesStep}, exactly one of PE, SE, or RE.  In PE,
\noderef{HierarchyBase} and \noderef{HierarchyStarredStage} identify
$A$ with the exact core $T_n\vee R_n$.  The identity coloring is proper,
leaves exactly $e_0$ uncolored, and is stable on both constituents, so
it satisfies \noderef{CombinedStableColoring}; conclusion (iii) of
\noderef{interchange} applies to the assumed missing and singleton-closed
color $\alpha$.

It remains to justify the SE/RE assertion without treating ``the
preceding rung'' as implicit data.  In either case the nonparallel field
of \noderef{HierarchyBase} gives $A=T_n$.  Write $n$ for the rung number.
When $n>1$, restrict every array in the certified series to indices
$0,\ldots,n-1$, retaining tree slots $0,\ldots,n$.  Each retained
certificate in \noderef{TashkinovSeries} has the same constructor and
the same witnesses because it refers only to these retained slots.  Its
last noninitial constructor supplies the strict inclusion from
$T_{n-1}$ to $T_n$.  Taking $T_n$ itself as displayed terminal tree,
this restricted series, the strict inclusion, and the reflexive upper
inclusion form an actual \noderef{ExtendedTashkinovTree}
$\mathcal E^{-}$ of rung $n-1$ and terminal tree $T_n$.  Its generating
coloring is the preceding coloring $\varphi_{n-1}$.  Every bound in
\noderef{MaximumProperty} for $\mathcal E^{-}$ is one of the old bounds
at an index $i<n-1<n$, and the initial maximum-order equality is
unchanged; hence $\mathcal E^{-}$ has maximum property.

In the SE case the last constructor provides a
\noderef{MaximumDefectiveChoice}.  Its selected coloring is exactly
$\varphi_n$; its `selectedProper' and `selectedOneUncolored' fields give
the two coloring premises, and its `selectedStable' field says precisely
that $\varphi_n$ is
$(T_n,D_{n-1},\varphi_{n-1})$-stable.  The symmetric clause of
\noderef{sc2} also gives the reverse stable relation, so in particular
the two colorings have the same missing sets on $T_n$; no unmentioned
coloring is substituted here.  In the RE case its constructor gives the
literal equation $\varphi_n=\varphi_{n-1}$, and reflexivity in
\noderef{sc2} supplies the same stable premise.  We may therefore apply
lower assertion (ii) to the actual maximum ETT $\mathcal E^{-}$ and the
actual stable coloring $\varphi_n$.  Since $\alpha$ is missing on
$A=T_n$, it yields $T_n$-interchangeability of $\alpha,\beta$.

When $n=1$, slot zero of the original certified series is its initial
constructor and $T_n=T_1$.  The same SE `selectedStable' field, or the
RE coloring equality and reflexive stability, supplies exactly the
stable-coloring premise of \noderef{TechnicalTheoremBase}; the proper
and unique-uncolored premises are again constructor fields.  Its
interchangeability conclusion applies because $\alpha$ is missing on
$T_1=A$.  Thus in every case
\[
                 \alpha,\beta\text{ are }A\text{-interchangeable}.
                                                               \tag{1}
\]

Suppose for contradiction that $K_{v_\alpha}\ne K_{v_\beta}$.
The component $K_{v_\alpha}$ meets $A$ at $v_\alpha$.  By (1),
\noderef{TreeInterchangeable}, and
\noderef{TwoColorComponentsDisjoint}, the component $K_{v_\beta}$ is
vertex-disjoint from $A$ and has no $\alpha$- or $\beta$-colored edge
incident with $A$.  Let
\[
       \sigma_n=\varphi_n/K_{v_\beta}
\]
be the full component switch of \noderef{TwoColorComponentSwap}.
The theorem \noderef{TwoColorComponentSwapProper} makes $\sigma_n$
proper and the switch does not alter the unique uncolored edge, so
\noderef{IsOneEdgeUncolored} still holds.  It leaves
$v_\alpha$ missing $\alpha$ and makes $v_\beta$ missing $\alpha$.

Let $B=T(v_\beta)$ be the exact ordered prefix of $T$ ending at the
entry $v_\beta$.  Since $v_\beta$ lies in $T(y_{\ell-1})-A$, if
$s=|B|-|A|$, then
\[
                         1\leq s\leq\ell-1<\ell.               \tag{2}
\]
Both $v_\alpha$ and $v_\beta$ lie in $B$, so $B$ is not elementary
under $\sigma_n$.

Before transporting the coloring, apply
\noderef{GoodHierarchyTerminalPrefixRepackage} to
$A\prec B\preccurlyeq T$.  It returns an old-color ETT
$\mathcal E_B$ on the actual ordered tree $B$, with the same complete
certified series and maximum property, a copied base $\mathcal B_B$, a
same-divider hierarchy $H_B$ whose nonterminal and starred stages are
the old ones, and a genuine good witness $\mathcal G_B$.  In particular,
the computed $D_{n,j}$ sets, stage-missing sets, and Gamma values agree
at every index.  Its proof handles the totalized no-candidate case of
\noderef{HierarchyFirstMissingPosition} and gives only the needed
containment of the new segment-color set in the old one.  Thus no
unsupported equality of full \noderef{HierarchySegmentColors} functions
is being used here.

The switch is outside $A$.  Applying
\noderef{OutsideKempeRecoloringStable} first to the terminal ladder tree
and, in PE, separately to both constituents of the exact core, gives
terminal stability and the required
\noderef{CombinedStableColoring}.  In PE,
\noderef{CombinedStableClosurePreserved} transports the same closure
$R_n$ and its individual augmentation edges.

We check the route from the core to $B$ before invoking a correspondence
theorem.  The ordered-unused hypothesis gives
\[
 \alpha\notin\varphi_n\langle B-A\rangle,                     \tag{3}
\]
and the definition of $i_\beta$ gives
$\beta\notin\overline\varphi_n(B-v_\beta)$.  Each ordinary displayed
edge of $B-A$ is supplied by \noderef{TashkinovAugmentation}; if it had
color $\beta$, its preceding prefix would already contain a vertex
missing $\beta$, contrary to the minimality of $i_\beta$.  The only
nominal exception is the exact first SE/RE connecting seed when
$q=0$, $A=T_n$, and $\beta\in D_n$.  That individual seed is incident
with $T_n$ by the SE or RE constructor of
\noderef{TashkinovSeriesStep}, so the incident-edge clause of the
terminal \noderef{StableColoring} fixes its color.  Thus (3), the
first-missing property, and this exact first-seed check show that every
displayed edge used from the appropriate PE or SE/RE seed to $B$ has
the same color under $\sigma_n$ and $\varphi_n$.  At every old
augmentation witness before $v_\beta$, the switched component either
does not meet the vertex or exchanges two present incident colors; the
missing set is therefore unchanged.  The exact old augmentation route
to $B$ remains valid.  Equivalently, its required restriction is the
one supplied by
\noderef{TashkinovAugmentationSequenceRestrict}.

Now apply \noderef{Td} to the terminal stable relation, using the given
six lower-rung assertions and the maximum property of $\mathcal E_B$.
This gives a \noderef{ModColoring} of $\mathcal E_B$.

We separate the reconstruction by the actual terminal kind.  In PE,
apply \noderef{HierarchySplitter} only after supplying the old-color
prefix ETT $\mathcal E_B$, the two clauses of combined stability, the
same closure supplied by \noderef{CombinedStableClosurePreserved}, and
the verified restricted augmentation sequence.  It returns a
\noderef{CorrespondingExtendedTashkinovTree} $\mathcal E'_B$ on $B$,
maximum property, a base $\mathcal B'_B$ with the same core and closure,
and a hierarchy $H'_B$ satisfying \noderef{SameHierarchyStages}.

In SE or RE, use instead the exact edge $f_n$, singleton equality
$F_n=\{f_n\}$, exact one-edge extension $T_n+f_n$, and the verified route
to $B$ in the universal non-PE clause of \noderef{ModColoring}.  This
returns a corresponding ETT $\mathcal E'_B$; its equality of rung
numbers, positive ladder trees, raw stored sets, connecting-edge sets,
and extension kinds is exactly the data in
\noderef{CorrespondingExtendedTashkinovTree}.  Apply
\noderef{MaximumPropertyTransport} to obtain its maximum property.
Construct $\mathcal B'_B$ explicitly: take its core to be the replacement
terminal ladder tree, copy the unused closure and numerical support
position, use reflexivity for the terminal missing-color equality because
both displayed colorings are $\sigma_n$, use the retained non-PE kind to
make the PE implication vacuous, and use the chosen core equality for the
nonparallel field.  The old non-PE base has the same core $T_n$.

Still in SE/RE, construct $H'_B$ with the old divider count, the
nonterminal stages of $H_B$, terminal stage $B$, and the old numerical
divider positions.  The base-stage equality follows from equality of the
terminal ladder trees.  Terminal equality is reflexive; nestedness,
strictness, divider positivity and increase, and every exact
divider-prefix identity are the corresponding fields of $H_B$; and the
core-inside-first condition follows after rewriting both cores as $T_n$.
Thus every field of \noderef{TashkinovHierarchy} is present.  The copied
cores, counts, stages, and dividers satisfy
\noderef{SameHierarchyStages}; its PE closure clause is vacuous in this
branch.  This gives the same replacement package as the PE branch
without misusing \noderef{HierarchySplitter} outside PE.

It remains to construct, rather than assume, goodness under $\sigma_n$.
Transport the Gamma function of $\mathcal G_B$ along the established
divider-count equality.  First compare the raw inputs to the computed
stored sets.  The correspondence certificate makes each raw certified
stored set equal.  Every starred stage is contained in $A$, and the
switched component is vertex-disjoint from $A$ and has no incident
$\alpha$- or $\beta$-edge there.  All other edge colors are fixed by
\noderef{TwoColorComponentSwap}.  Hence the incident color set, and
therefore \noderef{MissingColors}, agrees under $\varphi_n$ and
$\sigma_n$ at every starred-stage vertex.  Unfolding
\noderef{HierarchyStoredColorsAt}, which subtracts exactly the starred
stage's missing colors from the raw stored set, proves
\[
                         D'_{n,j}=D_{n,j}                     \tag{4}
\]
for every $j$.  The same pointwise argument on $T_n$ and each unchanged
ordinary stage proves equality of all
\noderef{HierarchyStageMissingColors} sets.  This checks the raw
stored-set and starred-stage missing-set equalities separately; neither
is inferred from the other.

We next locate every totalized first-missing position in $B$.  Before
$v_\alpha$, no vertex misses $\alpha$ under $\varphi_n$ by the defining
minimality of $i_\alpha$.  Any such earlier vertex on the switched
component also lies in the elementary prefix $T(y_{\ell-1})$ and is
distinct from $v_\alpha$ and $v_\beta$, so it has both $\alpha$ and
$\beta$ present; switching exchanges the two incident colors and creates
no missing color.  The vertex $v_\alpha$ is on the other component and
still misses $\alpha$.  Thus its position remains the first
$\alpha$-missing position.  Similarly, every vertex of $B-v_\beta$
has $\beta$ present before and after the switch.  At $v_\beta$ the
switch replaces the missing color $\beta$ by $\alpha$, so no vertex of
$B$ misses $\beta$ under $\sigma_n$.  The fallback clause of
\noderef{HierarchyFirstMissingPosition} therefore assigns $\beta$ the
last position of $B$, which is exactly the displayed position of
$v_\beta$.  Finally, for every $\eta\notin\{\alpha,\beta\}$ the swap
does not change any incident $\eta$-edge, so its missing candidates in
$B$ agree pointwise; their minima agree when nonempty, and their common
last-position fallback agrees when empty.

We can now verify all six fields of \noderef{GoodHierarchy}.  Pair size
and pairwise disjointness are literal properties of the transported
Gamma pairs, using (4).  For avoidance, fix $j$, a relevant
$\eta\in D'_{n,j}$, and one exact edge position in the new
$(j,\eta)$ segment.  The preceding paragraph identifies the appropriate
first-missing bound in all three cases $\eta=\alpha$, $\eta=\beta$, and
$\eta\notin\{\alpha,\beta\}$.  Same hierarchy stages identify the
starred and next-stage bounds, while the component's disjointness from
$A$ fixes the color of this individual displayed edge.  It is therefore
an old segment edge avoided by the corresponding old Gamma pair.  This
is the required pointwise avoidance proof, not a claim that arbitrary
segment-color functions survive a Kempe switch.

For unique refresh, same hierarchy stages identify both exact stage
differences, (4) identifies the stored sets, the pointwise missing-set
equality identifies the union of missing colors on the difference, and
the Gamma pairs agree; the old existence-and-uniqueness witness is thus
the new one.  In PE, the same core and the closure transported by
\noderef{CombinedStableClosurePreserved}, together with pointwise
missing-set equality on both constituents, preserve the common-missing
cardinality part of the parallel-base field.  For its non-minus-closed
part, retain the old witness color and exact boundary edge: the whole
core lies in $A$, so the component disjointness fixes that edge and its
inside vertex's missing set.  In SE/RE the parallel premise is false.
Finally, every positive ordinary stage lies in $A$.  Each exact boundary
edge relevant to its minus-closed field is therefore fixed, as is every
stage missing set; (4) and Gamma equality identify the excluded union.
The old minus-closed proof applies at every index.

These are respectively the `twoColors', `avoidance', `disjointPairs',
`uniqueRefresh', `parallelBase', and `minusClosed' fields, so they
construct a complete good witness $\mathcal G'_B$.  Only now apply
\noderef{SameGoodHierarchy}: same hierarchy stages, (4), and the
transported Gamma equations give its required pointwise data.

The resulting ETT on $B$, its maximum property, base, hierarchy,
newly constructed good witness, and the two vertices missing $\alpha$
form a \noderef{HierarchyCounterexample} $C'$ with the same divider
count as $C$.  We finally compare the actual measures.  Put
$r_0=\noderef{HierarchyPathNumber}(H)$ and let $r'_0$ be the path
number of the hierarchy truncated at $B$.  If $s<r_0$, the fallback
clause gives $r'_0\leq s<r_0$.  If $r_0\leq s$, the path tail beginning
at $r_0$ restricts to a path tail in $B$, with a singleton allowed, so
$r'_0\leq r_0$.  In the equality case the second coordinate drops:
if $T_{n,q}$ is the last ordinary stage and
$d=|A|-|T_{n,q}|$, then
\[
 |T|-|T_{n,q}|=d+\ell,\qquad |B|-|T_{n,q}|=d+s<d+\ell.
\]
Hence
\[
 \noderef{HierarchyCounterexampleLess}
   \bigl(\noderef{HierarchyCounterexampleMeasure}(C'),
         \noderef{HierarchyCounterexampleMeasure}(C)\bigr)
\]
holds.  This contradicts the stated minimality of $C$.  Therefore
$K_{v_\alpha}=K_{v_\beta}$, and (1) supplies the asserted
singleton-inclusive interchangeability.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Reconstruction after a component outside the penultimate prefix]
Fix a minimum hierarchy counterexample and a raw
\noderef{HierarchySituationTwoCaseThreeData} \(D\).  Put
\[
 B=T(y_{p-2})=\noderef{HierarchyFullTailPrefix}(C,\ell-2),
 \qquad
 B^+=T(y_{p-1})=\noderef{HierarchyFullTailPrefix}(C,\ell-1),
\]
and let \(y_p\) be the terminal vertex stored by \(D\).  Suppose that a
color \(\beta\) is missing on \(B\), is absent from every exact edge of
the full tail above \(T_{n,q}^{*}\), and satisfies one of the following
three alternatives:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \(q>0\) and
      \(\beta\notin D_{n,q}\cup\Gamma^q\);
\item \(q=0\) and
      \(\beta\notin D_n\cup\Gamma^0\), where \(D_n\) is the raw terminal
      ladder stored set;
\item \(\beta\in D_{n,q}\) and
      \(\Gamma^q_\beta\) is disjoint from the colors of all exact edges
      of the full tail.
\end{enumerate}
Assume that \(\beta\) is not missing at \(y_p\).  Let
\[
 Q=P_{y_p}(\alpha,\beta,\varphi_n),
 \qquad
 \sigma_n=\varphi_n/Q,
\]
where \(\alpha=D.\alpha\), and assume that every vertex of \(Q\) is
outside \(B^+\).  Then \(Q\) contains \(y_p\), so it is not outside the
full tree \(T\).  Nevertheless there is a
\noderef{HierarchyReplacementPresentation} \(P\) on the same exact
ordered tree \(T\), with \(P.\mathit{coloring}=\sigma_n\), and a
\noderef{HierarchySituationTwoCaseThreeNormalizedData} \(N\) for \(P\)
whose normalized color is \(N.\alpha=\beta\).  In particular the returned
presentation contains an actual corresponding same-rung ETT, maximum
property, a rebuilt good hierarchy and counterexample, and the transported
minimum context with all six lower-rung assertions.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Write \(H=C.\mathit{hierarchy}\), let \(q\) be its last divider index,
put \(A=T_{n,q}^{*}\), and retain the exact displayed indices from \(D\).
The two position equations in
\noderef{HierarchySituationTwoCommonTailData} identify \(B^+\) with the
prefix obtained by deleting precisely the last edge \(e_p\) and last
vertex \(y_p\) from \(T\).  They also put \(y_p\) in
\(\noderef{TreeSequenceVertexSet}(T)\).  The root of a
\noderef{TwoColorComponent} belongs to its own component.  Hence
\(y_p\in Q\), proving both the first asserted membership and the fact that
\(Q\) meets \(T\).  The assumed disjointness says only that \(Q\) is
outside \(B^+\), as required; it does not make \(Q\) an outside component
of \(T\).

The raw \noderef{HierarchySituationTwoCaseThreeData} says that
\(\alpha\) is missing at \(y_p\), whereas the
present hypothesis says that \(\beta\) is not missing there.  Thus
\(\alpha\ne\beta\).  Use the singleton root set \(\{y_p\}\) in the
definition of \noderef{OutsideKempeRecoloring}.  The component-disjointness
hypothesis gives its outside clause, and the pointwise equation defining
\noderef{TwoColorComponentSwap} gives its coloring clause.  Therefore
\[
 \sigma_n\text{ is an outside Kempe recoloring of }B^+,
                                                               \tag{1}
\]
but, by the preceding paragraph, (1) is deliberately not asserted for
\(T\).

We next repackage the old hierarchy on the exact prefix \(B^+\).  Put
\(\ell=\noderef{HierarchyFullTailLength}(C)\).  The hierarchy stages put
\(A\) below \(T\), so the defining subtraction gives
\(|A|+\ell=|T|\).  The common-tail inequality \(\ell\ge2\), together
with the definition of \noderef{HierarchyFullTailPrefix}, gives
\[
 A\preccurlyeq B\prec B^+\prec T,\qquad
 |B|=|T|-2,\quad |B^+|=|T|-1,\quad |T|=|B^+|+1.            \tag{2}
\]
Apply \noderef{GoodHierarchyTerminalPrefixRepackage} to (2).  It gives a
positive-rung ETT \(\mathcal E^+\) on \(B^+\), with maximum property, the
same certified ladder series and rung number as the original ETT, a base
with the same core and PE closure, and a good hierarchy \(H^+\).  At each
divider its ordinary and starred stages, computed stored set, stage-missing
set, and Gamma pair are the corresponding old objects; the segment-color
sets are the exact restrictions to \(B^+\).

Now apply \noderef{HierarchyLEMStable} to \(\mathcal E^+\), \(H^+\), and
the outside recoloring (1).  Its lower-rung premise is exactly the
six-assertion lower-rung field of the supplied
\noderef{HierarchyMinimumCounterexampleContext}; its properness and
one-uncolored-edge premises are the corresponding context fields, while
positivity and maximum property came from the prefix repackaging.  Retain
the prefix corresponding certificate \(\mathcal C^+\), its replacement
base \(\mathcal B^+_\sigma\), its hierarchy \(K^+\), and its good witness
\(g^+_\sigma\).  The result yields
\[
 \begin{split}
 &\sigma_n\text{ proper and exactly-one-uncolored},\\
 &\sigma_n\text{ stable and }\noderef{TreeColoringInvariant}\text{ on }B^+,
                                                               \tag{3}\\
 &\mathcal C^+\text{ is corresponding and maximum on }B^+,\text{ and}\\
 &\noderef{SameGoodHierarchy}(g^+,g^+_\sigma).
 \end{split}
\]
This is the only invocation of the outside-Kempe hierarchy lemma; its tree
argument is \(B^+\), never \(T\).  In particular, \(\mathcal C^+\) is a
prefix certificate and will not be identified with the full-tree
certificate constructed below.

We now prove, directly from the two clauses in
\noderef{StableColoring}, the terminal-ladder stability needed later.
Let \(T_n\) be the old terminal ladder tree and put
\(D_n=\noderef{TashkinovStoredColors}(\mathcal E,n)\).  Since
\(\mathcal E^+\) retains the old certified series, its lower-prefix field
is the exact \noderef{TreeSequenceInitialSegment}
\(T_n\preccurlyeq B^+\).  Thus every vertex of \(T_n\) belongs to
\(B^+\) by \noderef{TreeSequenceVertexSet}, and the invariant part of
(3) gives
\[
 \overline{\sigma_n}(v)=\overline{\varphi_n}(v)
 \qquad(v\in V(T_n)).                                      \tag{3a}
\]
For the edge clause, take an individual edge \(f\) incident with \(T_n\)
in the sense of \noderef{TreeIncidentEdge}, and choose an incident
\(v\in V(T_n)\).  If \(f\) has neither affected color, the defining
formula for \noderef{TwoColorComponentSwap} fixes it.  If it has color
\(\alpha\) or \(\beta\) and its edge-node belonged to \(Q\), incidence
would join that edge-node to \(\operatorname{inl}v\) in
\noderef{TwoColorIncidenceGraph}.  Connectedness would put
\(\operatorname{inl}v\) in
\(Q\), contrary to \(v\in V(T_n)\subseteq V(B^+)\) and the outside
hypothesis.  Hence \(f\) is fixed in every case.  This proves more than
the controlled edge clause of \noderef{StableColoring}; together with
(3a) it gives the actual certificate
\[
       \noderef{StableColoring}(T_n,D_n,\varphi_n,\sigma_n). \tag{S}
\]
Certificate (S) has been proved field by field on the old \(T_n\); it is
not obtained by naively restricting the stable-coloring assertion on
\(B^+\).

We transport the old terminal augmentation route, including its last
individual edge.  The certification field in \noderef{TashkinovSeries}
provides a terminal \noderef{TashkinovSeriesStep}; positivity rules out
its initial constructor.  In the PE case, its terminal constructor,
unfolded through \noderef{TashkinovClosure}, supplies a
\noderef{TashkinovAugmentationSequence} from \(T_n\) to the final closure.
In the SE or RE case it supplies the exact singleton connecting edge
\(f_n\), the exact seed \(S=T_n+f_n\), and a route from \(S\) to the final
closure.  In the latter case, \(S\) and \(T\) are exact prefixes of that
same closure and \(|S|=|T_n|+1\leq |T|\); entrywise comparison in
\noderef{TreeSequenceInitialSegment} therefore gives \(S\preccurlyeq T\).
Since \noderef{ExtendedTashkinovTree} also places \(T\) as an exact
initial segment of the closure,
\noderef{TashkinovAugmentationSequenceRestrict} truncates the route at
the exact \(T\).  It also truncates it at \(B^+\).  Thus the final step of
the retained route is the displayed pair \((e_p,y_p)\), not an isomorphic
or parallel replacement edge.

The invariant part of (3) gives equality of the two missing-color sets at
every vertex of \(B^+\).  Every route edge preceding \(e_p\) is an exact
displayed edge of \(B^+\), and hence is fixed by the
\noderef{TreeColoringInvariant} in (3).  For \(e_p\), the attachment
field of \noderef{TreeSequence} makes it incident with the new terminal
vertex \(y_p\); the definition of \noderef{MissingColors} and the raw
terminal missingness of \(\alpha\) show
\(\varphi_n(e_p)\ne\alpha\).  The candidate \(\beta\) is absent from every
full-tail edge, so in particular
\(\varphi_n(e_p)\ne\beta\).  Consequently the component swap fixes the
individual edge \(e_p\) as well.  We may therefore apply
\noderef{TashkinovAugmentationSequenceColoringTransport}, with \(B^+\) as
the penultimate prefix and \(T\) as the target.  Its two pointwise premises
are exactly the missing-set equality from (3) and the edge equalities just
proved.  The result is the identical terminal augmentation route under
\(\sigma_n\), including \(e_p\); call this conclusion (4).

Pass the directly constructed certificate (S), together with the
properness and exact one-uncoloredness in (3), the original positivity and
maximum-property fields, and the lower-rung field of the minimum context,
to \noderef{Td} for the original ETT.  This gives
\[
                 \noderef{ModColoring}(\mathcal E,\sigma_n).   \tag{5}
\]
For PE, (4) is the first eligibility branch of the universal clause in
(5).  For SE or RE, the retained exact singleton \(f_n\), its exact
one-edge seed, and (4) give the second branch.  Hence (5) produces an
actual full-tree certificate
\(\mathcal C^T:\noderef{CorrespondingExtendedTashkinovTree}\) on the full
exact \(T\).  Applying \noderef{MaximumPropertyTransport} to (5),
\(\mathcal C^T\), and the old maximum property gives maximum property
\(M^T\) for its replacement ETT.  The prefix certificate
\(\mathcal C^+\) and this full-tree certificate \(\mathcal C^T\) are kept
distinct throughout what follows.

It remains to rebuild the full-tree hierarchy data, rather than transferring
the prefix data by assertion.  We first construct the
\noderef{HierarchyBase} on \(\mathcal C^T\) field by field.  The only
nonformal point is coherence of the terminal PE supporting vertex, because
\noderef{CorrespondingExtendedTashkinovTree} expressly imposes no equality
on internal PE or RE witnesses.

Suppose first that the common terminal kind is PE.  Inspect the old and
full-replacement terminal certificates supplied by
\noderef{TashkinovSeries} and \noderef{TashkinovSeriesStep}.  Kind
agreement forces both certificates to use the PE constructor.  Write
\((v,f,u)\) and \((v_T,f_T,u_T)\) for the inside vertex, individual
connecting edge, and outside endpoint in the two
\noderef{MaximumDefectiveChoice} records.  Their singleton connecting-set
equations and the connecting-set agreement of \(\mathcal C^T\) give
\[
              \{f_T\}=F_n^T=F_n=\{f\},\qquad f_T=f.          \tag{6}
\]
The two \(\mathit{edgeEnds}\) fields give
\[
 G[f]=\{v,u\}=\{v_T,u_T\},\qquad
 v,v_T\in V(T_n),\quad u,u_T\notin V(T_n).                  \tag{7}
\]
Here the two terminal ladder trees have first been identified by the exact
ladder-tree agreement.  The crossed orientation of the two unordered
endpoint pairs in (7) would identify an inside vertex with an outside one,
so it is impossible.  Hence \(v_T=v\), and the PE constructor fields give
equality of the recorded chosen vertices.

Make the same comparison between the old terminal certificate and the
prefix replacement certificate \(\mathcal C^+\).  Its terminal ladder
tree, kind, and exact connecting set also agree with the old data, so the
identical singleton-edge and inside/outside argument gives \(v_+=v\).
Consequently
\[
 v_T=v_+=v,\qquad
 \mathcal T^T.\mathit{chosenVertex}(n)
   =\mathcal T^+.\mathit{chosenVertex}(n).                   \tag{8}
\]
This derives the equality from the two individual edge identities and the
two endpoint splits; it is not inferred from correspondence.

Transport the support position of \(\mathcal B^+_\sigma\) across the exact
equality of the prefix and full replacement copies of \(T_n\), retaining
its numerical value.  Define \(\mathcal B^T_\sigma\) to have this support
position and the same ordered core and closure as
\(\mathcal B^+_\sigma\).  Support positivity copies.  Its terminal
missing-color equation is reflexive, since both its base coloring and its
ETT generating coloring are \(\sigma_n\).  In the PE case, (8) transports
the chosen-vertex clause of \(\mathcal B^+_\sigma\); exact equality of the
ladder trees transports its \(\sigma_n\)-closure of the support prefix and
its ordered-union certificate.  In the non-PE case, extension-kind
agreement makes both replacement certificates non-PE, and the
nonparallel clause of \(\mathcal B^+_\sigma\) identifies its core with its
terminal ladder tree and transports to the full copy.  These checks provide
every field of \(\mathcal B^T_\sigma\).  The prefix repackaging and the
first clause of \noderef{SameGoodHierarchy} also identify its ordered core
with the old core and, in the common PE case, its closure with the old
closure.

Construct a \noderef{TashkinovHierarchy} \(K^T\) on this base by retaining
from \(K^+\) the divider count, every nonterminal stage, and every divider
position at the same numerical index, and by replacing only its terminal
stage \(B^+\) by \(T\).  The base-stage and core-inside-first fields
transport across the terminal-ladder and core equalities just established.
All nestedness, strictness, and divider-prefix fields below the last stage
copy from \(K^+\).  For the final nesting, compose its old last nesting
into \(B^+\) with \(B^+\prec T\) from (2); strictness follows from the same
two length inequalities.  Thus every hierarchy field is supplied.
Combining the exact prefix-repackaging equalities with the
\noderef{SameGoodHierarchy} data in (3) shows that \(K^T\) has the old
divider count, old exact stage family, and old divider positions.

We verify goodness, including the last avoidance clause.  Every ordinary
and starred nonterminal stage lies in \(B^+\).  Equation (3), raw-set
agreement in the two corresponding certificates, and the definition of
\noderef{HierarchyStoredColorsAt} therefore identify every computed
\(D_{n,j}\) with the old one.  The definition of
\noderef{HierarchyStageMissingColors} likewise identifies all
stage-missing sets.  Give \(K^T\) the Gamma function of \(K^+\), which the
prefix same-good data and prefix-repackaging equalities identify with the
old Gamma function at every paper-relevant stored color.

For each color \(\eta\), its
\noderef{HierarchyFirstMissingPosition} is the same under
\(\sigma_n\) and \(\varphi_n\).  Indeed, if
\(\eta\notin\{\alpha,\beta\}\), the swap changes no edge to or from
\(\eta\).  For \(\eta=\alpha\), the raw earlier missing witness lies in
\(B\); for \(\eta=\beta\), the candidate missing witness lies in \(B\).
In either exceptional case the witness occurs before \(y_{p-1}\), and
(3) preserves missingness at it and at every earlier displayed vertex.
Unfolding the least-candidate definition proves equality of the least
positions, including its no-candidate fallback.

The component swap changes no displayed edge of \(T\): the invariant part
of (3) fixes the displayed prefix edges, while \(e_p\) was checked
separately before (4).  Hence the pointwise definition of
\noderef{HierarchySegmentColors}, using the same exact stage family and
the just-proved first-position equalities, gives equality of every old and
new segment-color set.  Old avoidance therefore proves the last avoidance
clause, including the possible occurrence of the individual edge \(e_p\).
All lower avoidance clauses, pair cardinality, disjointness, unique refresh,
and positive-stage minus-closedness copy from the good prefix hierarchy.
The PE-base clause also copies from that prefix hierarchy because
\(\mathcal B^T_\sigma\) has exactly its core and closure.  These checks fill
every field of \noderef{GoodHierarchy}; call the result \(g^T_\sigma\).
The core, conditional PE closure, stages, dividers, stored sets, and indexed
Gamma pairs just identified are exactly the two clauses of
\noderef{SameGoodHierarchy}(C.\mathit{good},g^T_\sigma).  At no point was
\noderef{HierarchyLEMStable} applied to \(T\).

The candidate \(\beta\) is missing at a vertex of \(B\).  That vertex lies
in \(B^+\), so (3) preserves its missingness.  Swapping the component at
\(y_p\), where \(\alpha\) was missing and \(\beta\) was present, makes
\(\beta\) missing at \(y_p\): the local condition in
\noderef{IsProperEdgeColoring} gives a unique incident
\(\beta\)-edge, its edge-node is adjacent to the root of \(Q\), and the
swap changes it to \(\alpha\), while the old missingness of \(\alpha\)
excludes any second affected incident edge.  The witness in \(B\) and
\(y_p\) are distinct by (2).  Their missing sets have the common color
\(\beta\), contrary to the pairwise-disjointness condition in
\noderef{ColoredVertexSetElementary}.  With positivity, \(M^T\), \(K^T\),
and \(g^T_\sigma\), this builds the replacement
\noderef{HierarchyCounterexample} \(C^T_\sigma\).

Finally transport the minimum-context fields explicitly.  Properness and
exact one-uncoloredness are in (3).  Rung agreement in \(\mathcal C^T\)
rewrites every strict lower-rung bound, so the entire six-assertion package
\(\mathit{ctx.lowerRung}\) applies unchanged.  The same equality transports
the fewer-divider universal assertion.  The established
\noderef{SameGoodHierarchy} relation identifies the divider count, every
exact stage and divider position, the starred core, and the terminal tree;
it therefore identifies both coordinates of
\noderef{HierarchyCounterexampleMeasure}.  Any same-rung,
equal-divider counterexample smaller than \(C^T_\sigma\) would then be
smaller than \(C\), contradicting the old lexicographic-minimality field.
Certificate (S), exact terminal-ladder equality, and rung agreement identify
the two \noderef{TreeMissingColors} sets, so the old \(2n+11\) estimate
is retained.  These are all five fields of
\noderef{HierarchyMinimumCounterexampleContext}, including all six
assertions inside its lower-rung field.  Together with \(\mathcal C^T\),
\(M^T\), \(\mathcal B^T_\sigma\), \(C^T_\sigma\), and the same-good
witness, they fill every field of the required
\noderef{HierarchyReplacementPresentation} \(P\), with
\(P.\mathit{coloring}=\sigma_n\).

For the normalized datum, retain all exact indices and the same last-edge
color \(\theta\), since the individual edge \(e_p\) is fixed.  Equality of
the full hierarchy stages and the definition of
\noderef{HierarchyFullTailLength} identify the new and old values of
\(\ell\); equality of the stage and divider arrays transports the
\noderef{HierarchyPathNumber} equation.  Choose as the earlier witness a
displayed vertex of \(B\) at which \(\beta\) is missing; by
\(|B|=|T|-2\), its index is strictly before that of \(y_{p-1}\).  The two
missing-vertex assertions were proved above.

Missingness on \(B\) transports through (3), and full-tail nonuse
transports through the exact displayed-edge equality used above.  For the
three-way normalized alternative, \(\mathcal C^T\) identifies the raw
\noderef{TashkinovStoredColors}, the same-good relation identifies
\(D_{n,q}\) and every indexed Gamma pair, the divider count is unchanged,
and the displayed-edge equality identifies the exact set of colors used on
the full tail.  Hence each alternative transports term by term.  This
fills every field of
\noderef{HierarchySituationTwoCaseThreeNormalizedData}, with normalized
color \(\beta\), and completes the reconstruction.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Undoing a parallel-extension switch preserves stability]
Let the positive slot \(q.\mathrm{succ}\), representing paper round
\(n=q+1\), be a PE with supporting vertex \(v\), stored gamma
\(\gamma\), and stored delta \(\delta\).  Let \(\sigma_n\) be a proper
coloring leaving exactly \(e_0\) uncolored and stable for
\((T_n,D_n,\varphi_n)\).  Suppose that the
\((\gamma,\delta)\)-component of \(v\) under \(\sigma_n\) meets
\(V(T_n)\) exactly in \(v\).  Define
\[
 \sigma_{n-1}=\sigma_n/P_v(\gamma,\delta,\sigma_n).
\]
Then the PE certificate supplies a selected coloring \(\pi_{n-1}\) and
a second stable coloring \(\pi'_{n-1}\) such that
\(\pi_{n-1}\) is \((T_n,D_{n-1},\varphi_{n-1})\)-stable,
\(\pi'_{n-1}\) is
\((T_n,D_{n-1}\cup\{\delta\},\pi_{n-1})\)-stable, and
\[
 \varphi_n=\pi'_{n-1}/P_v(\gamma,\delta,\pi'_{n-1}).             \tag{1}
\]
Moreover, \(\sigma_{n-1}\) is successively stable relative to
\(\pi'_{n-1}\) and to \(\pi_{n-1}\) for the enlarged control set
\(D_{n-1}\cup\{\delta\}\), and it is
\((T_n,D_{n-1},\varphi_{n-1})\)-stable.
All component switches are on edge identities in the incidence graph.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Take the certificate at slot \(q.\mathrm{succ}\) from
\noderef{TashkinovSeries}.  The PE-kind hypothesis eliminates the SE and
RE constructors of \noderef{TashkinovSeriesStep}.  Equality of the
chosen-vertex, gamma, and delta option fields identifies its witnesses
with the displayed \(v,\gamma,\delta\).  Let
\(\pi_{n-1}\) be the selected field of its
\noderef{MaximumDefectiveChoice}, and let \(\pi'_{n-1}\) be its
second-stable field.  The constructor gives properness and the exact-one-
uncolored condition for both, the two stability assertions in the
statement, and the switch identity (1).  Thus these are genuine recovered
constructor witnesses, not arbitrary existential colorings.

We record the geometry of the switch in (1).  By
\noderef{ParallelExtensionColor}, \(\gamma\) is missing at \(v\) under
\(\pi_{n-1}\), and the vertex clause of \noderef{StableColoring} makes
it missing there under \(\pi'_{n-1}\).  The selected connecting edge is
incident with \(v\) and has color \(\delta\) under \(\pi_{n-1}\).  The
second stability controls \(\delta\), so the same exact edge has color
\(\delta\) under \(\pi'_{n-1}\).  Hence
\(\gamma\ne\delta\), and properness makes this connecting edge the unique
two-color edge incident with \(v\).

The PE constructor also supplies a \noderef{TreeExit}.  Expanding
\noderef{TreeExitPath}, its simple path runs from an outside vertex to
\(v\) and has no other vertex of \(T_n\).  The preceding paragraph makes
the incidence-graph degree at \(v\) equal to one.  At the outside
endpoint, one of the two colors is missing and the path supplies an edge
of the other color, so its degree is also one.  All component degrees are
at most two by \noderef{TwoColorIncidenceDegreeAtMostTwo}.  If the full
component had a node outside the supplied path, a shortest connection to
the path would give a third neighbor at an internal path node or a second
neighbor at an endpoint.  Thus the path exhausts the component.  It
follows that the switch in (1) changes, among all edges incident with
\(T_n\), only the exact connecting edge: its value changes from
\(\delta\) to \(\gamma\).

Put
\[
 C=D_{n-1}\cup\{\delta\},\qquad
 \tau=\sigma_n/P_v(\gamma,\delta,\sigma_n).
\]
Here \(D_{n-1}\) and \(D_n\) are the exact finite sets from
\noderef{TashkinovStoredColors}.  The assumed component is the exact
connected support of \noderef{TwoColorComponent}, and its tree-vertex
intersection is expressed using \noderef{TreeSequenceVertexSet}.
The hypothesis on the \(\sigma_n\)-component likewise says that it meets
the current tree only at \(v\).  Stability of \(\sigma_n\) preserves the
missing set at \(v\) from \(\varphi_n\).  By (1), \(\delta\) is missing
and the exact connecting edge has color \(\gamma\) under \(\varphi_n\).
The edge clause of stability fixes this controlled connecting edge under
\(\sigma_n\).  Properness then makes it the only two-color edge of the
\(\sigma_n\)-component incident with \(T_n\).  Consequently the undo
switch changes that edge from \(\gamma\) back to \(\delta\), and changes
no other edge incident with \(T_n\).

We next prove that \(\tau\) is
\((T_n,C,\pi'_{n-1})\)-stable.  The two switch descriptions give, for
every incident edge other than the connecting edge,
\[
 \tau(f)=\sigma_n(f),\qquad
 \pi'_{n-1}(f)=\varphi_n(f),                                    \tag{2}
\]
while both values at the connecting edge are \(\delta\).
The vertex clauses of the two certificate stabilities give
\[
 \overline{\pi}'_{n-1}(T_n)
 =\overline\pi_{n-1}(T_n)
 =\overline\varphi_{n-1}(T_n).                                 \tag{3}
\]
The first inclusion in \noderef{HkuMissingStoredMonotone}, together
with the PE replacement that makes \(\delta\) missing under
\(\varphi_n\), yields
\[
 \overline{\pi}'_{n-1}(T_n)\cup C
 \subseteq \overline\varphi_n(T_n)\cup D_n.                    \tag{4}
\]

Let \(f\) be incident with \(T_n\), and suppose that either its
\(\pi'_{n-1}\)-color or its \(\tau\)-color lies in the set on the left
of (4).  If \(f\) is not the connecting edge, equations (2) translate
this condition to the corresponding condition for
\(\varphi_n,\sigma_n\) and the right side of (4).  The symmetric edge
criterion \noderef{sc1}, applied to the assumed
\((T_n,D_n,\varphi_n)\)-stability of \(\sigma_n\), gives
\(\sigma_n(f)=\varphi_n(f)\), and hence
\(\tau(f)=\pi'_{n-1}(f)\).  At the connecting edge both sides equal
\(\delta\).  Another application of \noderef{sc1} therefore proves
\[
 \tau\text{ is }(T_n,C,\pi'_{n-1})\text{-stable}.               \tag{5}
\]
The properness needed here follows from
\noderef{TwoColorComponentSwapProper}, using
\(\gamma\ne\delta\).  The definition
\noderef{TwoColorComponentSwap} only exchanges two present colors, so
the exact uncolored edge remains \(e_0\); this is precisely
\noderef{IsOneEdgeUncolored} for \(\tau\).

The PE certificate already states that \(\pi'_{n-1}\) is
\((T_n,C,\pi_{n-1})\)-stable.  Transitivity in \noderef{sc2}, combined
with (5), makes \(\tau\) \((T_n,C,\pi_{n-1})\)-stable.  Restricting the
controlling set from \(C\) to \(D_{n-1}\) preserves this stability
directly from \noderef{StableColoring}.  Finally,
\(\pi_{n-1}\) is
\((T_n,D_{n-1},\varphi_{n-1})\)-stable by the selected-coloring field.
A last use of transitivity in \noderef{sc2} gives
\[
 \tau\text{ is }(T_n,D_{n-1},\varphi_{n-1})\text{-stable}.
\]
Together with the recovered witnesses and (5), these are all three
stability conclusions in the Lean statement.
\end{proof}

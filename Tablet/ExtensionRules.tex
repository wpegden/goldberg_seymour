\begin{lemma}[Extension rules: technical assertion (iii)]
Let \(T\) be an extended Tashkinov tree with positive rung number \(n\),
generated by
\(
\mathcal T=\{(T_i,\varphi_{i-1},S_{i-1},F_{i-1},\Theta_{i-1}):
1\leq i\leq n+1\},
\)
and suppose that \(T\) has the maximum property.  Assume explicitly the
following six assertions for every positive-rung ETT \(T'\) with the same
input \(k\)-triple, maximum property, and rung number \(s<n\), writing
\(\varphi'_s\) for its generating coloring and \(\mathcal T'\) for its
certified series:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \(V(T')\) is elementary with respect to \(\varphi'_s\).
\item Under every proper \((T'_{s+1},D'_s,\varphi'_s)\)-stable coloring
leaving precisely \(e_0\) uncolored, \(T'_{s+1}\) is interchangeable for
any pair of colors of which at least one is missing on \(T'_{s+1}\).
\item For each PE slot \(a\leq s\) with supporting vertex \(u_a\) and
\(m(u_a)=b\), every proper \((T'_a,D'_a,\varphi'_a)\)-stable coloring
leaving precisely \(e_0\) uncolored is
\((T'(u_a)-u_a,D'_{b-1},\varphi'_{b-1})\)-stable.  When \(b>1\), it is
also \((T'_{b-1},D'_{b-1},\varphi'_{b-1})\)-stable.  Moreover, the stored
color pairs at two distinct PE slots with distinct supporting vertices
and the same first-appearance index are disjoint.
\item If the terminal slot is PE with supporting vertex \(u_s\) and
stored colors \(\gamma'_s,\delta'_s\), then under every proper
\((T'_s,D'_s,\varphi'_s)\)-stable coloring leaving precisely \(e_0\)
uncolored, the \((\gamma'_s,\delta'_s)\)-component through \(u_s\)
contains exactly the one vertex \(u_s\) of \(T'_s\).
\item If \(\sigma\) is any proper \((T'_s,D'_s,\varphi'_s)\)-stable
coloring leaving precisely \(e_0\) uncolored, \(\delta\) is defective on
\(T'_s\) under \(\sigma\), and \(u\) is a nonminimum vertex of
\(I[\partial_{\sigma,\delta}(T'_s)]\), then
\(u\preceq_{T'}u_a\) for every PE supporting vertex or SE extension
vertex \(u_a\) with \(m(u)\leq a\leq s\).
\item Every proper \((T'_s,D'_s,\varphi'_s)\)-stable coloring leaving
precisely \(e_0\) uncolored is a \(\varphi'_s\bmod T'_s\) coloring.
\end{enumerate}
Then assertion (iii) holds at rung \(n\):

for every \(1\leq i\leq n\), if \(\Theta_i=\mathrm{PE}\) has supporting
vertex \(v_i\) with \(m(v_i)=j\), every proper
\((T_i,D_i,\varphi_i)\)-stable coloring \(\sigma_i\) leaving precisely
\(e_0\) uncolored is
\((T(v_i)-v_i,D_{j-1},\varphi_{j-1})\)-stable.  If \(j>1\), it is also
\((T_{j-1},D_{j-1},\varphi_{j-1})\)-stable.
Furthermore, if two distinct PE slots \(i,h\leq n\) have distinct
supporting vertices and \(m(v_i)=m(v_h)\), then
\(S_i\cap S_h=\varnothing\).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The universal result \noderef{TechnicalTheoremBase} supplies the two
nonvacuous assertions at rung zero directly from the certified initial
tuple; there are no positive PE slots for assertion (iii) at that rung.
For every \(s\) with \(1\leq s<n\), apply
\noderef{CertifiedETTRestriction} with target rung \(r=s\).  It returns
an actual ETT on the original input, with rung exactly \(s\), displayed
tree exactly the original slot \(T_{s+1}\), generating coloring exactly
\(\varphi_s\), and inherited maximum property.  Its pointwise equalities
preserve every retained tree and data slot, its computed-set equalities
preserve every \(D_a\), and its heterogeneous equalities transport all
retained step certificates.  The explicit local lower-rung hypothesis
therefore supplies assertions (i)--(vi) for this certified restriction.

Apply this observation first to the stability assertion with \(i<n\), and
then to a pair of PE indices whose larger index is below \(n\).  In either
case lower-rung assertion (iii), including its conditional predecessor
clause, is literally the desired assertion: the
equalities returned by \noderef{CertifiedETTRestriction} do not change any of the relevant
trees, colorings, \(S\)-sets, or their order.  Thus it remains only to
prove the stability conclusion and its conditional predecessor consequence
when the current PE index is \(i=n\).  This is
the manuscript's reduction to the terminal slot.

Let \(v_n\) be the terminal supporting vertex, put \(m(v_n)=j\), and let
\(\sigma_n\) be proper, leave exactly \(e_0\) uncolored, and be
\((T_n,D_n,\varphi_n)\)-stable.  Write
\[
                       T^*=T(v_n)-v_n.
\]
The set of vertices of \(T^*\) is exactly
\noderef{TreeVerticesBefore}; stability on it means
\noderef{StableColoringBeforeVertex}.

Repeatedly apply the first inclusion of
\noderef{HkuMissingStoredMonotone} from slot \(j-1\) through slot \(n\),
and its second inclusion whenever the tree is enlarged.  With
\(D_t=\noderef{TashkinovStoredColors}(\mathcal T,t)\), this gives
\[
 \overline\varphi_{j-1}(T_j)\cup D_{j-1}
 \subseteq
 \overline\varphi_n(T_n)\cup D_n.                              \tag{1}
\]
In particular \(D_{j-1}\) is contained in the right-hand side.  The
step-at-index field of \noderef{TashkinovSeries} supplies the terminal PE
certificate.  Its support and ordered initial-segment clauses in
\noderef{TashkinovSeriesStep}, expanded using
\noderef{TreeSequenceInitialSegment}, put \(v_n\) in \(T_n\) and imply that
every vertex preceding \(v_n\) in the final display also belongs to
\(T_n\).  Thus \(V(T^*)\subseteq V(T_n)\).

Here is the missing-color restriction explicitly.  If
\(c\in\overline\varphi_n(T^*)\), then the union definition
\noderef{TreeMissingColors} supplies a vertex \(w\in V(T^*)\) at which
\(c\) is missing.  The displayed inclusion transports this same witness to
\(V(T_n)\), so \(c\in\overline\varphi_n(T_n)\).  Likewise, by the
vertex-witness definition \noderef{TreeIncidentEdge}, an edge incident with
\(V(T^*)\) is incident with \(V(T_n)\), using the same vertex witness.
Therefore the vertex clause of the given
\noderef{StableColoring} restricts to \(T^*\); for its edge clause, a
controlling color lies either in \(\overline\varphi_n(T^*)\), hence in
\(\overline\varphi_n(T_n)\) by the witness just transported, or in
\(D_{j-1}\), hence in the right-hand side of (1).  The given edge clause
then fixes that edge.  Consequently \(\sigma_n\) is
\((T^*,D_{j-1},\varphi_n)\)-stable.
It remains to prove that \(\varphi_n\) is
\((T^*,D_{j-1},\varphi_{j-1})\)-stable.                         \tag{2}
Indeed, once (2) holds, the vertexwise missing-set equalities in the two
stability relations compose.  If an edge \(f\) incident with \(T^*\) has
\(\varphi_{j-1}(f)\) in the controlling missing-color union, (2) first
fixes its value under \(\varphi_n\); equality of the missing sets then
puts that same color in the controlling union for the first relation, which
fixes its value under \(\sigma_n\).  These are exactly the two clauses of
the required stability relation.

Suppose first that \(j=n\).  The step-at-index field of
\noderef{TashkinovSeries} supplies the terminal certificate; eliminating
its PE constructor in \noderef{TashkinovSeriesStep} gives its
maximum-defective selected coloring \(\pi_{n-1}\), its second coloring
\(\pi'_{n-1}\), and colors \(\gamma_n,\delta_n\).  It states that \(\pi_{n-1}\) is
\((T_n,D_{n-1},\varphi_{n-1})\)-stable, that \(\pi'_{n-1}\) is stable from
\(\pi_{n-1}\) with control \(D_{n-1}\cup\{\delta_n\}\), and that
\(\varphi_n\) is obtained by switching the
\((\gamma_n,\delta_n)\)-component through \(v_n\).  Let \(R\) be the path
from its certified \noderef{TreeExit} witness.  By
\noderef{TreeExitPath}, \(R\) runs from a vertex outside \(T_n\) to \(v_n\)
and has no other vertex of \(T_n\).

We verify that \(R\) is the whole component, rather than infer this from
the exit-path definition.  The rule \noderef{ParallelExtensionColor}
makes \(\gamma_n\) missing at \(v_n\) under \(\pi_{n-1}\), and the vertex
clause of \noderef{StableColoring} makes it missing there under
\(\pi'_{n-1}\) as well.  The selected edge \(f_n\) in
\noderef{MaximumDefectiveChoice} is incident with \(v_n\) and has
\(\pi_{n-1}(f_n)=\delta_n\).  Because \(\delta_n\) belongs to the enlarged
control set, the edge clause of the same stability relation gives
\(\pi'_{n-1}(f_n)=\delta_n\).  Properness as defined in
\noderef{IsProperEdgeColoring}, together with the missingness of
\(\gamma_n\), therefore makes \(f_n\) the unique two-color incidence at
\(v_n\); in particular \(\gamma_n\ne\delta_n\), and the tree end of \(R\)
has degree one.  At the outside end,
\noderef{TreeExitPath} makes one of the two colors missing, while the path
edge incident there has the other color; properness again makes that the
unique two-color incidence.  By the adjacency definition
\noderef{TwoColorIncidenceGraph}, every internal node of \(R\) already has
its two path neighbors.  If the connected support in
\noderef{TwoColorComponent} contained a node outside \(R\), a shortest route
from that node to \(R\) would add a third neighbor at an internal path node,
contradicting
\noderef{TwoColorIncidenceDegreeAtMostTwo}, or a second neighbor at an
endpoint, contradicting the endpoint's just-proved total degree one.  Hence
\(R\) is the entire component, and it meets \(T_n\) only in \(v_n\).

By \noderef{TwoColorComponentSwap}, the switch consequently changes no
edge incident with \(T^*\): by \noderef{TwoColorIncidenceGraph} and
\noderef{TwoColorComponent}, if a component edge were incident with a
vertex \(w\) of \(T^*\), the corresponding adjacency would put the
vertex-node \(w\) in the same connected support, contrary to its intersection
with \(T_n\).  It therefore changes no missing set at a vertex of \(T^*\).
Using the incidence witness of \noderef{TreeIncidentEdge}, restrict the two
certified stability relations to \(T^*\), use the two clauses of
\noderef{StableColoring}, drop the unused extra control color \(\delta_n\),
and compose the resulting edge-value and vertexwise missing-set equalities.
This proves (2) when \(j=n\).

Now suppose \(j<n\).  The same terminal certificate, supplied at its index
by \noderef{TashkinovSeries}, and \noderef{MaximumDefectiveChoice} say that
\(\pi_{n-1}\) is proper, leaves
exactly \(e_0\) uncolored, and is
\((T_n,D_{n-1},\varphi_{n-1})\)-stable.  Because \(T_{n-1}\) is an ordered
initial segment of \(T_n\) by \noderef{TashkinovSeriesStep} and
\noderef{TreeSequenceInitialSegment}, this stability restricts to
\(T_{n-1}\).  The incidence witness in \noderef{TreeIncidentEdge} makes
every edge incident with \(T_{n-1}\) incident with \(T_n\).  If a color is
missing on \(T_{n-1}\), \noderef{TreeMissingColors} supplies a vertex
witness in \(V(T_{n-1})\), and the initial-segment inclusion makes the same
vertex a witness that it is missing on \(T_n\).

We now verify the two boundary hypotheses of lower-rung assertion (v) using
exact edge identities.  The choice certificate in
\noderef{MaximumDefectiveChoice} gives a defective vertex \(v_n\), a
selected defective boundary identity \(f_n\), an outside endpoint \(u_n\),
and
\[
 \operatorname{ends}(f_n)=\{v_n,u_n\},\qquad
 \pi_{n-1}(f_n)=\delta_n.                                      \tag{3}
\]
Expanding \noderef{DefectiveEdge} and
\noderef{BoundaryColorEdges}, the selected color \(\delta_n\) is defective
on \(T_n\).  Hence \noderef{DefectiveColor} supplies a second exact edge
identity \(g\ne f_n\) in the \(\delta_n\)-colored boundary of \(T_n\).
By \noderef{BoundaryColorEdges} and \noderef{BoundaryEdges}, write its
ends as \(\{w,x\}\), with \(w\in V(T_n)\), \(x\notin V(T_n)\), and
\(\pi_{n-1}(g)=\delta_n\).  We have \(w\ne v_n\): otherwise the distinct
identities \(f_n\) and \(g\) share \(v_n\), so they are adjacent in the
sense of \noderef{AdjacentEdges}, yet have the same color, contrary to the
properness condition \noderef{IsProperEdgeColoring}.

Since \(\delta_n\) is defective and \(g\) is a
\(\delta_n\)-colored boundary identity, \noderef{DefectiveEdge} makes
\(g\) defective, and \noderef{DefectiveVertex} makes its tree endpoint
\(w\) a defective vertex of \(T_n\).  Apply the maximality clause of
\noderef{MaximumDefectiveChoice} to the same admissible coloring
\(\pi_{n-1}\).  It gives \(w=v_n\) or \(w\prec v_n\); the first alternative
was just excluded, so \(w\prec v_n\).

Now use the first-appearance hypothesis rather than mere set inclusion.
By the least-index definition \noderef{ETTFirstAppearanceIndex},
\(m(v_n)=j\) puts \(v_n\) in the complete stage \(T_j\).  Since \(j<n\),
the entrywise prefix relation \noderef{TreeSequenceInitialSegment} along
the ladder puts \(T_j\) inside \(T_{n-1}\).  Moreover, because that ordered
prefix already contains \(v_n\), every final-display vertex before
\(v_n\), in particular \(w\), also lies in \(T_j\) and hence in
\(T_{n-1}\).  On the other hand, (3) gives
\(u_n\notin V(T_n)\), and the boundary description of \(g\) gives
\(x\notin V(T_n)\); therefore neither outside endpoint lies in the smaller
\(T_{n-1}\).  Thus \(f_n\) and \(g\) are two distinct
\(\delta_n\)-colored identities in the boundary of \(T_{n-1}\), by
\noderef{BoundaryColorEdges} and \noderef{BoundaryEdges}.  Consequently
\(\delta_n\) is defective there by \noderef{DefectiveColor}.  Both
\(v_n\) and \(w\) belong to
\(I[\partial_{\pi_{n-1},\delta_n}(T_{n-1})]\) by
\noderef{BoundaryColorVertex}, and \(w\prec v_n\); hence \(w\) is the
explicit witness that \(v_n\) is nonminimum in that set.

All hypotheses of local lower-rung assertion (v) at rung \(n-1\) are now
verified for \(\pi_{n-1},\delta_n,v_n\).  For every PE supporting vertex
\(v_h\), \(j\leq h\leq n-1\), it gives
\[
                              v_n\preceq v_h.                  \tag{4}
\]

No vertex \(w\prec v_n\) in \(T^*\) can consequently be used as a PE
support between slots \(j\) and \(n\).  Applying
\noderef{HkuUnsupportedVertexColors} to each such \(w\), interval by
interval, gives
\[
              \overline\varphi_{j-1}(w)=\overline\varphi_n(w)
              \qquad(w\in V(T^*)).                            \tag{5}
\]
For the edge clause, let \(f\) be incident with \(T^*\), let
\(\varphi_{j-1}(f)=c\), and suppose
\(c\in\overline\varphi_{j-1}(T^*)\cup D_{j-1}\).
The interval assertion \noderef{SameColorSeriesPreserved} says either
\(\varphi_n(f)=\varphi_{j-1}(f)\), or \(f\) is the connecting edge of a PE
in the interval.  In the exceptional alternative its unique endpoint in
the current tree is that PE's supporting vertex.  Since \(f\) is incident
with \(T^*\), that supporting vertex would precede \(v_n\), contradicting
(4); equality with \(v_n\) is also impossible because \(T^*\) excludes
\(v_n\).  Thus only the equality alternative occurs:
\[
                         \varphi_n(f)=\varphi_{j-1}(f).         \tag{6}
\]
Equations (5) and (6) are precisely the two clauses of (2).  Transitivity
of those same edge-value and missing-set equalities now proves, for every
value of \(j\), that
\(\sigma_n\) is stable on \(T^*\) relative to
\((D_{j-1},\varphi_{j-1})\).  This is the unconditional before-support
conclusion.

For the separate predecessor-stage conclusion, assume \(j>1\).  This
positivity is essential: \(T_{j-1}\) is now a completed ladder stage, not
the auxiliary one-edge seed \(T_0\).  The minimum in
\noderef{ETTFirstAppearanceIndex} ranges over the complete stages
\(T_1,\ldots,T_n,T\); hence \(m(v_n)=j\) says both that \(v_n\in V(T_j)\)
and that \(v_n\notin V(T_{j-1})\).  The step-at-index field of
\noderef{TashkinovSeries} supplies the certificate at the predecessor index.
Its ordered-prefix clause in \noderef{TashkinovSeriesStep}, expanded with
\noderef{TreeSequenceInitialSegment}, \(T_{j-1}\) is an entrywise initial
segment of \(T_j\).  Fix \(w\in V(T_{j-1})\), choose its displayed index
there using \noderef{TreeSequenceVertexSet}, and transport that index to
\(T_j\).  Choose also the displayed index of \(v_n\) in \(T_j\).  If the latter index were no larger than the
length of \(T_{j-1}\), entrywise agreement would put \(v_n\) in
\(V(T_{j-1})\), contrary to minimality.  Thus every index representing a
vertex of \(T_{j-1}\) is strictly smaller than the index of \(v_n\).
The vertex-injectivity field of \noderef{TreeSequence} makes these displayed
positions unambiguous.  Finally the successive series-step prefixes, followed
by the final ordered containment in \noderef{ExtendedTashkinovTree}, carry
\(T_j\) into the final display \(T\) while preserving both indices, so
\noderef{TreeVertexOrder} gives \(w\prec_Tv_n\).  Since \(w\) was
arbitrary, \(V(T_{j-1})\subseteq V(T^*)\).  By
\noderef{TreeIncidentEdge}, the same endpoint witness makes every edge
incident with \(T_{j-1}\) incident with \(T^*\).  Restrict that edge clause
and the vertexwise missing-set clause already proved on \(T^*\) to this
subset (transporting a missing color by its vertex witness, exactly as
above).  This proves
\((T_{j-1},D_{j-1},\varphi_{j-1})\)-stability when \(j>1\), and makes no
claim at \(j=1\).

It remains to prove stored-pair disjointness.  Let \(v_h\ne v_n\) be an
earlier PE support with \(m(v_h)=m(v_n)\).  The PE constructor gives
\[
 S_h=\{\delta_h,\gamma_h\},\qquad
 S_n=\{\delta_n,\gamma_n\}.                                   \tag{7}
\]
We show that both colors of \(S_h\) occur on internal edges at \(v_n\)
under \(\varphi_n\).

In the second stable coloring used at slot \(h\), let \(P\) and \(Q\) be the
connected supports of \noderef{TwoColorComponent} through \(v_h\) and
\(v_n\), respectively, in the incidence graph
\noderef{TwoColorIncidenceGraph}.  The step-at-index field of
\noderef{TashkinovSeries} supplies the certificate at slot \(h\), whose PE
constructor in \noderef{TashkinovSeriesStep} puts its support \(v_h\) in
\(T_h\).
Therefore the least-index definition
\noderef{ETTFirstAppearanceIndex} gives \(m(v_h)\leq h\).  Since
\(m(v_n)=m(v_h)\), \(v_n\) first appears no later than that same stage, and
the entrywise ladder prefixes from that same step certificate, as defined
by \noderef{TreeSequenceInitialSegment}, carry it into every subsequent
stage, in particular
\(v_n\in V(T_h)\).  The exit clause in the PE
constructor gives a path in the sense of \noderef{TreeExit} and
\noderef{TreeExitPath} from an outside vertex to \(v_h\), meeting \(T_h\)
only in \(v_h\).  The rule \noderef{ParallelExtensionColor} makes
\(\gamma_h\) missing at \(v_h\), while the selected connecting edge is
incident there with color \(\delta_h\); stability fixes this incidence in
the second coloring.  By the adjacency rule in
\noderef{TwoColorIncidenceGraph}, \(v_h\) therefore has degree one in the
two-color incidence graph.  At the outside end, \noderef{TreeExitPath} says one of
the two colors is missing and its last path edge has the other color, so
that end also has degree one.  All internal nodes of the path already have
their two path neighbors, and
\noderef{TwoColorIncidenceDegreeAtMostTwo} forbids any third neighbor.
Consequently the exit path exhausts the connected support
\noderef{TwoColorComponent}, namely the whole component \(P\), and \(P\)
meets \(T_h\) exactly in \(v_h\).  Since \(v_n\ne v_h\), this gives \(P\ne Q\);
\noderef{TwoColorComponentsDisjoint} makes them vertex-disjoint.  The
definition \noderef{TwoColorComponentSwap} of the switch to
\(\varphi_h\) changes only \(P\), so \(Q\) remains the component through
\(v_n\) under \(\varphi_h\).

The PE switch replaces the missing color \(\gamma_h\) at \(v_h\) by
\(\delta_h\), so \(\delta_h\) is missing on \(T_{h+1}\) under
\(\varphi_h\).  Local lower-rung assertion (ii) at rung \(h\), for the
closed stage \(T_{h+1}\), and the definition
\noderef{TreeInterchangeable}, says that the nontrivial path \(P\) is the
unique \((\gamma_h,\delta_h)\)-path component meeting that stage.

We next prove explicitly that the second pre-switch coloring
\(\pi'_{h-1}\) is closed on \(T_h\).  The step-at-index field of
\noderef{TashkinovSeries} supplies the slot-\(h\) certificate, and its
noninitial PE clause in \noderef{TashkinovSeriesStep} first says that
\(T_h\) is closed under
\(\varphi_{h-1}\) in the sense of \noderef{ColoredVertexSetClosed}.
That clause and \noderef{MaximumDefectiveChoice} also say that all three
colorings \(\varphi_{h-1},\pi_{h-1},\pi'_{h-1}\) are proper and leave the
same edge \(e_0\) uncolored, so \noderef{sc1} applies to each of the two
certified stability relations.  Let
\(b\) be a boundary-edge identity whose color under the selected coloring
\(\pi_{h-1}\) is a color \(c\) missing at a vertex of \(T_h\) under
\(\pi_{h-1}\).  By \noderef{BoundaryEdges}, \(b\) is incident with
\(T_h\).  The vertex clause of \noderef{StableColoring} makes \(c\)
missing at the same vertex under \(\varphi_{h-1}\).  Apply the reverse-color
disjunct of \noderef{sc1} to the certified stability from
\(\varphi_{h-1}\) to \(\pi_{h-1}\): since the later color
\(\pi_{h-1}(b)=c\) lies in the common tree-missing set, it gives
\(\varphi_{h-1}(b)=\pi_{h-1}(b)=c\), contrary to the stated closedness of
\(T_h\) under \(\varphi_{h-1}\).  Thus \(T_h\) is closed under
\(\pi_{h-1}\).  Repeat the same argument for the certified stability from
\(\pi_{h-1}\) to \(\pi'_{h-1}\): the vertex clause of
\noderef{StableColoring} puts any later boundary color which is missing on
the tree into the earlier tree-missing set, and the reverse-color disjunct
of \noderef{sc1} forces the same earlier color on that exact boundary
identity, contradicting the just-proved closedness under \(\pi_{h-1}\).
Hence \(T_h\) is closed under \(\pi'_{h-1}\), again exactly as defined in
\noderef{ColoredVertexSetClosed}.

Local lower-rung assertion (i) at rung \(h-1\), or
\noderef{TechnicalTheoremBase} when \(h=1\), says that \(T_h\) is
elementary under \(\varphi_{h-1}\) in the sense of
\noderef{ColoredVertexSetElementary}.  The vertex clauses of the two
\noderef{StableColoring} relations preserve each individual missing-color
set, so \(T_h\) is elementary under \(\pi'_{h-1}\).  Now \(\gamma_h\) is
missing at \(v_h\), while \(v_h\ne v_n\).  The disjointness of the two
missing sets at these distinct vertices therefore says that \(\gamma_h\)
is not missing at \(v_n\).  By \noderef{MissingColors}, nonmissing means
membership in the complementary set \noderef{PresentColors}; hence an
edge incident with \(v_n\) has color \(\gamma_h\) under
\(\pi'_{h-1}\).  By \noderef{MaximumDefectiveChoice}, the selected
connecting identity \(f_h\) is a boundary edge of \(T_h\) with color
\(\delta_h\) under \(\pi_{h-1}\); the edge clause of
\noderef{StableColoring} fixes that exact value under \(\pi'_{h-1}\).
If \(\delta_h\) were missing at any tree vertex, this exact
boundary identity would contradict \noderef{ColoredVertexSetClosed}.
Thus it is not missing at \(v_n\), and the same
\noderef{MissingColors}--\noderef{PresentColors} conversion supplies an
incident \(\delta_h\)-colored edge there.

By \noderef{TwoColorIncidenceGraph}, each of these two colored incident edge
identities is adjacent to the vertex-node \(v_n\), so the connected-support
definition \noderef{TwoColorComponent} puts both in the component \(Q\)
through \(v_n\).  Since the switch affects only the disjoint component \(P\), they
retain their colors under \(\varphi_h\).  Consequently \(Q\) has both
colored incidences at \(v_n\) under \(\varphi_h\) and is nonisolated.
Properness in \noderef{IsProperEdgeColoring}, the connected-support
interpretation in \noderef{TwoColorComponent}, and
\noderef{TwoColorIncidenceDegreeAtMostTwo} say that this finite nonisolated
two-color incidence support is a path or a cycle.  After the switch, \(P\) remains
a nontrivial path component meeting \(T_{h+1}\), whereas \(Q\) is the
distinct component through \(v_n\).  If \(Q\) were also a path, these
would be two distinct path components meeting \(T_{h+1}\), contrary to
the uniqueness clause in \noderef{TreeInterchangeable}.  Hence \(Q\) is a
two-color cycle.

Let \(r>h\) be the first slot which is not an RE.  Such an \(r\leq n\)
exists because the terminal slot is a PE.  The step-at-index field of
\noderef{TashkinovSeries} supplies the certificate at slot \(r\).  Its
priority field, together with the cycle support just obtained via
\noderef{TwoColorIncidenceGraph} and \noderef{TwoColorComponent}, lets the
cycle fields of \noderef{RevisitingExtensionCertificate} show that \(Q\) is contained in
\(G[T_r]\): if a node of \(Q\) still lay outside \(T_r\), the part of this
same cycle meeting \(T_h\) and leaving \(T_r\) would be an available RE
certificate at slot \(r\), contrary to the choice of \(r\) as non-RE.

We justify the required initial union one color at a time.  Fix
\(c\in S_h\).  If \(c\) is missing at some vertex of \(T_{h+1}\), the
vertex-union definition \noderef{TreeMissingColors} puts
\(c\in\overline\varphi_h(T_{h+1})\).  Otherwise unfold
\noderef{TashkinovStoredColors}: membership \(c\in S_h\) puts \(c\) in the
accumulated union \(\bigcup_{a\leq h}S_a\).  Also the slot-\(h\) certificate
provided by \noderef{TashkinovSeries} makes \(T_h\) an ordered initial segment
of \(T_{h+1}\) through \noderef{TashkinovSeriesStep} and
\noderef{TreeSequenceInitialSegment}.  Were \(c\) missing on \(T_h\),
\noderef{TreeMissingColors} would supply a vertex witness in \(T_h\), and
the initial-segment inclusion would transport it to \(T_{h+1}\), contrary
to the present case.  Thus \(c\notin\overline\varphi_h(T_h)\), so the
accumulated-union membership is not removed in the defining difference for
\(D_h\); hence \(c\in D_h\).  Thus in both cases
\[
                 S_h\subseteq
                 \overline\varphi_h(T_{h+1})\cup D_h.
\]
Repeated \noderef{HkuMissingStoredMonotone} carries these two colors into
\(\overline\varphi_{r-1}(T_r)\cup D_{r-1}\).  The containment of \(Q\) in
\(G[T_r]\) means by \noderef{InternalEdges} that every edge identity of
\(Q\) is internal to \(T_r\).  Hence the internal-edge conclusion of
\noderef{SameColorSeriesPreserved} fixes its color from \(\varphi_h\)
through \(\varphi_n\).  Thus \(Q\) is still a
\((\gamma_h,\delta_h)\)-cycle through \(v_n\) under \(\varphi_n\).  At
\(v_n\) it supplies two internal incident edge identities, one colored
\(\gamma_h\) and one colored \(\delta_h\).

Finally, the step-at-index field of \noderef{TashkinovSeries} supplies the
terminal PE certificate, which together with
\noderef{HkuParallelBoundaryStructure} identifies the connecting edge
incident with \(v_n\) as the unique \(\gamma_n\)-colored boundary edge,
and we now verify the other endpoint-color fact used here.  The PE rule
\noderef{ParallelExtensionColor} makes \(\gamma_n\) missing at \(v_n\)
before the switch, whereas the connecting edge has color \(\delta_n\)
there by \noderef{MaximumDefectiveChoice} and stability.  Expanding
\noderef{TwoColorComponentSwap}, the switch changes that unique incident
\(\delta_n\)-edge to \(\gamma_n\), and no incident edge becomes
\(\delta_n\); equivalently, the definition \noderef{MissingColors} puts
\(\delta_n\) in \(\overline\varphi_n(v_n)\).  Properness
therefore prevents either of the two internal edges at \(v_n\) from having
color \(\gamma_n\), and missingness prevents either from having color
\(\delta_n\).  Hence
\(\{\gamma_h,\delta_h\}\cap\{\gamma_n,\delta_n\}=\varnothing\).
Using the exact identities (7), this is \(S_h\cap S_n=\varnothing\).
Together with the lower-index reduction at the start, it proves the full
stored-pair assertion and completes part (iii).
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Lexicographic Step 1 exit-leg minimum]
Fix $G,k,e_0,n,q$.  Suppose that the family of Step 1 exit-leg
presentations of size $(n,q)$ is nonempty and that the terminal vertex
set of every member is elementary.  Then this family contains a Step 1
exit-leg minimum.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For a presentation $P$, let $d(P)$ be its divider count.  The set of
values $d(P)$ is a nonempty subset of $\mathbb N$, so it has a least
element $d_0$.  Restrict henceforth to presentations with divider count
$d_0$.

For such a presentation, assign secondary value zero when its second
color $b$ is missing on its terminal tree.  Otherwise, apply
\noderef{ElementaryClosedExitPairExtendsTriple} to its elementariness,
properness, missing and closed first color, and stored exit pair.  This
produces at least one third exit leg support-disjoint from the first two,
with distinct outside and tree ends and an exact retained $b$-colored
boundary identity.  The nonempty set of total lengths
$|P_1|+|P_2|+|P_3|$ therefore has a least element; assign one plus that
least length as the secondary value.  The secondary values among the
$d_0$-presentations again form a nonempty subset of $\mathbb N$, so
choose a presentation $P$ of least secondary value.

When the chosen secondary value is positive, choose a triple of least
total length.  Support-disjointness makes its three tree ends distinct.
Their unique positions in the injective vertex list of
\noderef{TreeSequence} are linearly ordered.  Relabel the complete
path/end packages so that the tree ends occur in increasing order, and
retain the first two packages in $P$.  Relabeling preserves all pairwise
support-disjointness conditions, outside-end inequalities, and total
length.  If this relabeled first pair admitted a shorter qualifying
third leg, it would itself define a same-divider presentation with
smaller secondary value, contradicting the global secondary choice;
hence it retains the selected least value.  The exact third boundary identity is the one furnished by
\noderef{ElementaryClosedExitPairExtendsTriple}.  Thus the asserted
three-leg alternative is available whenever $b$ is not missing.

We verify the two universal minimality certificates.  First take the
data in the primary comparison and suppose its two exit legs exist.
The replacement input, ETT, and certified series are actual fields of
\noderef{CorrespondingExtendedTashkinovTree}; its rung agreement and the
selected presentation's rung equality give rung number $n$.  The
comparison hypotheses give maximum property, properness,
exact-one-uncoloredness, its own large bound, and the genuine terminal
minus-closed certificate.  Its divider count is smaller than $d_0$ and
hence smaller than $q$.  For each exit path,
\noderef{ClosedColorExitPathBoundaryEdge} supplies its exact
second-color boundary identity.  The two tree ends are distinct, since
a common end would be a vertex-node in both supports, and they can be
strictly ordered as above, exchanging the two complete packages if
necessary.  These data form a Step 1 exit-leg presentation with divider
count smaller than $d_0$, contradicting the definition of $d_0$.

For the secondary comparison, the same normalization makes its data a
presentation with divider count $d_0$.  If its second color is missing,
its secondary value is zero, strictly below the positive selected value.
If it is not missing and the displayed third path gives a qualifying
triple of smaller total length, the least qualifying length for that
presentation is at most the displayed length.  Its secondary value is
therefore also smaller than the selected one.  Both alternatives
contradict the choice of $P$.

Finally equip $P$ with its assumed elementariness, the primary
certificate, and either the missing-color alternative or the ordered
least triple and secondary certificate just constructed.  By
\noderef{Step1ExitLegMinimum}, this is a Step 1 exit-leg minimum.
\end{proof}

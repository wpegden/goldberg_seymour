\begin{lemma}[Claim 4.3(b1): stability of the restoration output]
Let \(\mathcal R\) be a restoration trace satisfying
\noderef{ExtensionBaseRestorationInvariant}, with initial coloring
\(\sigma_n\) and output coloring \(\varrho_1\).  Let \(C\) be a set of
control colors, assume \(v_n\in V(T_j)\), and suppose that \(\sigma_n\) is
\[
 (T(v_n)-v_n,C,\varphi_{j-1})\text{-stable}
\]
in the sense of \noderef{StableColoringBeforeVertex}.  Then
\(\varrho_1\) is
\((T(v_n)-v_n,C,\varphi_{j-1})\)-stable as well.  In the extension-base
application, \(C=D_{j-1}\), and the displayed premise is the current-rung
part (iii) supplied by \noderef{ExtensionRules}.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Write the trace colorings as
\(\sigma^0,\sigma^1,\ldots,\sigma^r\), where the initial-coloring and
output clauses of \noderef{ExtensionBaseRestorationTrace} give
\(\sigma^0=\sigma_n\) and \(\sigma^r=\varrho_1\).  Denote the reference
stored tree in \noderef{ExtensionBaseRestorationInvariant} by
\(T_j=\mathcal T[j_{\rm slot}]\).

We first verify the tree-membership fact needed at every switch.  The
series certificate \noderef{TashkinovSeries} supplies an Algorithm 3.1
step certificate at every index.  Starting at the positive stage which
stores \(T_j\), induct on the number of remaining stages up to the
penultimate series tree.  At a successor stage the certificate cannot be
the initial constructor, since its index is positive.  Each of the three
remaining constructors in \noderef{TashkinovSeriesStep} has an
\({\tt orderedExtension}\) field saying that the preceding stored tree is
an ordered initial segment of the succeeding stored tree.  At the base
stage the initial-segment relation is reflexive.  At the induction step,
compose the two relations: by
\noderef{TreeSequenceInitialSegment}, their length inequalities compose,
and substituting their entrywise vertex and edge equalities gives the
entrywise equalities for the composite.  Hence \(T_j\) is an ordered
initial segment of the penultimate series tree.  The
\({\tt abovePenultimate}\) clause of
\noderef{ExtendedTashkinovTree} says that this penultimate tree is an
ordered initial segment of the displayed tree \(T\).  One more composition
therefore gives
\[
                         T_j\preceq T.                         \tag{1}
\]

Let \(w\in\noderef{TreeVerticesBefore}(T,v_n)\).  Expanding that
definition, \noderef{TreeVertexOrder}, and
\noderef{TreeSequenceVertexSet}, there are displayed indices \(p_w<p_n\)
of \(T\) containing \(w\) and \(v_n\), respectively.  The hypothesis
\(v_n\in V(T_j)\) supplies an index \(q_n\) of \(T_j\) containing \(v_n\).
By (1) and the entrywise clause of
\noderef{TreeSequenceInitialSegment}, the image of \(q_n\) at the same
natural position in \(T\) also contains \(v_n\).  Displayed vertices are
pairwise distinct by \noderef{TreeSequence}, so \(p_n\) is this image of
\(q_n\).  Thus \(p_w<q_n\) as natural positions; in particular \(p_w\)
lies inside the length of \(T_j\).  The entrywise clause of (1) then
identifies the \(p_w\)-entry of \(T_j\) with \(w\).  Consequently
\[
        w\in V(T_j)\quad\hbox{for every }w\in V(T(v_n)-v_n).   \tag{2}
\]

Fix a trace step \(a<r\), and let
\[
 K_a=P_{v_{t_a}}(\gamma_{t_a},\delta_{t_a},\sigma^a)
\]
be the incidence-graph component defined by
\noderef{TwoColorComponent}.  The step clause of
\noderef{ExtensionBaseRestorationInvariant} says that
\(T_j\preceq\mathcal T[t_a]\), that \(v_n\prec_Tv_{t_a}\), and that the
vertex-node intersection of \(K_a\) with
\(V(\mathcal T[t_a])\) is exactly \(\{v_{t_a}\}\).  By (2) and the
initial-segment definition, \(w\) belongs to
\(V(\mathcal T[t_a])\).  Moreover, the index inequalities defining
\noderef{TreeVertexOrder} give
\(w\prec_Tv_n\prec_Tv_{t_a}\), so the vertex-injectivity clause of
\noderef{TreeSequence} gives \(w\ne v_{t_a}\).  The exact-intersection
clause of the invariant now implies
\[
                         \operatorname{inl}(w)\notin K_a.      \tag{3}
\]

Let \(f\) be any edge identity incident with \(w\), in the sense of
\noderef{Incident}.  Suppose \(\operatorname{inr}(f)\in K_a\).  By the
connected-component definition in \noderef{TwoColorComponent}, there is
a walk in the two-color incidence graph from the root vertex-node
\(\operatorname{inl}(v_{t_a})\) to the edge-node
\(\operatorname{inr}(f)\).  These two nodes have different sum types, so
the walk has positive length.  Its last adjacency and
\noderef{TwoColorIncidenceGraph} show that
\(\sigma^a(f)\) is one of \(\gamma_{t_a},\delta_{t_a}\).  Since \(f\) is
incident with \(w\), that same definition makes
\(\operatorname{inl}(w)\) adjacent to \(\operatorname{inr}(f)\).
Connected components are closed under adjacency, so
\(\operatorname{inl}(w)\in K_a\), contradicting (3).  Therefore
\(\operatorname{inr}(f)\notin K_a\).

The \({\tt transition}\) field of
\noderef{ExtensionBaseRestorationTrace} identifies
\(\sigma^{a+1}\) with the exact component switch of \(\sigma^a\) on
\(K_a\).  By \noderef{TwoColorComponentSwap}, every edge-node outside
that component keeps its coloring value.  Hence
\[
 \sigma^{a+1}(f)=\sigma^a(f)
 \quad\text{whenever }w\in V(T(v_n)-v_n)\text{ and }f\text{ is incident
 with }w.                                                       \tag{4}
\]

Inducting on \(a=0,\ldots,r-1\) in (4), with the zero-step case given
directly by the initial-coloring field of the trace, gives
\[
 \varrho_1(f)=\sigma_n(f)
 \quad\text{for every edge identity }f\text{ incident with a vertex of
 }V(T(v_n)-v_n).                                               \tag{5}
\]

For each such vertex \(w\), the definition
\noderef{PresentColors} says that a color is present exactly when some
incident edge identity has that color.  Applying (5) to every incident
edge identity in both directions therefore makes the present-color sets
under \(\varrho_1\) and \(\sigma_n\) equal.  Since
\noderef{MissingColors} defines the missing-color set as the complement
of the present-color set in the common finite color set, it follows that
\[
 \overline{\varrho}_1(w)=\overline{\sigma}_n(w)
 \quad(w\in V(T(v_n)-v_n)).                                  \tag{6}
\]

It remains to check the two clauses of
\noderef{StableColoringBeforeVertex}.  For its missing-set clause, the
assumed stability gives
\(\overline{\sigma}_n(w)=\overline{\varphi}_{j-1}(w)\) for every
\(w\in V(T(v_n)-v_n)\); composing this equality with (6) gives the
required equality for \(\varrho_1\).  For its edge clause, let \(f\) be
incident with this vertex set and suppose its
\(\varphi_{j-1}\)-color belongs to the union of the
\(\varphi_{j-1}\)-missing colors on that set with \(C\).  The edge clause
of the assumed stability gives
\(\sigma_n(f)=\varphi_{j-1}(f)\), while (5) gives
\(\varrho_1(f)=\sigma_n(f)\).  Their composition is exactly the required
edge equality.  Both defining clauses hold, so \(\varrho_1\) is
\((T(v_n)-v_n,C,\varphi_{j-1})\)-stable, proving (b1).
\end{proof}

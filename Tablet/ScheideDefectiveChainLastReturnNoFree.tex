\begin{lemma}[Defective-chain structure, part (d)]
In the notation of part (c), the last returning vertex $v_2$ misses no free
color:
\[
 \overline\varphi(v_2)\cap\mathcal F(T,e_0,\varphi)=\varnothing.
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Let $R=v_2Pz$ be the terminal subpath.  The last-return condition in
\noderef{ScheideDepartureReturn} says that $R$ meets $V(T)$ only at $v_2$.
Consequently it contains exactly one boundary edge identity.  Suppose for
a contradiction that the free color $\beta$ is missing at $v_2$.  Since
$v_2$ occurs after the first departure, $v_2\ne u$; elementariness from
\noderef{ScheideGlobalMaximumOrder} and the fact that $\gamma$ is missing
at $u$ give $\beta\ne\gamma$.

Apply the outside-tree swap of \noderef{OutsideTreeColorSwap} to
$\beta,\gamma$.  Both colors are missing somewhere on the closed set
$V(T)$, so \noderef{ScheideGlobalMaximumBoundaryFacts} says that neither
has a boundary edge.  The outside swap therefore changes no edge identity
incident with $V(T)$, preserves properness and the unique uncolored edge,
and changes neither the Tashkinov sequence nor its free and defective color
sets.  Along $R$, it changes the outside portions of the old
$(\alpha,\gamma)$-path so that $R$ becomes the full
$(\alpha,\beta)$-chain from $v_2$.

Switch this full chain.  Properness is preserved by
\noderef{TwoColorComponentSwapProper}.  No tree edge changes, because
$\alpha$ and $\beta$ are unused on the tree by
\noderef{ScheideGlobalMaximumUnused}; at tree vertices the only
missing-color change is that $\alpha$ replaces $\beta$ at $v_2$.
Therefore the displayed sequence is still Tashkinov.  The exact boundary
identity statement \noderef{ScheideDefectiveChainBoundaryEdges} and
\noderef{ScheideGlobalMaximumBoundaryFacts} give at least three original
$\alpha$-boundary edges.  Only the single boundary edge of $R$ changes, so
at least two retain color $\alpha$.  Since $\alpha$ is now missing at
$v_2$, appending either retained edge and its outside endpoint contradicts
global maximality.  Thus no such $\beta$ exists.
\end{proof}

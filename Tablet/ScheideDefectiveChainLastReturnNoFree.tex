\begin{lemma}[Defective-chain structure, part (d)]
Let $(T,e_0,\varphi)$ be a globally maximum triple for $G$.  Let
$\alpha\in\mathcal D(V(T),\varphi)$ be defective, let
$\gamma\in\mathcal F(T,e_0,\varphi)$ be free and missing at a vertex
$u\in V(T)$, and let $z\notin V(T)$.  Let the supplied incidence walk $P$
from $u$ to $z$ be a full defective-chain path for
$(T,\varphi,\alpha,\gamma,u,z)$.  If the supplied indices $i_1<i_2$ and
vertices $v_1,v_2$ satisfy the first-departure/last-return predicate for
$(T,\varphi,\alpha,\gamma,u,z,P)$, then the last returning vertex $v_2$
misses no free color:
\[
 \overline\varphi(v_2)\cap\mathcal F(T,e_0,\varphi)=\varnothing.
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Let $R=v_2Pz$ be the terminal subpath.  The last-return condition in
\noderef{ScheideDepartureReturn} says that $R$ meets $V(T)$ only at $v_2$.
Consequently it contains exactly one boundary edge identity.  Suppose for
a contradiction that the free color $\beta$ is missing at $v_2$.  Since
$v_2$ occurs after the first departure, $v_2\ne u$; elementariness from
\noderef{ScheideGlobalMaximumOrder} and the fact that $\gamma$ is missing
at $u$ give $\beta\ne\gamma$.

Apply the outside-tree swap of \noderef{OutsideTreeColorSwap} to
$\beta,\gamma$.  Both colors are missing somewhere on the closed set
$V(T)$, so \noderef{ScheideGlobalMaximumBoundaryFacts} says that neither
has a boundary edge.  The outside swap therefore changes no edge identity
incident with $V(T)$ and changes neither the Tashkinov sequence nor its
free and defective color sets.  It also preserves the unique uncolored
edge, since the defining relabeling fixes the uncolored value.  To check
properness, which the $k$-triple clause of
\noderef{GlobalMaximumTriple} gives for $\varphi$, consider two adjacent
colored edges.  If both are incident with $V(T)$, both are fixed; if
neither is, both undergo the same transposition of the distinct colors
$\beta$ and $\gamma$.  In the remaining case their common endpoint is
outside $V(T)$ and the incident edge is a boundary edge.  Its color is
neither $\beta$ nor $\gamma$, by the preceding no-boundary-edge fact, so
the transposition on the other edge cannot create a new equality.  Thus
the outside swap is proper.  Along $R$, it changes the outside portions of the old
$(\alpha,\gamma)$-path so that $R$ becomes the full
$(\alpha,\beta)$-chain from $v_2$.

The two colors are distinct because $\alpha$ is present at every tree
vertex by the defective and missing-color clauses of
\noderef{ScheideGlobalMaximumBoundaryFacts}, while $\beta$ is missing at
$v_2$.  Switch this full chain.  Properness is preserved by
\noderef{TwoColorComponentSwapProper}.  No tree edge changes, because
$\alpha$ and $\beta$ are unused on the tree by
\noderef{ScheideGlobalMaximumUnused}; at tree vertices the only
missing-color change is that $\alpha$ replaces $\beta$ at $v_2$.
Therefore the displayed sequence is still Tashkinov.  The exact boundary
identity statement \noderef{ScheideDefectiveChainBoundaryEdges} and
\noderef{ScheideGlobalMaximumBoundaryFacts} give at least three original
$\alpha$-boundary edges.  Only the single boundary edge of $R$ changes, so
at least two retain color $\alpha$.  Since $\alpha$ is now missing at
$v_2$, appending either retained edge and its outside endpoint contradicts
global maximality.  Thus no such $\beta$ exists.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Scheide replacement-chain tail is external (5)]
For every defective $\delta$, the full
$(\gamma(\delta),\delta)$ incidence component recorded in the setup meets
$V(T)$ only at its initial vertex $v^0_\delta$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Fix a defective color $\delta$, and abbreviate
\[
 X=\noderef{TreeSequenceVertexSet}(T),\qquad
 i=\gammaIndex(\delta),\qquad \gamma=\gamma_i,
 \qquad v^0=v^0_\delta,\quad f^0=f^0_\delta,\quad u^0=u^0_\delta.
 \tag{1}
\]
All colorings below are functions of the type in
\noderef{PartialEdgeColoring}; in particular, every assertion that an
individual edge is fixed refers to its edge identity, not merely to its
endpoint pair.

We first unpack the statement-level data.  The definition
\noderef{ScheideDefectiveSetup} supplies a color $\alpha _0$ missing at
the first base vertex $y_0$, with $\alpha _0\ne\gamma_r$ for every block
index $r$.  It supplies a full simple $(\alpha _0,\delta)$ incidence path
\[
 P=P_{y_0}(\alpha _0,\delta,\varphi)
\]
from $y_0$ to an outside vertex $z_\delta$.  Its last tree vertex is
$v^0$, and the next two incidence nodes are the edge-node $f^0$ and the
vertex-node $u^0$.  Hence
\[
 \varphi(f^0)=\delta,\qquad
 \operatorname{ends}(f^0)=\{v^0,u^0\},\qquad u^0\notin X.     \tag{2}
\]
The same setup records the full simple $(\gamma,\delta)$ incidence path
\[
 P'=P_{v^0}(\gamma,\delta,\varphi)
\]
from $v^0$ to an outside end $z'_\delta$.  In both cases ``full'' has the
precise meaning in \noderef{ScheideDefectiveChainPath}: the support of the
recorded simple walk is exactly the relevant set
\noderef{TwoColorComponent}, including every edge-node identity.

Unpack \noderef{ScheideReducedConfiguration} and its inherited
\noderef{ScheideStructuralConfiguration}.  The ordered sequence consists
of the base
\[
 y_0,e_1,y_1,\ldots,e_{p-1},y_{p-1},\qquad p\geq2,
\]
followed, in increasing block index, by the two pairs
\[
 (f^1_{\gamma_r},u^1_{\gamma_r}),
 (f^2_{\gamma_r},u^2_{\gamma_r}).                            \tag{3}
\]
The position equations in that definition are exact, the colors
$\gamma_r$ are injectively indexed, each displayed block edge has color
$\gamma_r$ and attaches its block vertex to an earlier base vertex, a
base vertex misses $\gamma_r$, and every colored nonfirst base edge avoids
all block colors.  The configuration also contains a globally maximum
triple.  Expanding \noderef{GlobalMaximumTriple}, denote its
\noderef{KTriple} component by $K$.  Thus $\varphi$ is proper in the
sense of \noderef{IsProperEdgeColoring}, it satisfies
\noderef{IsOneEdgeUncolored}$(\varphi,e_0)$, $T$ satisfies
\noderef{TashkinovTree}$(G,e_0,\varphi,T)$, and
\[
 |X|=\noderef{TashkinovOrder}(G).                             \tag{4}
\]
By \noderef{ScheideGlobalMaximumOrder}, $|X|$ is odd and $X$ is
elementary and closed in the senses of
\noderef{ColoredVertexSetElementary} and
\noderef{ColoredVertexSetClosed}.

The setup puts $v^0$ in the finite exit set
\noderef{ScheideExitVertices}.  Therefore
\noderef{ScheideStructuralExitsInBase} puts $v^0$ in the base $Y$.
In particular, the exact positions in (3) and vertex injectivity in
\noderef{TreeSequence} separate $v^0$ from every block vertex.

Suppose, contrary to the claim, that the full component $P'$ contains a
tree vertex other than $v^0$.  Since its support is the full component,
any putative vertex in the statement occurs on the finite path $P'$.
Among all occurrences after the initial one whose vertex lies in $X$,
choose the last, call it $v_1$.  Simplicity gives
$v_1\ne v^0$.  The following two incidence nodes are an edge-node $f_1$
and a vertex-node $u_1$.  Lastness gives $u_1\notin X$, and
\noderef{TwoColorIncidenceGraph} together with
\noderef{Incident} gives
\[
 \operatorname{ends}(f_1)=\{v_1,u_1\}.                       \tag{5}
\]
The same full path, with $v_1$ as its last tree occurrence, witnesses the
predicate \noderef{ScheideExitVertex}: the incidence graph is unchanged
when its two named colors are interchanged, $\delta$ is defective, and
$\gamma$ is missing at the initial tree vertex $v^0$.  Thus $v_1$ is an
exit vertex.  Applying \noderef{ScheideStructuralExitsInBase} again puts
$v_1$ in $Y$.

The edge $f_1$ has color $\gamma$ or $\delta$ by
\noderef{TwoColorIncidenceGraph}.  It is a boundary identity of $X$ by
(5), lastness, and \noderef{BoundaryEdges}.  Since $\gamma$ is missing
at $v^0\in X$ and $X$ is closed, no $\gamma$-colored boundary edge can
exist.  Hence
\[
 \varphi(f_1)=\delta.                                        \tag{6}
\]
The exact-one clause of \noderef{ScheideReducedConfiguration} now gives
a unique block index $j$ such that $\gamma_j$ is missing at $v_1$.
Elementariness of $X$, the missingness of $\gamma$ at $v^0$, and
$v_1\ne v^0$ imply
\[
 \gamma_j\ne\gamma.                                         \tag{7}
\]
Injectivity of the block colors therefore gives $j\ne i$.

We also record the inequalities involving $\delta$.  Membership of
$\delta$ in \noderef{DefectiveColors} says that at least two individual
members of
\noderef{BoundaryColorEdges}$(G,\varphi,X,\delta)$ exist.  Choose one.
Closedness of $X$ says that its color is missing at no vertex of $X$.
Since \noderef{MissingColors} is the complement of
\noderef{PresentColors}, $\delta$ is present at every vertex of $X$.
It is consequently different from $\alpha _0$, $\gamma$, and
$\gamma_j$, which are missing respectively at $y_0$, $v^0$, and $v_1$.
Together with the setup and (7),
\[
 \alpha _0,\gamma,\gamma_j,\delta
 \quad\hbox{are pairwise distinct}.                          \tag{8}
\]

We now carry out the two recolorings independently.

For the first recoloring, both $\alpha _0$ and $\gamma$ are missing on
$X$.  Closedness and the definition \noderef{BoundaryColorEdges} give
\[
 \partial_{\varphi,\alpha _0}(X)=\varnothing,
 \qquad \partial_{\varphi,\gamma}(X)=\varnothing.            \tag{9}
\]
Set
\[
 \varphi_1=\noderef{OutsideTreeColorSwap}
   (T,\varphi,\alpha _0,\gamma).
\]
By that definition, every identity satisfying
\noderef{TreeIncidentEdge}$(T,f)$ is fixed; on every other identity the
two colors $\alpha _0,\gamma$ are transposed, while all other colored and
uncolored values are fixed.

We verify properness of $\varphi_1$.  Let $a,b$ be distinct adjacent
identities which are colored under $\varphi_1$.  The definition
\noderef{AdjacentEdges} supplies a common incident vertex.  If both
identities are incident with $X$, both colors are fixed.  If neither is,
the same injective transposition is applied to both old values.  Equality
of the new colors would in either case contradict properness of
$\varphi$.  If exactly one, say $a$, is incident with $X$, their common
vertex lies outside $X$, for otherwise $b$ would also be tree-incident.
Thus $a$ is a boundary edge by \noderef{BoundaryEdges}.  Equation (9)
says that the fixed color of $a$ is neither $\alpha _0$ nor $\gamma$.
Transposing those two values on $b$ cannot create equality unless the old
values were already equal.  This proves
\[
 \noderef{IsProperEdgeColoring}(G,\varphi_1).                 \tag{10}
\]
The outside swap fixes the uncolored value and maps every colored value
to a colored value.  Applying this to $e_0$ and then to every
$f\ne e_0$ in the exact clauses of $K$ proves
\[
 \noderef{IsOneEdgeUncolored}(\varphi_1,e_0).                 \tag{11}
\]

Let $R_0=v^0Pz_\delta$ be the terminal subpath of the recorded initial
path.  The successive-index and last-tree-vertex clauses of
\noderef{ScheideDefectiveSetup} give
\[
 V(R_0)\cap X=\{v^0\},                                      \tag{12}
\]
and its first two later nodes are exactly $f^0,u^0$.  The edge $f^0$ is
incident with $X$, so it is fixed by the outside swap and remains
$\delta$-colored by (2).  Every later edge of $R_0$ has both ends outside
$X$.  Its old color is $\alpha _0$ or $\delta$ by
\noderef{TwoColorIncidenceGraph}; properness forces successive edge
colors on the simple path to alternate.  Thus the outside swap changes
each later $\alpha _0$-edge to $\gamma$ and fixes each $\delta$-edge.
Consequently $R_0$ is a $(\gamma,\delta)$ walk under $\varphi_1$.

We check that $R_0$ is exactly the component which will be switched.  At
$v^0$, the color $\gamma$ is missing, while $f^0$ is the unique incident
$\delta$-edge by (10).  If $v^0\ne y_0$, the predecessor edge of $P$ at
$v^0$ has old color $\alpha _0$; it is tree-incident, is therefore fixed,
and is not an edge of the new $(\gamma,\delta)$ graph.  If $v^0=y_0$,
$\alpha _0$ is missing there and there is no predecessor.  Hence in both
cases $v^0$ has exactly one adjacency in the new component, through
$f^0$.  At every internal vertex-node of $R_0$, its two path edges have
the two different colors $\gamma,\delta$, so properness (10) excludes a
third incident edge of either color.  At each edge-node,
\noderef{TwoColorIncidenceGraph} permits only its two endpoint
incidences, already used by the path.

For the other endpoint, the original component containing $P$ starts at
$y_0$, where $\alpha _0$ is missing and $\delta$ is present.  Its degree
is therefore one.  The theorem
\noderef{TwoColorIncidenceDegreeAtMostTwo} bounds all degrees in this
finite component by two.  Since the full-path clause says that the simple
path $P$ has the entire component as support, following the unique unused
adjacency from $y_0$ cannot branch; a cycle reached from this degree-one
node would create a first junction of degree at least three.  It follows
that the other endpoint $z_\delta$ also has degree one.  At an edge with
both ends outside $X$, the new $\gamma,\delta$ status is exactly the old
$\alpha _0,\delta$ status.  At a boundary edge the value is fixed, and
(9) excludes both $\alpha _0$ and $\gamma$.  Thus $z_\delta$ still has
degree one in the new two-color graph.  We have checked both endpoints
and every internal incidence node, so no adjacency leaves $R_0$.  By
\noderef{TwoColorComponent},
\[
 \operatorname{supp}(R_0)=
 \noderef{TwoColorComponent}
   (G,\varphi_1,\gamma,\delta,\operatorname{inl}v^0).         \tag{13}
\]

Switch precisely this component and put
\[
 \varphi_2=\noderef{TwoColorComponentSwap}
   (G,\varphi_1,\gamma,\delta,\operatorname{inl}v^0).
\]
By (8), (10), and \noderef{TwoColorComponentSwapProper}, $\varphi_2$ is
proper.  The definition \noderef{TwoColorComponentSwap} again fixes an
uncolored value and maps colored values to colored values, so (11) gives
\[
 \noderef{IsOneEdgeUncolored}(\varphi_2,e_0).                 \tag{14}
\]
The first swap fixed every edge incident with $v^0$.  Equations
(12)--(13) say that the component switch changes exactly one incident
edge there, namely $f^0$, from $\delta$ to $\gamma$, and fixes all other
incident identities.  Therefore, using the definitions
\noderef{PresentColors} and \noderef{MissingColors},
\[
 \overline{\varphi_2}(v^0)
 =\operatorname{insert}\delta
   \bigl(\overline\varphi(v^0)\setminus\{\gamma\}\bigr).     \tag{15}
\]
In particular, $\delta$ is missing at $v^0$.  For every
$x\in X\setminus\{v^0\}$, (12)--(13) exclude both its vertex-node and
every incident edge-node from the switched component, so its present and
missing sets remain exactly those under $\varphi$.

The identity $f_1$ remains $\delta$-colored under $\varphi_2$.  The
outside swap fixes it because it is incident with $v_1\in X$.  If its
edge-node lay in (13), its incidence with $v_1$ would put the vertex-node
$v_1$ in that component, contradicting (12) and $v_1\ne v^0$.  Thus the
component switch also fixes its exact identity, and (6) yields
\[
 \varphi_2(f_1)=\delta.                                      \tag{16}
\]

For the independent second recoloring, both $\gamma$ and $\gamma_j$ are
missing on $X$, at $v^0$ and $v_1$.  Closedness gives
\[
 \partial_{\varphi,\gamma}(X)=\varnothing,
 \qquad \partial_{\varphi,\gamma_j}(X)=\varnothing.          \tag{17}
\]
Set
\[
 \varphi'_1=\noderef{OutsideTreeColorSwap}
   (T,\varphi,\gamma,\gamma_j).
\]
The same three-case adjacent-edge argument used for (10), now with (17),
proves that $\varphi'_1$ is proper: two tree-incident edges are fixed, two
non-tree-incident edges receive one injective transposition, and in the
mixed case the fixed boundary color is outside
$\{\gamma,\gamma_j\}$.  The colored/uncolored check used for (11) proves
that $\varphi'_1$ still leaves exactly $e_0$ uncolored.

Let $R_1=v_1P'z'_\delta$ be the terminal suffix of the full replacement
path.  By the choice of $v_1$,
\[
 V(R_1)\cap X=\{v_1\},                                      \tag{18}
\]
and its first later edge and vertex are precisely $f_1,u_1$.  The edge
$f_1$ is tree-incident and remains $\delta$-colored.  Every later edge of
$R_1$ has both ends outside $X$; the outside swap changes each such
$\gamma$-edge to $\gamma_j$ and fixes each $\delta$-edge.  Thus $R_1$ is
a $(\gamma_j,\delta)$ walk under $\varphi'_1$.

We again verify full support.  The color $\gamma_j$ is missing at $v_1$,
and $f_1$ is its unique incident $\delta$-edge.  The predecessor path
edge at $v_1$ has color $\gamma$, since the following edge has color
$\delta$ and the original proper two-color path alternates.  That
predecessor is tree-incident, remains $\gamma$, and is excluded from the
new $(\gamma_j,\delta)$ graph by (7).  Hence $v_1$ has degree one through
$f_1$.  Every internal vertex- and edge-node has no unused adjacency by
properness and the bipartite incidence definition, exactly as for
$R_0$.  At the initial endpoint $v^0$ of the old full path $P'$, the color
$\gamma$ is missing and $\delta$ is present, so its old degree is one.
The full support clause, simplicity, finiteness, and
\noderef{TwoColorIncidenceDegreeAtMostTwo} therefore make $P'$ a path
component whose other endpoint $z'_\delta$ also has degree one.  The
outside transposition, together with (17) on possible boundary edges,
transports that degree-one conclusion to the new endpoint.  Thus both
endpoints and every internal incidence node have been checked, and
\[
 \operatorname{supp}(R_1)=
 \noderef{TwoColorComponent}
   (G,\varphi'_1,\gamma_j,\delta,\operatorname{inl}v_1).      \tag{19}
\]

Switch precisely this component:
\[
 \varphi'_2=\noderef{TwoColorComponentSwap}
   (G,\varphi'_1,\gamma_j,\delta,\operatorname{inl}v_1).
\]
By \noderef{TwoColorComponentSwapProper}, $\varphi'_2$ is proper, and
the edgewise colored/uncolored check proves that it leaves exactly $e_0$
uncolored.  At $v_1$, (18)--(19) say that the switch changes precisely
$f_1$ from $\delta$ to $\gamma_j$ and changes no other incident identity.
Consequently
\[
 \overline{\varphi'_2}(v_1)
 =\operatorname{insert}\delta
   \bigl(\overline\varphi(v_1)\setminus\{\gamma_j\}\bigr),   \tag{20}
\]
so $\delta$ is missing at $v_1$; every other vertex of $X$ keeps its
original present and missing sets.  The first outside swap fixes $f^0$
because it is incident with $v^0\in X$.  The component in (19) cannot
contain $f^0$, since its incidence with $v^0\ne v_1$ would put $v^0$ in
that component, contrary to (18).  Hence
\[
 \varphi'_2(f^0)=\delta.                                     \tag{21}
\]

We next construct the two partial Tashkinov trees, checking all retained
data.  For a block color $\eta$, let $T^-_\eta$ mean the ordered sequence
obtained from (3) by deleting exactly the two pairs belonging to the
$\eta$-block and retaining the base and every other block in their old
relative order.  Since $p\ge2$, a positive base segment remains and the
first edge is still $e_0$.  The retained vertices and edges are pairwise
distinct by \noderef{TreeSequence}.  Every base attachment is unchanged,
and every retained block edge still attaches its block vertex to an
earlier retained base vertex.  Thus
\[
 V(T^-_\gamma)=X\setminus U_\gamma,\qquad
 V(T^-_{\gamma_j})=X\setminus U_{\gamma_j},                  \tag{22}
\]
where $U_{\gamma_r}=\{u^1_{\gamma_r},u^2_{\gamma_r}\}$, and both are
tree-sequences.

Every retained edge of $T^-_\gamma$ has the same color under $\varphi_2$
as under $\varphi$.  The outside swap fixes it because both endpoints are
tree vertices.  The switched component (13) meets $X$ only at $v^0$ and
contains no edge with both endpoints in $X$, so it contains no retained
tree edge.  Equivalently, the defective-color conclusion
\noderef{ScheideGlobalMaximumUnused}, expanded using
\noderef{TreeUsedColors}, says that $\delta$ occurs on no colored tree
edge, while the structural color and injectivity clauses say that no
retained edge of $T^-_\gamma$ has color $\gamma$.

The old Tashkinov witness for a retained nonfirst base edge is an earlier
base vertex and remains earlier.  Its color is not $\gamma$ by the
base-avoidance clause, so even if that witness is $v^0$, (15) preserves
its missingness.  A retained block edge has color $\gamma_r$ with
$r\ne i$ and has a base vertex missing $\gamma_r$; injectivity gives
$\gamma_r\ne\gamma$, so (15) preserves that witness as well.  These
cases exhaust the nonfirst edges, proving
\[
 \noderef{TashkinovTree}(G,e_0,\varphi_2,T^-_\gamma).         \tag{23}
\]
Append the pair $(f_1,u_1)$.  The new vertex $u_1$ lies outside $X$, its
edge has exact endpoints $v_1,u_1$ with the retained base vertex $v_1$
earlier, and (16) colors it $\delta$, which is missing at the retained
base vertex $v^0$ by (15).  The identity $f_1$ is not a retained tree
edge: it is colored, hence not $e_0$, and a retained colored tree edge
cannot have the unused defective color $\delta$.  Thus all identity,
attachment, new-vertex, color, and missing-witness clauses of
\noderef{TreeSequence} and \noderef{TashkinovTree} hold for
\[
 S_\gamma=T^-_\gamma\mathbin{\Vert}(f_1,u_1).                \tag{24}
\]

The same verification, independently, applies to the other recoloring.
Every retained edge of $T^-_{\gamma_j}$ is fixed first because it is
tree-incident and then because the component (19) meets $X$ only at
$v_1$.  A retained base edge avoids $\gamma_j$, and a retained block edge
has color $\gamma_r\ne\gamma_j$ and retains its base missing witness
through (20).  Therefore $T^-_{\gamma_j}$ is Tashkinov under
$\varphi'_2$.  Append $(f^0,u^0)$.  Its exact attachment is the retained
base vertex $v^0$, its new vertex lies outside $X$, (21) gives its color
$\delta$, and (20) makes $\delta$ missing at the earlier retained base
vertex $v_1$.  The unused-color argument separates $f^0$ from every
retained identity.  Hence
\[
 S_{\gamma_j}=T^-_{\gamma_j}\mathbin{\Vert}(f^0,u^0)         \tag{25}
\]
is a tree-sequence and a Tashkinov tree under $\varphi'_2$.

Now put
\[
 Z=X\setminus(U_\gamma\cup U_{\gamma_j}).                   \tag{26}
\]
The indices $i,j$ are distinct by (7), and the exact block positions in
(3), together with vertex injectivity in \noderef{TreeSequence}, show
that the two blocks consist of four distinct vertices of $X$.  Thus
$|Z|=|X|-4$, which is odd by \noderef{ScheideGlobalMaximumOrder}.

We count $\delta$-incidences at $Z$ without collapsing parallel
identities.  As observed before (8), defectiveness supplies a
$\delta$-colored boundary identity of $X$.  Closedness then says that
$\delta$ is missing at no $x\in X$, so
\noderef{PresentColors} supplies at least one incident
$\delta$-colored identity at each $x$.  It is unique: two distinct such
identities would be adjacent at $x$ by \noderef{AdjacentEdges} and
\noderef{Incident}, contrary to properness.  Therefore exactly one
$\delta$-edge identity is incident with each $z\in Z$.

Let $I_\delta(Z)$ be the finite set of pairs $(z,f)$ with $z\in Z$,
$f$ incident with $z$, and $\varphi(f)=\delta$.  The preceding uniqueness
gives
\[
 |I_\delta(Z)|=|Z|.                                         \tag{27}
\]
Count the same set by edge identity.  An identity in
\noderef{InternalEdges}$(G,Z)$ has two distinct endpoints in $Z$ by the
loopless clause of \noderef{LooplessMultigraph}, and hence contributes
two pairs.  An identity in \noderef{BoundaryEdges}$(G,Z)$ has exactly one
endpoint in $Z$ and contributes one.  These are all nonempty fibers, so
\[
 |I_\delta(Z)|=
 2|E^{\rm int}_\delta(Z)|+
 |\noderef{BoundaryColorEdges}(G,\varphi,Z,\delta)|.          \tag{28}
\]
Equations (27)--(28) and oddness of $Z$ show that an odd number of
individual $\delta$-edge identities leave $Z$.

Two such identities are $f^0$ and $f_1$: their tree endpoints $v^0,v_1$
are base vertices and hence lie in $Z$, while $u^0,u_1$ lie outside $X$.
They are distinct.  Indeed, if the identities were equal, (2) and (5)
would make $v_1$ an endpoint of $f^0$ different from $v^0$; its other
endpoint would then be $u^0$, contradicting $v_1\in X$ and
$u^0\notin X$.  An odd finite set containing these two distinct members
has at least a third.  Choose an identity $f_2$ and name its exact inside
and outside endpoints $z_2,u_2$ so that
\[
 \varphi(f_2)=\delta,\qquad
 z_2\in Z,\quad u_2\notin Z,\qquad
 \operatorname{ends}(f_2)=\{z_2,u_2\},\qquad
 f_2\notin\{f^0,f_1\}.                                     \tag{29}
\]

The edge $f_2$ is unchanged in both recolorings.  First, its endpoint
$z_2\in X$ makes it tree-incident, so both outside swaps fix it.  Moreover
$z_2\ne v^0$: otherwise the distinct identities $f_2,f^0$ would be two
$\delta$-edges incident at $v^0$, contrary to properness.  Likewise
$z_2\ne v_1$ by comparing $f_2$ with $f_1$.  If $f_2$ lay in the switched
component (13), its incidence would put $z_2$ in that component and (12)
would force $z_2=v^0$.  If it lay in (19), (18) would force
$z_2=v_1$.  Both are impossible.  Thus
\[
 \varphi_2(f_2)=\varphi'_2(f_2)=\delta.                      \tag{30}
\]

We exclude the possible locations of $u_2$.  If
$u_2\in U_\gamma$, then (29) is a $\delta$-colored identity joining
$U_\gamma$ to $X\setminus U_\gamma$ because $z_2\in Z$.  This is exactly
the configuration excluded by the statement
\noderef{ScheideDefectiveNoCross}; this is the sole use of that theorem.
Hence $u_2\notin U_\gamma$.

Suppose instead that $u_2\in U_{\gamma_j}$.  Append
$(f_2,u_2)$ to the second partial tree (25).  Its earlier endpoint is the
retained vertex $z_2$, its identity is new by (29) and by the fact that
the defective color $\delta$ is unused on retained tree edges, and its
color is $\delta$, missing at the retained vertex $v_1$ by (20).  Thus
the appended sequence is still a Tashkinov tree under $\varphi'_2$.

The first base vertex $y_0$ lies in
$X\setminus U_{\gamma_j}$ and still misses $\alpha _0$: the second
recoloring changes only $\gamma,\gamma_j,\delta$, all distinct from
$\alpha _0$ by (8).  Since $\alpha _0\ne\gamma_j$,
\noderef{ScheideStructuralInternalPair} supplies an individual identity
$a$ with
\[
 \varphi(a)=\alpha _0,\qquad
 \operatorname{ends}(a)=
   \{u^1_{\gamma_j},u^2_{\gamma_j}\}.                        \tag{31}
\]
This theorem is used only for the internal identity asserted in (31).
The outside swap fixes $a$ because both endpoints lie in $X$, and the
component switch (19) fixes it because neither block vertex equals the
base vertex $v_1$; hence $\varphi'_2(a)=\alpha _0$.  One endpoint of $a$
is the just-appended $u_2$ and the other, call it $u_2^*$, is the other
deleted block vertex.  Append $(a,u_2^*)$.  The endpoint $u_2$ occurs
earlier; $u_2^*$ is new and distinct by the exact block positions; and
$a$ is a new identity.  Indeed it differs by color from $f^0,f_2$, and
its exact two nonbase endpoints prevent it from being any retained base
or block edge in (3).  The retained vertex $y_0$ is an earlier
$\alpha _0$-missing witness.  Thus every clause of
\noderef{TreeSequence} and \noderef{TashkinovTree} holds after both
appendings.

Rebuild the $k$-triple for $\varphi'_2$: criticality, the chromatic-index
equality, and the maximum-degree inequality are the unchanged
coloring-independent clauses of $K$, while properness and the exact
one-uncolored property were proved after (19).  The resulting Tashkinov
tree deletes the two vertices of $U_{\gamma_j}$ and appends $u^0$,
$u_2$, and $u_2^*$.  The first lies outside $X$ and the latter two are the
two distinct deleted vertices, so its order is $|X|-2+3=|X|+1$.  This
contradicts (4) and the maximum in \noderef{TashkinovOrder}.  Therefore
$u_2\notin U_{\gamma_j}$.

The disjoint union in (26), together with $u_2\notin Z$, now gives
\[
 u_2\notin X.                                                \tag{32}
\]
It is also distinct from $u^0$ and $u_1$.  If, for example, $u_2=u_1$,
then the distinct $\delta$-identities $f_2,f_1$ would be adjacent at that
vertex, contradicting properness; the argument with $f^0$ is identical.

Append $(f_2,u_2)$ to both partial trees (24)--(25).  For the first one,
$z_2\in Z\subseteq X\setminus U_\gamma$ is an earlier attachment,
(32) makes $u_2$ a new vertex, (29) and the unused defective-color clause
separate $f_2$ from every retained identity, and $f_2\ne f_1$.  Its color
is $\delta$ by (30), with earlier missing witness $v^0$ from (15).  Thus
\[
 \widehat T_\gamma
  =T^-_\gamma\mathbin{\Vert}(f_1,u_1,f_2,u_2)                \tag{33}
\]
is a tree-sequence and a Tashkinov tree under $\varphi_2$.  The same
identity, new-vertex, and attachment checks apply to
\[
 \widehat T_{\gamma_j}
  =T^-_{\gamma_j}\mathbin{\Vert}(f^0,u^0,f_2,u_2),           \tag{34}
\]
and its $\delta$-missing witness is $v_1$ from (20).  Hence (34) is a
Tashkinov tree under $\varphi'_2$.

Both new vertex sets have exactly the asserted form:
\[
 V(\widehat T_\gamma)=(X\setminus U_\gamma)\cup\{u_1,u_2\},
 \quad
 V(\widehat T_{\gamma_j})=(X\setminus U_{\gamma_j})
    \cup\{u^0,u_2\}.                                       \tag{35}
\]
All unions in (35) are disjoint in the required places by (2), (5),
(32), and the preceding distinctness check.  Consequently each cardinality
is $|X|-2+2=|X|$.

We now rebuild, rather than assume, global maximality.  For $\varphi_2$,
the coloring-independent fields of $K$, the properness following (13),
and (14) give \noderef{KTriple}$(G,k,e_0,\varphi_2)$.  Combine it with
the Tashkinov certificate (33), the first equality in (35), and (4).
Expanding \noderef{GlobalMaximumTriple}, this proves that
$(\widehat T_\gamma,e_0,\varphi_2)$ is globally maximum.  The identical
argument using the properness and one-uncolored conclusions after (19),
(34), and the second equality in (35) proves that
$(\widehat T_{\gamma_j},e_0,\varphi'_2)$ is globally maximum.  Applying
\noderef{ScheideGlobalMaximumOrder} separately to these two reconstructed
triples shows that both vertex sets in (35) are elementary and closed in
their respective recolorings.

It remains to derive the final contradiction.  The first edge of
\noderef{TreeSequence} is $e_0$ and its endpoints are its new vertex at
position one and an earlier vertex.  The structural position equations
in (3) identify these two ends exactly as the first two base vertices
$y_0,y_1$; they are distinct by vertex injectivity, consistently with
the loopless clause of \noderef{LooplessMultigraph}.  Apply
\noderef{ScheideGlobalMaximumMissingCounts} to the original global
maximum triple, once at $y_0$ and once at $y_1$.  Each missing-color set
has at least two members.  Since $X$ is elementary and $y_0\ne y_1$,
those two sets are disjoint.  Their union therefore contains at least four
distinct colors.

Choose a color $\beta$ in that union but outside the three-element set
$\{\alpha _0,\gamma,\gamma_j\}$.  Let $y_\beta\in\{y_0,y_1\}$ be an end
at which $\beta$ is missing.  The color $\delta$ is present at every
vertex of $X$, as proved before (8), so
\[
 \beta\notin\{\alpha _0,\gamma,\gamma_j,\delta\}.            \tag{36}
\]
Both base vertices are retained in (33)--(34).  Check each recoloring
component by component: the first outside swap only transposes
$\alpha _0,\gamma$, its component switch only transposes
$\gamma,\delta$, the second outside swap only transposes
$\gamma,\gamma_j$, and its component switch only transposes
$\gamma_j,\delta$.  By (36), every $\beta$-colored edge identity is fixed
and no non-$\beta$ identity becomes $\beta$-colored.  The exact set of
incident $\beta$-edges at the retained vertex $y_\beta$ is therefore the
same in $\varphi,\varphi_2,\varphi'_2$; in particular, $\beta$ remains
missing there in both new maximum trees.

In the first new maximum tree (33), elementariness and the distinct
vertices $y_\beta,u_2$ imply that $\beta$ is not missing at $u_2$.
By \noderef{MissingColors} and \noderef{PresentColors}, choose an exact
identity $b$ such that
\[
 \noderef{Incident}(G,u_2,b),\qquad \varphi_2(b)=\beta.       \tag{37}
\]
Properness makes this incident identity unique.  Closedness of the first
new tree, together with the retained $\beta$-missing witness
$y_\beta$, says that $b$ cannot be a boundary edge of its vertex set.
By \noderef{Incident}, write its endpoint pair as $\{u_2,q\}$; the
loopless clause gives $q\ne u_2$.  The preceding closedness conclusion
puts $q$ in the first vertex set in (35).

The original set $X$ is also closed and $\beta$ is missing at
$y_\beta\in X$.  If $q$ belonged to $X$, then (32), (37), and
\noderef{BoundaryEdges} would make $b$ a $\beta$-colored boundary edge of
$X$, contradicting that original closedness.  Hence $q\notin X$.
The first equality in (35) says that the only vertices of the first new
tree outside $X$ are $u_1,u_2$.  Since $q\ne u_2$, we obtain
\[
 \operatorname{ends}(b)=\{u_2,u_1\}.                         \tag{38}
\]

The identity $b$ has color $\beta$ under the original coloring as well:
each operation producing $\varphi_2$ fixes the value $\beta$ by (36).
Each operation producing $\varphi'_2$ also fixes it.  Therefore
\[
 \varphi'_2(b)=\beta.                                       \tag{39}
\]
The vertex $u_1$ is absent from the second vertex set in (35): it lies
outside $X$, is distinct from $u^0$ because the two distinct
$\delta$-edges $f_1,f^0$ could not otherwise be proper, and is distinct
from $u_2$ as proved after (32).  Equations (38)--(39) thus make $b$ a
boundary edge of the second new maximum tree, with its other endpoint
$u_1$ new.  The color $\beta$ is still missing at the earlier retained
base vertex $y_\beta$.

Append the exact pair $(b,u_1)$ to $\widehat T_{\gamma_j}$.  Its earlier
attachment is $u_2$, and $u_1$ is a new vertex.  Its identity is new: it
has both endpoints outside $X$, whereas every retained tree edge has both
ends in $X$; it differs by color from the appended $\delta$-edges
$f^0,f_2$.  All previous tree-sequence and Tashkinov clauses remain
unchanged, and (39) together with the missing witness $y_\beta$ verifies
the new Tashkinov clause.  The already rebuilt $k$-triple for
$\varphi'_2$ is unchanged.  The resulting Tashkinov tree has
$|X|+1$ vertices by (35), contradicting (4) and the definition
\noderef{TashkinovOrder}.

This contradiction rules out every second tree vertex on the full
replacement component.  Therefore any $w\in X$ whose vertex-node lies in
that component equals $v^0_\delta$, as required.
\end{proof}

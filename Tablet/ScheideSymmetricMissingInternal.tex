\begin{lemma}[Scheide symmetric missing-color internal edges (13)--(14)]
Fix defective $\delta$.  Every color missing at one of the replacement
vertices in $U_\delta$ has an edge identity internal to
$U_{\gamma(\delta)}$; symmetrically, every color missing at one of the two
vertices of $U_{\gamma(\delta)}$ has an edge identity internal to
$U_\delta$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Let $\alpha$ be missing at a vertex of $U_\delta$.  The two incident
$\delta$-edges and the internal $\gamma(\delta)$-edge from
\noderef{ScheideSymmetricDefectiveInternal} show that
$\alpha\notin\{\delta,\gamma(\delta)\}$.  Thus its edge identities agree
under $\varphi$ and $\varphi_\delta$.  Also $\alpha\ne\alpha_0$, because
the globally maximum replacement tree is elementary by
\noderef{ScheideReplacementMaximum}, $\alpha$ is missing in $U_\delta$,
and $\alpha_0$ is missing at $y_0\in Z$.

The $(\alpha_0,\alpha)$ component through
$u^1_{\gamma(\delta)}$ is disjoint from the closed replacement tree:
both colors are missing on that tree.  It consequently meets the original
tree only in $U_{\gamma(\delta)}$ and contains no tree edge identity.
Switching it preserves properness by
\noderef{TwoColorComponentSwapProper}, fixes the unique uncolored edge, and
preserves every tree-edge missing witness in the avoided base.  Assertion
(3), \noderef{ScheideStructuralInternalPair}, gives its internal
$\alpha_0$ edge in $U_{\gamma(\delta)}$.  An $\alpha$-edge leaving this
pair either exits $V(T)$ and becomes an appendable $\alpha_0$ edge, or
enters $Z$ and violates closure of the replacement tree in a color missing
there.  Both are impossible.  If there were no internal $\alpha$ edge,
both vertices would miss $\alpha$, contradicting elementariness of $V(T)$.
This proves (13).

Now let $\alpha$ be missing at a vertex of
$U_{\gamma(\delta)}$.  The original block edges and the internal
$\delta$ edge from \noderef{ScheideSymmetricDefectiveInternal} give
$\alpha\notin\{\gamma(\delta),\delta\}$.  The
$(\alpha_0,\alpha)$ component through $u^1_\delta$ is disjoint from the
original closed tree and meets the replacement tree only in $U_\delta$.
The internal-edge statement
\noderef{ScheideReplacementInternalMissing} supplies its internal
$\alpha_0$ edge there.  Switching preserves the replacement tree: all
inherited missing witnesses lie in the avoided base, and the two appended
$\delta$ edges use the avoided vertex $v^0_\delta$.  The same
switch-and-append argument, now with the original and replacement pairs
interchanged, excludes every $\alpha$ edge leaving $U_\delta$.
Elementariness of the replacement tree then forces an internal
$\alpha$-edge, and \noderef{ScheideReplacementPairFixed} transfers its
color to $\varphi$.  This proves (14).
\end{proof}

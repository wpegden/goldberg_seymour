\begin{lemma}[Step 1, Subcase 2.1: exact prefix and two-edge construction]
Let $G$ be a finite loopless multigraph retaining individual parallel-edge
identities, let $e_0$ be distinguished, and let $[k]$ be the color set.
Let $T$ be a positive-rung certified ETT with generating coloring
$\varphi$, maximum property, properness, and exactly $e_0$ uncolored.
Assume the complete six-part lower-rung package.  Let $H$ be a good
hierarchy over a hierarchy base, with $d<q_0$ dividers.  Assume uniformly
that every same-rung maximum ETT built from an arbitrary certified input
and generating coloring and having a good hierarchy with fewer than
$q_0$ dividers is elementary.  Assume the large bound
$|\overline\varphi(T_n)|\geq2n+11$, the separate terminal certificate
\[
 T\text{ is }
 \left(\bigcup_{\eta\in D^H_{n,d+1}}\Gamma^d_\eta\right)^-
 \text{-closed under }\varphi,
\]
and elementarity of $V(T)$.  Put $A=H.\mathit{starred}(d)$.

Let $\alpha\ne\beta$, with $\alpha$ missing and singleton-closed on $T$
and $\beta$ not missing on $T$.  Let $P_1,P_2,P_3$ be pairwise
support-disjoint exact exit paths for $\alpha,\beta$.  Their outside ends
$a_1,a_2,a_3$ and tree ends $b_1,b_2,b_3$ are pairwise distinct and
$b_1\prec_T b_2\prec_T b_3$.  Retain, for every $j$, the exact
$\beta$-colored boundary-edge identity $f_j$ on $P_j$ incident with
$b_j$.  Suppose $b_3\notin V(A)$.

Then there exist a color $\gamma\ne\beta$, a proper coloring $\mu$
leaving exactly $e_0$ uncolored, and an exact ordered tree $J$, together
with a corresponding same-rung ETT $\mathcal E_J$ under $\mu$, a
maximum-property certificate, and a good hierarchy $K_J$ on $J$ with
exactly $d$ dividers, such that the following all hold.
The coloring $\mu$ is $(T_n,D_n,\varphi)$-stable and is a mod-coloring of
the original ETT, the replacement has the
same large missing-color estimate, and neither $\gamma$ nor $\beta$
belongs to the union of the last indexed Gamma pairs of $K_J$.
The color $\beta$ is missing at $b_3$ under $\mu$.  There are three exact
$(T,\mu,\{\gamma,\beta\})$-exit paths $R_1,R_2,R_3$ with the same
respective endpoints, supports, and lengths as $P_1,P_2,P_3$; hence their
supports remain pairwise disjoint.  Finally $b_1,b_2,b_3$ lie in $J$, and
both endpoints of each retained individual edge identity $f_1$ and $f_2$
lie in $J$.  More precisely, $C$ is the exact ordered prefix of $T$
ending at $b_3$, $J_1=C+f_1$, and $J=J_1+f_2$ in the sense of
\noderef{TreeSequenceInitialSegment} and
\noderef{TreeSequenceAdjoinEdge}.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Write $n$ for the positive rung, $T_n$ for the last ladder tree,
$D_n$ for its computed stored-color set, $\mathcal E$ for the given ETT,
and $\mathcal G$ for the given good-hierarchy witness.  Put
\[
 A=H.\mathit{starred}(d),\qquad D^+=D^H_{n,d+1},\qquad
 C_\Gamma=\bigcup_{\eta\in D^+}\Gamma^d_\eta .
\]

We first establish the exact prefix and color-set facts used later.
Unfold \noderef{ExtendedTashkinovTree}: its generating-coloring field
identifies $\varphi$ with the coloring in the final certified slot, its
lower field puts $T_n$ strictly before $T$, and its upper field puts $T$
before the last closure tree.  Unfold \noderef{TashkinovHierarchy}.  If
$d>0$, \noderef{HierarchyStarredStage} identifies $A$ with the $d$-th
ordinary stage, and the nestedness and terminal fields put $A$ before
$T$.  If $d=0$, $A$ is the hierarchy core and the core-inside-first and
terminal fields give the same conclusion.  These are entrywise ordered
prefixes in \noderef{TreeSequenceInitialSegment}, so
\noderef{TreeSequenceVertexSet} gives
\[
                     V(T_n)\subseteq V(A)\subseteq V(T).       \tag{1}
\]
For the first inclusion, the non-PE field of \noderef{HierarchyBase}
identifies the core with $T_n$, while its PE field and
\noderef{OrderedTreeUnion} put $T_n$ before the core; the hierarchy then
puts the core before $A$.

The initial member of the certified \noderef{TashkinovSeries} is the
initial constructor of \noderef{TashkinovSeriesStep}, whose
\noderef{KTriple} field gives $k\geq\Delta(G)+1$.  Unfold
\noderef{IsProperEdgeColoring}.  Two different exact edges incident with
$b_3$ are adjacent by \noderef{AdjacentEdges}, so they cannot carry the
same nonempty color.  Consequently the map choosing, for every member of
\noderef{PresentColors} at $b_3$, its unique incident edge witness is
injective.  By \noderef{Degree} and \noderef{MaximumDegree}, there are
at most $d_G(b_3)\leq\Delta(G)$ present colors.  Since
\noderef{MissingColors} is the complementary subset of $[k]$, choose
\[
                         \gamma\in\overline\varphi(b_3).       \tag{2}
\]

The ordered-prefix inclusion $A\preccurlyeq T$ and
\noderef{TreeMissingColors} give
$\overline\varphi(A)\subseteq\overline\varphi(T)$.  Expanding
\noderef{TashkinovStoredColors},
\noderef{HierarchyStoredColorsAt}, and
\noderef{HierarchyTerminalStoredColors} therefore gives
\[
 D^+=D_n\setminus\overline\varphi(T)
       \subseteq D_n\setminus\overline\varphi(A)=D^H_{n,d}.  \tag{3}
\]
If $\gamma\in\Gamma^d_\eta$ for some $\eta\in D^H_{n,d}$, then
\noderef{GoodHierarchyReservedDisjoint} supplies a vertex of $A$ missing
$\gamma$.  It is different from $b_3$, because $b_3\notin V(A)$, while
both vertices lie in $T$ by (1).  Together with (2) this contradicts the
stated \noderef{ColoredVertexSetElementary} property.  Thus $\gamma$
belongs to no last indexed Gamma pair, and in particular
$\gamma\notin C_\Gamma$ by (3).  Since it is missing on $T$, the terminal
\noderef{TreeColorSetMinusClosed} certificate, unfolded through
\noderef{TreeColorSetClosed}, says that its exact colored boundary is
empty.  The same is true for $\alpha$ by hypothesis.  Finally
$\gamma\ne\beta$: the retained identity $f_3$ is incident with $b_3$
and has color $\beta$, whereas (2), expanded using
\noderef{PresentColors} and \noderef{MissingColors}, excludes every
incident $\gamma$-edge.

We next construct the first recoloring and all three transformed exit
paths.  If $\alpha=\gamma$, put $\mu_4=\varphi$.  Otherwise select every
full $(\alpha,\gamma)$ component containing an $\alpha$-edge node of one
of $P_1,P_2,P_3$, and also the component through $a_j$ whenever the
outside-end alternative of $P_j$ says that $a_j$ misses $\alpha$.
We first verify that each of these components has an outside vertex-node
from which a first-crossing argument may start.  Let $e$ be an
$\alpha$-edge node of $P_j$.  Unfold \noderef{TreeExitPath}.  Since
$P_j$ is a simple path whose two ends are vertex-nodes, the occurrence
of $e$ is internal and has two distinct neighboring vertex-nodes, say
$x$ and $y$.  By the adjacency definition in
\noderef{TwoColorIncidenceGraph}, $x$ and $y$ are the two distinct ends
of the exact edge identity $e$.  The unique-$T$-vertex field of the exit
path says that either endpoint, if in $V(T)$, must equal $b_j$.  The two
cannot both equal $b_j$.  If exactly one did, the other would be outside
$T$, so the $\alpha$-colored identity $e$ would belong to
$\partial_G(V(T))$ by \noderef{BoundaryEdges}, contrary to the assumed
singleton-$\alpha$ closedness after unfolding
\noderef{TreeColorSetClosed}.  Thus
\[
                 x,y\notin V(T)                               \tag{3a}
\]
for every selected $\alpha$-edge node.  A component selected through
$a_j$ also has an outside vertex-node, namely $a_j$, by the outside-end
field of \noderef{TreeExitPath}.

Now let a selected component meet $T$, start at one of the outside
vertex-nodes just identified, and take in the connected support of
\noderef{TwoColorComponent} a shortest walk in
\noderef{TwoColorIncidenceGraph} to a vertex-node of $T$.  Immediately
before the first vertex-node in $T$ the walk has an edge-node, and the
preceding vertex-node is outside $T$ by first choice.  Hence that exact
edge has precisely one end in $V(T)$ and, by
\noderef{TwoColorIncidenceGraph}, has color $\alpha$ or $\gamma$.
It is therefore an exact boundary identity by \noderef{BoundaryEdges},
contradicting the singleton closedness of those two colors.  Every
selected component is consequently wholly outside $T$.  Finiteness now
gives an \noderef{OutsideKempeRecoloring}; swap all selected components
and call the result $\mu_4$.

In the unequal branch
\noderef{OutsideKempeRecoloringStable} makes $\mu_4$ proper, leaves
exactly $e_0$ uncolored, fixes every exact edge incident with $T$,
preserves every missing-color set on $T$, and gives stability there for
every control set.  The equality branch has the same properties by
reflexivity in \noderef{sc2}.  Apply \noderef{HierarchyLEMStable} in the
unequal branch and use the original data in the equality branch.  In
both cases retain an arbitrary replacement input, a corresponding
same-rung ETT $\mathcal E_1$ under $\mu_4$, maximum property, a base
$B_1$, a hierarchy $H_1$, and a good witness $\mathcal G_1$.  The
\noderef{CorrespondingExtendedTashkinovTree} and
\noderef{SameGoodHierarchy} conclusions identify all ordered ladder
trees, exact connecting-edge sets, raw stored sets, extension kinds,
hierarchy stages, dividers, indexed stored sets, and Gamma pairs; the
terminal minus-closed certificate is also transported.

For $j=1,2,3$, let $Q_j$ have exactly the ordered vertex- and edge-node
list of $P_j$.  We check every \noderef{TreeExitPath} field for
$(T,\mu_4,\gamma,\beta,a_j,b_j,Q_j)$.  An old $\beta$-edge remains
$\beta$, because $\beta$ is different from both colors of the first
swap.  Every old $\alpha$-edge of $P_j$ lies in one of the selected
outside components and becomes $\gamma$.  Hence every consecutive pair
of nodes is adjacent in $I_G(\mu_4;\gamma,\beta)$ by
\noderef{TwoColorIncidenceGraph}; the unchanged node list is simple and
has the same positive length, ends, and support.  Its only vertex-node in
$T$ remains $b_j$.

The outside-end alternative needs a separate check.  If $a_j$ missed
$\beta$ under $\varphi$, no edge acquires color $\beta$, so
\noderef{PresentColors} and \noderef{MissingColors} show that it still
misses $\beta$.  If it missed $\alpha$, then in the identity branch it
already misses $\gamma=\alpha$.  In the unequal branch the full component
through $a_j$ was selected.  Every incident edge of either selected
color belongs to it by \noderef{TwoColorIncidenceGraph}; after the swap,
the incident $\gamma$-edges are precisely the old incident
$\alpha$-edges, of which there were none.  Thus it misses $\gamma$.
This proves, field by field,
\[
 Q_j:\operatorname{TreeExitPath}(T,\mu_4,\gamma,\beta,a_j,b_j)
 \quad(j=1,2,3).                                               \tag{4}
\]
Their exact supports remain pairwise disjoint and their outside ends
remain pairwise distinct.  No separation of their full transformed
components has been assumed.

Let $K_3$ be the full $(\gamma,\beta)$ component of $b_3$ under
$\mu_4$.  We prove that its support is exactly the support of $Q_3$.
At $b_3$, (2) and preservation of its missing set give no incident
$\gamma$-edge, while the fixed identity $f_3$ is an incident
$\beta$-edge.  Properness, unfolded from
\noderef{IsProperEdgeColoring}, excludes a second edge of that color;
hence $b_3$ has degree exactly one in
\noderef{TwoColorIncidenceGraph}.  At $a_3$, (4) says one of the two
colors is missing and the positive walk supplies an incident edge of
the other color; properness again gives degree exactly one.  Every
internal node of the simple walk has its two distinct walk neighbors,
and \noderef{TwoColorIncidenceDegreeAtMostTwo} excludes a third.

If a node of $K_3$ lay outside $Q_3$, take a shortest component walk from
it to the support of $Q_3$.  Its first attachment cannot be internal,
where the degree is already two, and cannot be at either endpoint, whose
unique neighbor is already the path neighbor.  This contradicts the
connected-support definition in \noderef{TwoColorComponent}.  Thus
\[
                         K_3=\operatorname{supp}(Q_3),         \tag{5}
\]
also agreeing with the spanning path supplied by
\noderef{TwoColorComponentSpanningPath}.  In particular $K_3$ has
exactly the one vertex-node $b_3$ in $T$.

Unfold \noderef{TreeVertexOrder}.  Let $s$ be the unique displayed
vertex position of $b_3$ in $T$, viewed as a natural number, so
$s\leq |T|$.  The two order hypotheses put $b_1$ and $b_2$ at earlier
positions.  Write $a=|A|$.  Because $A$ is an exact ordered prefix of
$T$ and $b_3\notin V(A)$, the position $s$ is strictly beyond every
vertex position of $A$, hence $a<s$.  The positive-length field of
\noderef{TreeSequence} gives $1\leq |T_n|$, while
$T_n\preccurlyeq A$ gives $|T_n|\leq a$.  Consequently
$1\leq a<s$, and therefore
\[
                         2\leq s.                              \tag{5a}
\]
In particular $1\leq s-1$, and $s-1\leq |T|$ follows from
$s\leq |T|$; also $1\leq s\leq |T|$.  These are respectively the
positive-length and upper-bound witnesses required by
\noderef{TreeSequencePrefix}.  We may therefore define the exact prefix
objects
\[
 B^-:=\operatorname{TreeSequencePrefix}(T,s-1),
 \qquad C:=\operatorname{TreeSequencePrefix}(T,s).             \tag{6}
\]
Since $a\leq s-1$, entrywise comparison in
\noderef{TreeSequenceInitialSegment} gives, with the strict inequality
of lengths between the middle two prefixes,
\[
              T_n\preccurlyeq A\preccurlyeq B^-\prec C
                 \preccurlyeq T.                             \tag{7}
\]
If $g$ is the displayed edge at position $s$, then
\noderef{TreeSequenceAdjoinEdge} says exactly that $C=B^-+g$ and that
$g$ joins an old vertex of $B^-$ to $b_3$.  By (5), $K_3$ contains no
vertex of $B^-$.  It contains no edge-node incident with $B^-$ either,
since that node would be adjacent to a vertex-node of $B^-$ in the
incidence graph.  In particular $g\notin K_3$.

Swap $\gamma$ and $\beta$ on $K_3$ and call the result $\mu_5$.
The inequality $\gamma\ne\beta$ permits
\noderef{TwoColorComponentSwapProper};
\noderef{TwoColorComponentSwap} changes only those two nonempty colors,
so \noderef{IsOneEdgeUncolored} still singles out $e_0$.  The preceding
disjointness shows that this swap fixes every exact edge incident with
$B^-$ and every vertex missing-color set there.  We now unfold both
fields of \noderef{StableColoring}.  For its incident-edge field, an
exact edge incident with $T_n$ is incident with $B^-$ by (7), so the
second swap fixes its value; this proves the required equality whenever
its $\mu_4$-color lies in
$\overline{\mu_4}(T_n)\cup D_n$ (indeed, without needing that
restriction).  For its vertex field, every vertex of $T_n$ belongs to
$B^-$, and no incident edge at such a vertex was changed, so its
\noderef{PresentColors}, hence its \noderef{MissingColors}, is identical
under $\mu_4$ and $\mu_5$.  Thus $\mu_5$ is
$(T_n,D_n,\mu_4)$-stable.

The first outside recoloring fixes every edge incident with $T$ and
every missing set at a vertex of $T$ by
\noderef{OutsideKempeRecoloringStable}.  Since $T_n\preccurlyeq T$,
the same two clauses of \noderef{StableColoring} make $\mu_4$
$(T_n,D_n,\varphi)$-stable.  Transitivity in \noderef{sc2} now gives
\[
       \mu_5\text{ is }(T_n,D_n,\varphi)\text{-stable}.        \tag{8}
\]
At $b_3$, the unique incident edge of the component was the
$\beta$-edge $f_3$; it becomes $\gamma$, and no incident edge becomes
$\beta$.  Thus
\[
                         \beta\in\overline{\mu_5}(b_3).       \tag{9}
\]

We also rebuild all three exit paths after this second swap.  For
$j=1,2$, support disjointness and (5) put every node of $Q_j$ outside
$K_3$, so its exact edges and endpoint missing alternative are fixed.
For $j=3$, the same node list is still a simple positive walk because
every $\gamma$-edge and $\beta$-edge on its full component merely
exchange labels.  If $a_3$ missed $\gamma$ before, all incident
$\gamma,\beta$ edges belong to $K_3$, and after the swap it misses
$\beta$; if it missed $\beta$, it now misses $\gamma$.  Its ends,
support, and unique vertex in $T$ do not change.  Calling these walks
$R_1,R_2,R_3$, we have checked every field of
\[
 R_j:\operatorname{TreeExitPath}(T,\mu_5,\gamma,\beta,a_j,b_j)
 \quad(j=1,2,3),                                               \tag{10}
\]
with the same pairwise-disjoint exact supports and the same three
outside ends.

We now construct an actual corresponding ETT on the exact prefix $C$.
Inspect the last positive certificate in \noderef{TashkinovSeries} and
\noderef{TashkinovSeriesStep}; positivity excludes the initial
constructor.  In PE it contains a \noderef{TashkinovClosure} of $T_n$
under $\varphi$.  In SE or RE it contains an exact edge identity $f_n$,
the equality $F_n=\{f_n\}$, the exact seed $T_n+f_n$, and a closure of
that seed.  The upper field of \noderef{ExtendedTashkinovTree} says that
the last tree of this closure contains $T$, hence $C$, as an exact
prefix.  In PE, truncate its finite
\noderef{TashkinovAugmentationSequence} when its length first reaches
$|C|$.  In SE/RE, both the seed and $C$ are prefixes of the same closure,
and (7) gives $|T_n|+1\leq|C|$; equality of entries through the shorter
length shows that the exact seed, including $f_n$, is a prefix of $C$.
Truncate the closure at $C$.  Thus the appropriate old augmentation
route ends exactly at $C$.

Transport that route step by step from $\varphi$ to $\mu_5$.  For each
step before $B^-\to C$, its new displayed edge is incident with its old
prefix inside $B^-$, and its old-prefix missing witness is also there.
The first recoloring fixes every edge incident with $T$ and every missing
set on $T$; the second fixes every edge incident with $B^-$ and every
missing set there.  Hence the same exact displayed edge has the same
color and the same old vertex still misses that color.  For the last
step, the missing witness remains in $B^-$ and the new edge is the exact
identity $g$, proved outside $K_3$.  Length, attachment, and ordered
prefix fields are coloring-free.  These are all fields of
\noderef{TashkinovAugmentation}; induction gives the exact
\noderef{TashkinovAugmentationSequence} under $\mu_5$, from $T_n$ in PE
or from the same exact SE/RE seed $T_n+f_n$.

Apply \noderef{Td} to properness, exact one-uncoloredness, (8), positivity,
maximum property, and the complete lower-rung package.  It says precisely
that $\mu_5$ is a \noderef{ModColoring} of $\mathcal E$.  The transported
route, the strict prefix $T_n\prec C$, and the retained exact SE/RE
singleton identity verify the appropriate eligibility alternative in
that definition.  Its universal clause therefore supplies an actual
\noderef{CorrespondingExtendedTashkinovTree} certificate $\mathcal C_C$
on $C$.  By \noderef{MaximumPropertyTransport}, its replacement ETT
$\mathcal E_C$ has maximum property and the same positive rung $n$.

We construct its hierarchy base rather than copying an ill-typed old PE
witness through correspondence.  The certificate $\mathcal C_C$
identifies every ladder tree, raw stored set, exact connecting-edge set,
and extension kind.  If the terminal kind is not PE, take the new core
to be the common $T_n$, copy the old positive support position and its
positivity proof, and copy the mathematically unused closure field.  The
nonparallel clause of \noderef{HierarchyBase} is then definitional.

If the terminal kind is PE, extract separately the actual terminal PE
constructors of the original and replacement
\noderef{TashkinovSeriesStep}.  Write their maximum-defective data as
$(v,u,f,\delta_n,\gamma_n)$ and
$(v',u',f',\delta'_n,\gamma'_n)$.  Their singleton connecting-edge
equations and connecting-edge agreement in $\mathcal C_C$ give
\[
                        \{f'\}=F'_n=F_n=\{f\},\qquad f'=f.     \tag{11}
\]
The endpoint fields of \noderef{MaximumDefectiveChoice} identify the
ends of this individual edge both with $\{v,u\}$ and with $\{v',u'\}$.
Their \noderef{DefectiveVertex} fields put $v,v'$ in the common $T_n$,
while their other-end fields put $u,u'$ outside it.  Equality of the
unordered endpoint pairs therefore has only the orientation
$v'=v, u'=u$.  The original PE clause of \noderef{HierarchyBase} says
that $v$ occurs at its recorded positive support position; the same
position now certifies the actual replacement support $v'$.

Use the original core and closure $R_n$ for the new PE base, but do not
treat $R_n$ as a prefix of the core.  The vertex-set field of
\noderef{OrderedTreeUnion}, followed by the hierarchy-core prefix and
(7), gives only the inclusions that are needed:
\[
 V(R_n)\subseteq V(\mathit{core})\subseteq V(A)\subseteq V(B^-).
                                                                  \tag{11a}
\]
The exact-entry bookkeeping supplied by that ordered union is as
follows.  Its left-segment field retains the common $T_n$ entries.
For a later right-hand entry of $R_n$ whose new vertex is outside
$V(T_n)$, its \emph{everyNewRightEntry} field places the same exact edge
identity and new vertex in the added part of the core.  No field asserts,
or is used to infer, that the ordered route of $R_n$ is a core prefix.

We instead transport the closure by incidence using (11a).  Every exact
displayed edge of $R_n$, and in particular every new edge in its
\noderef{TashkinovAugmentationSequence}, is incident with its displayed
new vertex in $V(R_n)\subseteq V(B^-)$.  The first outside recoloring
therefore fixes its exact color because $B^-\preccurlyeq T$, and the
second swap fixes it because it is incident with $B^-$.  Every old-prefix
missing witness used by an augmentation is a vertex of $R_n$, hence lies
in $B^-$ by (11a), so its missing-color set is fixed by both swaps.
Checking these two facts at every \noderef{TashkinovAugmentation}
transports the full augmentation sequence from $\varphi$ to $\mu_5$.
Finally, any exact boundary edge of $V(R_n)$ is incident with its endpoint
in $V(R_n)\subseteq V(B^-)$, and every color missing on $R_n$ has a
missing witness at such a vertex.  Thus a $\mu_5$ boundary edge whose
color is missing on $R_n$ would have the same identity and color and the
same missing witness under $\varphi$, contrary to the original
closedness.  We have established once and for all the actual
\noderef{TashkinovClosure} of the same $R_n$ under $\mu_5$ from the same
support prefix of $T_n$; call this the PE closure-transport certificate
(11b).  The original \noderef{OrderedTreeUnion} witness is coloring-free
and is reused unchanged.  Together with (11), the support equality, and
(11b), these are every PE-base field.  In both terminal-kind branches,
(8) gives the terminal missing-set compatibility.  Call the resulting
base $B_C$.

We next build a genuine $d$-divider good hierarchy on $C$.  If
$A\prec B^-$, apply \noderef{GoodHierarchyTerminalPrefixRepackage} to
$\mathcal E_1,B_1,H_1,\mathcal G_1$ and the exact prefix $B^-$.  It
returns the same rung and complete certified series, maximum property,
the same $d$ stages, base core and closure, all stored and Gamma data,
and every good-hierarchy field on $B^-$.  If $B^-=A$ and $d>0$, apply
\noderef{GoodHierarchyPrefixRepackage} at its exact last ordinary stage.
It returns the same-rung maximum ETT on $A$, a good $(d-1)$-divider
prefix, and separately the last internal minus-closed certificate; append
$A$ as the $d$-th ordinary stage below $C$.  If $B^-=A$ and $d=0$, work
directly with the positive-rung ETT $\mathcal E_C$: take stage zero to be
$T_n$, terminal stage $C$, and core $A$ from the rebuilt base $B_C$.
The inclusions $T_n\preccurlyeq A=B^-\prec C$ supply all base, nesting,
and strictness fields.  No zero-rung ETT and no initial-tuple theorem is
used as a constructor.

In all three cases define a hierarchy $K_C$ over $B_C$ with exactly $d$
dividers, the old stages through $A$, and terminal stage $C$.  Every
starred stage lies in $A\subseteq B^-$, where the two colorings have the
same incident-edge values and vertex missing sets.  Correspondence gives
the same raw stored sets; unfolding \noderef{TashkinovStoredColors} and
\noderef{HierarchyStoredColorsAt} proves equality of every indexed stored
set.  Use the Gamma family of $\mathcal G_1$.

We check every field of \noderef{GoodHierarchy}.  Pair cardinality and
pairwise disjointness copy through the stored-set equalities.  Ordinary
stages, their starred predecessors, and missing sets on their differences
are unchanged, so the unique-refresh existence and uniqueness clauses
copy.  All internal exact colored boundaries are fixed; the appropriate
prefix theorem supplies the last internal minus-closed field in the
equality case.  In PE, $B_C$ was rebuilt with the same support, core, and
closure, and certificate (11b) fixes the closure colors and missing sets
without making the closure a core prefix, so the parallel-base
cardinality and non-minus-closed clauses copy.  In non-PE that implication
is vacuous.

For avoidance, unfold \noderef{HierarchySegmentColors} and the totalized
\noderef{HierarchyFirstMissingPosition}.  At every $j<d$ the next stage
lies in $A\subseteq B^-$, so its exact displayed colors and first-missing
positions are unchanged.  At $j=d$, the displayed edges of $C$ are also
unchanged: the only new one over $B^-$ is $g\notin K_3$.  For
$\eta\notin\{\beta,\gamma\}$ all missing sets on $C$ are unchanged, and
the terminal-prefix argument makes the new segment a subset of the old
one.  For $\eta=\gamma$, elementarity under $\varphi$ and (2) make $b_3$
its unique missing vertex in $T$; after the second swap no vertex of $C$
misses $\gamma$, so both the old first position and the new no-candidate
fallback cap the relevant segment at position $s$.  For $\eta=\beta$,
the old coloring has no missing $\beta$ on $T$, while (9) makes $b_3$
the first new missing vertex; the new segment is again only the unchanged
part before $s$.  Old avoidance therefore implies new avoidance in every
case.  This constructs a good witness $\mathcal G_C$ with all fields,
including terminal avoidance.

We now adjoin the two retained boundary identities.  By (5) and pairwise
support disjointness, the exact edge-nodes $f_1,f_2$ lie outside $K_3$;
\noderef{TwoColorComponentSwap} therefore fixes them, so both remain
$\beta$-colored under $\mu_5$.  Their inside endpoints $b_1,b_2$ occur
before $b_3$ and hence lie in $C$.  Their other endpoints lie outside

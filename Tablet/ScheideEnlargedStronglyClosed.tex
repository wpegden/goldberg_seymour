\begin{lemma}[Scheide enlarged set is strongly closed (21)]
The enlarged set $X=V(T)\cup\bigcup_\delta U_\delta$ is strongly closed
with respect to $\varphi$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Put
\[
 X_0=\noderef{TreeSequenceVertexSet}(T),\qquad
 X=\noderef{ScheideEnlargedVertexSet}(R),\qquad
 y_0=C.y(0).
\]
The inherited \noderef{ScheideStructuralConfiguration} in
\noderef{ScheideReducedConfiguration} contains a globally maximum triple.
Unfolding \noderef{GlobalMaximumTriple} and then \noderef{KTriple}, its
coloring $\varphi$ is proper in the sense of
\noderef{IsProperEdgeColoring}.  The base-position equation and the image
definition of $X_0$ put $y_0$ in $X_0$.  The setup
\noderef{ScheideDefectiveSetup} gives
\[
 \alpha_0\in\noderef{MissingColors}(G,\varphi,y_0).          \tag{1}
\]
Moreover, \noderef{ScheideGlobalMaximumOrder} says that $X_0$ has odd
order and is closed, while \noderef{ScheideEnlargedClosed} says that $X$
is closed.

Suppose that $X$ is not strongly closed.  By
\noderef{ColoredVertexSetStronglyClosed}, there are distinct identities in
\noderef{BoundaryEdges}$(G,X)$ having a common color $\alpha$.  Closedness
of $X$ implies that $\alpha$ is present at every vertex of $X$: if it were
missing at one such vertex, \noderef{ColoredVertexSetClosed} would forbid
either boundary identity from having color $\alpha$.  In particular,
\noderef{PresentColors} and (1) give
\[
 \alpha\ne\alpha_0.                                        \tag{2}
\]

We first justify the parity count on individual edge identities.  At each
vertex of $X$, presentness supplies an incident $\alpha$-edge.  There is at
most one: two distinct such edges would be adjacent by
\noderef{AdjacentEdges}, contradicting properness.  Hence every vertex of
$X$ has exactly one $\alpha$ incidence.  By \noderef{Incident} and the
distinct-end law of \noderef{LooplessMultigraph}, an $\alpha$-edge in
\noderef{InternalEdges}$(G,X)$ contributes two incidences, an
$\alpha$-edge in \noderef{BoundaryEdges}$(G,X)$ contributes one, and every
other $\alpha$-edge contributes none.  Thus
\[
 |X|=2|E_\alpha(X)|+|\partial_{\varphi,\alpha}(X)|.          \tag{3}
\]

The set $X$ has odd order.  Its subset $X_0$ has odd order by
\noderef{ScheideGlobalMaximumOrder}.  For each defective color $\delta$,
the internal $\gamma(\delta)$-edge in $U_\delta$ supplied by
\noderef{ScheideSymmetricDefectiveInternal}, together with looplessness,
shows that $U_\delta$ consists of two distinct vertices.  Both are outside
$X_0$ by the exact data in \noderef{ScheideReplacementData}.
The alternatives in \noderef{ScheideReplacementPairsLaminar} say that,
after equal pairs have been identified, these two-vertex sets are pairwise
disjoint.  Consequently the indexed union defining $X$ adds an even number
of vertices to $X_0$.  Hence $|X|$ is odd.  Equation (3) makes the number
of $\alpha$ boundary identities odd; it is at least two by assumption, so
it is at least three.

We next prove that at most one of these identities has its end in $X_0$.
If $\alpha$ is not in
\noderef{DefectiveColors}$(G,\varphi,X_0)$, this is exactly the assertion
that its boundary-color set has cardinality less than two.

Suppose instead that $\alpha$ is defective, regard it as the defective
index $a$, and write
\[
 U_\alpha=\{(R a).u1,(R a).u2\},\qquad
 X_\alpha=X_0\cup U_\alpha.
\]
The first conclusion of \noderef{ScheideSymmetricDefectiveInternal}
gives an $\alpha$-colored identity internal to the two deleted block
vertices $U_{\gamma(\alpha)}$.  Looplessness makes those its two distinct
ends.  Properness and \noderef{AdjacentEdges} therefore exclude any other
$\alpha$-edge incident with either deleted vertex.  Thus every
$\alpha$-boundary identity of $X_0$ has its tree end in the retained set
$Z_{\gamma(\alpha)}$.  The exact-three clause of
\noderef{ScheideReplacementData} now says that all of them are
\[
 f^0_\alpha,\quad f^1_\alpha,\quad f^2_\alpha.              \tag{4}
\]
The last two identities join the retained set to the two vertices of
$U_\alpha$ and hence are internal to $X_\alpha$.  The two vertices of
$U_\alpha$ are distinct by the internal-edge argument used in the parity
count.  Thus each is incident with its corresponding one of
$f^1_\alpha,f^2_\alpha$, and properness excludes every further
$\alpha$-edge incident with either vertex.  The outside end of
$f^0_\alpha$ is not in $U_\alpha$: equality with either member would make
$f^0_\alpha$ and the corresponding one of the distinct identities
$f^1_\alpha,f^2_\alpha$ adjacent $\alpha$-edges, again contradicting
properness.  Therefore $f^0_\alpha$ is the unique $\alpha$-colored
boundary identity of $X_\alpha$.  In particular, only this one among (4)
can leave the larger set $X$.  This proves the asserted bound in both
cases.  Since at least three $\alpha$-edges leave $X$, at least two of
them have their in-ends in $X\setminus X_0$.

Let $P$ be the full $(\alpha_0,\alpha)$ incidence component through
$y_0$.  By \noderef{ScheideSingleBoundaryCrossing}, exactly one individual
edge-node of $P$ belongs to $\partial_G(X)$.  That edge has color $\alpha$:
every edge-node of $P$ has color $\alpha_0$ or $\alpha$ by
\noderef{TwoColorComponent} and \noderef{TwoColorIncidenceGraph}, while
closedness of $X$ and the witness (1) forbid an $\alpha_0$ boundary edge.
Choose a different $\alpha$ boundary identity
\[
 G.ends(f)=s(w,u),\qquad w\in X\setminus X_0,\quad u\notin X,
                                                                    \tag{5}
\]
among the two just found.  Here (5) records the two distinct endpoints
provided by \noderef{BoundaryEdges} and
\noderef{LooplessMultigraph}.  The union formula for $X$ supplies a
defective color $\delta$ with
\[
 w\in U_\delta=\{(R\delta).u1,(R\delta).u2\}.               \tag{6}
\]
Let $Q$ be the full $(\alpha_0,\alpha)$ component through the vertex-node
$u$.  The identity $f$ is an edge-node of $Q$, because it has color
$\alpha$ and is incident with $u$.  Since $f$ is not the unique boundary
edge-node in $P$, the two connected components $P$ and $Q$ are disjoint.

We now prove the avoidance statement needed for both switches.  In the
nondefective case, no $\alpha_0$-edge leaves $X_0$, because $X_0$ is closed
and (1) holds.  The component $P$ starts at $y_0\in X_0$ and contains a
vertex outside $X$ at its unique crossing, so it must leave $X_0$.  Its
first crossing has color $\alpha$, and nondefectiveness makes it the unique
possible $\alpha$ boundary identity of $X_0$.  Thus that unique possible
identity lies in $P$.  If $Q$, which starts at $u\notin X_0$, entered
$X_0$, its first crossing would have color $\alpha_0$ or $\alpha$; the
former is impossible and the latter is already in the disjoint component
$P$.  Hence $Q$ avoids $X_0$.

In the defective case, the set $X_\alpha$ is closed by
\noderef{ScheideReplacementUnionClosed}.  It contains $y_0$, so (1)
forbids every $\alpha_0$ boundary identity of $X_\alpha$.  We proved that
$f^0_\alpha$ is its unique $\alpha$ boundary identity.  The component $P$
starts in $X_\alpha$ and reaches a vertex outside $X\supseteq X_\alpha$;
its first crossing of $X_\alpha$ must therefore be $f^0_\alpha$.  Thus
this unique identity belongs to $P$.  Since $Q$ starts outside
$X\supseteq X_\alpha$, the same first-entry argument shows that $Q$ cannot
enter $X_\alpha$.  In particular, in both cases
\[
 Q\cap X_0=\varnothing,                                    \tag{7}
\]
where (7) means that no vertex-node of $X_0$ lies in $Q$.  By the incidence
definition, no edge-node of $Q$ is incident with a vertex of $X_0$ either.

Put $r=D.\gammaIndex(\delta)$, $\gamma=C.\gamma(r)$,
$Z=X_0\setminus U_\gamma$, and
$T_\delta=(R\delta).replacementTree$.  We record all four inequalities
needed to compare the two labeled incidence graphs.  The appropriate one
of $f^1_\delta,f^2_\delta$ is a $\delta$-colored edge incident with $w$
and with its other end in $Z\subseteq X$.  The second conclusion of
\noderef{ScheideSymmetricDefectiveInternal} gives a $\gamma$-colored edge
internal to $U_\delta$, hence incident with $w$.  Both identities are
different from $f$, because all of their ends lie in $X$ whereas $f$
leaves $X$.  Properness and \noderef{AdjacentEdges} therefore give
\[
 \alpha\ne\delta,\qquad \alpha\ne\gamma.                  \tag{8}
\]
The setup gives $\alpha_0\ne\gamma$, since $\gamma$ is an indexed block
color.  Since $\delta$ is defective for $X_0$, it has a
$\delta$-colored boundary identity there; closedness of $X_0$ and (1)
forbid that identity from being $\alpha_0$-colored.  Hence
\[
 \alpha_0\ne\gamma,\qquad \alpha_0\ne\delta.              \tag{9}
\]

Let
\[
 \varphi_\delta=\noderef{TwoColorComponentSwap}
   (G,\varphi,\gamma,\delta,\operatorname{inl}v^0_\delta).
\]
The theorem \noderef{ScheideDefectiveSwitchEffect} gives the exact switch
effects, and \noderef{ScheideReplacementMaximum} says that
$\varphi_\delta$ is proper, leaves exactly $e_0$ uncolored, and makes
$(T_\delta,e_0,\varphi_\delta)$ globally maximum.

For every edge identity $q$, the three branches in
\noderef{TwoColorComponentSwap} change only $\gamma$ and $\delta$.
Equations (8)--(9) therefore prove both directions of
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \varphi_\delta(q)=\alpha_0&\iff\varphi(q)=\alpha_0,\\
 \varphi_\delta(q)=\alpha&\iff\varphi(q)=\alpha.
 \end{aligned}                                             \tag{10}
\]
Indeed, the forward direction cannot have arisen from a switched
$\gamma$- or $\delta$-value, and the reverse direction is fixed by the
swap.  Thus \noderef{TwoColorIncidenceGraph} has exactly the same labeled
$(\alpha_0,\alpha)$ incidences under the two colorings.  By
\noderef{TwoColorComponent}, $Q$ is the same full component through $u$
under $\varphi$ and $\varphi_\delta$.

No edge identity of $T_\delta$ lies in $Q$.  The exact edge list in
\noderef{ScheideReplacementData} says that such an identity is either a
retained identity of $T$ or one of $f^1_\delta,f^2_\delta$.  Every old tree
identity has both ends in $X_0$ by the attachment clause of
\noderef{TreeSequence}, so (7) excludes it from $Q$.  The two appended
identities have color $\delta$ under $\varphi$, whereas $Q$ contains only
$\alpha_0$- and $\alpha$-colored edge-nodes; (8)--(9) exclude them as
well.  Therefore
\[
 E(T_\delta)\cap Q=\varnothing.                             \tag{11}
\]

Let $\psi$ be the result of switching $\alpha_0$ and $\alpha$ on $Q$ in
$\varphi_\delta$.  We next construct, locally, the missing witnesses for
the tree edges; we do not infer them from the proof of the replacement
maximum theorem.  Put $b=p-1+2r$ and $n=T.length$.  The exact position
clauses of \noderef{ScheideReplacementData} say that $T_\delta$ also has
length $n$, keeps positions below $b$, shifts later old positions down by
two, and places $f^1_\delta,f^2_\delta$ in the last two edge positions.

Consider first a retained noninitial base edge.  The Tashkinov clause in
the original globally maximum triple, unfolded through
\noderef{TashkinovTree}, gives its missing-color witness at an earlier base
vertex.  The base-edge avoidance clause in
\noderef{ScheideStructuralConfiguration} says that its color is different
from every block color, in particular from $\gamma$.  Next consider a
retained block edge.  Its color is the corresponding block color, and the
same structural data supply a vertex in the base missing that color.
Injectivity of the block-color list, and the fact that this is not the
deleted block, again make its color different from $\gamma$.  In both
cases the exact replacement positions retain the base witness earlier than
the retained or shifted edge.

Every retained old tree edge is incident with $X_0$ and differs from
$f^0_\delta$, whose other end lies outside $X_0$.  Hence
\noderef{ScheideDefectiveSwitchEffect} fixes its color.  That node fixes
the missing set at its base witness unless the witness is $v^0_\delta$;
at $v^0_\delta$ its displayed erase-and-insert formula removes only
$\gamma$, which is different from the retained edge color.  Thus every
retained edge has its required earlier missing witness under
$\varphi_\delta$ in the retained base $Z$.  Here every base vertex lies in
$Z$, since base and block positions are disjoint by vertex injectivity in
\noderef{TreeSequence}.

For the last two edges, \noderef{ScheideReplacementData} gives their old
color $\delta$, their retained endpoints in $Z$, and their inequalities
from $f^0_\delta$.  The fixed-edge clause of
\noderef{ScheideDefectiveSwitchEffect} therefore leaves both colors equal
to $\delta$.  The setup places $v^0_\delta$ among the exit vertices, and
\noderef{ScheideStructuralExitsInBase} places it in the base, hence in
$Z$.  The exact switch formula inserts $\delta$ into the missing set at
$v^0_\delta$.  If its base index is $a<p$, then
$r\in\{0,\ldots,s-1\}$ makes $s\geq1$, and the structural length equation
$n+1=p+2s$ gives
\[
 a\leq p-1\leq n-2<n-1.
\]
Thus its retained base position precedes both final edge positions.
Consequently it is the missing witness for both appended edges.

This exhausts every noninitial position of $T_\delta$ and produces, for
each one, an earlier witness in $Z\subseteq X_0$.  Equation (7) and the
incidence definition say that the second component $Q$ contains no edge
incident with such a witness, so the second switch preserves its entire
missing set.  Equation (11) says that it also fixes the tree-edge identity
and its color.  The same witnesses therefore verify every clause of
\noderef{TashkinovTree} for $T_\delta$ under $\psi$.

We explicitly reconstruct the remaining maximum-triple data.  By (2) and
\noderef{TwoColorComponentSwapProper}, switching $Q$ preserves properness
of $\varphi_\delta$.  The definition
\noderef{TwoColorComponentSwap} fixes the uncolored value and maps each
colored value to a colored value.  Thus the assertion from
\noderef{ScheideReplacementMaximum} that exactly $e_0$ is uncolored is
preserved in the sense of \noderef{IsOneEdgeUncolored}.  Criticality of
$G$, the equality $\chi'(G)=k+1$, and the inequality
$\Delta(G)+1\leq k$ are unchanged graph-and-integer clauses of the old
\noderef{KTriple}.  These facts give a $k$-triple $(G,e_0,\psi)$.  The
cardinality clause of the globally maximum replacement triple gives
\[
 |V(T_\delta)|=t(G),                                       \tag{12}
\]
and neither the tree nor its vertex set was changed by the second switch.
Together with the Tashkinov verification above, all three clauses of
\noderef{GlobalMaximumTriple} now hold for
$(T_\delta,e_0,\psi)$.

It remains to construct the strict augmentation.  The identity $f$ lies in
$Q$, has color $\alpha$ under $\varphi$, and by (10) still has color
$\alpha$ under $\varphi_\delta$.  The second switch therefore gives
\[
 \psi(f)=\alpha_0.                                         \tag{13}
\]
The base vertex $y_0$ is retained in $Z$: its position is zero, whereas
the two deleted block vertices have positions $p+2r$ and $p+2r+1$, both
at least two.  Under the first switch, (1), (9), and
\noderef{ScheideDefectiveSwitchEffect} show that $\alpha_0$ remains
missing there.  If $y_0\ne v^0_\delta$ its missing set is fixed; if they
are equal, the exact formula removes $\gamma\ne\alpha_0$ and inserts
$\delta$.  The second switch fixes every edge incident with $y_0$ by (7).
Hence
\[
 \alpha_0\in\noderef{MissingColors}(G,\psi,y_0).            \tag{14}
\]

The exact replacement vertex equation puts $w\in U_\delta$ in
$V(T_\delta)$ and retains $y_0$ there.  It also gives
$V(T_\delta)\subseteq X_0\cup U_\delta\subseteq X$, so (5) makes $u$ a
fresh vertex.  The edge $f$ is fresh by (11), and its endpoint equation in
(5) attaches it to the retained vertex $w$ and the new vertex $u$.

Form a \noderef{TreeSequence} $S$ of length
$T_\delta.length+1$ by keeping all old vertex and edge positions and
appending first $f$ and then $u$.  The old vertex and edge maps remain
injective, and freshness of $u$ and $f$ proves injectivity at the new
positions.  The first edge remains $e_0$; every old attachment is
unchanged, and the new attachment is the endpoint equation $G.ends(f)=s(w,u)$.
Thus $S$ is a tree-sequence.  Every old noninitial edge retains the earlier
missing witness constructed above.  At the new last edge, (13)--(14) give
the color $\alpha_0$ and its missing witness at the earlier retained vertex
$y_0$.  These are all the clauses of \noderef{TashkinovTree}, so $S$ is a
Tashkinov tree for the reconstructed $k$-triple $(G,e_0,\psi)$.

Finally, the image definition \noderef{TreeSequenceVertexSet}, vertex
injectivity, freshness of $u$, and (12) give
\[
 |V(S)|=|V(T_\delta)|+1=t(G)+1.                             \tag{15}
\]
The reconstructed \noderef{KTriple} has $k=\chi'(G)-1$, so $S$ is one of
the candidates whose cardinalities are bounded by
\noderef{TashkinovOrder}.  Equation (15) contradicts that bound.  Hence no
two distinct boundary identities of $X$ have the same color.  Combining
this conclusion with \noderef{ScheideEnlargedClosed} gives exactly
\noderef{ColoredVertexSetStronglyClosed}.
\end{proof}

\begin{helper}[Exit localization for the two order-nine blocks]
Let $(T,e_0,\varphi,N)$ be a balanced normal globally maximum triple with
$T.\mathsf{length}=8$ and $N.\mathsf{height}=5$.  Let
$\gamma_0,\gamma_1$ be distinct colors such that the identities in
positions $4,5$ have color $\gamma_0$, the identities in positions $6,7$
have color $\gamma_1$, each $\gamma_i$ is missing at one of the first five
vertices, and the colored base identities in positions $1,2,3$ avoid both
$\gamma_i$.  Then every Scheide exit vertex of $T$ misses one of
$\gamma_0,\gamma_1$.
\end{helper}
\begin{proof}
Put $X=\noderef{TreeSequenceVertexSet}(T)$ and write
$y_r=T.\mathsf{vertex}(r)$.  We first make all coloring data of the
current normal triple explicit.  Its globally maximum field, expanded by
\noderef{GlobalMaximumTriple}, supplies a
$\noderef{KTriple}(G,k,e_0,\varphi)$.  Expanding that predicate with
\noderef{KTriple} supplies
\[
 \Delta(G)+1\leq k,\qquad
 \noderef{IsProperEdgeColoring}(G,\varphi),\qquad
 \noderef{IsOneEdgeUncolored}(\varphi,e_0).
\tag{1}
\]
Thus properness means that distinct identities incident with a common
vertex cannot have the same assigned color, while the last clause says
that $\varphi(e_0)$ is uncolored and every identity other than $e_0$ is
colored.  These are precisely the clauses in
\noderef{IsProperEdgeColoring} and \noderef{IsOneEdgeUncolored}; no
coloring property of a pre-replacement triple is being used.

Let $\alpha,\beta$ be the two normal colors.  By
\noderef{NormalGlobalMaximumTriple}, $\alpha$ is missing at $y_0$,
$\beta$ is missing at $y_1$, and the normal height-five incidence path
has as its displayed tree-edge nodes exactly the identities in positions
$1,2,3$.  By the adjacency definition in
\noderef{TwoColorIncidenceGraph}, every one of those three identities has
color $\alpha$ or $\beta$.  The endpoint field of the same normal data
puts the position-$1$ identity on $y_1y_2$.  It cannot have color
$\beta$: such an incidence would put $\beta$ in
$\noderef{PresentColors}(G,\varphi,y_1)$, contrary to its membership in
$\noderef{MissingColors}(G,\varphi,y_1)$.  Hence position $1$ has color
$\alpha$.  The position-$2$ identity meets it at $y_2$, and position $3$
meets position $2$ at $y_3$.  The identities are distinct by
\noderef{TreeSequence}; their common incidences are instances of
\noderef{Incident}, hence make the successive pairs adjacent in the
sense of \noderef{AdjacentEdges}.  Properness from (1) therefore forces
the colors in positions $1,2,3$ to be $\alpha,\beta,\alpha$.

The base-avoidance hypothesis applied to positions $1$ and $2$ now says
that both $\alpha$ and $\beta$ differ from both block colors, and
injectivity of the block-color map says
$\gamma_0\ne\gamma_1$.  The four block identities in positions
$4,5,6,7$ have colors $\gamma_0,\gamma_0,\gamma_1,\gamma_1$ by
hypothesis.  Finally, \noderef{TreeUsedColors} ranges exactly over the
colored displayed positions after the first one; that first identity is
$e_0$ by \noderef{TreeSequence} and is uncolored by (1).  Consequently
\[
 \noderef{TreeUsedColors}(\varphi,T)
   =\{\alpha,\beta,\gamma_0,\gamma_1\}.
\tag{2}
\]
Choose $z_i$ among $y_0,\ldots,y_4$ with $\gamma_i$ missing at $z_i$,
as supplied by the hypotheses.  The elementary clause of
\noderef{ScheideGlobalMaximumOrder}, expanded through
\noderef{ColoredVertexSetElementary}, says that missing-color sets at
distinct displayed vertices are disjoint.  Consequently $y_0$ is the
only displayed vertex that can miss $\alpha$, $y_1$ the only one that can
miss $\beta$, and $z_i$ the only one that can miss $\gamma_i$.

We next perform the missing-color count directly from the same current
$k$-triple.  By \noderef{Degree}, $d_G(v)$ is the number of individual
edge identities incident with $v$, and by \noderef{MaximumDegree},
$d_G(v)\leq\Delta(G)$.  Thus (1) gives
$d_G(v)+1\leq k$.  Properness in (1) makes the colors on the colored
identities incident with $v$ pairwise distinct.  If $v$ is not an end of
$e_0$, exact-one-uncoloredness says that all $d_G(v)$ incident identities
are colored, so exactly $d_G(v)$ colors are present.  Since
\noderef{MissingColors} is the complement of
\noderef{PresentColors} in the $k$ colors,
\[
 |\overline\varphi(v)|=k-d_G(v)\geq1.              \tag{3}
\]
If $v$ is an end of $e_0$, the identity $e_0$ is uncolored and every
other incident identity is colored.  Hence exactly $d_G(v)-1$ distinct
colors are present and
\[
 |\overline\varphi(v)|=k-(d_G(v)-1)
   =k-d_G(v)+1\geq2.                               \tag{4}
\]
The first-edge equation and the index-zero attachment equation in
\noderef{TreeSequence} give
$\operatorname{ends}_G(e_0)=\{y_1,y_0\}$, so these are exactly the two
displayed vertices to which (4), rather than (3), applies.

Let $v$ be a displayed vertex different from both $z_0,z_1$.  If $v$ is
not an end of $e_0$, then it is also different from $y_0,y_1$.  Its
missing color furnished by (3) is therefore none of the four colors in
(2), by elementarity and the four displayed witnesses.  If $v$ is an end
of $e_0$, then it is $y_0$ or $y_1$.  Among the four colors in (2),
elementarity permits it to miss at most its corresponding one of
$\alpha,\beta$: it differs from both $z_i$, and it differs from the other
normal-color witness.  The two missing colors furnished by (4) therefore
include a color outside (2).  In either case $v$ misses a color unused on
all colored tree identities, hence a color in
\noderef{TreeFreeColors}.

Now take an arbitrary $v\in\noderef{ScheideExitVertices}(T,\varphi)$.
Unfolding that finite set gives
$\noderef{ScheideExitVertex}(T,\varphi,v)$.  In particular, the specified
last tree occurrence in the exit certificate explicitly includes
$v\in X$; thus the displayed-vertex conclusion of the preceding
paragraph applies to this arbitrary exit.  By
\noderef{ScheideExitNoFree}, its missing-color set is disjoint from
$\noderef{TreeFreeColors}(\varphi,T)$.  It cannot therefore be different
from both $z_0,z_1$.  Hence $v=z_i$ for some $i\in\{0,1\}$, and the
defining choice of $z_i$ gives
$\gamma_i\in\noderef{MissingColors}(G,\varphi,v)$, as required.
\end{proof}

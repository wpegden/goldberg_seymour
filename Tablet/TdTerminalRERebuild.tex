\begin{helper}[Terminal RE reconstruction]
Let (T) be a positive-rung extended Tashkinov tree generated by
\[
 \mathcal T=\{(T_i,\varphi_{i-1},S_{i-1},F_{i-1},\Theta_{i-1}):
                   1\leq i\leq n+1\},
\]
and suppose that (T) has the maximum property.  Keep the graph (G),
palette size (k), and distinguished uncolored edge (e_0) fixed, but
allow the certified input coloring to vary arbitrarily.  Assume explicitly
the following six assertions for every positive-rung ETT (T') on these
fixed data, with arbitrary certified input coloring \(\varphi'_0\), maximum
property, and rung number (s<n), writing \(\varphi'_s\) for its generating
coloring and \(\mathcal T'\) for its certified series:
\begin{enumerate}
\item (V(T')) is elementary with respect to \(\varphi'_s\).
\item Under every proper \((T'_{s+1},D'_s,\varphi'_s)\)-stable coloring
leaving precisely (e_0) uncolored, (T'_{s+1}) is interchangeable for
any pair of colors of which at least one is missing on (T'_{s+1}).
\item For each PE slot (a\leq s) with supporting vertex (u_a) and
(m(u_a)=b), every proper \((T'_a,D'_a,\varphi'_a)\)-stable coloring
leaving precisely (e_0) uncolored is
\((T'(u_a)-u_a,D'_{b-1},\varphi'_{b-1})\)-stable.  When (b>1), it is
also \((T'_{b-1},D'_{b-1},\varphi'_{b-1})\)-stable.  Moreover, the stored
color pairs at two distinct PE slots with distinct supporting vertices
and the same first-appearance index are disjoint.
\item If the terminal slot is PE with supporting vertex (u_s) and
stored colors \(\gamma'_s,\delta'_s\), then under every proper
\((T'_s,D'_s,\varphi'_s)\)-stable coloring leaving precisely (e_0)
uncolored, the \((\gamma'_s,\delta'_s)\)-component through (u_s)
contains exactly the one vertex (u_s) of (T'_s).
\item If \(\tau\) is any proper \((T'_s,D'_s,\varphi'_s)\)-stable
coloring leaving precisely (e_0) uncolored, (d) is defective on
(T'_s) under \(\tau\), and (x) is a nonminimum vertex of
\(I[\partial_{\tau,d}(T'_s)]\), then
(x\preceq_{T'}u_a) for every PE supporting vertex or SE extension
vertex (u_a) with (m(x)\leq a\leq s).
\item Every proper \((T'_s,D'_s,\varphi'_s)\)-stable coloring leaving
precisely (e_0) uncolored is a \(\varphi'_s\bmod T'_s\) coloring.
\end{enumerate}

Assume in addition that the terminal extension kind is RE.  Then every proper \((T_n,D_n,\varphi_n)\)-stable coloring \(\sigma_n\)
leaving precisely (e_0) uncolored is a
\(\varphi_n\bmod T_n\) coloring in the sense of
\(\noderef{ModColoring}\).  In particular, the conclusion is universal
over every eligible ordered tree-sequence strictly extending (T_n),
and not merely over one selected closure.
\end{helper}
\begin{proof}
Write \(n\) for the rung number and let \(T_n\) be the terminal ladder
tree in the statement.  Fix a proper terminal coloring \(\sigma_n\) which
leaves exactly \(e_0\) uncolored and is
\((T_n,D_n,\varphi_n)\)-stable.  The final certified member of the
\noderef{TashkinovSeries} is an RE constructor of
\noderef{TashkinovSeriesStep}.  In particular, it contains a
\noderef{RevisitingExtensionCertificate} at current index \(n-1\), and
that certificate contains an earlier index \(h\) satisfying

\[
 0<h\leq n-1,\qquad \Theta_h=\mathrm{PE}.                    \tag{1}
\]

This immediately disposes of the one-rung case: if \(n=1\), then (1)
would say \(0<h\leq0\).  Hence a terminal RE constructor is impossible
when \(n=1\), and from now on \(n>1\).

We prove the universal clause of \noderef{ModColoring}.  Let \(T^*\) be
an arbitrary eligible ordered tree-sequence strictly extending \(T_n\).
Because the terminal kind is RE, eligibility supplies an edge identity
\(f^*\), the exact equality \(F_n=\{f^*\}\), an exact one-edge seed
\(A=T_n+f^*\) in the sense of \noderef{TreeSequenceAdjoinEdge}, and a
\(\sigma_n\)-\noderef{TashkinovAugmentationSequence} from \(A\) to
\(T^*\).  Apply \noderef{TashkinovClosureExists} to \(T^*\) under
\(\sigma_n\), obtaining a tree \(C\).  By \noderef{TashkinovClosure},
there is an augmentation sequence from \(T^*\) to \(C\), and \(V(C)\)
is closed under \(\sigma_n\).  Transitivity of the reflexive-transitive
relation in \noderef{TashkinovAugmentationSequence} therefore gives a
route

\[
                 A\longrightarrow T^*\longrightarrow C.     \tag{2}
\]

Every one-step \noderef{TashkinovAugmentation} retains its source as an
ordered initial segment.  Thus
\(T_n\prec T^*\preccurlyeq C\), and (2) makes \(C\) a closure of the
exact seed \(A\).

Apply \noderef{TdReplacementPrefixExact} to \(\sigma_n\).  It gives a
proper coloring \(\sigma_{n-1}\), leaving exactly \(e_0\) uncolored, such
that

\[
 \sigma_{n-1}\text{ is }(T_n,D_{n-1},\varphi_{n-1})
 \text{-stable}.                                             \tag{3}
\]

The same result says that in an RE slot
\(\sigma_{n-1}=\sigma_n\).  Since \(n>1\), its second reconstruction
branch also supplies an actual rung-\(n-1\) restriction \(B\) of the
original ETT, with displayed tree exactly \(T_n\), generating coloring
\(\varphi_{n-1}\), and maximum property.  At every retained index its
ordered trees, colorings, raw stored sets, exact connecting-edge sets,
kinds, and computed stored sets agree with the original series.  Finally,
it supplies a \noderef{CorrespondingExtendedTashkinovTree} certificate
\(\mathcal B^*\) over \(B\) whose replacement ETT has displayed tree
exactly \(T_n\), final coloring exactly \(\sigma_{n-1}\), rung number
\(n-1\), and the maximum property.  We use this actual positive-rung
replacement ETT, rather than an informal truncation, when invoking the
lower-rung hypotheses below.

We next identify the data at the earlier PE slot \(h\) from (1).  Write

\[
 (v_h,u_h,f_h,\delta_h,\gamma_h),\qquad
 (v_h^*,u_h^*,f_h^*,\delta_h^*,\gamma_h^*)                 \tag{4}
\]

for, respectively, the support vertex, outside endpoint, selected edge,
defective color, and extra color in the original and replacement PE
constructors.  The corresponding certificate identifies their ordered
tree at this slot; call it \(T_h\).  By
\noderef{TashkinovSeriesStep}, the two PE constructors give

\[
 F_h=\{f_h\},\quad F_h^*=\{f_h^*\},\qquad
 S_h=\{\delta_h,\gamma_h\},\quad
 S_h^*=\{\delta_h^*,\gamma_h^*\}.                           \tag{5}
\]

The corresponding certificate identifies \(F_h^*=F_h\) and
\(S_h^*=S_h\).  The singleton equalities in (5) first give
\(f_h^*=f_h\) as an individual edge identity.  The endpoint equations in
the two \noderef{MaximumDefectiveChoice} certificates then write the
same unordered endpoint pair as
\(\{v_h,u_h\}=\{v_h^*,u_h^*\}\).  Both \(v_h,v_h^*\) belong to
\(V(T_h)\), by the defective-vertex fields, whereas both
\(u_h,u_h^*\) lie outside \(V(T_h)\).  The crossed orientation is
therefore impossible, and

\[
                  v_h^*=v_h,\qquad u_h^*=u_h.                \tag{6}
\]

We now establish the missing/present hypotheses needed to orient the
unordered color pair in (5).  In the original PE constructor,
\noderef{ParallelExtensionColor} says that \(\gamma_h\) is missing at
\(v_h\) in the selected coloring.  Its second-stable relation preserves
that missing set by the vertex clause of \noderef{StableColoring}.  The
edge \(f_h\) is incident with \(v_h\) and has color \(\delta_h\) in the
selected coloring by \noderef{MaximumDefectiveChoice}; since
\(\delta_h\) belongs to the enlarged control set, the edge clause of
\noderef{StableColoring} gives the same exact color to \(f_h\) in the
second-stable coloring.  Properness, as defined in
\noderef{IsProperEdgeColoring}, makes it the unique incident
\(\delta_h\)-edge, while missingness of \(\gamma_h\), using
\noderef{PresentColors} and \noderef{MissingColors}, says there is no
incident \(\gamma_h\)-edge.  The PE current-coloring equation and
\noderef{TwoColorComponentSwap} recolor \(f_h\) from \(\delta_h\) to
\(\gamma_h\), leave no incident \(\delta_h\)-edge, and hence prove

\[
 \delta_h\in\overline\varphi_h(v_h),\qquad
 \gamma_h\notin\overline\varphi_h(v_h),\qquad
 \delta_h\ne\gamma_h.                                      \tag{7}
\]

Exactly the same argument inside the replacement PE constructor proves

\[
 \delta_h^*\in\overline\sigma_h(v_h^*),\qquad
 \gamma_h^*\notin\overline\sigma_h(v_h^*),\qquad
 \delta_h^*\ne\gamma_h^*.                                  \tag{8}
\]

The positive-stage stability field of
\noderef{CorrespondingExtendedTashkinovTree}, evaluated at the common
vertex in (6), and the vertex clause of \noderef{StableColoring} give

\[
             \overline\varphi_h(v_h)=
             \overline\sigma_h(v_h^*).                      \tag{9}
\]

Equations (5)--(9) are every hypothesis of
\noderef{TdEarlierPEWitnessIdentification}.  Applying it yields

\[
 f_h^*=f_h,\quad v_h^*=v_h,\quad u_h^*=u_h,\quad
 \delta_h^*=\delta_h,\quad\gamma_h^*=\gamma_h.               \tag{10}
\]

Thus the colors have been oriented using their post-switch status; no
equality of nondeterministic PE witnesses has been read from the
corresponding-ETT certificate.

Return to the old terminal
\noderef{RevisitingExtensionCertificate}.  Besides (1), it supplies a
full \((\gamma_h,\delta_h)\)-cycle \(O\) under
\(\varphi_{n-1}\), an exact boundary edge \(f_n\) on \(O\), its inside
and outside endpoints, a return vertex \(w\in V(T_h)\), and a simple
return path \(L\) in \(O\) from the edge-node \(f_n\) to \(w\), all of
whose graph-vertex nodes lie in \(V(T_n)\).  It also states that every
positive slot \(j\) with \(h<j\leq n-1\) is RE.  The kind equalities in
the corresponding certificate give the same intervening history in the
replacement prefix.  Inspecting the actual certified steps through
\noderef{TashkinovSeries} and \noderef{TashkinovSeriesStep}, the
current-coloring equation at each such RE slot is literal equality with
the preceding coloring.  Induction over these indices gives

\[
              \varphi_h=\varphi_{n-1},\qquad
              \sigma_h=\sigma_{n-1}=\sigma_n.                \tag{11}
\]

We record precisely the PE path component which persists through (11).
The replacement PE constructor supplies a
\noderef{TreeExit} at \(v_h\), hence, by \noderef{TreeExitPath}, a
simple two-color path \(P_h\) from an outside vertex to \(v_h\), using at
least one graph edge and meeting \(V(T_h)\) only at \(v_h\).  A component
swap interchanges the two colors on this component without changing its
incidence-graph support.  At \(v_h\), (8) says that after the switch
\(\delta_h\) is missing and \(P_h\) supplies the incident
\(\gamma_h\)-edge, so that endpoint has incidence degree one.  At the
outside endpoint, \noderef{TreeExitPath} says that one of the two colors
was missing and the path supplies an edge of the other; after the swap
the two roles are merely interchanged, so this endpoint also has degree
one.  Every internal node of the simple path already has two path
neighbors, and \noderef{TwoColorIncidenceDegreeAtMostTwo} bounds its
entire incidence degree by two because the post-switch coloring is
proper.  No further node can therefore attach to the path.  Consequently
\(P_h\) is the full edge-bearing component, namely a
\noderef{TwoColorNontrivialPathComponent}, and it meets \(T_h\) exactly
at \(v_h\).  Equation (11) carries this same full component and the
missing color \(\delta_h\) to the final replacement-prefix coloring
\(\sigma_{n-1}\).

We next copy the return path \(L\).  Every edge-node of \(L\) is adjacent
along \(L\) to a graph-vertex node in \(V(T_n)\), including the first
edge-node \(f_n\); hence each exact edge identity of \(L\) is incident
with \(T_n\) in the sense of \noderef{TreeIncidentEdge}.  Because \(L\)
lies on \(O\), every such edge has old color \(\gamma_h\) or
\(\delta_h\).  Both colors belong to the raw pair \(S_h\).  Expanding
\noderef{TashkinovStoredColors} at \(n-1\), either such a color lies in
\(D_{n-1}\), or it is missing on the preceding ladder tree.  In the
latter case the same missing vertex remains in \(T_n\), since the ordered
extension fields of \noderef{TashkinovSeriesStep} make every earlier
ladder tree an ordered initial segment of \(T_n\).  By
\noderef{TreeMissingColors}, in either case the color belongs to
\(\overline\varphi_{n-1}(T_n)\cup D_{n-1}\).  The edge clause of (3),
that is, the first clause of \noderef{StableColoring}, now copies the
color of every exact edge of \(L\), including \(f_n\), from
\(\varphi_{n-1}\) to \(\sigma_{n-1}\).

Let \(Q\) be the full \noderef{TwoColorComponent} under
\(\sigma_{n-1}\) containing this copied path.  Then \(Q\) contains
\(w\in V(T_h)\) and the exact edge identity \(f_n\).  The endpoint
equation of the old RE certificate is structural and still holds; since
\(f_n\) has one endpoint outside \(T_n\), that endpoint is adjacent to
the colored edge-node \(f_n\) and also lies in \(Q\).  Thus \(Q\) meets
\(T_h\) and leaves \(T_n\).

The return vertex is not the PE support:

\[
                             w\ne v_h.                        \tag{12}
\]

Indeed, (7) and (11) say that \(\delta_h\) is missing at \(v_h\) under
\(\varphi_{n-1}\).  Properness permits at most one incident
\(\gamma_h\)-edge, so \(v_h\) has degree at most one in the old
two-color incidence graph.  A vertex-node on the simple full cycle \(O\)
from \noderef{TwoColorCycle} has two distinct cycle neighbors and hence
degree at least two.  Since \(w\) lies on \(L\subseteq O\), equality
\(w=v_h\) would be impossible.  By (10), the replacement PE support is
the same \(v_h\), and the full path component \(P_h\) meets \(T_h\) only
there.  The component \(Q\), on the other hand, contains the different
vertex \(w\in V(T_h)\).  Therefore

\[
                              Q\ne P_h.                       \tag{13}
\]

Suppose for contradiction that \(Q\) were a path component.  It is
nontrivial because it contains the graph-edge node \(f_n\).  Thus (13)
would give two distinct \((\gamma_h,\delta_h)\)-path components meeting
\(T_n\), namely \(P_h\) and \(Q\).  Apply the lower-rung package to the
actual replacement-prefix ETT supplied above.  Its rung number is
\(n-1>0\), it is strictly below \(n\), and it has the maximum property.
Its exact displayed tree and last output tree are \(T_n\), and its
generating coloring is \(\sigma_{n-1}\).  The coloring
\(\sigma_{n-1}\) is stable relative to itself: both clauses of
\noderef{StableColoring} are reflexive.  It is proper and leaves exactly
\(e_0\) uncolored.  Finally, \(\delta_h\) is missing at
\(v_h\in V(T_n)\), so \noderef{TreeMissingColors} puts it among the
colors missing on \(T_n\).  Lower assertion (ii) therefore makes
\(\gamma_h,\delta_h\) interchangeable on \(T_n\).  This contradicts
the uniqueness of a tree-meeting path component in
\noderef{TreeInterchangeable}.  Hence \(Q\) is not a path component.

For completeness, we now obtain a full cycle rather than merely naming
the alternative.  The colors are distinct by (7), \(Q\) contains the
edge-node \(f_n\), and the properness of \(\sigma_{n-1}\) gives degree at
most two at every node by
\noderef{TwoColorIncidenceDegreeAtMostTwo}.  The finite-component lemma
\noderef{TwoColorComponentSpanningPath} supplies a simple path whose
support is all of \(Q\).  If either endpoint of that spanning path had no
second neighbor, the component would satisfy
\noderef{TwoColorPathComponent}, contrary to the preceding paragraph.
Both endpoints therefore have degree two.  There are no nodes outside the
spanning path, and every internal path node already has its two path
neighbors, so neither endpoint's remaining neighbor can be internal.
Their remaining neighbors are therefore each other; adding this last
incidence edge closes the spanning path to a simple cycle whose support is
all of \(Q\).  This is a full
\noderef{TwoColorCycle}; denote it by \(O^*\).

We also prove the two fields concerning closure of the previous tree.
The replacement-prefix series ends at the exact ordered tree \(T_n\)
under \(\sigma_{n-1}\).  Since its last index \(n-1\) is positive, its
last certificate is one of the three noninitial constructors of
\noderef{TashkinovSeriesStep}; in every constructor the final tree is the
target of a \noderef{TashkinovClosure}.  Hence \(V(T_n)\) is closed under
\(\sigma_{n-1}\), in the sense of
\noderef{ColoredVertexSetClosed}.

The cycle \(O^*\) contains \(w\in V(T_n)\) and the outside endpoint of
\(f_n\).  Traverse its simple closed walk from the inside to the outside
in each of the two directions.  In each direction, the first exact
multigraph edge which leaves \(V(T_n)\) belongs to
\(\partial_G(V(T_n))\) by \noderef{BoundaryEdges}; simplicity of the
cycle makes these two crossing edge identities distinct.  The color
\(\delta_h\) is missing at \(v_h\in V(T_n)\).  Closedness therefore
excludes \(\delta_h\) from every boundary edge, by
\noderef{ColoredVertexSetClosed}.  Both crossing identities on the
two-color cycle consequently have color \(\gamma_h\).  Thus two distinct
colored boundary edges have the same color, so the boundary-color
uniqueness clause of \noderef{ColoredVertexSetStronglyClosed} fails.
We have proved that \(T_n\) is closed but not strongly closed under
\(\sigma_{n-1}\).

We can now build the replacement
\noderef{RevisitingExtensionCertificate} field by field.  Take its
earlier index to be \(h\); positivity and \(h\leq n-1\) are (1).  The
earlier PE kind and the ordered colors are transported by the
corresponding kind equality and (10).  The required all-RE history is the
old certificate's history transported through the pointwise kind
equalities.  Take its full cycle to be \(O^*\).  It meets the earlier
tree at \(w\) and leaves the current tree at the outside endpoint of
\(f_n\).  Take its selected edge to be \(f_n\): the copied path puts its
edge-node on \(O^*\), the old structural data put it in
\(\partial_G(V(T_n))\), and the old inside/outside endpoints and exact
endpoint equation are unchanged.  Take the return vertex and return path
to be \(w\) and the copied path \(L\).  The path is still simple, every
one of its nodes lies in the full component \(Q=\operatorname{supp}(O^*)\),
and every graph-vertex node of it still lies in \(V(T_n)\).  These are
all fields of the replacement RE certificate.

It remains to append the terminal step and check the correspondence
payload.  The old terminal RE constructor gives
\(F_n=\{f_n\}\).  Comparing this with the eligibility equality
\(F_n=\{f^*\}\) identifies \(f^*=f_n\), so the exact seed \(A\) in
(2) is available to the replacement constructor.  Retain the certified
replacement prefix through \(T_n\), use the just-constructed RE
certificate, set its next coloring equal to
\(\sigma_{n-1}=\sigma_n\), and set its raw stored set, exact connecting
edge set, and kind to
\(\{\delta_h,\gamma_h\}\), \(\{f_n\}\), and RE.  These are exactly
the corresponding old fields by the old RE constructor and (10).  Set
the chosen vertex and outside vertex to the certificate's unchanged
inside and outside endpoints, and set the optional delta and gamma fields
to \(\delta_h\) and \(\gamma_h\), exactly as the RE constructor requires.
Use
the properness and one-uncoloredness hypotheses for the previous-coloring
fields, the closure and non-strong-closure argument above for the next
two fields, the exact adjoin certificate for \(A=T_n+f_n\), and the
composed route (2) ending at \(C\) for the closed-extension field.  Since
\(T_n\prec T^*\preccurlyeq C\), the ordered-extension and strict-extension
fields also hold.  Thus every field of the RE constructor in
\noderef{TashkinovSeriesStep} has been verified.

Appending this constructor to the certified prefix produces, by
\noderef{TashkinovSeries}, a replacement series of rung \(n\) whose
last closure tree is \(C\).  The strict relation
\(T_n\prec T^*\) and the ordered relation \(T^*\preccurlyeq C\), together
with the final-color equation \(\sigma_n\), give an
\noderef{ExtendedTashkinovTree} presentation of the originally arbitrary
\(T^*\) using this same dependent series.

Finally, the prefix certificate already gives equality with the original
ordered ladder trees through \(T_n\), all earlier raw stored sets, exact
connecting-edge sets, and kinds.  The appended RE constructor gives the
same equalities at the final slot.  All prefix colorings are proper and
leave exactly \(e_0\) uncolored, and their positive stages are stable
relative to the corresponding original stages; the pointwise data
equalities for \(B\) identify these with the original series.  At the
new terminal stage, properness and exact one-edge uncoloredness are the
hypotheses on \(\sigma_n\), and the required
\((T_n,D_n,\varphi_n)\)-stability is also the hypothesis on
\(\sigma_n\).  These are precisely all fields of
\noderef{CorrespondingExtendedTashkinovTree}.  We have therefore produced
such a certificate for the arbitrary eligible \(T^*\).  Together with
the initial properness and exact one-uncolored-edge hypotheses, this is
the universal clause of \noderef{ModColoring}, and proves the claim.
\end{proof}

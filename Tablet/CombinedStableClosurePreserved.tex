\begin{lemma}[Combined stability preserves the PE closure]
Let the terminal step be a PE, let $R_n$ be the chosen
$\varphi_n$-closure of $T_n(v_n)$, and let $\sigma_n$ and
$\varphi_n$ be proper colorings leaving exactly $e_0$ uncolored.
If $\sigma_n$ is
$(T_n\oplus R_n,D_n,\varphi_n)$-stable, then
\[
 \sigma_n(f)=\varphi_n(f)
\]
for every displayed edge identity $f$ of $T_n$ and of $R_n$.
Moreover, the very same ordered tree $R_n$ is a closure of
$T_n(v_n)$ under $\sigma_n$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Write $n$ for the positive terminal rung number, $T_n$ for the last ladder
tree, and
\[
 A=T_n(v_n)=T_n|y_r
\]
for the supporting prefix at the positive position $r$ recorded by
\noderef{HierarchyBase}.  By \noderef{TreeSequencePrefix}, $A$ has exactly
the vertex and edge entries of $T_n$ through position $r$.  In particular,
every displayed edge identity of $A$ is a displayed edge identity of $T_n$,
and \noderef{TreeSequenceVertexSet} gives $V(A)\subseteq V(T_n)$.

The first conjunct of \noderef{CombinedStableColoring} is precisely
$(T_n,D_n,\varphi_n)$-stability in the sense of
\noderef{StableColoring}.  Let $q=n-1$; positivity of $n$ makes $q$ a valid
index of the length-$n$ finite series, and its successor is the terminal
slot.  After using the assumed identification of $\varphi_n$ with that
terminal coloring, \noderef{HkuTreeColorsControlled} applies at $q$: its
properness and unique-uncolored-edge hypotheses are the corresponding
hypotheses on $\sigma_n$, and its stability hypothesis is the first
conjunct just identified.  It gives, entry by entry,
\[
 \sigma_n(f)=\varphi_n(f)\qquad(f\in E(T_n))
                                                               \tag{1}
\]
including the root identity $e_0$.

The second conjunct of \noderef{CombinedStableColoring} says that
$\sigma_n$ is $(R_n,\varnothing,\varphi_n)$-stable.  Because both
colorings are proper and leave exactly $e_0$ uncolored, symmetry in
\noderef{sc2} also says that $\varphi_n$ is
$(R_n,\varnothing,\sigma_n)$-stable.  We shall use the forward and reverse
relations only through the two clauses in \noderef{StableColoring}.

The terminal-PE clause of \noderef{HierarchyBase}, applied to the given PE
identity, says that $R_n$ is a $\varphi_n$-closure of $A$.  Expanding
\noderef{TashkinovClosure} gives both a
\noderef{TashkinovAugmentationSequence}
\[
 A=A_0,A_1,\ldots,A_m=R_n
                                                               \tag{2}
\]
under $\varphi_n$, followed by closedness of $R_n$.

Here (2) is a finite chain because
\noderef{TashkinovAugmentationSequence} is the reflexive-transitive closure
of the one-step relation.  For each $i<m$,
\noderef{TashkinovAugmentation} says that $A_i$ is an ordered initial
segment of $A_{i+1}$.  The entrywise definition in
\noderef{TreeSequenceInitialSegment} composes: successively substituting
the vertex and edge equalities shows that $A_i$ is an ordered initial
segment of every later $A_j$, in particular of $A_m=R_n$.  Consequently
every displayed edge entry of $A_i$ persists with the same edge identity
in $R_n$, and \noderef{TreeSequenceVertexSet} gives
\[
                  V(A_i)\subseteq V(R_n).                     \tag{3}
\]

We now induct along (2), simultaneously proving that every displayed edge
of $A_i$ has the same value under $\sigma_n$ and $\varphi_n$, and that
$A_0,\ldots,A_i$ are also successive $\sigma_n$-augmentations.  At
$i=0$, the edge assertion follows from the prefix fact above and (1), and
there is no augmentation to transport.

Suppose the assertions hold through $A_i$, where $i<m$.  Unpacking the
next step with \noderef{TashkinovAugmentation} supplies its exact new last
edge identity $f_i$ and a color $c_i$ such that
\[
 \varphi_n(f_i)=c_i,
 \qquad c_i\in\overline{\varphi_n}(A_i).                       \tag{4}
\]
By \noderef{TreeMissingColors}, the second part of (4) supplies a vertex
$w_i\in V(A_i)$ at which $c_i$ is missing under $\varphi_n$.  Inclusion
(3) puts $w_i$ in $V(R_n)$, so a second use of
\noderef{TreeMissingColors} gives
$c_i\in\overline{\varphi_n}(R_n)$.

The attachment clause for the new edge in
\noderef{TashkinovAugmentation}, together with persistence from
\noderef{TreeSequenceInitialSegment}, gives an endpoint of $f_i$ in
$V(R_n)$.  By the definition in \noderef{TreeIncidentEdge}, this says
that $f_i$ is incident with $R_n$.  Now the forward edge clause of
\noderef{StableColoring}, applied to the
$(R_n,\varnothing,\varphi_n)$-stable relation, (4), and
$c_i\in\overline{\varphi_n}(R_n)$, yields
\[
             \sigma_n(f_i)=\varphi_n(f_i)=c_i.                 \tag{5}
\]
All older displayed identities persist from $A_i$ to $A_{i+1}$ by
\noderef{TreeSequenceInitialSegment} and are fixed by the induction
hypothesis; (5) fixes the sole new one.  This proves the edge assertion
for $A_{i+1}$.

It remains at this step to transport the augmentation.  The vertexwise
missing-set clause of \noderef{StableColoring}, used at
$w_i\in V(R_n)$, gives
\[
 \overline{\sigma_n}(w_i)=\overline{\varphi_n}(w_i).
\]
Thus $c_i$ is missing at $w_i$ under $\sigma_n$, and
\noderef{TreeMissingColors} converts this to
$c_i\in\overline{\sigma_n}(A_i)$.  Equation (5) gives
$\sigma_n(f_i)=c_i$.  The length equality and ordered-initial-segment
parts of the step are independent of the coloring, so all clauses of
\noderef{TashkinovAugmentation} now hold for
$\sigma_n,A_i,A_{i+1}$.  The induction is complete.  At $i=m$, it proves
both equality of $\sigma_n$ and $\varphi_n$ on every displayed edge
identity of $R_n$ and, by
\noderef{TashkinovAugmentationSequence}, a $\sigma_n$-augmentation
sequence from $A$ to $R_n$.

We finally prove that the last vertex set is closed under $\sigma_n$.
Fix a boundary edge identity $g$ of $V(R_n)$, a vertex
$v\in V(R_n)$, and a color $c$ such that
\[
             \sigma_n(g)=c,
             \qquad c\in\overline{\sigma_n}(v).                \tag{6}
\]
By \noderef{BoundaryEdges}, the boundary membership supplies an endpoint
$u\in V(R_n)$ incident with $g$.  Hence \noderef{TreeIncidentEdge} says
that $g$ is incident with $R_n$.  Also
\noderef{TreeMissingColors} turns the second part of (6) into
$c\in\overline{\sigma_n}(R_n)$.  Apply the reverse
$(R_n,\varnothing,\sigma_n)$-stable relation supplied by
\noderef{sc2}.  Its edge clause in \noderef{StableColoring} applies to
this incidence and tree-missing color, and gives
\[
                  \varphi_n(g)=\sigma_n(g)=c.                  \tag{7}
\]
The vertexwise missing-set clause of the forward relation in
\noderef{StableColoring}, applied at $v$, gives
$\overline{\sigma_n}(v)=\overline{\varphi_n}(v)$; hence (6) says that
$c$ is missing at $v$ under $\varphi_n$.  But the original
$\varphi_n$-closure certificate gives closedness by
\noderef{TashkinovClosure}, and \noderef{ColoredVertexSetClosed} forbids
a boundary edge such as $g$ from having the value (7) of a color missing
at $v$.  This contradiction proves closedness under $\sigma_n$.

We have proved a $\sigma_n$-augmentation sequence from $A$ to $R_n$ and
closedness of its final vertex set.  By \noderef{TashkinovClosure}, the
same ordered tree $R_n$ is therefore a closure of $A=T_n(v_n)$ under
$\sigma_n$, as required.
\end{proof}

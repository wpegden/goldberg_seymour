\begin{lemma}[Interchangeability on the closure constituent of a PE]
Let $T$ be a positive-rung extended Tashkinov tree constructed from a
$k$-triple $G,e_0,\varphi$, with arbitrary certified input coloring,
terminal ladder tree $T_n$, terminal generating coloring $\varphi_n$,
and terminal stored-color set $D_n$.  Assume that $T$ has maximum
property and that its terminal extension is a PE.  Let $R_n$ and
$T_n\vee R_n$ be the closure and exact ordered union in a hierarchy
base under $\varphi_n$ (respectively `base.closure' and `base.core'
in Lean).

Assume, uniformly over every certified input coloring and generating
coloring, that every positive-rung ETT $T'$ of rung number $s<n$ with
maximum property satisfies all six lower-rung assertions:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $V(T')$ is elementary under its generating coloring;
\item under every proper coloring leaving exactly $e_0$ uncolored and
stable on its final ladder closure, that closure is interchangeable for
two colors whenever at least one of them is missing there;
\item stability descends unconditionally at each PE support to the prefix before the
support and, when its first-appearance index is greater than one, also to
the preceding ladder stage; stored pairs at
distinct PE slots with distinct supports and the same first-appearance
index are disjoint;
\item at a terminal PE, the stored two-color component through the
support meets the terminal ladder tree only at that support;
\item every nonminimum endpoint of a defective boundary color precedes
each later PE support or SE extension vertex allowed by its
first-appearance index; and
\item every proper terminal stable coloring leaving exactly $e_0$
uncolored is a mod-coloring of the ETT.
\end{enumerate}

Let $\sigma_n$ be proper, leave exactly $e_0$ uncolored, and be
$T_n\oplus R_n,D_n,\varphi_n$-stable.

For arbitrary $\alpha,\beta\in[k]$,
\[
 \alpha\in\overline\sigma_n(R_n)
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 \alpha,\beta\text{ are $R_n$-interchangeable under }\sigma_n.
\]
All boundaries, trees, and two-color components use the original edge
type, so distinct parallel edges retain their identities.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Write $n$ for the rung number, let
\[
 T_n=\mathcal T.\mathit{tree}(n),\qquad R=R_n,
\]
and let $v=v_n$ be the vertex at the positive support position recorded
by the hierarchy base.  The generating-color field of
\noderef{ExtendedTashkinovTree} identifies $\varphi_n$ with the last
coloring of the certified series.  The certificate at slot $n$ in
\noderef{TashkinovSeries}, eliminated using the terminal-PE hypothesis,
is the PE constructor of \noderef{TashkinovSeriesStep}.  In particular it
says that $\varphi_n$ is proper and leaves exactly $e_0$ uncolored.  The
parallel clause of \noderef{HierarchyBase} identifies its chosen support
with $v$ and, if
\[
 B=T_n|v
   =\noderef{TreeSequencePrefix}(T_n,\text{the support position}),
\]
says that $R$ is a $\varphi_n$-closure of $B$.  Here the cited prefix
definition fixes every vertex and edge entry of $B$, not only its vertex
set.

The two conjuncts of \noderef{CombinedStableColoring} say respectively
that $\sigma_n$ is $(T_n,D_n,\varphi_n)$-stable and that it is
$(R,\varnothing,\varphi_n)$-stable.  Apply
\noderef{CombinedStableClosurePreserved}, using the properness and
one-uncoloredness of both colorings.  Besides equality of the two
colorings on every displayed edge identity of $T_n$ and $R$, its third
conclusion is the exact certificate
\[
             \noderef{TashkinovClosure}(\sigma_n,B,R).       \tag{1}
\]
Thus (1), when unfolded, supplies a $\sigma_n$-augmentation sequence
from $B$ to $R$ and closedness of the final vertex set of $R$.

Let $j=m(v)$ be the least complete ladder stage containing $v$, in the
sense of \noderef{ETTFirstAppearanceIndex}.  Since $v\in V(T_n)$, one
has $1\leq j\leq n$.  Successive ordered-stage inclusions from the
certificates in \noderef{TashkinovSeriesStep}, expanded using
\noderef{TreeSequenceInitialSegment}, identify the occurrence of $v$ in
$T_j$ with its occurrence in $T_n$.  Consequently the prefix $B$ just
defined is also the exact ordered prefix of $T_j$ through $v$.

Apply \noderef{ExtensionRules} to the current ETT, its maximum property,
and the stated lower-rung six-pack.  Use its terminal-support clause with
the chosen-vertex equation above and with the given properness,
one-uncoloredness, and $(T_n,D_n,\varphi_n)$-stability.  Its first
conclusion gives
\[
 \sigma_n\text{ is }(T(v)-v,D_{j-1},\varphi_{j-1})
 \text{-stable}                                             \tag{2}
\]
in the precise sense of \noderef{StableColoringBeforeVertex}.  If
$j>1$, its second conclusion additionally gives
\[
 \sigma_n\text{ is }(T_{j-1},D_{j-1},\varphi_{j-1})
 \text{-stable}.                                            \tag{3}
\]
No use of (3) will be made when $j=1$.

We next build the augmentation part of the required presentation.  Inspect
the certificate which constructs the stage $T_j$.  There are exactly the
following possibilities, all supplied by
\noderef{TashkinovSeriesStep}.
\begin{itemize}
\item If $j=1$, the initial constructor gives the original one-edge seed
      $S=T_0$ and a $\varphi_0$-closure from $S$ to $T_1$.
\item If $j>1$ and the slot $j-1$ is PE, its exact seed is
      $S=T_{j-1}$ and its closure ends at $T_j$.
\item If $j>1$ and the slot $j-1$ is SE or RE, its certificate gives the
      individual connecting edge $f_{j-1}$, the exact singleton
      connecting-edge set, and a seed $S$ satisfying
      \noderef{TreeSequenceAdjoinEdge}$(T_{j-1},f_{j-1},S)$; its closure
      from this exact adjoined seed ends at $T_j$.
\end{itemize}
In every case, unfolding \noderef{TashkinovClosure} in that constructor
gives a
$\varphi_{j-1}$-\noderef{TashkinovAugmentationSequence} from $S$ to
$T_j$.  The seed $S$ is an exact ordered initial segment of $B$.  Indeed,
for $j=1$ the initial closure makes $S$ an exact initial segment of
$T_1$, the definition of $B$ makes $B$ another exact initial segment of
$T_1$, the initial seed has length one, and the support position gives
$1\leq |B|$.  Comparing the entry equalities of these two prefixes inside
the common tree $T_1$ gives $S\leq B$.  For $j>1$, minimality of $j$ gives
$v\notin V(T_{j-1})$; hence the prefix through $v$ strictly extends
$T_{j-1}$.  Both are exact initial segments of the common tree $T_j$, so
in the PE case their entry equalities give $S=T_{j-1}\leq B$.  In the
SE/RE case, \noderef{TreeSequenceAdjoinEdge} makes $S$ the first one-edge
extension of $T_{j-1}$; the strict integer inequality just obtained gives
$|S|=|T_{j-1}|+1\leq |B|$.  Since $S$ and $B$ are both exact initial
segments of $T_j$, their entrywise initial-segment equalities then put
$S$ inside $B$.

We may therefore apply
\noderef{TashkinovAugmentationSequenceRestrict} to the source sequence,
the initial-segment relation $S\leq B$, and the exact prefix relation
$B\leq T_j$.  It gives a $\varphi_{j-1}$-augmentation sequence
\[
                         S\longrightarrow B.                 \tag{4}
\]
This restriction includes the step adjoining $v$ whenever $B$ strictly
extends $S$.  When $j=1$ and $v$ already lies in the one-edge seed, one
has $B=S$, and (4) is correctly the reflexive, empty sequence.

We now transport (4), one nontrivial step at a time, to $\sigma_n$.
Consider a step $A\to A'$ in (4).  By
\noderef{TashkinovAugmentation}, its new final edge is one exact identity
$g$, there is a color $c$ with
\[
             \varphi_{j-1}(g)=c,\qquad
             c\in\overline\varphi_{j-1}(A),                 \tag{5}
\]
and $A$ is the exact ordered initial segment of $A'$ obtained by deleting
the new last edge and vertex.  Unfolding
\noderef{TreeMissingColors} in (5) supplies a particular
$x\in V(A)$ at which $c$ is missing.  Every later augmentation retains
the preceding tree as an exact initial segment, so composing the remaining
initial-segment relations in (4) gives $A'\leq B$.  Since this step adds
one entry, $|A|<|B|$; the last vertex of $B$ is $v$.  Thus every vertex of
$A$, including $x$ and the attachment vertex of $g$, lies strictly before
$v$.  The vertex clause
of (2) therefore keeps $c$ missing at $x$ under $\sigma_n$.  The edge
clause of (2) applies to the same edge identity $g$: it is incident with
the before-$v$ set at its attachment vertex, and its
$\varphi_{j-1}$-color $c$ lies in the union of missing colors there.
It consequently gives
\[
                         \sigma_n(g)=c.                       \tag{6}
\]
The length and exact ordered-entry clauses are independent of the
coloring.  Equations (5)--(6) and the retained witness $x$ are therefore
all the clauses of a $\sigma_n$-Tashkinov augmentation $A\to A'$.
Induction over the reflexive-transitive relation in
\noderef{TashkinovAugmentationSequence} transports all of (4), including
its possible reflexive case, to a $\sigma_n$-augmentation sequence from
$S$ to $B$.

Concatenate that sequence with the sequence from $B$ to $R$ in (1).
Transitivity gives
\[
                         S\longrightarrow R                  \tag{7}
\]
under $\sigma_n$.  Composing the exact initial-segment clauses at every
augmentation in (7) also proves that $S$ is an ordered initial segment of
$R$.  Moreover, when $j>1$, the vertex $v$ belongs to $B\leq R$ but not
to $T_{j-1}$, so the same entrywise argument gives
\[
 T_{j-1}\leq R,\qquad |T_{j-1}|<|B|\leq |R|.                 \tag{8}
\]
These are the ordered-initial-segment and strict-extension facts required
below; they do not follow merely from calling $R$ a closure.

Suppose first that $j>1$.  Apply
\noderef{CertifiedETTRestriction} to the original maximum ETT at the
positive target rung $j-1$.  This theorem is obtained by iterating the
actual predecessor construction \noderef{CertifiedETTPredecessor}; it
returns an actual maximum ETT $\mathcal B$ of rung $j-1$, with displayed
terminal tree exactly $T_j$, last ladder tree exactly $T_{j-1}$,
generating coloring exactly $\varphi_{j-1}$, and all retained trees,
colorings, raw stored sets, exact connecting-edge sets, kinds, computed
\noderef{TashkinovStoredColors}, and dependent step certificates equal to
the original retained data.

Every ETT below $\mathcal B$ has rung below $j-1<n$, so the universal
lower-rung hypothesis of the present lemma supplies the complete
lower-rung package required to apply \noderef{Td} to $\mathcal B$.
Transporting (3) across the restriction's tree, coloring, and computed
stored-set equalities gives precisely the terminal stability premise of
that theorem.  With the given properness and one-uncoloredness,
\noderef{Td} yields
\[
                    \noderef{ModColoring}(\mathcal B,\sigma_n). \tag{9}
\]

We verify its universal-extension premise on the particular tree $R$.
The initial-segment and strict-length premises are exactly (8).  If slot
$j-1$ is PE, then $S=T_{j-1}$ and (7) is the required augmentation
sequence.  If it is SE or RE, its retained constructor supplies the same
individual $f_{j-1}$, the exact equation
$F_{j-1}=\{f_{j-1}\}$, the exact adjoined seed $S=T_{j-1}+f_{j-1}$,
and (7).  Thus the appropriate disjunct in the definition of
\noderef{ModColoring} holds with no change of edge identity.  Its
universal clause now returns an actual
\noderef{CorrespondingExtendedTashkinovTree} certificate $\mathcal C$
displayed on $R$, with an actual replacement input, replacement series,
and replacement ETT generated by $\sigma_n$.  Applying
\noderef{MaximumPropertyTransport} to (9), $\mathcal C$, and the maximum
property of $\mathcal B$ gives maximum property for that replacement ETT.

Let $L$ be the replacement series' final ladder closure, the tree to which
lower assertion (ii) applies.  The `belowFinal' field of the replacement
\noderef{ExtendedTashkinovTree} is an exact ordered initial-segment
certificate $R\leq L$.  We explicitly transport the missing color needed
by lower (ii).  From the assumed
$\alpha\in\noderef{TreeMissingColors}(\sigma_n,R)$, the union definition
supplies a vertex $a\in V(R)$ at which $\alpha$ is missing.  The vertex
entry equality in $R\leq L$ puts that same vertex in $V(L)$; repacking the
same witness in \noderef{TreeMissingColors} gives
\[
                   \alpha\in\overline\sigma_n(L).            \tag{10}
\]
The replacement certificate's final-coloring equality identifies its last
generating coloring with $\sigma_n$.  Hence the stability hypothesis of
lower (ii), relative to the replacement series' own computed terminal
stored set, is reflexive after expanding \noderef{StableColoring}.
Properness and one-uncoloredness are the given hypotheses (and are also
recorded by the corresponding certificate).  Since the replacement rung
is the positive number $j-1<n$ and it has maximum property, lower
assertion (ii), applied with (10), makes $L$ interchangeable for
$\alpha,\beta$.

It remains to restrict that conclusion to $R$, and here full components
must be retained.  Let $z,w$ be arbitrary roots of two
$(\alpha,\beta)$-path components meeting $R$, as quantified in
\noderef{TreeInterchangeable}.  Choose their two meeting vertices in
$V(R)$.  The vertex-entry part of $R\leq L$ places the same witnesses in
$V(L)$.  Interchangeability on $L$ therefore equates the two full sets
\[
 \noderef{TwoColorComponent}(G,\sigma_n,\alpha,\beta,z)
 =
 \noderef{TwoColorComponent}(G,\sigma_n,\alpha,\beta,w).
\]
This is exactly the required universal conclusion on $R$.  The argument
does not discard trivial components: an isolated vertex component is one
of the path components quantified by \noderef{TreeInterchangeable}, and
its meeting vertex is transported in exactly the same way.

It remains to treat $j=1$.  Here (7) starts at the original one-edge tree
$T_0$, and (1) supplies closedness of its endpoint $R$; hence together
they give the exact certificate
\[
                    \noderef{TashkinovClosure}(\sigma_n,T_0,R). \tag{11}
\]
We construct its rounds-zero \noderef{TashkinovSeriesData} explicitly.
Its two tree slots are $T_0$ and $R$; its sole coloring slot and its input
coloring are both $\sigma_n$; its sole raw stored set and connecting-edge
set are both empty; and its kind, chosen vertex, outside vertex, delta,
and gamma fields are all absent.  Eliminate the original certificate at
slot zero.  Its
\noderef{KTriple} supplies criticality, the chromatic-index equation, and
the degree bound, none of which depends on the coloring, while the assumed
properness and exact one-uncoloredness supply the remaining two clauses
for $(G,e_0,\sigma_n)$.  The same constructor supplies
$|T_0|=1$.  These facts, the displayed empty and absent fields, and (11)
are exactly every field of the initial constructor in
\noderef{TashkinovSeriesStep}.  Since zero rounds has only slot zero to
certify, they define an actual
$\noderef{TashkinovSeries}(G,k,0,e_0,\sigma_n)$ with $T_1=R$.

At its sole index, \noderef{TashkinovStoredColors} computes
$D_0=\varnothing$ from the explicit zero-index clause.  Apply the
interchangeability conclusion of \noderef{TechnicalTheoremBase} to this
actual series, taking its test coloring also to be $\sigma_n$.  Its
properness and one-uncoloredness are assumed, and
$(R,\varnothing,\sigma_n)$-stability is reflexive directly from the two
clauses of \noderef{StableColoring}.  The original missing-color
hypothesis is already the required missing-color disjunct.  The base
theorem therefore gives that $\alpha,\beta$ are $R$-interchangeable.
This construction uses no zero-rung ETT, no maximum property at rung zero,
and no stability assertion on the one-edge seed.

Both cases prove the required implication.
\end{proof}

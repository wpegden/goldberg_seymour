\begin{lemma}[Minimum bad pair: no repeated color on the path suffix]
Let \((\varphi,T)\) be a minimum bad pair with \(\rho(T)>2\) and
\(\rho(T)<|E(T)|\).  It is also impossible that no color missing at the
final vertex \(y_n\) is missing at any \(y_i\) with
\(\rho(T)\le i<n\).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Write \(T=(y_0,e_1,y_1,\ldots,e_n,y_n)\) and
\(q=\rho(T)<n\).  Unfolding \noderef{TashTreeMinimumBadPair} and
\noderef{TashTreeBadPair} gives the fixed \(k\)-triple, the Tashkinov
condition, non-elementariness of \(V(T)\), and the universal exclusion of
all smaller bad pairs.  Since \(q>2\), all prefixes below have positive
length.

The proper prefix \(T_{n-1}\), as defined by
\noderef{TreeSequencePrefix}, is elementary by
\noderef{TashTreeProperPrefixElementary}, whereas \(T\) is not.
By \noderef{ColoredVertexSetElementary}, there is therefore a color
\(\beta\) missing at \(y_n\) and at some earlier vertex \(y_i\): a pair
of repeated missing-color witnesses cannot both lie in the elementary
prefix.  The hypothesis of the present case forces \(i<q\).

We first arrange \(i\le q-2\).  Suppose \(i=q-1\).  Apply
\noderef{TashTreeFourUnusedColors} to \(T_{q-2}\).  Its four colors in
\noderef{TreeFreeColors} are missing at vertices of that prefix by
\noderef{TreeMissingColors} and unused on its colored edge identities by
\noderef{TreeUsedColors}.  The one additional edge \(e_{q-1}\) excludes
at most one of them, so at least three remain unused through
\(y_{q-1}\).  Choose one \(\alpha\ne\beta\), missing at \(y_m\) with
\(m\le q-2\).

The color \(\beta\) is also unused through \(y_{q-1}\).  If an edge
\(e_h\), \(2\le h\le q-1\), had color \(\beta\), its earlier witness from
\noderef{TashkinovTree} would be distinct from the
\(\beta\)-missing \(y_{q-1}\), contradicting elementariness of the proper
prefix \(T_{q-1}\) supplied by
\noderef{TashTreeProperPrefixElementary}.  Thus
\noderef{TashTreeControlledInterchange} applies to
\(\alpha,\beta\) at \(y_m,y_{q-1}\), producing another minimum bad pair
in which \(\beta\) is missing at \(y_m\).

The vertex \(y_n\) lies outside this controlled component.  The component
is a path with the two distinct ends \(y_m,y_{q-1}\), while
\noderef{MissingColorIncidenceEndpoint} makes the
\(\beta\)-missing \(y_n\) a third vertex of degree at most one.
\noderef{TwoColorComponentSpanningPath} therefore rules out its membership.
Consequently \(\beta\) remains missing at \(y_n\).

This normalization also preserves the present case hypothesis.  Its two
path ends have indices below \(q\).  Every vertex \(y_s\) with
\(q\le s\le n\) is either outside the component, when all its incident
colors are fixed, or internal to the two-color path, when one edge of each
affected color remains present after the transposition.  We just proved
the endpoint \(y_n\) is outside.  Thus every missing-color set on the
suffix \(y_q,\ldots,y_n\) is unchanged, and no color missing at \(y_n\)
becomes missing at \(y_q,\ldots,y_{n-1}\).  We may henceforth use the
normalized minimum bad pair, still satisfying the case hypothesis, with
\(\beta\) missing at \(y_i,y_n\) for some \(i\le q-2\).

Choose a color \(\gamma\) missing at \(y_q\).  We derive its existence
from the local \(k\)-triple, as follows.  By \noderef{KTriple},
\(\varphi\) is proper and \(k\ge\Delta(G)+1\).  Choosing one edge for
each color in \noderef{PresentColors} gives exact identities incident
with \(y_q\) in the sense of \noderef{Incident}.  Two such identities of
one color would be adjacent by \noderef{AdjacentEdges} and would violate
\noderef{IsProperEdgeColoring}.  Hence the choices are injective and, by
\noderef{Degree} and
\noderef{MaximumDegree},
\[
 |\varphi(y_q)|\le d_G(y_q)\le\Delta(G)<k.
\]
The complement definition \noderef{MissingColors} now supplies
\(\gamma\).

The color \(\gamma\) is unused on \(e_2,\ldots,e_q\).  Otherwise an edge
of this proper prefix with color \(\gamma\) would have an earlier
missing-\(\gamma\) witness by \noderef{TashkinovTree}, distinct from
\(y_q\), contradicting \noderef{TashTreeProperPrefixElementary} for
\(T_q\).  Also \(\gamma\ne\beta\): the current case hypothesis forbids a
color missing at \(y_n\), as \(\beta\) is, from also being missing at the
suffix vertex \(y_q\).

Apply \noderef{TashTreeFourUnusedColors} to \(T_{q-2}\) once more.
After excluding the at most two colors on the additional exact edges
\(e_{q-1},e_q\), at least two free colors remain unused through \(y_q\).
Choose one \(\varepsilon\ne\gamma\), missing at a vertex \(y_{m'}\) with
\(m'\le q-2\).

If \(\varepsilon\) is missing at \(y_n\), apply
\noderef{TashTreeControlledInterchange} to
\(\varepsilon,\gamma\) at \(y_{m'},y_q\).  The vertex \(y_n\) remains
outside the controlled component: it is a third distinct
degree-at-most-one vertex by
\noderef{MissingColorIncidenceEndpoint}, and this is impossible on the
spanning path in \noderef{TwoColorComponentSpanningPath}.  Hence
\(\varepsilon\) remains missing at \(y_n\), while the controlled
interchange makes it missing at \(y_q\) and returns a minimum bad pair.
This satisfies the repeated-color hypothesis of
\noderef{TashTreeCaseRepeatedOnPath} with index \(q<n\), a contradiction.
We may therefore assume that \(\varepsilon\) is present at \(y_n\).
Since \noderef{PresentColors} and \noderef{MissingColors} are
complements and \(\beta\) is missing there, \(\varepsilon\ne\beta\).

Let \(Q\) be the \((\varepsilon,\beta)\)-component
\noderef{TwoColorComponent} through \(y_n\).  The vertex \(y_n\) misses
\(\beta\) but has an incident \(\varepsilon\)-edge, so
\noderef{MissingColorIncidenceEndpoint} and the incidence definition
\noderef{TwoColorIncidenceGraph} make it an end of a nontrivial component.
Orient the spanning path from
\noderef{TwoColorComponentSpanningPath} from this end.

Suppose first that \(Q\) meets \(V(T_{q-1})\), and let \(y_h\) be the
first prefix vertex-node on the oriented path.  If \(h\le q-2\), retain
\(T_{q-2}\); if \(h=q-1\), retain \(T_{q-1}\).  In either case the
retained prefix contains the missing-\(\varepsilon\) witness \(y_{m'}\)
and the missing-\(\beta\) witness \(y_i\).  Append the simple alternating
segment from \(y_h\) to \(y_n\), traversed away from the prefix.  Its
internal vertex-nodes are outside the retained prefix, its vertices and
exact edge identities are distinct, its first edge attaches at the
earlier \(y_h\), and each later edge attaches to the preceding new vertex.
Thus it is a tree-sequence by \noderef{TreeSequence}.  Every appended
edge has color \(\varepsilon\) or \(\beta\) and therefore has one of the
two retained earlier missing witnesses, so it is a Tashkinov tree by
\noderef{TashkinovTree}.  It is bad by
\noderef{TashTreeBadPair}, since \(\beta\) remains missing at the
retained \(y_i\) and at \(y_n\).

If the retained prefix is \(T_{q-1}\), the entire appended part is in
path order starting at position \(q-1\).  If it is \(T_{q-2}\), the first
appended edge may attach away from the last retained vertex, but all
subsequent appended edges are in path order, again making \(q-1\) a
suffix-path start.  In both cases the new path index is at most
\(q-1<q\), so \noderef{TashTreeRankLess} and the universal clause of
\noderef{TashTreeMinimumBadPair} give a contradiction.

It remains that \(Q\) avoids \(V(T_{q-1})\).  Every exact edge identity
of \(T_q\) has an earlier endpoint in that vertex set by the attachment
field of \noderef{TreeSequence}.  Hence no such edge-node belongs to
\(Q\), since its incidence edge would put that endpoint in the same
component.  Let
\[
 \varphi_1=
 \operatorname{TwoColorComponentSwap}
   (G,\varphi,\varepsilon,\beta,y_n)
\]
be the swap from \noderef{TwoColorComponentSwap}.  We verify directly
that \((\varphi_1,T)\) is a minimum bad pair.

It is proper by \noderef{TwoColorComponentSwapProper}.  The swap fixes
uncolored values and sends colored values to colored values, so
\noderef{IsOneEdgeUncolored} remains true for the same \(e_0\), while
all other graph and numerical clauses of \noderef{KTriple} are unchanged.
For each displayed edge through \(T_q\), both the edge-node and every
earlier witness vertex lie outside \(Q\), so the old color and missing
witness are fixed.  For a later edge, colors outside
\(\{\varepsilon,\beta\}\) and their witnesses are unchanged.  If an
affected edge or witness changes, use the fixed vertex \(y_{m'}\), which
misses \(\varepsilon\), or \(y_i\), which misses \(\beta\), according
to the edge's new color.  Both indices are below \(q\), hence earlier than
every such edge.  These cases prove the full condition
\noderef{TashkinovTree} under \(\varphi_1\).

At the end \(y_n\), the unique incident \(\varepsilon\)-edge becomes
\(\beta\), so \(\varepsilon\) becomes missing.  The vertex \(y_{m'}\)
lies in \(T_{q-1}\), outside \(Q\), and remains missing
\(\varepsilon\).  Thus \(V(T)\) remains non-elementary, and
\noderef{TashTreeBadPair} holds for \((\varphi_1,T)\).  Its sequence and
rank are unchanged.  The original universal minimality clause quantifies
over all colorings, so it combines with this badness to give
\noderef{TashTreeMinimumBadPair} for \((\varphi_1,T)\).

We also check that this second normalization preserves the no-repetition
case hypothesis.  The proper prefix \(T_{n-1}\) is elementary for the
pre-swap minimum bad pair by
\noderef{TashTreeProperPrefixElementary}.  Since \(\varepsilon\) is
missing at \(y_{m'}\) and \(\beta\) at \(y_i\), no other prefix vertex,
in particular no \(y_s\) with \(q\le s<n\), misses either affected
color before the swap.  A suffix vertex outside \(Q\) is fixed; one
internal to its spanning path has both affected colors present before and
after; and it cannot be the other end, since an end would miss one of
those two colors.  At \(y_n\), the swap replaces missing \(\beta\) by
missing \(\varepsilon\).  Therefore after the swap neither affected color
is missing both at \(y_n\) and at a vertex \(y_s\), \(q\le s<n\), and
all other colors are unchanged.  The case hypothesis is preserved.

No edge of \(T_q\) changed, so \(\varepsilon,\gamma\) are still unused
through \(y_q\).  The vertex \(y_{m'}\) still misses \(\varepsilon\),
and \(y_q\) still misses \(\gamma\), since
\(\gamma\notin\{\varepsilon,\beta\}\).  Apply
\noderef{TashTreeControlledInterchange} to these colors and vertices.
The final vertex \(y_n\), now missing \(\varepsilon\), is outside this
controlled component: the component already has the distinct ends
\(y_{m'},y_q\), while
\noderef{MissingColorIncidenceEndpoint} and
\noderef{TwoColorComponentSpanningPath} forbid \(y_n\) as a third end.
Thus \(\varepsilon\) remains missing at \(y_n\), and the controlled swap
makes it missing at \(y_q\).  The returned pair is minimum bad and falls
under \noderef{TashTreeCaseRepeatedOnPath}, again a contradiction.  This
exhausts the no-repeated-color case and proves it impossible.
\end{proof}

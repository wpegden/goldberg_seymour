\begin{lemma}[Scheide structural reduction and exit-color bijection (1)]
Every Scheide structural configuration admits a reordered tree-sequence
$T'$ and reduced configuration on $T'$ with $V(T')=V(T)$.  Every old base
vertex remains in the new base.  For each old block, either its color,
two vertex identities, and two edge identities occur together as a new
block, or both of its vertices have been absorbed into the new base.
Consequently the exits are in bijection with the remaining, pairwise
distinct block colors as specified by the reduced configuration.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Write
\[
 X=V(T)=\operatorname{TreeSequenceVertexSet}(T),\qquad
 F=\operatorname{ScheideExitVertices}(T,\varphi).
\]
The last field of the given
\noderef{ScheideStructuralConfiguration} is a
\noderef{GlobalMaximumTriple}.  Applying
\noderef{ScheideGlobalMaximumOrder} to that field says, in particular,
that $X$ is elementary.  We use elementariness in the precise sense of
\noderef{ColoredVertexSetElementary}: two distinct members of $X$ have
disjoint sets of missing colors, where those sets are the sets defined in
\noderef{MissingColors}.

We first record uniqueness for every old block color.  Fix
$i\in\operatorname{Fin}(C.s)$.  The missing-on-base field (S2) of
\noderef{ScheideStructuralConfiguration} gives $q\in\operatorname{Fin}(C.p)$
such that $C.\gamma(i)$ is missing at $C.y(q)$.  The exact base-position
field identifies $C.y(q)$ with the vertex of $T$ at position $q$, so
\noderef{TreeSequenceVertexSet} gives $C.y(q)\in X$.  Thus the color is
missing at at least one member of $X$.  If it were missing at
$x,x'\in X$, with $x\ne x'$, then it would belong to both missing-color
sets, contradicting their disjointness.  Therefore
\[
 \tag{A}
 \text{for each old index $i$, $C.\gamma(i)$ is missing at exactly one
 member of $X$.}
\]

Membership in $F$, after unfolding \noderef{ScheideExitVertices}, gives
the predicate \noderef{ScheideExitVertex}.  One of the final clauses of
that predicate is that the chosen exit vertex belongs to $X$.  Hence every
$v\in F$ belongs to $X$.  For each member $x=(v,h_v)$ of the finite subtype
\[
 \widehat F:=\{v:V\mid v\in F\},
\]
the exit-coverage field (S4) of
\noderef{ScheideStructuralConfiguration} supplies an index
$\iota(x)\in\operatorname{Fin}(C.s)$ with
\[
 \tag{B} C.\gamma(\iota(x))\in\overline\varphi(v).
\]
Make these finitely many choices once and for all.  If
$\iota(x)=\iota(x')$, then (B) makes the same old block color missing at
the two underlying exit vertices.  Both lie in $X$, so (A) makes those
vertices equal, and equality in the subtype then gives $x=x'$.  Thus
$\iota$ is injective.  Since the old color map $C.\gamma$ is injective as
well, $x\mapsto C.\gamma(\iota(x))$ is injective.  More strongly, if the
selected color belonging to $x'$ is missing at the vertex underlying $x$,
then (A), applied also to (B) for $x'$, gives $x=x'$.  Consequently every
exit misses exactly its one selected old block color.

We now fix exact finite enumerations.  Put $r=|F|$ and choose a bijection
\[
 b:\operatorname{Fin}(r)\longrightarrow\widehat F.
\]
Define the retained old index
$\rho(t)=\iota(b(t))$.  The map $\rho$ is injective.  Let
\[
 U=\{i\in\operatorname{Fin}(C.s):
       i\notin\operatorname{range}(\rho)\},\qquad a=|U|,
\]
and choose a bijection $\upsilon:\operatorname{Fin}(a)\to U$.
The range of $\rho$ has $r$ members, its complement has $a$ members, and
the two sets partition $\operatorname{Fin}(C.s)$.  Counting that partition
gives
\[
 \tag{C} C.s=a+r.
\]
It also gives the following exact alternative for every old index $i$:
either there is a unique $h\in\operatorname{Fin}(a)$ with
$\upsilon(h).1=i$, or there is a unique $t\in\operatorname{Fin}(r)$ with
$\rho(t)=i$.  The alternatives are disjoint.  Define the new indexed block
colors by
\[
 \tag{D} \gamma'(t)=C.\gamma(\rho(t))
 \quad(t\in\operatorname{Fin}(r)).
\]
They are pairwise distinct because both $\rho$ and $C.\gamma$ are
injective.

Here is the reordered list with all positions specified.  Its vertices,
in zero-based order, are
\[
\begin{array}{ll}
 C.y(q) & (0\le q<C.p),\\
 C.\operatorname{blockVertex}(\upsilon(h).1,j)
   & (q=C.p+2h+j,\ 0\le h<a,\ 0\le j<2),\\
 C.\operatorname{blockVertex}(\rho(t),j)
   & (q=C.p+2a+2t+j,\ 0\le t<r,\ 0\le j<2),
\end{array}
\]
and its edge identities, also in zero-based order, are
\[
\begin{array}{ll}
 C.\operatorname{baseEdge}(q) & (0\le q<C.p-1),\\
 C.\operatorname{blockEdge}(\upsilon(h).1,j)
   & (q=C.p-1+2h+j,\ 0\le h<a,\ 0\le j<2),\\
 C.\operatorname{blockEdge}(\rho(t),j)
   & (q=C.p-1+2a+2t+j,\ 0\le t<r,\ 0\le j<2).
\end{array}
\]
Call this alternating list $T'$.  The three intervals in each display are
disjoint and exhaustive; within each two-place block the quotient and
remainder on division by two recover the indicated $h$ or $t$ and $j$.
By (C), its number of vertices is
$C.p+2a+2r=C.p+2C.s=T.\operatorname{length}+1$, using the original exact
length field.  Thus its number of edge identities is
$T.\operatorname{length}$.

We next check every field of \noderef{TreeSequence}.  Positivity follows
from $C.p\ge2$.  Because $C.p-1\ge1$, the first new edge is the old
$C.\operatorname{baseEdge}(0)$; the old base-position equation and the
first-edge field of $T$ show that this exact identity is $e_0$.  Every new
vertex entry is an old entry, at respectively an old base, unselected-block,
or retained-block position.  Conversely, the exact alternative following
(C) puts every old block entry into exactly one of the last two classes.
Thus the new vertex-position map is a permutation of the old one.  The same
argument, using the displayed edge positions, makes the new edge-position
map a permutation of the old one.  Vertex and individual-edge injectivity
therefore follow from the two injectivity fields of the old
\noderef{TreeSequence}; in particular, no two parallel edge identities are
identified by the reordering.

For attachment, consider an exact new edge position.  An old-base edge has
the same position as in $T$, and the earlier endpoint furnished by the old
tree-sequence lies in the unchanged old-base prefix.  An absorbed edge is
$C.\operatorname{blockEdge}(\upsilon(h).1,j)$; the block-attachment field
(S1) supplies $q<C.p$ and the exact endpoint equation
\[
 G.\operatorname{ends}
   (C.\operatorname{blockEdge}(\upsilon(h).1,j))
 =\{C.y(q),C.\operatorname{blockVertex}(\upsilon(h).1,j)\}.
\]
The vertex $C.y(q)$ occurs in the old-base prefix, strictly before this
absorbed vertex.  A retained edge has the identical verification with
$\rho(t)$ in place of $\upsilon(h).1$; its old-base endpoint is also
strictly earlier.  These three cases establish the attachment field for
every edge of $T'$, so the displayed data do define a tree-sequence.

The new sequence is also a Tashkinov tree.  By
\noderef{GlobalMaximumTriple}, the old globally maximum field contains a
proof of \noderef{TashkinovTree} for $T$.  For a nonfirst old-base edge,
its old Tashkinov witness is at an earlier old-base vertex, and both exact
positions are unchanged.  For an absorbed block edge, (S2) gives
\[
 \varphi(C.\operatorname{blockEdge}(\upsilon(h).1,j))
   =C.\gamma(\upsilon(h).1)
\]
and gives an old base vertex missing that color.  The same two clauses with
$\rho(t)$ verify every retained block edge.  In each case the missing
witness occurs in the first $C.p$ positions and is therefore earlier than
the edge being checked.  This proves the defining missing-color clause of
\noderef{TashkinovTree} for every nonfirst edge of $T'$.

The permutation argument and \noderef{TreeSequenceVertexSet} give
\[
 \tag{E} \operatorname{TreeSequenceVertexSet}(T')=X.
\]
Indeed, the left side is the image of the displayed new vertex map: every
new entry is an old entry, and every old base or old block entry occurs in
one of the three new intervals.  We can therefore rebuild global
maximality, rather than merely assert that it survives.  Unfolding
\noderef{GlobalMaximumTriple} in the old global field gives a
\noderef{KTriple} for the unchanged quadruple $(G,k,e_0,\varphi)$, the old
Tashkinov assertion, and $|X|=t(G)$.  The $K$-triple is literally unchanged,
the preceding paragraph supplies the new Tashkinov assertion, and (E)
transfers the cardinality equality.  Hence
$(T',e_0,\varphi)$ is a global maximum triple.

Before constructing the new structural data, we prove the decisive exit-set
identity
\[
 \tag{F}
 \operatorname{ScheideExitVertices}(T',\varphi)
 =\operatorname{ScheideExitVertices}(T,\varphi)=F.
\]
By \noderef{ScheideExitVertices}, it is enough to prove the two exit
predicates equivalent for each vertex $v$.  Unfold
\noderef{ScheideExitVertex} and take witnesses
$u,\gamma,\delta,z$, the same incidence walk $P$, and the same selected
position on $P$.  Every occurrence of the tree in this predicate is an
occurrence of its vertex set.  More explicitly, (E) rewrites the assertion
that the start vertex $u$ lies in the tree.  It also rewrites
\[
 \delta\in
 \operatorname{DefectiveColors}(G,\varphi,
     \operatorname{TreeSequenceVertexSet}(T)).
\]
Here \noderef{DefectiveColors} is the finite set filtered by the predicate
\noderef{DefectiveColor}, and that predicate uses the given vertex set in
its boundary-edge count, so replacing equal vertex sets replaces this
membership with the corresponding one for $T'$.

The remaining tree-dependent assertion is
\noderef{ScheideDefectiveChainPath}.  Its start-membership condition is
rewritten by (E), its assertion that the terminal vertex $z$ is outside the
tree is rewritten by (E), and its path and full-component assertions do not
change because $G,\varphi,\delta,\gamma,u,z$, and the exact walk $P$ have
not changed.  Finally (E) rewrites both the membership of the selected exit
vertex $v$ and, for every later vertex-node of $P$, the assertion that this
later vertex is outside the tree.  Thus the same witnesses prove the
$T'$-exit predicate from the $T$-exit predicate.  The same replacements in
the reverse direction prove the converse.  This proves (F), including all
endpoint-outside and later-vertex exclusions and without changing any
edge-node identity in the defective chain.

We now define the new \noderef{ScheideStructuralConfiguration}.  Put
\[
 p'=C.p+2a,\qquad s'=r.
\]
Its base vertices are the first $p'$ vertices in the first two intervals
of the display for $T'$, and its base edges are the first $p'-1$ edge
identities in the corresponding first two intervals.  Its $t$-th block
uses color (D) and the two vertices and two exact edge identities of the old
$\rho(t)$-block.  We verify its fields in their defining order.

First, $p'\ge2$ follows from $C.p\ge2$, and (D) is injective.  Equation (C)
and the displayed list give
$T'.\operatorname{length}+1=p'+2s'$.  The four exact-position fields for
base vertices, block vertices, base edges, and block edges hold by the
three defining intervals.  The retained block-attachment equations are the
old (S1) equations; their endpoints $C.y(q)$ occur among the first $C.p$
new base vertices.  The retained block-color and missing-on-base fields are
the old (S2) equations and witnesses, again with their witnesses in that
old-base prefix.

For the new base-color exclusion (S3), let a nonfirst new base edge have
color $c$.  If it is an old base edge, the old (S3) clause says that $c$
differs from every old block color, hence from every retained color (D).
If it is an absorbed edge, then old (S2) identifies its color as
$C.\gamma(\upsilon(h).1)$.  Equality with a retained color
$C.\gamma(\rho(t))$ would, by injectivity of $C.\gamma$, give
$\upsilon(h).1=\rho(t)$.  This is impossible because $\upsilon(h)$ lies in
the complement of the range of $\rho$.  Thus (S3) holds both for the old
base edges and for every absorbed block edge now lying in the enlarged
base.

For exit coverage (S4), take $v$ in the new exit set.  By (F),
$x=(v,h_v)$ is a member of $\widehat F$.  There is a unique
$t\in\operatorname{Fin}(r)$ with $b(t)=x$.  Equations (B) and (D) say that
the retained color $\gamma'(t)$ is missing at $v$.  The no-isolated-vertex
field is copied verbatim from $C$, since neither the graph nor its edge
identities changed.  The final global-maximum field was established above
from the unchanged \noderef{KTriple}, the checked Tashkinov property, and
(E).  This constructs every field of the new structural configuration,
including its new values $p'$ and $s'$.

It remains to supply exactly the three additional fields of
\noderef{ScheideReducedConfiguration}.  By (F), the definition of $r$, and
$s'=r$, the new exit set has cardinality $s'$.  Let $v$ be a new exit and
again write $x=(v,h_v)$.  The unique index $t=b^{-1}(x)$ has
$\gamma'(t)$ missing at $v$ by (B).  If also $\gamma'(t')$ is missing at
$v$, then it is the selected old block color belonging to the exit
$b(t')$.  Assertion (A), together with (B) for $b(t')$, forces the
underlying vertex of $b(t')$ to be $v$.  Hence $b(t')=x=b(t)$ and, by
injectivity of $b$, $t'=t$.  This is the exact `exit exactly one' field.
Conversely, for each $t\in\operatorname{Fin}(s')$, the exit underlying
$b(t)$ belongs to the new exit set by (F), and (B) says it misses
$\gamma'(t)$.  This is the `every block color at an exit' field.  Thus
$\operatorname{Fin}(s')$ has been identified with the retained colors, and
the cardinality, unique-missing, and surjectivity assertions have all been
proved separately.

Let $R$ denote this reduced configuration.  Equation (E) is the required
vertex-set equality.  Each old base vertex $C.y(q)$ is literally the new
base vertex at position $q$, so it lies in the image of $R.y$.  Finally,
fix an old block index $i$.  In the retained alternative after (C), its
unique $t$ satisfies $i=\rho(t)$, and the definitions give simultaneously
\[
 C.\gamma(i)=R.\gamma(t),\qquad
 C.\operatorname{blockVertex}(i,j)=R.\operatorname{blockVertex}(t,j),
 \qquad
 C.\operatorname{blockEdge}(i,j)=R.\operatorname{blockEdge}(t,j)
\]
for both $j\in\operatorname{Fin}(2)$.  In the complementary alternative,
the unique $h$ with $\upsilon(h).1=i$ puts both old vertices at the new base
positions $C.p+2h+j$, so both belong to the image of $R.y$.  These are the
two alternatives in the conclusion, with every original parallel-edge
identity retained exactly.
\end{proof}

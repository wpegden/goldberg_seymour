\begin{lemma}[Step 1, Subcase 1.1: the later end is outside the starred predecessor]
Let $G$ be a finite loopless multigraph with individual edge identities,
$e_0$ a distinguished edge, and $[k]$ the color set.  Let $T$ be a
positive-rung ETT with generating coloring $\varphi$, maximum property,
and a certified Tashkinov series.  Assume that $\varphi$ is proper and
leaves exactly $e_0$ uncolored.  Assume the complete lower-rung package:
for every positive-rung maximum ETT of smaller rung, its terminal vertex
set is elementary, its final ladder closure has the stated stable
interchangeability property, PE stability descends and equal-level PE
stored pairs are disjoint, a terminal PE component meets its ladder tree
only at its support, nonminimum defective endpoints precede the later
allowed PE/SE vertices, and every terminal stable coloring is a
mod-coloring.

Let $B$ be a hierarchy base for $T$, let $H$ be a good hierarchy with
$d$ dividers, and fix an integer $q_0>d$.  Assume uniformly that every
same-rung maximum ETT, built from an arbitrary certified input and
generating coloring and carrying a good hierarchy with fewer than $q_0$
dividers, is elementary.  Assume also the large-branch estimate
$|\overline\varphi(T_n)|\geq 2n+11$.  Write
$A=H.\mathit{starred}(d)$ for the final starred predecessor.  Besides all
five fields of the good hierarchy, assume separately that the terminal
tree is
\[
 \left(\bigcup_{\eta\in D^H_{n,d+1}}\Gamma^d_\eta\right)^-
 \text{-closed under }\varphi,
\]
and assume that $V(T)$ is elementary under $\varphi$.

Let $\alpha\ne\beta$, with $\alpha$ missing and singleton-closed on $T$
and $\beta$ missing on $T$.  Let $P_1,P_2$ be exact
$(T,\varphi,\{\alpha,\beta\})$-exit paths, with respective outside ends
$a_1,a_2$, tree ends $b_1,b_2$, disjoint incidence-walk supports, and
distinct outside and tree ends.  Order them so that $b_1\prec_T b_2$.
For each $j$, retain the exact edge identity $f_j$ on $P_j$ incident
with $b_j$, and assume that $f_j$ is a $\beta$-colored boundary edge of
$T$.  If $b_2\notin V(A)$, then these data are contradictory.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Write $n$ for the positive rung number, $T_n$ for the last ladder tree,
$D_n$ for its exact cumulative stored-color set, $\mathcal E$ for the
given ETT certificate, and $\mathcal G$ for the given good-hierarchy
witness.  Put
\[
 A=H.\mathit{starred}(d),\qquad
 C_\Gamma=\bigcup_{\eta\in D^H_{n,d+1}}\Gamma^d_\eta.
\]
We first justify all of the prefix and missing-color facts used below.
Unfold \noderef{TashkinovHierarchy}.  When $d>0$,
\noderef{HierarchyStarredStage} identifies $A$ with the $d$-th ordinary
stage, and the nested and terminal-stage fields put it before $T$.  When
$d=0$, $A$ is the hierarchy core, and the core-inside-first and terminal
fields give the same conclusion.  Each comparison is an entrywise
ordered prefix in the sense of \noderef{TreeSequenceInitialSegment}; in
particular \noderef{TreeSequenceVertexSet} gives $V(A)\subseteq V(T)$.

The identity $f_2$ lies in the exact $\beta$-colored boundary of $T$.
If $\beta\notin C_\Gamma$, the definition
\noderef{TreeColorSetMinusClosed}, together with
$\beta\in\overline\varphi(T)$, would say that this boundary is empty.
Thus $\beta\in\Gamma^d_\eta$ for some
$\eta\in D^H_{n,d+1}$.  By
\noderef{HierarchyTerminalStoredColors}, the latter set is
$D_n-\overline\varphi(T)$, whereas
\noderef{HierarchyStoredColorsAt} identifies $D^H_{n,d}$ with
$D_n-\overline\varphi(A)$.  The ordered-prefix inclusion just proved and
\noderef{TreeMissingColors} give
$\overline\varphi(A)\subseteq\overline\varphi(T)$, and hence
\[
                 D^H_{n,d+1}\subseteq D^H_{n,d}.              \tag{1}
\]
This is the terminal instance of the same subtraction argument as
\noderef{HierarchyStoredColorsAntitone}, whose ordinary-stage instances
give all earlier stored-set inclusions.  Now
\noderef{GoodHierarchyReservedDisjoint}, applied at the exact index
$(d,\eta)$, puts $\Gamma^d_\eta$ inside
$\overline\varphi(A)$.  Consequently
\[
                       \beta\in\overline\varphi(A).           \tag{2}
\]

There is a color $\gamma$ missing at $b_2$.  Indeed, the initial member
of the certified \noderef{TashkinovSeries} is the initial constructor of
\noderef{TashkinovSeriesStep}; its \noderef{KTriple} field gives
$k\geq\Delta(G)+1$.  Unfold \noderef{IsProperEdgeColoring}.  For each
color, at most one edge of that color is incident with $b_2$, since two
such identities are adjacent by \noderef{AdjacentEdges} and would be
colored edges of equal color.  Thus the
map which chooses the unique incident edge of each member of
\noderef{PresentColors} at $b_2$ is injective.  By \noderef{Degree} and
\noderef{MaximumDegree}, there are at most
$d_G(b_2)\leq\Delta(G)$ present colors.  The complementary set in
\noderef{MissingColors} is therefore nonempty; choose $\gamma$ in it.

If $\gamma\in C_\Gamma$, (1) and
\noderef{GoodHierarchyReservedDisjoint} give a vertex of $A$ missing
$\gamma$.  It is different from $b_2$, because $b_2\notin V(A)$, while
both vertices lie in $T$.  This contradicts the stated
\noderef{ColoredVertexSetElementary} property.  Therefore
$\gamma\notin C_\Gamma$.  Since $\gamma$ is missing on $T$ at $b_2$,
the terminal minus-closed certificate and
\noderef{TreeColorSetClosed} show that its exact colored boundary is
empty.  The same is true for $\alpha$ by hypothesis.  Finally
$\gamma\ne\beta$: the edge $f_2$ is incident with $b_2$ and has color
$\beta$, whereas $\gamma$ is missing there.

We next construct the first recoloring and both of its exit paths.  If
$\alpha=\gamma$, put $\mu_1=\varphi$.  Otherwise select every full
$(\alpha,\gamma)$ component containing an $\alpha$-edge node of either
$P_1$ or $P_2$, and also the component through $a_j$ whenever $a_j$
misses $\alpha$, and swap all selected components.  Every selected
component is wholly outside $T$.  To see this, use the connected-component
definition \noderef{TwoColorComponent}; if such a component met $T$, a
shortest walk in \noderef{TwoColorIncidenceGraph} from its selected
outside node to $T$ would have a first vertex-node in $T$.  The preceding
edge identity would be a boundary edge of color $\alpha$ or $\gamma$,
contrary to the two closedness conclusions.  This also proves directly
that every component through one of the outside ends which we selected is
wholly outside $T$.  There are only finitely many components, so these
data satisfy every field of \noderef{OutsideKempeRecoloring}.

By \noderef{OutsideKempeRecoloringStable}, in the unequal branch
$\mu_1$ is proper, leaves exactly $e_0$ uncolored, fixes every exact edge
identity incident with $T$, and preserves every vertex missing-color set
on $T$; it is stable there for every control set.  The equality branch
has the same assertions by the reflexive clause of \noderef{sc2}.
In the unequal branch apply \noderef{HierarchyLEMStable}; retain from its
conclusion an actual corresponding same-rung ETT $\mathcal E_1$ under
$\mu_1$, maximum property, a base $B_1$, a hierarchy $H_1$, and a good
witness $\mathcal G_1$.  In the equality branch take
$(\mathcal E_1,B_1,H_1,\mathcal G_1)$ to be the original
$(\mathcal E,B,H,\mathcal G)$ data.  Thus no identity recoloring is
passed to a predicate requiring distinct colors.  In both branches the
relation \noderef{SameGoodHierarchy} (reflexive in the equality branch)
identifies the ordered stages, dividers, paper-relevant stored sets and
Gamma pairs with those of $H$, and in the PE case identifies the base
closure.  The terminal minus-closed certificate is transported in the
unequal branch and is the original certificate in the equality branch.

For $j=1,2$, let $Q_j$ have exactly the same ordered vertex- and
edge-node list as $P_j$.  We verify all fields of
\noderef{TreeExitPath} for $(T,\mu_1,\gamma,\beta,a_j,b_j,Q_j)$.
Every old $\beta$-edge stays $\beta$, since $\beta$ is distinct from both
colors of the first swap.  Every old $\alpha$-edge of $P_j$ lies outside
$T$ and its full component was selected, so it becomes $\gamma$.
Therefore each consecutive incidence in the old walk is an incidence in
$I_G(\mu_1;\gamma,\beta)$ by
\noderef{TwoColorIncidenceGraph}; the same node list is still a simple,
positive-length walk.  Its support, its two ends, and the assertion that
its only tree vertex is $b_j$ are literally unchanged.

It remains to check the outside-end missing-color alternative, which is
not implied merely by relabeling the walk.  If $a_j$ missed $\beta$ under
$\varphi$, no edge acquires color $\beta$, so the definitions
\noderef{PresentColors} and \noderef{MissingColors} show that it still
misses $\beta$.  If it missed $\alpha$, then in the equality branch it
already misses $\gamma=\alpha$.  In the unequal branch we selected the
full $(\alpha,\gamma)$ component through $a_j$; every incident edge of
either selected color belongs to that component by
\noderef{TwoColorIncidenceGraph}.  After the swap the incident
$\gamma$-edges are exactly the old incident $\alpha$-edges.  There were
none, so $a_j$ misses $\gamma$ under $\mu_1$.  This completes, field by
field, the two exit-path certificates
\[
       Q_j:\operatorname{TreeExitPath}
         (T,\mu_1,\gamma,\beta,a_j,b_j)\qquad(j=1,2).          \tag{3}
\]

Let $K_2$ be the full $(\gamma,\beta)$ component of $b_2$ under
$\mu_1$.  We prove that the support of $K_2$ is exactly the support of
$Q_2$.  In \noderef{TwoColorIncidenceGraph}, the neighbors of the
vertex-node $b_2$ are precisely its incident $\gamma$- or
$\beta$-colored edge identities.  It has no $\gamma$-neighbor because
$\gamma$ is missing there.  It has the $\beta$-neighbor $f_2$, and
unfolding \noderef{IsProperEdgeColoring} excludes a second incident
$\beta$-edge.  Hence its incidence degree is exactly one.  At $a_2$,
(3) says that one of $\gamma,\beta$ is missing, while the nontrivial path
supplies an incident edge of the other color.  Properness again gives
exactly one neighbor.  Every internal node of the simple path $Q_2$ has
its two distinct path neighbors, and
\noderef{TwoColorIncidenceDegreeAtMostTwo} says it has no third.

If a node of the connected component $K_2$ were outside $Q_2$, take a
shortest component walk from it to $Q_2$.  Its first attachment cannot be
at an internal node, which already has degree two, and cannot be at either
endpoint, whose exact degree is one and whose unique neighbor is already
the path neighbor.  This contradiction, using the support definition in
\noderef{TwoColorComponent}, proves $K_2=\operatorname{supp}(Q_2)$; it is
also the concrete spanning path supplied by
\noderef{TwoColorComponentSpanningPath}.  By (3), its only vertex-node in
$T$ is $b_2$.

Unfold \noderef{TreeVertexOrder}.  Let $s$ be the unique displayed
position of $b_2$ in $T$.  The last ladder tree $T_n$ is an initial
segment of the core: this is equality in the non-PE clause of
\noderef{HierarchyBase}, and is the left-segment clause of
\noderef{OrderedTreeUnion} in PE.  The core in turn is an initial segment
of $A$ by the hierarchy fields.  Since every \noderef{TreeSequence} has
length at least one and $b_2\notin V(A)$, its position satisfies
$s>|A|\geq1$, so $s\geq2$.  In particular the predecessor prefix below
is a valid tree-sequence.  Define the exact ordered prefixes
\[
 T^-:=T\text{ through the vertex immediately before position }s,
 \qquad C:=T\text{ through }b_2.
                                                               \tag{4}
\]
These are the corresponding instances of
\noderef{TreeSequencePrefix}; entrywise they satisfy
$A\preccurlyeq T^-\prec C\preccurlyeq T$ by the prefix description of
$A$ above and $b_2\notin V(A)$.  If $g$ is the displayed edge at
position $s$, then \noderef{TreeSequenceAdjoinEdge} says exactly that
$C=T^-+g$.  Since $K_2$ has no vertex of $T^-$, it has no edge node
incident with $T^-$: such an incidence would connect the edge node to a
vertex-node of $T^-$ in the incidence graph.  In particular $g\notin
K_2$, because its earlier endpoint belongs to $T^-$.  Thus $K_2$ is
disjoint from every displayed edge of $C$ and from every edge incident
with $T^-$.

Define $\mu_2$ by swapping $\gamma$ and $\beta$ on $K_2$.  The two
colors are distinct, so \noderef{TwoColorComponentSwapProper} makes
$\mu_2$ proper.  By \noderef{TwoColorComponentSwap}, the swap fixes the
uncolored value and only transposes two nonempty colors; hence the unique
uncolored edge remains exactly $e_0$ by
\noderef{IsOneEdgeUncolored}.  The preceding disjointness says
that $\mu_2$ and $\mu_1$ agree on every edge incident with $T^-$ and at
every missing-color set of a vertex of $T^-$.  Since
$T_n\preccurlyeq A\preccurlyeq T^-$, both clauses of
\noderef{StableColoring} hold on $T_n$ with control $D_n$.  Composing
this relation with the $(T_n,D_n,\varphi)$-stability of $\mu_1$ by
\noderef{sc2} gives
\[
       \mu_2\text{ is }(T_n,D_n,\varphi)\text{-stable}.        \tag{5}
\]

We now construct, rather than assume, a corresponding ETT on the exact
tree $C$.  Inspect the terminal positive certificate in
\noderef{TashkinovSeries} and
\noderef{TashkinovSeriesStep}.  In the PE case it contains a
\noderef{TashkinovClosure} of $T_n$ under $\varphi$; in the SE and RE
cases it contains an exact edge identity $f_n$, the equality
$F_n=\{f_n\}$, the exact seed $T_n+f_n$, and a closure of that seed.
The below-final field of \noderef{ExtendedTashkinovTree} says that the
final tree of this closure contains $T$, hence $C$, as an exact ordered
prefix.  In PE, truncate its
\noderef{TashkinovAugmentationSequence} when its length first reaches
$|C|$.  In SE/RE, the seed and $C$ are both prefixes of the same final
closure and $|T_n|+1\leq |C|$; entrywise prefix equality shows that the
exact seed, including the identity $f_n$, is a prefix of $C$.  Truncate
that closure at $C$.  Thus the appropriate old fixed-color augmentation
route ends exactly at $C$ in all three cases.

Transport this route one augmentation at a time from $\varphi$ to
$\mu_2$.  For a step before the last one, its new displayed edge and its
old-prefix missing witness lie in $T^-$.  The first recoloring fixes every
edge incident with $T$ and every missing set on $T$, and the second fixes
every edge incident with $T^-$ and every missing set there.  Hence the
same exact edge has the same color under $\mu_2$, and the same old vertex
still misses that color.  For the last step $T^-\to C$, the missing
witness still lies in $T^-$ and the new edge is the exact identity $g$,
which was proved not to lie in $K_2$; its color is again unchanged.
Length, attachment, and ordered-prefix clauses do not involve the
coloring.  These are every field of
\noderef{TashkinovAugmentation}; induction gives the corresponding
\noderef{TashkinovAugmentationSequence} under $\mu_2$, with the PE seed
$T_n$ or the same exact SE/RE seed $T_n+f_n$.

Apply \noderef{Td} to properness, exact one-uncoloredness, (5), the
given maximum property, positivity, and the complete lower-rung package.
It says that $\mu_2$ is a \noderef{ModColoring} of the original ETT.
The just-constructed route, $T_n\prec C$, and the exact SE/RE singleton
identity are precisely the relevant eligibility alternative in that
definition.  Its universal clause therefore supplies an actual
\noderef{CorrespondingExtendedTashkinovTree} certificate
$\mathcal C$ displayed on $C$, with arbitrary replacement input and
generating coloring $\mu_2$.  By
\noderef{MaximumPropertyTransport}, its replacement ETT
$\mathcal E_2$ has maximum property and the same positive rung $n$.

We next build the hierarchy base of $\mathcal E_2$ without reading
internal PE data from correspondence.  The certificate $\mathcal C$
does identify every ladder tree, raw stored set, connecting-edge set, and
extension kind, but, as its definition explicitly warns, it does not
identify internal PE witnesses.  If the terminal kind is not PE, take
the new core to be the common $T_n$, copy the original positive support
position and its positivity proof, and copy the arbitrary closure field.
This is the nonparallel clause of \noderef{HierarchyBase}; the copied
closure field is unused.

Suppose instead that the terminal kind is PE.  Extract the actual terminal
PE constructors of the original and replacement
\noderef{TashkinovSeriesStep}.  Write their maximum-defective data as
$(v,u,f,\delta_n,\gamma_n)$ and
$(v',u',f',\delta'_n,\gamma'_n)$.  Their singleton connecting-edge
equations and the connecting-edge agreement in $\mathcal C$ give
\[
                         \{f'\}=F'_n=F_n=\{f\},
 \quad\text{so }f'=f.                                       \tag{6}
\]
The two \noderef{MaximumDefectiveChoice} certificates identify the exact
endpoint set of this individual edge both with $\{v,u\}$ and with
$\{v',u'\}$.  Their \noderef{DefectiveVertex} fields put $v,v'$ in the
common ordered tree $T_n$, while their other-end fields put $u,u'$ outside
it.  Equality of the two unordered endpoint pairs therefore has only one
orientation: $v'=v$ and $u'=u$.  The original parallel clause of
\noderef{HierarchyBase} says that $v$ is the vertex at its recorded
positive support position, so that same position certifies the actual
replacement chosen vertex $v'$; this conclusion was reconstructed from
the replacement PE constructor, not assumed from $\mathcal C$.

Use the original core and the original closure $R_n$ for the new base.
Both $T_n$ and $R_n$ embed in that core by its
\noderef{OrderedTreeUnion} witness, and the core embeds in $A$ by the
hierarchy fields.  Hence every vertex and displayed edge used by the old
closure lies in $T^-$.  The first recoloring fixes all edges incident with
$T$, and the second component has no vertex in $T^-$; consequently both
the exact displayed-edge colors and all old-prefix missing witnesses in
the old closure route are unchanged under $\mu_2$.  Transporting each
\noderef{TashkinovAugmentation} proves the same augmentation sequence.
An alleged $\mu_2$-colored boundary edge of $R_n$ in a color missing on
$R_n$ would, by the same incident-edge and vertex equalities, be the same
for $\varphi$, contradicting the old closedness.  Thus the actual
\noderef{TashkinovClosure} field is reconstructed under $\mu_2$.
The old ordered-union witness is coloring-free and copies unchanged.
Together with the support equality proved from (6), these are all fields
of the PE base.  In either terminal-kind case the terminal missing-color
compatibility field is reflexive, since both the new base coloring and
the generating coloring of $\mathcal E_2$ are $\mu_2$.  Call the resulting
base $B_2$.

It remains to construct the good hierarchy on $C$.  We make the three
prefix cases explicit.  If $A\prec T^-$, apply directly
\noderef{GoodHierarchyTerminalPrefixRepackage} to
$\mathcal E_1,B_1,H_1,\mathcal G_1$ and the exact prefix $T^-$.  It gives
a maximum ETT on $T^-$, the same $d$ dividers and stages, the same base
core and closure, identical stored sets and Gamma data, and every good
field on that terminal prefix.  If $T^-=A$ and $d>0$, apply
\noderef{GoodHierarchyPrefixRepackage} to the exact $d$-th stage.  It
gives the maximum ETT and good $(d-1)$-divider prefix through $A$, and
separately retains the last internal minus-closed certificate on $A$;
append $A$ as the $d$-th ordinary stage below $C$ and copy the old
$d$-th Gamma family and unique-refresh witness.  Finally, if $T^-=A$ and
$d=0$, work directly with the positive-rung ETT $\mathcal E_2$: take
stage zero to be $T_n$, take terminal stage $C$, and use the rebuilt core
$A$ in $B_2$.  The inclusions $T_n\preccurlyeq A=T^-\prec C$ supply
base-inside-first and strictness.  This is a direct zero-divider hierarchy
on a positive-rung ETT; no zero-rung ETT and no use of
\noderef{TechnicalTheoremBase} as a constructor occurs.

In all three cases we obtain a hierarchy $K$ over $B_2$ with exactly
$d$ dividers, ordinary stages and starred stages equal to those of $H_1$,
and terminal stage $C$.  The corresponding certificate identifies raw
stored sets, and the stability (5) identifies missing-color sets on
$T_n$.  Every starred stage lies in $A\subseteq T^-$, where $\mu_1$ and
$\mu_2$ have identical incident-edge values and vertex missing sets.
Unfolding \noderef{TashkinovStoredColors} and
\noderef{HierarchyStoredColorsAt} therefore proves, at every exact index,
\[
                 D^K_{n,j}=D^{H_1}_{n,j}.                    \tag{7}
\]
Define the new Gamma data to be the corresponding Gamma data of
$\mathcal G_1$.

We verify every field of \noderef{GoodHierarchy}.  The two-element and
pairwise-disjoint fields copy through (7).  The ordinary stages, their
starred predecessors, and every missing-color set on their vertex
differences are unchanged, so every unique-refresh existence and
uniqueness clause copies.  The same observation identifies all positive
internal colored boundaries; the prefix theorem supplies the last one in
the equality case, so every internal minus-closed field holds.  In PE,
the base was reconstructed with the same core and closure, and both
missing sets and every incident edge on them are fixed; hence both the
non-minus-closed assertion and the cardinality assertion of the
parallel-base field copy.  In non-PE that implication is vacuous.

Only avoidance can still mention the changed terminal tree, and we now
check it rather than calling it unchanged.  For $j<d$, the common next
ordinary stage lies in $A\subseteq T^-$.  If $\eta$ is first missing
before the end of that stage, the first position is the same under
$\mu_1$ and $\mu_2$; if not, both minima in
\noderef{HierarchySegmentColors} are capped by the common next-stage
length.  The displayed edge identities and their colors on that stage
are fixed, so the two segment-color sets at $j$ are identical.  At
$j=d$, fix $\eta\in D^K_{n,d}$ and unfold both
\noderef{HierarchySegmentColors} and the totalized
\noderef{HierarchyFirstMissingPosition}.

If $\eta=\beta$, (2) says that $\beta$ is missing on the starred stage
$A$.  The definition \noderef{HierarchyStoredColorsAt} therefore says
$\beta\notin D^K_{n,d}$, contrary to the fixed membership; this case is
empty.  If $\eta=\gamma$, elementarity of $V(T)$ under $\varphi$ and the
fact that $\gamma$ is missing at $b_2$ show that no other vertex of $T$
misses $\gamma$.  The first outside recoloring preserves every missing
set on $T$, so under $\mu_1$ the first $\gamma$-missing position is
exactly the position $s$ of $b_2$.  Under $\mu_2$, $b_2$ acquires the
$\gamma$-edge $f_2$ and no vertex of $T^-$ changes; thus no vertex of
$C$ misses $\gamma$, and the no-candidate clause totalizes its first
position to the last position of $C$, again $s$.  Every edge counted in
the new $(d,\gamma)$ segment is therefore an edge before position $s$,
with the same identity and color as in the old $(d,\gamma)$ segment of
$H_1$.

Finally suppose $\eta\notin\{\beta,\gamma\}$.  Its missing status at every
vertex of $C$ is unchanged by the second swap, and every displayed edge
of $C$ has the same color.  If its first missing vertex in the old
terminal $T$ lies in $C$, it is the same first vertex in $C$.  If it lies
later, or there is no such vertex, the totalized position in $C$ is its
last position.  In either case an edge position below the new first
position is below the old first position; this is the two-branch
first-missing argument in
\noderef{GoodHierarchyTerminalPrefixRepackage}, using the exact prefix
$C\preccurlyeq T$.  Thus also in this case the new segment-color set is
contained in the old one.  Old avoidance for $\mathcal G_1$ and this
containment prove new avoidance.  All five mathematical clauses of the
good hierarchy, including every totalized fallback, are now verified.
Denote the resulting good witness by $\mathcal G_2$.

It remains only the contradiction.  By (2), choose a vertex of $A$
missing $\beta$.  The first recoloring preserves that missingness, and
the second component has no vertex of $A\subseteq T^-$, so the same
vertex misses $\beta$ under $\mu_2$.  At $b_2$, before the second swap
there is no incident $\gamma$-edge and, by properness, the only incident
$\beta$-edge in the component is $f_2$.  The swap changes it to
$\gamma$ and leaves no incident $\beta$-edge; hence $b_2$ misses
$\beta$ under $\mu_2$.  These two vertices are distinct and both lie in
$C$.  Since $\mathcal E_2$ has the same rung, maximum property, and the
good hierarchy $K$ has $d<q_0$ dividers, the stated fewer-divider
hypothesis makes $V(C)$ elementary under $\mu_2$.  The two
$\beta$-missing vertices contradict that conclusion.  Therefore the
Subcase 1.1 data cannot occur.
\end{proof}

\begin{helper}[Replacement free-color full path]
With $\gamma,\varphi_\delta,T_\delta,X_\delta$, and $Z$ as above, there
are a color $\beta$, vertices $v\in Z$ and $z$, and a simple full
$(\gamma,\beta)$ incidence path $P_\delta$ under $\varphi_\delta$ from
$v$ to $z$ such that $\beta$ is missing at $v$ under both
$\varphi_\delta$ and $\varphi$, is free for $T_\delta$, differs from
both $\delta$ and $\gamma$, and $P_\delta$ has precisely the support of
the two-color component through $v$ and ends outside $X_\delta$.
\end{helper}
\begin{proof}
Put $r=\gammaIndex(\delta)$,
$U_\gamma=\{u^1_{\gamma(r)},u^2_{\gamma(r)}\}$,
$Z=V(T)\setminus U_\gamma$, and $X_\delta=V(T_\delta)$.
The ordered layout in \noderef{ScheideStructuralConfiguration} places
the base vertices $y_0,\ldots,y_{p-1}$ in positions strictly below $p$,
whereas the two vertices of the deleted block occupy positions
$p+2r$ and $p+2r+1$.  The vertex map is injective by
\noderef{TreeSequence}, so no base vertex is either deleted vertex.
Consequently
\[
                         Y\subseteq Z.                              \tag{1}
\]
Moreover, \noderef{ScheideDefectiveSetup} says that
$v^0_\delta$ is an exit vertex, and
\noderef{ScheideStructuralExitsInBase} localizes every exit vertex in
$Y$.  Thus
\[
                         v^0_\delta\in Y\subseteq Z.                \tag{2}
\]
The exact vertex-set equality in \noderef{ScheideReplacementData} is
$X_\delta=Z\cup\{u^1_\delta,u^2_\delta\}$, and hence $Z\subseteq
X_\delta$.

The same ordered layout and injectivity show that $V(T)$ has
$p+2s$ vertices and that the two deleted vertices are distinct.  Hence
\[
                         |Z|=p+2s-2.                               \tag{3}
\]
The first-edge and attachment fields of \noderef{TreeSequence} put the
two ends of $e_0$ in positions $0$ and $1$.  They are distinct because
the graph is loopless, and $p\geq2$ puts both positions in $Y$, hence in
$Z$ by (1).

By \noderef{ScheideReplacementMaximum}, the switched coloring is proper,
leaves exactly $e_0$ uncolored, and makes
$(T_\delta,e_0,\varphi_\delta)$ globally maximum.  Unpacking
\noderef{GlobalMaximumTriple}, its ordered tree is a
\noderef{TashkinovTree}.  Also,
\noderef{ScheideGlobalMaximumOrder} makes $X_\delta$ elementary and
closed.  By the definition \noderef{ColoredVertexSetElementary}, missing
sets at distinct vertices of $Z\subseteq X_\delta$ are disjoint.

Apply \noderef{ScheideGlobalMaximumMissingCounts} to each vertex of
$Z$.  Every vertex has at least one missing color, while each of the two
ends of $e_0$ has at least two.  Choose one missing color at every vertex
and a second, different missing color at each end of $e_0$.  The
elementarity just noted makes all $|Z|+2$ choices distinct.  If $M$ is
the set of colors missing somewhere on $Z$, (3) gives
\[
                         |M|\geq p+2s.                             \tag{4}
\]

We next count the colors used on displayed colored edges of $T_\delta$.
There are at most $p-2$ colors on the retained nonfirst base edges.  The
selected $r$-th block is deleted, so the other blocks contribute at most
$s-1$ colors.  Finally the appended pair has the single color $\delta$:
its two original values are $\delta$ by
\noderef{ScheideReplacementData}, and
\noderef{ScheideReplacementPairFixed} says that the switch fixes every
edge incident with either appended vertex.  Thus, in the terminology of
\noderef{TreeUsedColors},
\[
       |\varphi_\delta\langle T_\delta\rangle|
          \leq (p-2)+(s-1)+1=p-2+s.                               \tag{5}
\]
No retained displayed edge can secretly be $f^0_\delta$: such an edge
would have original color $\delta$, whereas
\noderef{ScheideGlobalMaximumUnused} says that the original defective
color $\delta$ is unused on $T$.

For later use, every color counted in (5) has a missing witness in $Z$.
First consider a color $c$ on a retained nonfirst base edge in formal
position $j$, where $1\leq j<p-1$.  Apply the
\noderef{TashkinovTree} certificate just extracted from the replacement
globally maximum triple.  It gives an index $i<j+1$ such that $c$ is
missing at the replacement vertex in position $i$.  Since $i\leq j<p-1$,
this is a base position.  The exact retention clauses of
\noderef{ScheideReplacementData} identify it with the original base
vertex $y_i$, which belongs to $Z$ by (1).  Notice that this argument
uses the replacement Tashkinov certificate; it does not assume an old
witness survives the switch.

Next let $c=\gamma(q)$ be the color of a retained block, so $q\ne r$.
The structural field in \noderef{ScheideStructuralConfiguration}
supplies a base vertex $y_a$ missing $\gamma(q)$ under $\varphi$.  It is
in $Z$ by (1).  If $y_a\ne v^0_\delta$, the pointwise missing-set clause
of \noderef{ScheideDefectiveSwitchEffect} leaves its missing set
unchanged.  If $y_a=v^0_\delta$, the exact formula in that same theorem is
\[
 \overline\varphi_\delta(v^0_\delta)
   =\{\delta\}\cup
     \bigl(\overline\varphi(v^0_\delta)\setminus\{\gamma(r)\}\bigr).
\]
Injectivity of the structural map $\gamma$ and $q\ne r$ give
$\gamma(q)\ne\gamma(r)$, so the old witness survives in this case too.
Finally, (2) and the displayed switch formula show that the appended
color $\delta$ is missing at $v^0_\delta\in Z$.  This proves the claimed
witness assertion for all three classes of used colors.

Since $r$ exists, $s\geq1$; hence (4)--(5) imply
$p-2+s<p+2s\leq|M|$.  Choose $\beta\in M$ which is not in
$\varphi_\delta\langle T_\delta\rangle$, and choose
$v\in Z$ missing $\beta$.  Since $v\in X_\delta$, the definitions
\noderef{TreeFreeColors} and \noderef{TreeUsedColors} say exactly that
$\beta$ is free for $T_\delta$.  The color $\delta$ is used on the
appended pair, so $\beta\ne\delta$.

The boundary theorem
\noderef{ScheideSymmetricReplacementBoundaryConfiguration} says that
$\gamma(r)$ is defective on $X_\delta$.  By
\noderef{DefectiveColors}, there is a $\gamma(r)$-colored boundary edge.
Closedness from \noderef{ScheideGlobalMaximumOrder}, interpreted through
\noderef{ColoredVertexSetClosed}, therefore forbids $\gamma(r)$ from
being missing at any vertex of $X_\delta$.  Since $v\in Z\subseteq
X_\delta$ misses $\beta$, it follows that $\beta\ne\gamma(r)$.

It remains to compare the two colorings at $v$.  If
$v\ne v^0_\delta$, \noderef{ScheideDefectiveSwitchEffect} says their
missing sets there are equal.  If $v=v^0_\delta$, its exact formula above,
together with $\beta\ne\delta,\gamma(r)$, says that membership of $\beta$
in the new missing set can only come from the old missing set with
$\gamma(r)$ erased.  Thus in either case $\beta$ is also missing at $v$
under $\varphi$.

Finally apply \noderef{ScheideDefectiveChainPathExists} to the replacement
globally maximum triple, the defective color $\gamma(r)$, the free color
$\beta$, and the vertex $v\in X_\delta$ missing $\beta$.  It supplies a
vertex $z$ and a walk $P_\delta$ from $v$ to $z$ satisfying
\noderef{ScheideDefectiveChainPath}.  Unpacking that predicate, the walk
is simple, its support is the full $(\gamma(r),\beta)$ component through
$v$, and $z\notin X_\delta$, which is the required conclusion.
\end{proof}

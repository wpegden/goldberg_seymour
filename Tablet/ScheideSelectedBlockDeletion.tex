\begin{helper}[Deletion and reindexing of one Scheide block]
Let a reduced Scheide configuration consist of its base followed by its
ordered two-vertex blocks.  For any selected block index $i$, there is a
tree-sequence $S$ obtained by deleting exactly the two displayed edges
and two displayed vertices of block $i$.  It has length
$\operatorname{len}(T)-2$ and vertex set
\[
 V(S)=V(T)\setminus
 \{u^1_{\gamma_i},u^2_{\gamma_i}\}.
\]
Every base vertex and base edge retains its original index.  Every entry
of every block $r\ne i$ is retained, with its edge immediately before its
new vertex and after the base.  Conversely every edge of $S$ is either a
base edge or an edge of a retained block and is an exact edge
identity of $T$.  No retained edge is incident with either deleted block
vertex.
\end{helper}
\begin{proof}
By the definition of a reduced configuration in
\noderef{ScheideReducedConfiguration}, $C$ has an underlying Scheide
structural configuration; denote it by $C_0$.  We use the inherited
symbols $p,s,y,\mathit{baseEdge},\mathit{blockVertex}$, and
$\mathit{blockEdge}$ for the corresponding data of $C_0$.  Put
$n=\operatorname{len}(T)$.  The structural length and lower-bound fields
give
\[
 n+1=p+2s\qquad\hbox{and}\qquad 2\leq p
\tag{1}
\]
by \noderef{ScheideStructuralConfiguration}.  Since $i\in\operatorname{Fin}(s)$,
$i<s$, so in particular $1\leq s$.  It follows from (1) that $3\leq n$.
Define
\[
 \operatorname{len}(S):=n-2.
\]
Thus $1\leq\operatorname{len}(S)$ and
$\operatorname{len}(S)+2=n$.  This supplies the positivity field that a
tree-sequence requires in \noderef{TreeSequence}, before its vertex and
edge maps are defined.

Set
\[
 m=p+2i,
 \qquad
 d=p-1+2i.
\]
Because $2\leq p$, we have $d+1=m$.  Moreover, $i<s$ and (1) imply
$m+1<n+1$, so $m,m+1$ are old vertex positions, while $d,d+1$ are old
edge positions.  These are exactly the positions of the two vertices and
two edges of block $i$, by the block-position fields of
\noderef{ScheideStructuralConfiguration}.

For $0\leq q\leq n-2$, define the old vertex index
\[
 r_V(q)=\begin{cases}
 q,&q<m,\\
 q+2,&m\leq q.
 \end{cases}
\tag{2}
\]
For $0\leq q<n-2$, define the old edge index
\[
 r_E(q)=\begin{cases}
 q,&q<d,\\
 q+2,&d\leq q.
 \end{cases}
\tag{3}
\]
These formulas really land in the required old ranges.  For (2), the
only nontrivial bound is $q+2\leq(n-2)+2=n$; for (3), from $q<n-2$ one
gets $q+2<n$.  On either side of a gap the corresponding map is a
translation and hence injective.  Across the vertex gap its two values
lie respectively below $m$ and at least $m+2$, and across the edge gap
they lie respectively below $d$ and at least $d+2$.  Hence both maps are
injective.  The same formulas show, by taking the inverse $t\mapsto t$
below the gap and $t\mapsto t-2$ above it, that
\[
 \operatorname{im}(r_V)=\{0,\ldots,n\}\setminus\{m,m+1\},
 \qquad
 \operatorname{im}(r_E)=\{0,\ldots,n-1\}\setminus\{d,d+1\}.
\tag{4}
\]
Indeed, $i<s$ gives $m\leq n-1$ and $d\leq n-2$.  Hence an old index
below either gap is still in the corresponding new range, while an old
index at least two above a gap becomes a new index in range after
subtracting two.  This also proves both inclusions in (4), not merely
avoidance of the deleted positions.

Define
\[
 S.\mathit{vertex}(q)=T.\mathit{vertex}(r_V(q)),
 \qquad
 S.\mathit{edge}(q)=T.\mathit{edge}(r_E(q)).
\tag{5}
\]
The injectivity fields of $T$ in \noderef{TreeSequence}, together with
the injectivity of $r_V$ and $r_E$, prove the vertex- and edge-injectivity
fields for $S$.  Also $d\geq p-1\geq1$, using the bound $p\geq2$ from
\noderef{ScheideStructuralConfiguration}.  Thus $r_E(0)=0$, and (5) and
the first-edge field of $T$ in \noderef{TreeSequence} give
$S.\mathit{edge}(0)=T.\mathit{edge}(0)=e_0$.

It remains to verify the attachment field of $S$.  Fix a new edge index
$q$.  First suppose $q<d$.  Then $r_E(q)=q$.  The attachment field of
$T$ in \noderef{TreeSequence} supplies an old vertex index $a\leq q$
such that the endpoints of $T.\mathit{edge}(q)$ are
$T.\mathit{vertex}(q+1)$ and $T.\mathit{vertex}(a)$.  Since
$a\leq q<d<m$ and $q+1\leq d<m$, formula (2) fixes both $a$ and $q+1$.
The same $a$, now regarded as an index at most $q$, therefore proves the
required attachment equation for $S.\mathit{edge}(q)$.

Now suppose $d\leq q$ and put $t=r_E(q)=q+2$.  Then
$t\geq d+2=p-1+2(i+1)$ and $t<n=p+2s-1$.  Euclidean division of
$t-(p-1)$ by two gives unique $r<s$ and $j<2$ such that
\[
 t=p-1+2r+j;
\]
the displayed bounds imply $i<r$.  The exact block-edge and block-vertex
position fields of \noderef{ScheideStructuralConfiguration} say
\[
 T.\mathit{edge}(t)=\mathit{blockEdge}(r,j),
 \qquad
 T.\mathit{vertex}(t+1)=\mathit{blockVertex}(r,j).
\tag{6}
\]
Here $q+1\geq d+1=m$, so (2) gives $r_V(q+1)=q+3=t+1$.
The block-attachment field of \noderef{ScheideStructuralConfiguration}
provides $a<p$ for which the endpoints of
$\mathit{blockEdge}(r,j)$ are the unordered pair
$\{y(a),\mathit{blockVertex}(r,j)\}$.  Its base-position field gives
$T.\mathit{vertex}(a)=y(a)$.  Since
$a<p\leq m\leq q+1$, formula (2) fixes $a$, and $a\leq q$ because
$a\leq p-1\leq d\leq q$.  Equations (5)--(6) therefore identify the
endpoints of $S.\mathit{edge}(q)$ with
$S.\mathit{vertex}(q+1)$ and the earlier vertex
$S.\mathit{vertex}(a)$.  This proves the remaining attachment field and
hence constructs the promised tree-sequence $S$ in the sense of
\noderef{TreeSequence}.

We next check every assertion about the ordering of $S$.  Equations
(2), (3), and (5) are precisely the required witnesses $r_V,r_E$ and
give all four stated order equations, while the definition of the new
length gives $\operatorname{len}(S)+2=\operatorname{len}(T)$.  If $a<p$,
then $a<m$, so (2) fixes the vertex index $a$; if $a<p-1$, then $a<d$,
so (3) fixes the edge index $a$.  These are valid new indices because
(1) and $s\geq1$ give $p-1\leq n-2$ and $p-2<n-2$.  The exact
base-position fields of
\noderef{ScheideStructuralConfiguration} consequently give
\[
 S.\mathit{vertex}(a)=y(a),
 \qquad
 S.\mathit{edge}(a)=\mathit{baseEdge}(a).
\tag{7}
\]

Let $r\ne i$ and $j<2$.  If $r<i$, choose
\[
 q_v=p+2r+j,
 \qquad q_e=p-1+2r+j.
\tag{8}
\]
Both indices lie before their respective gaps, so (2)--(3) fix them.  If
$i<r$, choose instead
\[
 q_v=p+2r+j-2,
 \qquad q_e=p-1+2r+j-2.
\tag{9}
\]
Both old positions lie at least two places after their respective gaps,
so (2)--(3) send the indices in (9) to those old positions.  The choices
are in the new index ranges: in (8), $q_v<m\leq n-1$ and
$q_e<d\leq n-2$; in (9), the old vertex position is at most $n$ and the
old edge position is at most $n-1$, so subtraction by two gives
$q_v\leq n-2$ and $q_e<n-2$.  In both
cases $q_e+1=q_v$, $p\leq q_v$, and $p-1\leq q_e$.  Applying the exact
block-position fields of \noderef{ScheideStructuralConfiguration} in
(5) yields
\[
 S.\mathit{vertex}(q_v)=\mathit{blockVertex}(r,j),
 \qquad
 S.\mathit{edge}(q_e)=\mathit{blockEdge}(r,j).
\tag{10}
\]
Thus every retained block occurs after the base with its edge immediately
before its new vertex.

Conversely, fix an edge index $q$ of $S$ and set $t=r_E(q)$.  If
$t<p-1$, then (3) forces $t=q$, and the exact base-edge position in
\noderef{ScheideStructuralConfiguration} identifies
$S.\mathit{edge}(q)$ with $\mathit{baseEdge}(t)$.  If $p-1\leq t$, then
$t<n=p+2s-1$, so division of $t-(p-1)$ by two gives unique $r<s$ and
$j<2$ with $t=p-1+2r+j$.  By (4), $t$ is neither $d$ nor $d+1$, hence
$r\ne i$.  Also $p-1\leq q$: this follows from $q=t$ below the gap and
from $q\geq d\geq p-1$ above it.  The exact block-edge position in
\noderef{ScheideStructuralConfiguration} now identifies
$S.\mathit{edge}(q)$ with $\mathit{blockEdge}(r,j)$.  This proves the
stated exhaustive edge classification.  Independently, (5) shows for
every $q$ that $S.\mathit{edge}(q)$ is the exact old edge identity
$T.\mathit{edge}(r_E(q))$; thus the argument does not collapse parallel
edges having equal endpoints.

By \noderef{TreeSequenceVertexSet}, the vertex set of a tree-sequence is
the image of its vertex map over all its vertex indices.  Equations
(4)--(5) therefore express $V(S)$ as the image under
$T.\mathit{vertex}$ of all old indices except $m,m+1$.  The vertex map
of $T$ is injective by \noderef{TreeSequence}, so deleting those two
indices deletes exactly their two vertex values.  The exact selected
block-position equations in \noderef{ScheideStructuralConfiguration}
identify these values as
$\mathit{blockVertex}(i,0)$ and $\mathit{blockVertex}(i,1)$.  Hence
\[
 V(S)=V(T)\setminus
 \{\mathit{blockVertex}(i,0),\mathit{blockVertex}(i,1)\}.
\tag{11}
\]

Finally, we prove the two non-incidence assertions.  Every base vertex
$y(a)$ occurs at an old index $a<p\leq m$, whereas the two deleted
vertices occur at $m,m+1$.  Every retained block vertex
$\mathit{blockVertex}(r,j)$ occurs at $p+2r+j$; for $r\ne i$ this index
is different from both $m$ and $m+1$.  The exact base and block vertex
positions in \noderef{ScheideStructuralConfiguration}, together with
vertex injectivity from \noderef{TreeSequence}, therefore show that no
base vertex and no retained block vertex equals either deleted vertex.

For a base edge, the attachment field of $T$ in
\noderef{TreeSequence} places both endpoints at old indices below $p$:
the edge at $a<p-1$ joins the vertex at $a+1<p$ to one at an index at
most $a$.  The base-position equations of
\noderef{ScheideStructuralConfiguration} identify both as base vertices.
For a retained block edge, the block-attachment field of that same node
says that its endpoints are its own retained block vertex and a base
vertex.  The edge classification above therefore shows that neither
endpoint of any edge of $S$ is a deleted vertex.  Since incidence means
membership in the endpoint pair by \noderef{Incident}, no edge of $S$
is incident with either $\mathit{blockVertex}(i,0)$ or
$\mathit{blockVertex}(i,1)$, as required.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Scheide defective-color setup]
Let a reduced Scheide configuration have at least one defective color.
Then it admits defective-color setup data: a common
$\alpha_0\in\overline\varphi(y_0)\setminus\Gamma$ and, for every defective
$\delta$, the two full incidence paths, last-tree-vertex indices, named
boundary edge, outside endpoint, and missing block color specified in
\noderef{ScheideDefectiveSetup}.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Write
\[
 X=V(T),\qquad
 y_0=C.y(0),\qquad
 \Gamma=\{C.\gamma(i):i\in\operatorname{Fin}(C.s)\}.
\]
Here $X$ is the set defined by \noderef{TreeSequenceVertexSet}.  The
inherited globally-maximum field of \noderef{ScheideReducedConfiguration}
is the one in \noderef{ScheideStructuralConfiguration}.  Consequently
\noderef{ScheideGlobalMaximumOrder} says that $X$ is elementary and closed
in the respective senses of \noderef{ColoredVertexSetElementary} and
\noderef{ColoredVertexSetClosed}.  Moreover, unfolding
\noderef{GlobalMaximumTriple} and \noderef{KTriple} shows that $\varphi$ is
proper in the sense of \noderef{IsProperEdgeColoring}.  Thus two distinct
edge identities incident with one vertex cannot have the same color: they
are adjacent by \noderef{AdjacentEdges} and properness applies.

We first choose the common color $\alpha _0$.  The first-edge and attachment
clauses of \noderef{TreeSequence}, together with the base-position clauses
of \noderef{ScheideStructuralConfiguration}, identify $e_0$ with the first
edge between the distinct base vertices $y_0$ and $C.y(1)$.  In particular,
$y_0\in X$ and $y_0$ is incident with $e_0$ in the sense of
\noderef{Incident}.  Hence \noderef{ScheideGlobalMaximumMissingCounts}
gives
\[
 |\overline\varphi(y_0)|\geq2. \tag{1}
\]
There is at most one index $i$ for which $C.\gamma(i)$ is missing at
$y_0$.  Indeed, the every-block-color clause of
\noderef{ScheideReducedConfiguration} supplies, for such an $i$, an exit
$x_i$ at which $C.\gamma(i)$ is missing.  Unfolding
\noderef{ScheideExitVertices} and then \noderef{ScheideExitVertex} shows
that $x_i$ is a tree vertex.  If $x_i\ne y_0$,
then the same color belongs to the two missing-color sets at the distinct
tree vertices $x_i,y_0$, contrary to elementariness.  Thus $x_i=y_0$, so
$y_0$ is itself an exit; the exactly-one clause of
\noderef{ScheideReducedConfiguration} now makes $i$ unique.  Since the
indexed block colors are distinct, (1) therefore contains a color outside
$\Gamma$.  Fix
\[
 \alpha _0\in\overline\varphi(y_0)\setminus\Gamma. \tag{2}
\]
This gives both the missing-color and all the non-block-color fields
required by \noderef{ScheideDefectiveSetup}.

Fix from now on
$\delta\in\noderef{DefectiveColors}(G,\varphi,X)$.  By
\noderef{DefectiveColor} and \noderef{BoundaryColorEdges}, there is a
boundary edge identity $b$ with $\varphi(b)=\delta$.  For every $x\in X$,
closedness applied to $b$ says
$\delta\notin\overline\varphi(x)$.  The definitions
\noderef{MissingColors} and \noderef{PresentColors} therefore give an edge
identity incident with $x$ and colored $\delta$; properness makes that
identity unique.  We have proved
\[
 \text{$\delta$ is present exactly once at every vertex of $X$.} \tag{3}
\]
On the other hand, (2) and elementariness imply that $\alpha _0$ is not
missing at any $x\in X\setminus\{y_0\}$.  Hence it too is present exactly
once there.  At $y_0$ it is absent, by (2) and the complement definition
\noderef{MissingColors}.  In particular $\alpha _0\ne\delta$, since (3)
says that $\delta$ is present at $y_0$.

Let
\[
 H_\delta=\noderef{TwoColorIncidenceGraph}
       (G,\varphi;\alpha _0,\delta),\qquad
 K_\delta=\noderef{TwoColorComponent}
       (G,\varphi;\alpha _0,\delta,y_0).
\]
We record the exact degrees in $H_\delta$.  Its vertex-node $y_0$ has
degree one: its unique neighbor is the unique incident $\delta$-edge from
(3), while (2), \noderef{PresentColors}, and
\noderef{TwoColorIncidenceGraph} exclude every $\alpha _0$-edge.  Every
other tree vertex-node has degree two, because it has one incident edge of
each of the two distinct colors, those two edge identities are distinct,
since the coloring map in \noderef{PartialEdgeColoring} cannot give one
edge identity two distinct colors, and these are all possible neighbors in
$H_\delta$.  Finally, every
edge-node belonging to $K_\delta$ has degree two.  Such a node is not the
vertex-node $y_0$; connectedness to $y_0$ therefore gives it an adjacency,
so \noderef{TwoColorIncidenceGraph} says that its edge identity is colored
$\alpha _0$ or $\delta$.  Its neighbors are precisely its two endpoints.
Those endpoints are distinct by the loopless clause of
\noderef{LooplessMultigraph}, and both are incident by
\noderef{Incident}, so both and only both are neighbors.  These exact
counts agree with the global upper bound
\noderef{TwoColorIncidenceDegreeAtMostTwo}, whose properness hypothesis was
verified above.

Apply \noderef{TwoColorComponentSpanningPath} at the node $y_0$.  It gives
a simple path $Q$ whose support is exactly $K_\delta$.  Since $y_0$ belongs
to that support, it occurs on $Q$.  It cannot be internal on $Q$: the two
distinct path neighbors at an internal occurrence would contradict its
degree one.  Orient $Q$ (reversing it if necessary) from $y_0$, and call
the resulting path $P_\delta$.  It has positive length, since the unique
$\delta$-edge at $y_0$ also belongs to $K_\delta$ and hence to the support
of $P_\delta$.  Let $z_\delta$ denote its other endpoint.

The component degree of $z_\delta$ is exactly one.  It has its predecessor
on $P_\delta$.  If it had a different neighbor $q$, then $q$ would lie in
the same component and hence, by the support equality, on $P_\delta$.  If
$q=y_0$, this would give $y_0$ a second neighbor (unless it were the
already-counted predecessor, contrary to the choice of $q$).  Otherwise
$q$ is internal on the simple path and already has its two distinct path
neighbors; adjacency to the different endpoint $z_\delta$ would give it
degree at least three, contrary to
\noderef{TwoColorIncidenceDegreeAtMostTwo}.  Thus no such $q$ exists.
The endpoint $z_\delta$ cannot be an edge-node, since every edge-node of
$K_\delta$ has degree two.  It is not $y_0$ because the path has positive
length, and it cannot be another vertex of $X$, since every such
vertex-node has degree two.  Therefore $z_\delta$ is a vertex-node with
\[
 z_\delta\notin X. \tag{4}
\]
The root membership, simplicity, (4), and support equality are precisely
the four clauses of the initial full-chain predicate
\noderef{ScheideDefectiveChainPath}.

We next extract all the ordered data from $P_\delta$.  Among its indices
whose nodes are vertex-nodes belonging to $X$, choose the largest one and
call it $i_\delta$.  This finite set of indices is nonempty because index
zero is $y_0$.  Write
\[
 P_\delta(i_\delta)=v^0_\delta.
\]
The terminal node is the outside vertex $z_\delta$, so
$i_\delta<\operatorname{length}(P_\delta)$.  By the definition of
\noderef{TwoColorIncidenceGraph}, successive nodes alternate between the
vertex and edge sides.  Starting at a vertex-node, all even indices are
vertex-nodes and all odd indices are edge-nodes.  Both $i_\delta$ and the
terminal index are therefore even.  Their strict inequality implies
$i_\delta+2\leq\operatorname{length}(P_\delta)$.  Consequently there are
unique identities $f^0_\delta$ and $u^0_\delta$ such that
\[
 P_\delta(i_\delta+1)=f^0_\delta,\qquad
 P_\delta(i_\delta+2)=u^0_\delta, \tag{5}
\]
where the first is an edge-node and the second a vertex-node.  Take the
bounded indices $i_\delta,i_\delta+1,i_\delta+2$ as respectively the
$v^0$-, $f^0$-, and $u^0$-indices in
\noderef{ScheideDefectiveSetup}.  Equation (5) gives both index equations
and the two required successive-index equalities.  The maximality of
$i_\delta$ gives the full quantified assertion that every later
vertex-node is outside $X$, and in particular
\[
 u^0_\delta\notin X. \tag{6}
\]

The two successive adjacencies in (5), unfolded through
\noderef{TwoColorIncidenceGraph}, say that $f^0_\delta$ is incident with
both $v^0_\delta$ and $u^0_\delta$.  Since
\noderef{LooplessMultigraph} makes its two endpoints distinct, the two
incidences and \noderef{Incident} give the identity of unordered endpoint
pairs
\[
 G.\operatorname{ends}(f^0_\delta)
    =\{v^0_\delta,u^0_\delta\}. \tag{7}
\]
Thus (6)--(7) also show directly from \noderef{BoundaryEdges} that
$f^0_\delta$ is a boundary edge.  Its color is one of
$\alpha _0,\delta$ by the same incidence-graph adjacency.  It cannot be
$\alpha _0$: (2) puts $\alpha _0$ in the tree-missing set of
\noderef{TreeMissingColors}, and
\noderef{ScheideGlobalMaximumBoundaryFacts} says that such a color has no
boundary edge (equivalently, this follows by applying
\noderef{ColoredVertexSetClosed} at $y_0$).  Hence
\[
 \varphi(f^0_\delta)=\delta. \tag{8}
\]
Because every displayed node of $P_\delta$ belongs to its support, support
equality also gives the two required memberships of $v^0_\delta$ and
$f^0_\delta$ in $K_\delta$.

We now verify, rather than merely name, the exit assertion.  In the
definition \noderef{ScheideExitVertex}, take the starting tree vertex to
be $y_0$, the missing color to be $\alpha _0$, the defective color to be
$\delta$, the outside endpoint to be $z_\delta$, the full path to be
$P_\delta$, and the last-tree index to be $i_\delta$.  The incidence graphs
and components are unchanged when the displayed pair of colors is swapped,
as is immediate from the two disjuncts in
\noderef{TwoColorIncidenceGraph}.  Thus the initial full-chain clauses
above also give the order $(\delta,\alpha _0)$ used in
\noderef{ScheideExitVertex}.  The missing-color and defective-color clauses
are (2) and the choice of $\delta$; membership of $y_0$ in $X$ was proved
above; the last-index equality is the definition of $v^0_\delta$; and the
entire quantified last-tree-vertex clause is the maximality assertion after
(5).  All clauses of \noderef{ScheideExitVertex} therefore hold, and
unfolding \noderef{ScheideExitVertices} gives
\[
 v^0_\delta\in F(T,e_0,\varphi). \tag{9}
\]
Apply the exactly-one clause of
\noderef{ScheideReducedConfiguration} to (9), and denote its unique index
by $g_\delta\in\operatorname{Fin}(C.s)$.  Then
\[
 C.\gamma(g_\delta)\in\overline\varphi(v^0_\delta). \tag{10}
\]
This is exactly the $\gamma$-index and missing-color datum required in the
setup.

It remains to construct the replacement path through this specific
$v^0_\delta$.  Put $\gamma_\delta=C.\gamma(g_\delta)$ and consider the
$(\gamma_\delta,\delta)$ component through $v^0_\delta$.  Equation (10)
and (3) imply $\gamma_\delta\ne\delta$.  Its root vertex-node has degree
exactly one: no $\gamma_\delta$-edge is incident there by
\noderef{MissingColors} and \noderef{PresentColors}, while its unique
$\delta$-edge exists by (3).  Every other tree vertex-node has degree
exactly two.  Indeed, $\delta$ is present there by (3), while elementarity
and (10) forbid $\gamma_\delta$ from being missing there, so each of the
two distinct colors occurs on exactly one incident edge.  As before, every
edge-node in this particular component has degree exactly two by
\noderef{TwoColorIncidenceGraph}, \noderef{Incident}, and the loopless
two-endpoint condition of \noderef{LooplessMultigraph}.

Apply \noderef{TwoColorComponentSpanningPath} to this component, obtaining
a simple path whose support is the whole component.  The root
$v^0_\delta$ occurs on this path.  It is an endpoint, since an internal
occurrence would give it two distinct path neighbors although its degree is
one.  Orient the path from $v^0_\delta$ and call it $P'_\delta$.  It has
positive length because the root's unique $\delta$-edge belongs to the
component.  Let $z'_\delta$ be the opposite endpoint.

As in the initial component, $z'_\delta$ has component degree exactly one,
but we repeat the check for this particular component.  It has its path
predecessor.  If it had a different neighbor $q$, component membership and
the support equality would put $q$ on $P'_\delta$.  If $q=v^0_\delta$,
then the positive simple path either has length one, when $q$ is the
already-counted predecessor, or gives the root a second neighbor.  Both
contradict the choice of $q$ or the root's exact degree.  If
$q\ne v^0_\delta$, then $q$ is an internal path node, already with two
distinct path neighbors, and adjacency to the different endpoint
$z'_\delta$ gives degree at least three.  This contradicts
\noderef{TwoColorIncidenceDegreeAtMostTwo}.  Hence the predecessor is the
only neighbor of $z'_\delta$.  The opposite endpoint is not an edge-node,
because all edge-nodes in this component have degree two; it is not the
root, because the path has positive length; and it is not any other tree
vertex-node, because those too have degree two.  Therefore it is a vertex
with $z'_\delta\notin X$.

The root of $P'_\delta$ lies in $X$ by construction, the path is simple,
its other end is outside $X$, and its support equals the full component.
These are all four clauses of the replacement
\noderef{ScheideDefectiveChainPath}; importantly, the argument concerns the
component through $v^0_\delta$ itself and does not use an unrelated
$\delta$-boundary edge to infer component membership.

For each member $\delta$ of the finite set
\noderef{DefectiveColors}$(G,\varphi,X)$, the preceding construction gives
simultaneously
\[
 (v^0_\delta,f^0_\delta,u^0_\delta,g_\delta,z_\delta,P_\delta,
   i_\delta,i_\delta+1,i_\delta+2,z'_\delta,P'_\delta).
\]
Finite dependent choice collects these witnesses into the corresponding
functions.  Equations (2), (4)--(10), the support memberships following
(8), the quantified maximal-index assertion, and the two explicitly
verified \noderef{ScheideDefectiveChainPath} predicates fill, field by
field and in the displayed order, every component of
\noderef{ScheideDefectiveSetup}.  Therefore that structure is nonempty.
\end{proof}

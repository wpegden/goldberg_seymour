\begin{lemma}[Scheide maximum-triple facts (a)--(b)]
If $(T,e_0,\varphi)$ is globally maximum, then
$t(G)=|V(T)|\geq3$, this number is odd, and $V(T)$ is elementary and
closed with respect to $\varphi$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The equality $|V(T)|=t(G)$ is part of
\noderef{GlobalMaximumTriple}.  The $k$-triple equality there gives
$k=\chi'(G)-1$, so every Tashkinov tree for the same pair is among the
trees maximized in \noderef{TashkinovOrder}.  Such a tree is fixed-pair
maximal in the sense of \noderef{FixedPairMaximalTashkinovTree}.  Indeed,
if a Tashkinov tree $S$ for the same pair strictly contained $V(T)$ in
$V(S)$, then finiteness would give $|V(S)|>|V(T)|=t(G)$, contrary to the
maximum defining \noderef{TashkinovOrder}.  Therefore
\noderef{ScheideMaximalOddOrder} shows that $|V(T)|$ is odd, while
\noderef{ScheideMaximalElementaryClosed} shows that $V(T)$ is elementary
and closed.

The positive-length condition in \noderef{TreeSequence} gives at least the
two distinct initial vertices, so $|V(T)|\geq2$.  An odd natural number
which is at least two is at least three.  Combining this with the defining
equality gives the result.
\end{proof}

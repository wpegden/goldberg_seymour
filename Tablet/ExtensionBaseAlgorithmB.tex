\begin{lemma}[Part IV, Algorithm (B) and invariant (16)]
Use the Case 1 data from
\noderef{ExtensionBaseCaseOneSelection}.  Let
\(1_1<\cdots<1_{p+1}\) be its strictly increasing finite list of exact
PE slots, all supported at \(v_n\); in the extension-base application
this is the exact class \(L_1\) and \(1_{p+1}=n\).  The chosen missing color at \(u\) is
\(\gamma_{1_{b+1}}\), no earlier gamma in this list is missing at
\(u\), and
\(L_1^*=\{1_1,\ldots,1_b\}\).  Assume the exact gamma, delta, stored-pair,
PE-kind, and supporting-vertex fields, the chain identities
\(\gamma_{1_{a+2}}=\delta_{1_{a+1}}\), and their stated distinctness.
Assume that every pair indexed by \(L_1^*\) is strongly interchangeable
under \(\varrho_1\), that \(T_j\) is an ordered initial segment of each
corresponding \(T_t\), and that (15) holds at \(v_n\).  If
\(\varrho_1\) is proper and leaves exactly \(e_0\) uncolored, then there
is an \noderef{ExtensionBaseAlgorithmBTrace} satisfying
\noderef{ExtensionBaseAlgorithmBInvariant}.

Equivalently, Algorithm (B) can be carried out in decreasing order on
the exact set \(L_1^*\), and before every iteration \(B(t)\):
\(\delta_t\) is missing at \(u\); the switched path contains at most the
single vertex \(v_n\) of \(T_t\), with \(v_n\) not an end; and every
unprocessed pair remains strongly interchangeable after the switch.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
If \(b=0\), then \(L_1^*=\varnothing\).  Take the trace of length zero
whose only coloring is \(\varrho_1\).  Properness, the exact-one-uncolored
condition, and the final survivor clause are immediate from the
hypotheses.  Hence suppose \(b>0\).

List the exact predecessor slots in decreasing position order:
\[
 t_0=1_b>t_1=1_{b-1}>\cdots>t_{b-1}=1_1.                    \tag{1}
\]
Strict increase of the original enumeration proves strict decrease in
(1), and the image equality is exactly
\noderef{ExtensionBaseCaseOneIndices}.  Define
\(\varrho^0=\varrho_1\) and recursively set
\[
 \varrho^{a+1}=\noderef{TwoColorComponentSwap}
   (\varrho^a,\gamma_{t_a},\delta_{t_a},u).                   \tag{2}
\]
The exact field equalities at the listed slots and (1)--(2) define an
\noderef{ExtensionBaseAlgorithmBTrace}.  We verify its invariant by
induction on \(a\).

At the first slot \(t_0=1_b\), the chain identity gives
\(\delta_{t_0}=\gamma_{1_{b+1}}\), which is missing at \(u\) by the
Case 1 choice.  Its other color \(\gamma_{t_0}\) is not missing at
\(u\), by minimality of \(b\).  Switching the component therefore makes
\(\gamma_{t_0}\) missing at \(u\).  This is
\(\delta_{t_1}\) when a next slot exists.  Repeating the same argument
down (1) proves inductively that, immediately before slot \(t_a\),
\(\delta_{t_a}\) is missing and \(\gamma_{t_a}\) is present at \(u\).
The pairwise distinctness of
\(\gamma_0,\delta_0,\ldots,\delta_p\) ensures that later switches do not
prematurely alter an earlier gamma.  This proves the missing-color part
of (16).

By hypothesis and \noderef{ETTStrongInterchangeable}, the current pair is
strongly interchangeable before its
first possible use; the survivor induction below preserves that fact at
subsequent steps.  Since its delta is missing and its gamma is present at
\(u\), its two-color component contains an edge and has \(u\) as an
endpoint.  The finite spanning-path statement
\noderef{TwoColorComponentSpanningPath} therefore gives
\noderef{TwoColorPathComponent}.  Suppose that the component also
contains a vertex \(x\in V(T_{t_a})\setminus\{v_n\}\).  Strong
interchangeability makes the component through \(x\) a
\noderef{TwoColorComponentCycle}.  It is the same connected component as
the \noderef{TwoColorPathComponent} through \(u\), contradicting the
endpoint of the latter.  Hence the path meets \(T_{t_a}\) in at most
\(v_n\).

Both colors of the current pair belong to the stored-color union over
\(L_1^*\).  Equation (15), as stated in the terminal-no-missing
hypothesis, says that neither is missing at \(v_n\) under
\(\varrho_1\).  Earlier switches only use colors in the same union and,
by the just-proved intersection property, can meet \(T_{t_a}\) only at
\(v_n\).  At such an internal vertex the switch exchanges its two
incident component edges, so it preserves the fact that both colors are
present.  Inductively neither current color is missing at \(v_n\).
Thus, if \(v_n\) lies on the current component, its node has two distinct
neighbors in \noderef{TwoColorIncidenceGraph}; it is not an end.  This is
the remaining path assertion in (16).

The switch preserves properness by
\noderef{TwoColorComponentSwapProper}.  It exchanges two
\(\mathsf{some}\)-values and changes no \(\mathsf{none}\)-value, so the
same edge \(e_0\) remains uniquely uncolored.  Let \(s\) be an
unprocessed slot after switching at \(t_a\).  All slots have the same
support \(v_n\), and decreasing order gives \(s<t_a\).  The current
component is a path, both the current and survivor pairs are strongly
interchangeable, and their optional fields are the exact certified
fields.  Therefore the same-support, earlier-index alternative of
\noderef{StrongInterchangeabilityKempePreserved} applies and proves that
the pair at \(s\) remains strongly interchangeable under
\(\varrho^{a+1}\).

This establishes every per-step clause and closes the induction.  After
the last step the survivor statement is vacuous; more generally, the
same induction proves the invariant's final clause for any exact slot
absent from the trace.  Together with the fixed initial-segment
hypotheses \(T_j\subseteq T_t\), all clauses of
\noderef{ExtensionBaseAlgorithmBInvariant} hold.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Boundary structure after a parallel extension]
Let \(n=q+1\), let \(T_n=\mathcal T.\mathrm{tree}(q+1)\), and let
\(\varphi_n=\mathcal T.\mathrm{coloring}(q+1)\).  Suppose the certified
current step is a PE, with exact connecting-edge set \(F_n=\{f_n\}\),
stored colors \(\delta_n,\gamma_n\), and supporting vertex \(v_n\).  Then
\[
 \partial_{\varphi_n,\gamma_n}(T_n)=\{f_n\}.
\]
For every identity \(g\in\partial_{\varphi_n,\delta_n}(T_n)\), there is a
tree vertex \(w\prec_{T_n}v_n\) incident with \(g\).  Finally, if
\(V(T_n)\) is elementary under the pre-step coloring
\(\varphi_{n-1}=\mathcal T.\mathrm{coloring}(q)\), then every
\(\alpha\in\overline\varphi_n(T_n)\) with \(\alpha\ne\delta_n\) satisfies
\[
 \partial_{\varphi_n,\alpha}(T_n)=\varnothing.
\]
All three assertions concern exact edge identities, so the singleton in
the first assertion is not merely a singleton endpoint pair.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Put \(X=V(T_n)\), and use the step certificate supplied by
\noderef{TashkinovSeries}.  Since the index is positive, its certificate
cannot be the initial constructor.  The displayed PE-kind equation rules
out the SE and RE constructors of \noderef{TashkinovSeriesStep}.
Injectivity of \(\mathrm{some}\) and of singleton finite sets therefore
identifies the PE constructor's edge, colors, and support with
\(f_n,\delta_n,\gamma_n,v_n\).  Write \(\pi\) for the selected
maximum-defective coloring, \(\rho\) for the second stable coloring, and
\(K\) for the \((\gamma_n,\delta_n)\)-component of \(v_n\) under
\(\rho\).  The same constructor gives
\[
 \varphi_n=\rho/K.                                                \tag{1}
\]

We first transport the pre-switch invariants.  The certificate
\noderef{MaximumDefectiveChoice} says that \(\pi\) is proper, leaves
exactly \(e_0\) uncolored, and is
\((T_n,D_{n-1},\varphi_{n-1})\)-stable.  It also says that \(f_n\) is a
defective boundary-edge identity incident with \(v_n\), that
\(\pi(f_n)=\delta_n\), and that \(v_n\in X\) by
\noderef{DefectiveVertex}.  The PE constructor says that \(\rho\) is
proper, leaves exactly \(e_0\) uncolored, and is stable relative to
\((T_n,D_{n-1}\cup\{\delta_n\},\pi)\).  The vertex clause of
\noderef{StableColoring} consequently gives, for every \(x\in X\),
\[
 \overline{\varphi}_{n-1}(x)=\overline\pi(x)=\overline\rho(x).    \tag{2}
\]
Taking the union over \(x\in X\), as prescribed by
\noderef{TreeMissingColors}, gives the analogous equality of the three
tree-missing color sets.

The PE constructor also says that \(X\) is closed under
\(\varphi_{n-1}\).  We claim that it is closed under both \(\pi\) and
\(\rho\).  For the first claim, suppose that a boundary identity \(a\)
has \(\pi(a)=c\), where \(c\) is missing at some \(x\in X\).  By (2),
\(c\) is missing at \(x\) under \(\varphi_{n-1}\), and hence belongs to
the corresponding tree-missing set.  A boundary identity is incident with
the tree by \noderef{BoundaryEdges}, \noderef{Incident}, and
\noderef{TreeIncidentEdge}.  Symmetry of stability from \noderef{sc2},
followed by the edge clause of \noderef{StableColoring}, therefore gives
\(\varphi_{n-1}(a)=\pi(a)=c\).  This contradicts
\noderef{ColoredVertexSetClosed}.  Thus \(X\) is closed under \(\pi\).
The identical argument with the symmetric second stable relation sends a
hypothetical \(\rho\)-boundary color missing at a vertex of \(X\) back to
\(\pi\), proving that \(X\) is closed under \(\rho\).  In particular,
every color in \(\overline\rho(T_n)\) has empty \(\rho\)-boundary.

We next record the pre-switch \(\delta_n\)-boundary order.  The statements
\noderef{DefectiveEdge} and \noderef{BoundaryColorEdges}, together with
\(\pi(f_n)=\delta_n\), show that \(\delta_n\) is the defective color
witnessed by \(f_n\).  The edge clauses of the second stable relation and
its symmetric form from \noderef{sc2} apply to every boundary identity
colored \(\delta_n\), because \(\delta_n\) is in the enlarged control
set.  Hence the exact \(\delta_n\)-boundary sets under \(\pi\) and
\(\rho\) coincide, and \(\rho(f_n)=\delta_n\).

Let \(g\) belong to this common boundary and let \(w\in X\) be its inside
endpoint, whose existence follows from \noderef{BoundaryEdges}.  Then
\(g\) is itself a defective edge incident with \(w\), so
\noderef{DefectiveVertex} makes \(w\) a defective vertex under \(\pi\).
Maximality in \noderef{MaximumDefectiveChoice} gives
\(w=v_n\) or \(w\prec_{T_n}v_n\), the latter being the order of
\noderef{TreeVertexOrder}.  If \(w=v_n\), then \(g\) and \(f_n\) are
both incident with \(v_n\) and both have color \(\delta_n\) under
\(\pi\).  Were their identities distinct, they would be adjacent by
\noderef{AdjacentEdges}, contrary to \noderef{IsProperEdgeColoring}.
Therefore
\[
 g=f_n\quad\text{or}\quad
 \text{an inside endpoint of }g\text{ strictly precedes }v_n.     \tag{3}
\]

We now identify \(K\).  The PE rule \noderef{ParallelExtensionColor}
says that \(\gamma_n\) is missing at \(v_n\) under \(\pi\); equation
(2) gives the same fact under \(\rho\).  Since \(f_n\) is incident there
and \(\rho(f_n)=\delta_n\), the complement description in
\noderef{MissingColors} and the edge-witness description in
\noderef{PresentColors} imply \(\gamma_n\ne\delta_n\).  Moreover,
properness and \noderef{AdjacentEdges} show that \(f_n\) is the only
\(\gamma_n\)- or \(\delta_n\)-colored edge incident with \(v_n\).
By the adjacency rule in \noderef{TwoColorIncidenceGraph}, the vertex-node
\(v_n\) consequently has degree one in that graph.

The exit certificate \noderef{TreeExit} supplies a simple exit path
\(P\) from a vertex \(u\notin X\) to \(v_n\).  By
\noderef{TreeExitPath}, it meets \(X\) in the single vertex-node \(v_n\),
and \(u\) misses at least one of \(\gamma_n,\delta_n\).  The path is
nontrivial, so \(u\) has an incident edge of the other color; properness
then makes the vertex-node \(u\) degree one as well.  Every node of the
two-color incidence graph has degree at most two by
\noderef{TwoColorIncidenceDegreeAtMostTwo}.  If the connected component
of \(v_n\) had a node outside the support of \(P\), choose a shortest
route from such a node to \(P\).  Its first contact with an internal node
of \(P\) would give that node a third neighbor, while contact with either
endpoint would give that degree-one endpoint a second neighbor.  Both are
impossible.  Thus \(P\) is the whole component \(K\), as defined in
\noderef{TwoColorComponent}, and
\[
 K\cap\{\text{vertex-nodes of }X\}=\{v_n\}.                       \tag{4}
\]

There is exactly one boundary-edge node in \(K\), namely \(f_n\).  Indeed,
\(f_n\in K\) because it is a \(\delta_n\)-edge incident with \(v_n\).
Conversely, if a boundary identity \(a\) has its edge-node in \(K\), its
inside endpoint \(x\in X\) is adjacent to that edge-node by
\noderef{TwoColorIncidenceGraph}, and hence its vertex-node lies in
\(K\).  Equation (4) gives \(x=v_n\).  Since \(\gamma_n\) is missing at
\(v_n\), the edge has color \(\delta_n\); properness then gives
\(a=f_n\).

Expand the switch in \noderef{TwoColorComponentSwap}.  It exchanges
\(\gamma_n\) and \(\delta_n\) on precisely the edge-nodes in \(K\) and
fixes every other edge value.  Thus it changes the unique component
boundary identity \(f_n\) from \(\delta_n\) to \(\gamma_n\).  Outside
\(K\), a new \(\gamma_n\)-boundary identity cannot appear, and an old one
does not exist because \(X\) is closed under \(\rho\) while
\(\gamma_n\in\overline\rho(v_n)\).  Using (1) and the filter definition
\noderef{BoundaryColorEdges}, we obtain
\[
 \partial_{\varphi_n,\gamma_n}(T_n)=\{f_n\}.                      \tag{5}
\]
If \(g\) is a \(\delta_n\)-boundary identity under \(\varphi_n\), it
cannot lie in \(K\), since its pre-switch color there would be
\(\gamma_n\), whose \(\rho\)-boundary is empty.  Hence the switch fixes
\(g\), so \(g\) is a \(\rho\)-boundary identity colored \(\delta_n\),
and \(g\ne f_n\).  Equation (3) supplies an inside endpoint strictly
preceding \(v_n\), proving the second unconditional assertion.

It remains to prove the conditional assertion.  Assume that \(X\) is
elementary under \(\varphi_{n-1}\).  For distinct \(x,y\in X\), equation
(2) identifies their \(\rho\)-missing sets with their respective
\(\varphi_{n-1}\)-missing sets.  Their disjointness from
\noderef{ColoredVertexSetElementary} therefore proves that \(X\) is also
elementary under \(\rho\).  Since \(v_n\in X\) and \(\gamma_n\) is
missing there under \(\rho\), elementariness shows that \(\gamma_n\) is
missing at no other vertex of \(X\).

After the switch, \(f_n\) makes \(\gamma_n\) present at \(v_n\).  If
\(x\in X-\{v_n\}\), no edge incident with \(x\) has its edge-node in
\(K\): otherwise the adjacency rule of \noderef{TwoColorIncidenceGraph}
would put the vertex-node \(x\) in \(K\), contradicting (4).  Hence the
switch fixes every edge incident with \(x\), and
\noderef{MissingColors} gives
\(\overline\varphi_n(x)=\overline\rho(x)\).  Thus \(\gamma_n\) is
missing at no vertex of \(X\) after the switch.  By the union definition
\noderef{TreeMissingColors},
\[
 \gamma_n\notin\overline\varphi_n(T_n).                           \tag{6}
\]

Finally take \(\alpha\in\overline\varphi_n(T_n)\) with
\(\alpha\ne\delta_n\).  Equation (6) also gives
\(\alpha\ne\gamma_n\).  By \noderef{TreeMissingColors}, choose
\(x\in X\) at which \(\alpha\) is missing under \(\varphi_n\).  If it
were present under \(\rho\), \noderef{PresentColors} would supply an
incident edge identity colored \(\alpha\); since \(\alpha\) is neither
switch color, \noderef{TwoColorComponentSwap} would leave that value
unchanged, contradicting its being missing under \(\varphi_n\).  Hence
\(\alpha\) is missing at \(x\) under \(\rho\).  The same edge-by-edge
switch definition shows, in both directions, that an identity has color
\(\alpha\) under \(\rho\) exactly when it has color \(\alpha\) under
\(\varphi_n\).  Its membership in the coloring-independent boundary is
unchanged, so the two \(\alpha\)-boundary sets are equal.  The
\(\rho\)-boundary is empty because \(X\) is closed under \(\rho\) and
\(\alpha\) is missing at \(x\in X\).  Therefore
\(\partial_{\varphi_n,\alpha}(T_n)=\varnothing\), as required.
\end{proof}

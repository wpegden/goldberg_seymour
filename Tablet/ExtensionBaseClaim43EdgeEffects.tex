\begin{lemma}[Claim 4.3(b3): exact edge effects of restoration]
Let \(\mathcal R\) be a restoration trace satisfying
\noderef{ExtensionBaseRestorationInvariant}, with initial coloring
\(\sigma_n\) and output \(\varrho_1\).  Put
\[
                         \mathcal S^*
   =\bigcup_{t\in L^*}S_t,
\]
using the exact stored pairs of the certified PE slots in
\(L^*=\noderef{ExtensionBaseRestorationIndices}\).  If an exact edge
identity \(f\) has
\(\varrho_1(f)\ne\sigma_n(f)\), then \(f\) is not internal to \(T_j\),
and there are colors \(c,d\in\mathcal S^*\) such that
\[
                     \sigma_n(f)=c,\qquad \varrho_1(f)=d.
\]
This is Claim 4.3(b3), with the two optional coloring values made
explicitly nonempty.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Write the trace from \noderef{ExtensionBaseRestorationTrace} as
\(\sigma^0,\ldots,\sigma^r\), and fix a transition \(a<r\).  Clause (1)
of \noderef{ExtensionBaseRestorationInvariant} gives
\(t_a\in L^*\).  Unfolding
\noderef{ExtensionBaseRestorationIndices}, this says that \(t_a\) lies
in the active set and not in the support class of the distinguished
vertex.  In particular, it lies in the active set.  Unfolding
\noderef{ExtensionBaseActiveIndices} at \(t_a\) now gives
\[
       \Theta_{t_a+1}=\mathrm{PE}.
\]
The certificate field in \noderef{TashkinovSeries} supplies a
\noderef{TashkinovSeriesStep} certificate at the positive index
\(t_a+1\).  Inspect its four constructors.  The initial constructor is
impossible because its index is zero, whereas \(t_a+1\) is positive.
The SE and RE constructors respectively set the kind at this index to
SE and RE, contradicting the displayed PE equality.  Consequently the
certificate has the PE constructor.

In that PE constructor, if its maximum-defective color and chosen
parallel-extension color are denoted by \(\delta\) and \(\gamma\), the
fields called currentStored, currentGamma, and currentDelta state
\[
 S_{t_a}=\{\delta,\gamma\},\qquad
 \Gamma_{t_a+1}=\mathsf{some}(\gamma),\qquad
 \Delta_{t_a+1}=\mathsf{some}(\delta).
\]
The gammaField and deltaField clauses of
\noderef{ExtensionBaseRestorationTrace} identify the latter two option
values with
\(\mathsf{some}(\gamma_{t_a})\) and
\(\mathsf{some}(\delta_{t_a})\), respectively.  Injectivity of
\(\mathsf{some}\) therefore gives the exact equality
\[
          S_{t_a}=\{\delta_{t_a},\gamma_{t_a}\}.
\]
Since \(t_a\in L^*\), both colors on the right belong to the
\(t_a\)-summand of the defining union \(\mathcal S^*\), and hence both
belong to \(\mathcal S^*\).

The transition clause of
\noderef{ExtensionBaseRestorationTrace} says that \(\sigma^{a+1}\) is
obtained from \(\sigma^a\) by the component switch rooted at the exact
vertex node \(v_{t_a}\).  Unfolding
\noderef{TwoColorComponentSwap}, if this transition changes the value
on the exact edge identity \(f\), then the edge node
\(\mathsf{inr}(f)\) lies in that component, the value before the switch
is one of
\(\mathsf{some}(\gamma_{t_a})\) and
\(\mathsf{some}(\delta_{t_a})\), and the value after the switch is the
other one.  By the preceding paragraph, the colors underlying both
values lie in \(\mathcal S^*\).

Now suppose \(\varrho_1(f)\ne\sigma_n(f)\).  If no transition changed
\(f\), induction along the finite trace, starting with the
initialColoring clause of \noderef{ExtensionBaseRestorationTrace}, would
give \(\varrho_1(f)=\sigma_n(f)\), a contradiction.  Thus there is a
first and a last transition changing \(f\).  No earlier transition
changes \(f\), so immediately before the first one its value is
\(\sigma_n(f)\).  The preceding switch analysis therefore gives a color
\(c\in\mathcal S^*\) with
\(\sigma_n(f)=\mathsf{some}(c)\).  No later transition changes \(f\), so
immediately after the last one its value is the final value
\(\varrho_1(f)\).  The same analysis gives a color
\(d\in\mathcal S^*\) with
\(\varrho_1(f)=\mathsf{some}(d)\).  This proves the asserted color
control, including that both optional values are nonempty.

It remains to exclude an internal edge.  Fix, for example, the first
transition changing \(f\), and again write its slot as \(t_a\).  Clause
(1) of \noderef{ExtensionBaseRestorationInvariant} states that the
reference tree \(T_j\) is an ordered initial segment of \(T_{t_a}\).
By \noderef{TreeSequenceInitialSegment}, every indexed vertex entry of
\(T_j\) is the equal entry of \(T_{t_a}\) at the embedded index.  Hence,
using the indexed-entry description in \noderef{TreeSequenceVertexSet},
\[
                         V(T_j)\subseteq V(T_{t_a}).
\]
Indeed, a vertex in \(V(T_j)\) is the value of some vertex index of
\(T_j\); embed that index using the length inequality in the
initial-segment certificate and use its vertex-entry equality to exhibit
the same vertex in \(V(T_{t_a})\).

Assume for contradiction that \(f\) is internal to \(T_j\).  Unfolding
\noderef{InternalEdges}, every vertex incident with this exact edge
identity belongs to \(V(T_j)\).  Choose representatives \(x,y\) of its
unordered endpoint pair, so that
\(G.\operatorname{ends}(f)=\{x,y\}\).  By \noderef{Incident}, both
\(x\) and \(y\) are incident with \(f\); hence both lie in \(V(T_j)\),
and the displayed vertex-set inclusion puts both in \(V(T_{t_a})\).

The switch analysis already put the exact edge node
\(\mathsf{inr}(f)\) in the chosen two-color component and showed that
its pre-switch color is one of
\(\gamma_{t_a},\delta_{t_a}\).  By
\noderef{TwoColorIncidenceGraph}, incidence of \(x\) and \(y\) with
that same edge identity therefore gives graph edges from
\(\mathsf{inr}(f)\) to each of the vertex nodes
\(\mathsf{inl}(x)\) and \(\mathsf{inl}(y)\).  Since
\noderef{TwoColorComponent} is the connected component through
\(\mathsf{inl}(v_{t_a})\), adjoining either one-edge walk shows that
both vertex nodes also belong to that component.  Clause (3) of
\noderef{ExtensionBaseRestorationInvariant} says that its vertex-node
intersection with \(V(T_{t_a})\) is exactly
\(\{v_{t_a}\}\).  Applying that equivalence first to \(x\) and then to
\(y\) gives \(x=v_{t_a}=y\).  Thus
\(G.\operatorname{ends}(f)=\{x,x\}\), contrary to the loopless law in
\noderef{LooplessMultigraph}.  Therefore \(f\) is not internal to
\(T_j\).  Throughout the argument the node \(\mathsf{inr}(f)\), rather
than merely its endpoint pair, was retained, so parallel edge identities
were never identified.
\end{proof}

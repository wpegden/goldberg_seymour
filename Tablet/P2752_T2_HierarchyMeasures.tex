\begin{lemma}[Exact hierarchy transport preserves the minimum measures]
Let $H_1,H_2$ be hierarchies on the same exact terminal tree $T$, possibly
with different generating colorings and certified inputs. Suppose they
satisfy SameHierarchyStages. Then their last starred stages are the same
exact tree, their path numbers are equal, and their ordinary stages at
their respective last internal indices are the same exact tree. In
particular the full-tail lengths and both coordinates of the hierarchy
counterexample measure are unchanged by this transport.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Expand \noderef{SameHierarchyStages}. It supplies equal cores, equal
divider counts, and equality of the whole ordinary stage family after
identifying those counts. Identify the counts first. If their common
value is zero, the last starred stages are the two cores, so they agree.
If the count is positive, \noderef{HierarchyStarredStage} selects the
ordinary stage at that last internal index, so equality of the stage
families again gives the same starred stage. The ordinary stage at the
last internal index agrees in all cases by the same family equality.
In the definition \noderef{HierarchyPathNumber}, the starred-stage
length, terminal tree and every displayed edge endpoint are consequently
identical. Therefore both the interval of candidate indices and the
path-tail predicate on that interval agree; their least candidates, or
their fallback full-tail lengths when there is no candidate, are equal.
Finally \noderef{HierarchyFullTailLength} subtracts the common starred
length from $|T|$, while \noderef{HierarchyCounterexampleMeasure} pairs
this common path number with $|T|$ minus the common last ordinary-stage
length. These formulas give the stated numerical consequences.
\end{proof}

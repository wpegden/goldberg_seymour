\begin{lemma}[Defective-chain structure, part (b)]
Let $(T,e_0,\varphi)$ be a globally maximum triple for $G$.  Let
$\alpha\in\mathcal D(V(T),\varphi)$ be defective, let
$\gamma\in\mathcal F(T,e_0,\varphi)$ be free and missing at a vertex
$u\in V(T)$, and let $z\notin V(T)$.  If the supplied incidence walk $P$
from $u$ to $z$ is a full defective-chain path for
$(T,\varphi,\alpha,\gamma,u,z)$, then
\[
 \partial_{\varphi,\alpha}(V(T))
 =\{f\in\partial_G(V(T)): \text{the edge-node of }f\text{ lies on }P\}.
\]
The equality is between sets of edge identities, including distinct
parallel edges.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Let $X=V(T)$.  By
\noderef{ScheideGlobalMaximumBoundaryFacts}, no $\gamma$-colored edge
leaves $X$.  Since every edge-node on the two-color path has color
$\alpha$ or $\gamma$, it follows immediately that every boundary edge-node
on $P$ has color $\alpha$.  This gives the inclusion from right to left.

For the converse, suppose that an $\alpha$-colored boundary edge identity
$f$ is not on $P$.  Its edge-node belongs to a different
$(\alpha,\gamma)$-component $Q$, since $P$ spans the complete component of
$u$ by the supplied hypothesis and
\noderef{ScheideDefectiveChainPath}.  In particular $Q$ does not contain
$u$.  The defective-color clause of
\noderef{ScheideGlobalMaximumBoundaryFacts} gives a nonempty
$\alpha$-boundary set.  Its missing-color clause therefore shows that
$\alpha$ is missing at no vertex of $X$.  Also $X$ is elementary by
\noderef{ScheideGlobalMaximumOrder}; since $\gamma$ is missing at $u$, it
is present at every other vertex of $X$.  Properness, supplied by the
$k$-triple clause of \noderef{GlobalMaximumTriple}, makes the incident
edge of each present color unique.  Hence every vertex-node of $Q$ that
lies in $X$ is incident in $Q$ with one $\alpha$-edge and one
$\gamma$-edge and is not an end of $Q$.  Finally neither color is used on
a tree edge by \noderef{ScheideGlobalMaximumUnused}.  Thus $Q$ contains
no tree edge and has no end in $X$.  The colors $\alpha$ and $\gamma$ are
distinct: $\alpha$ is present at $u$, whereas $\gamma$ is missing there.

Switch $\alpha$ and $\gamma$ on the entire component $Q$.  Properness is
preserved by \noderef{TwoColorComponentSwapProper}.  The distinguished
edge remains the unique uncolored edge because all edge-nodes of $Q$ are
colored.  No displayed tree edge changes, and no missing-color witness
used by the Tashkinov sequence is lost; hence the same sequence remains a
Tashkinov tree for the switched coloring.  The edge identity $f$ now has
color $\gamma$, while $\gamma$ remains missing at $u$.  Appending $f$ and
its outside endpoint produces a Tashkinov tree with one more vertex.  This
contradicts the global maximality clause of
\noderef{GlobalMaximumTriple}.  Therefore every
$\alpha$-colored boundary edge identity lies on $P$, proving the reverse
inclusion and the claimed identity-wise equality.
\end{proof}

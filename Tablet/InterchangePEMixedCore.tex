\begin{lemma}[Interchangeability across the two PE constituents]
Let $T$ be a positive-rung extended Tashkinov tree constructed from a
$k$-triple $G,e_0,\varphi$, with arbitrary certified input coloring,
terminal ladder tree $T_n$, terminal generating coloring $\varphi_n$,
and terminal stored-color set $D_n$.  Assume that $T$ has maximum
property and that its terminal extension is a PE.  Let $R_n$ and
$T_n\vee R_n$ be the closure and exact ordered union in a hierarchy
base under $\varphi_n$ (respectively `base.closure' and `base.core'
in Lean).

Assume, uniformly over every certified input coloring and generating
coloring, that every positive-rung ETT $T'$ of rung number $s<n$ with
maximum property satisfies all six lower-rung assertions:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $V(T')$ is elementary under its generating coloring;
\item under every proper coloring leaving exactly $e_0$ uncolored and
stable on its final ladder closure, that closure is interchangeable for
two colors whenever at least one of them is missing there;
\item stability descends unconditionally at each PE support to the prefix before the
support and, when its first-appearance index is greater than one, also to
the preceding ladder stage; stored pairs at
distinct PE slots with distinct supports and the same first-appearance
index are disjoint;
\item at a terminal PE, the stored two-color component through the
support meets the terminal ladder tree only at that support;
\item every nonminimum endpoint of a defective boundary color precedes
each later PE support or SE extension vertex allowed by its
first-appearance index; and
\item every proper terminal stable coloring leaving exactly $e_0$
uncolored is a mod-coloring of the ETT.
\end{enumerate}

Let $\sigma_n$ be proper, leave exactly $e_0$ uncolored, and be
$T_n\oplus R_n,D_n,\varphi_n$-stable.

For arbitrary $\alpha,\beta\in[k]$, if
$\alpha\in\overline\sigma_n(T_n)$ and
$\beta\in\overline\sigma_n(R_n)$, then $\alpha,\beta$ are
$T_n\vee R_n$-interchangeable under $\sigma_n$.
All boundaries, trees, and two-color components use the original edge
type, so distinct parallel edges retain their identities.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Write $X=V(T_n)$, $Y=V(R_n)$, and
$U=V(T_n\vee R_n)$, with vertex sets understood as in
\noderef{TreeSequenceVertexSet}.  The generating-color field of
\noderef{ExtendedTashkinovTree} identifies $\varphi_n$ with the last
coloring of the certified series.  Eliminate the last certificate in
\noderef{TashkinovSeries} using the terminal-PE hypothesis.  The PE
constructor of \noderef{TashkinovSeriesStep} supplies the exact support
$v_n$, exact connecting-edge identity $f_n$, and colors
$\gamma_n,\delta_n$.  It also supplies properness and exact
one-uncoloredness of $\varphi_n$, and the equations recording the
terminal $\gamma_n,\delta_n$-switch.  No endpoint pair is substituted
for $f_n$.

The parallel clause of \noderef{HierarchyBase} says that $R_n$ is a
$\varphi_n$-closure of the ordered prefix $T_n(v_n)$, and supplies
an \noderef{OrderedTreeUnion} certificate with
\[
                         U=X\cup Y.                       \tag{1}
\]
It also says that the missing-color set of $T_n$ under the base
coloring is its missing-color set under $\varphi_n$.
The two conjuncts of \noderef{CombinedStableColoring}, with stability
expanded as in \noderef{StableColoring}, say that
$\sigma_n$ is $(T_n,D_n,\varphi_n)$-stable and simultaneously
$(R_n,\varnothing,\varphi_n)$-stable.  Applying
\noderef{CombinedStableClosurePreserved} gives, for every displayed edge
identity $g$ of either tree,
\[
             \sigma_n(g)=\varphi_n(g),                    \tag{2}
\]
and proves that the same ordered tree $R_n$, with the same edge
identities, is a $\sigma_n$-closure of $T_n(v_n)$.

We record three preliminary facts, with their hypotheses checked at the
coloring at which they will be used.

First, $U$ is elementary under $\sigma_n$.  Indeed, retain the same
core, closure, and support position from the hierarchy base.  Equality
of missing-color sets on $T_n$ is the vertex clause of the first stable
relation.  The preceding paragraph supplies the required
$\sigma_n$-closure, and the old ordered-union certificate is unchanged.
These data form a hierarchy base under $\sigma_n$.  The first stable
relation, properness, exact one-uncoloredness, positivity, maximum
property, the terminal-PE equation, and the displayed six lower-rung
assertions are precisely the hypotheses of
\noderef{ParallelClosureUnionElementary}.  Hence
\[
                  U\text{ is elementary under }\sigma_n.   \tag{3}
\]

By \noderef{InterchangePECommonMissingClosed}, a color missing
on both constituents is closed on $U$:
\[
 c\in\overline\sigma_n(T_n)\cap\overline\sigma_n(R_n)
 \Longrightarrow \{c\}\text{ is closed on }U.                \tag{4}
\]
By \noderef{InterchangePEClosureConstituent},
\[
 c\in\overline\sigma_n(R_n)
 \Longrightarrow c,d\text{ are $R_n$-interchangeable},       \tag{5}
\]
and \noderef{InterchangePETreeConstituent} gives
\[
 c\in\overline\sigma_n(T_n)
 \Longrightarrow c,d\text{ are $T_n$-interchangeable}.       \tag{6}
\]
Finally, \noderef{InterchangePEClosedCore} gives
\[
 c\in\overline\sigma_n(U),\quad \{c\}\text{ closed on }U
 \Longrightarrow c,d\text{ are $U$-interchangeable}.         \tag{7}
\]
Each conclusion uses the full incidence-graph definition of
\noderef{TreeInterchangeable}, so it includes singleton components under
\noderef{TwoColorPathComponent}, not only components containing an
edge-node.

It remains to prove the mixed assertion (iv).  Unfold the two hypotheses
with \noderef{TreeMissingColors}.  Choose
\[
 u\in X\quad\hbox{with }\alpha\in\overline\sigma_n(u),
 \qquad
 v\in Y\quad\hbox{with }\beta\in\overline\sigma_n(v).        \tag{8}
\]
Suppose, contrary to the desired conclusion, that the universal statement
\noderef{TreeInterchangeable} fails.  Then there are roots $z_1,z_2$ such
that the full sets
\[
 C_i=\noderef{TwoColorComponent}
       (G,\sigma_n;\alpha,\beta;z_i)
\]
are unequal, each is a \noderef{TwoColorPathComponent}, and each contains
a vertex-node belonging to $U$.  Unfolding either path-component
certificate gives $\alpha\ne\beta$.

We first locate the two witnesses in (8).  If $u\in Y$, then $\alpha$ is
missing on both constituents.  Applying
\noderef{InterchangePECommonMissingClosed}, rather than appealing to an
unnamed intermediate assertion, gives the closedness in (4).  Since
$u\in U$, equation (7) makes $\alpha,\beta$ interchangeable on $U$,
contrary to $C_1\ne C_2$.  Hence $u\notin Y$.  If $v\in X$, the same
argument with $\beta$ gives its closedness from
\noderef{InterchangePECommonMissingClosed}; equation (7), applied with
the colors reversed, gives $\beta,\alpha$ interchangeability.  The
adjacency rule in \noderef{TwoColorIncidenceGraph}, the connected set in
\noderef{TwoColorComponent}, the leaf condition in
\noderef{TwoColorPathComponent}, and the universal condition in
\noderef{TreeInterchangeable} are unchanged when the two displayed colors
are exchanged.  Thus this also contradicts $C_1\ne C_2$.  We have proved
\[
                         u\in X-Y,\qquad v\in Y-X,             \tag{9}
\]
and in particular $u\ne v$.

We next construct, from definitions, the two path components rooted at
$u$ and $v$.  If $\beta$ were also missing at $u$, then (3), unfolded as
\noderef{ColoredVertexSetElementary}, would give disjoint missing-color
sets at the distinct vertices $u,v\in U$, contradicting that both contain
$\beta$.  Hence $\beta\notin\overline\sigma_n(u)$.  Since
\noderef{MissingColors} is the complement of \noderef{PresentColors},
$\beta$ is present at $u$.  The definition of
\noderef{PresentColors} therefore supplies an exact edge identity $b_u$
such that
\[
          \noderef{Incident}(G,u,b_u),\qquad
          \sigma_n(b_u)=\beta.                               \tag{10}
\]
Likewise, $\alpha$ cannot be missing at $v$, because it is missing at the
distinct vertex $u$; hence there is an exact edge identity $a_v$ with
\[
          \noderef{Incident}(G,v,a_v),\qquad
          \sigma_n(a_v)=\alpha.                              \tag{11}
\]

There is no $\alpha$-colored edge incident with $u$: such an edge would
put $\alpha$ in \noderef{PresentColors} at $u$, contrary to (8) and the
complement definition \noderef{MissingColors}.  There is at most one
$\beta$-colored edge incident with $u$.  Indeed, if two distinct exact
identities had that color and were incident with $u$, they would be
\noderef{AdjacentEdges} with common incident vertex $u$, and
\noderef{IsProperEdgeColoring} would contradict their equal nonempty
colors.  Together with (10), this says that $b_u$ is the unique incident
$\beta$-edge at $u$.  By the explicit bipartite adjacency rule in
\noderef{TwoColorIncidenceGraph}, the neighbor set of the vertex-node
$\operatorname{inl}u$ is therefore exactly
$\{\operatorname{inr}b_u\}$.  In particular it is subsingleton.

The root $\operatorname{inl}u$ belongs to its own
\noderef{TwoColorComponent}, by the reflexive connectedness used in that
definition.  Adjacency from (10) gives a one-edge incidence-graph walk
from $\operatorname{inl}u$ to $\operatorname{inr}b_u$, so the latter
edge-node belongs to the same full component.  Taking
$\operatorname{inl}u$ as the subsingleton-neighbor witness and using
$\alpha\ne\beta$ proves that the component
\[
 P_u:=\noderef{TwoColorComponent}
          (G,\sigma_n;\alpha,\beta;\operatorname{inl}u)
\]
satisfies \noderef{TwoColorPathComponent}; the membership of the exact
edge-node $\operatorname{inr}b_u$ also proves
\noderef{TwoColorNontrivialPathComponent}.

The symmetric calculation at $v$ is equally explicit.  Missingness of
$\beta$ rules out every incident $\beta$-edge, (11) and properness make
$a_v$ the unique incident $\alpha$-edge, and hence
\[
 N_{I_G(\sigma_n;\alpha,\beta)}(\operatorname{inl}v)
     =\{\operatorname{inr}a_v\}.
\]
The root lies in its own \noderef{TwoColorComponent}; the adjacency
certified by (11) puts $\operatorname{inr}a_v$ in it.  Thus
\[
 P_v:=\noderef{TwoColorComponent}
          (G,\sigma_n;\alpha,\beta;\operatorname{inl}v)
\]
also satisfies both \noderef{TwoColorPathComponent} and
\noderef{TwoColorNontrivialPathComponent}.  This completes the
definition-level certification needed before invoking either constituent
interchangeability conclusion.

We now identify these rooted components with the counterexample components.
By (1), each
$C_i$ meets $X$ or $Y$.  Equation (5), applied with the common
$Y$-missing color $\beta$, permits at most one
of $C_1,C_2$ to meet $Y$.  Equation (6), using $\alpha$ missing on
$X$, permits at most one to meet $X$.  Since both components meet
$X\cup Y$, no one of them can meet both constituents: the other component
would then have to meet one constituent and violate the corresponding
uniqueness.  After relabeling, $C_1$ meets $X$ and not $Y$, while $C_2$
meets $Y$ and not $X$.  Since $P_u$ is a path component meeting $X$,
(6), applied to $P_u$ and $C_1$, identifies their full
\noderef{TwoColorComponent} sets.  Similarly (5) identifies $P_v$ with
$C_2$.  Thus $P_u\ne P_v$,
and \noderef{TwoColorComponentsDisjoint} makes their full node supports
disjoint.  If any vertex-node of $P_v$ lay in $X$, then $P_v$ and $P_u$
would be two path components meeting $T_n$, and (6) would force them
equal.  Therefore the full component $P_v$ has no
vertex-node belonging to $X$.

Define $\sigma'_n$ by \noderef{TwoColorComponentSwap}, swapping
$\alpha,\beta$ on the full component rooted at the vertex-node $v$.
To instantiate \noderef{OutsideKempeRecoloring}, take the singleton root
set $\{\operatorname{inl}v\}$.  The required distinct-color field is
$\alpha\ne\beta$ above.  The selected object is the full component
$P_v$, not merely a tail.  Its outside-$T_n$ field is exactly the absence
of all $X$-vertex-nodes just proved.  Finally, expanding
\noderef{TwoColorComponentSwap} says for each exact identity $f\in E$
that its value is transposed precisely when the edge-node
$\operatorname{inr}f$ belongs to $P_v$, and is fixed otherwise.  This is
word-for-word the edge-value field of
\noderef{OutsideKempeRecoloring} for the singleton root set.  Hence
$\sigma'_n$ is an outside Kempe recoloring of $T_n$.
Apply \noderef{OutsideKempeRecoloringStable} to $T_n$, with control
set $D_n$, the stated properness and one-uncoloredness, and this
outside-chain fact.  It proves that $\sigma'_n$ is proper, leaves
exactly $e_0$ uncolored, and is
$T_n,D_n,\sigma_n$-stable.  Composing with the given
$(T_n,D_n,\varphi_n)$-stability via \noderef{sc2} makes it
$(T_n,D_n,\varphi_n)$-stable.  This is exactly the stability conclusion
for which the manuscript invokes LEM-Stable; the direct
\noderef{OutsideKempeRecoloringStable} interface has exactly the hypotheses
available here.

Before copying the closure, we record the swap's exact effect at the two
witness vertices.  The stability just obtained has the vertex clause of
\noderef{StableColoring} on every vertex of $X$.  Hence its equality of
\noderef{MissingColors} at $u\in X$ preserves
$\alpha\in\overline\sigma'_n(u)$.  At $v$, equation (11) and the adjacency
rule in \noderef{TwoColorIncidenceGraph} put the exact edge-node
$\operatorname{inr}a_v$ in $P_v$.  The definition
\noderef{TwoColorComponentSwap} therefore changes $a_v$ from $\alpha$ to
$\beta$.  If $f\ne a_v$ is any other exact edge identity incident with
$v$, then $\sigma_n(f)\ne\beta$ because $\beta$ is missing at $v$, and
$\sigma_n(f)\ne\alpha$ because $a_v$ is the unique incident
$\alpha$-edge proved above.  Such an $f$ is fixed by the component-swap
formula, whether or not its edge-node is tested for component membership.
Thus no edge incident with $v$ has color $\alpha$ under $\sigma'_n$.
Using \noderef{PresentColors} and its complement
\noderef{MissingColors}, we obtain
\[
       \alpha\in\overline\sigma'_n(u)
          \cap\overline\sigma'_n(v).                         \tag{12}
\]

We now construct the precise finite part of the closure which must be
copied.  Let
\[
 A=T_n(v_n)
\]
be the exact ordered prefix given by \noderef{HierarchyBase}.  By
\noderef{TreeSequencePrefix}, every vertex of $A$ is a vertex of $T_n$;
hence $v\notin V(A)$ by (9).  The $\sigma_n$-closure supplied by
\noderef{CombinedStableClosurePreserved}, unfolded through
\noderef{TashkinovClosure}, contains a finite
\noderef{TashkinovAugmentationSequence} from $A$ to $R_n$.  Write an
explicit finite presentation of this reflexive-transitive chain as
\[
 A=A_0\longrightarrow A_1\longrightarrow\cdots
   \longrightarrow A_m=R_n,                                 \tag{13}
\]
where every arrow is one \noderef{TashkinovAugmentation} under
$\sigma_n$.  Since $v\in Y=V(R_n)$ but $v\notin V(A_0)$, there is a least
$t\in\{1,\ldots,m\}$ for which $v\in V(A_t)$.  Put $S=A_t$.

At every arrow in (13), \noderef{TashkinovAugmentation} gives a one-step
length increase and an exact \noderef{TreeSequenceInitialSegment}; these
relations compose entry by entry.  Consequently $A$ is an exact ordered
initial segment of $S$, and $S$ is an exact ordered initial segment of
$R_n$.  Equivalently, using the inherited data in
\noderef{TreeSequencePrefix}, $S$ is the prefix of $R_n$ through its
$t$-th newly added vertex.  Applying
\noderef{TashkinovAugmentationSequenceRestrict} to the closure sequence,
these two exact initial-segment certificates, and $S$, isolates the
$\sigma_n$-augmentation sequence
\[
                    A=A_0\longrightarrow\cdots\longrightarrow A_t=S.
                                                               \tag{14}
\]
The last arrow is included.  The length-one extension and vertex
injectivity in \noderef{TreeSequence} say that its newly displayed vertex
is precisely $v$: it is in $A_t$ and, by minimality of $t$, not in
$A_{t-1}$.

We copy (14) one exact step at a time.  For $1\le i\le t$, let $g_i$ be
the exact new last edge identity of $A_i$, and unfold its
\noderef{TashkinovAugmentation} certificate.  It supplies a color $c_i$
with
\[
                 \sigma_n(g_i)=c_i,
       \qquad c_i\in\overline\sigma_n(A_{i-1}).               \tag{15}
\]
Unfold \noderef{TreeMissingColors} in the second clause and choose a
vertex
\[
       w_i\in V(A_{i-1}),\qquad
       c_i\in\overline\sigma_n(w_i).                          \tag{16}
\]
Every $A_{i-1}$ is an exact initial segment of $R_n$, so
\noderef{TreeSequenceInitialSegment} and
\noderef{TreeSequenceVertexSet} put $w_i$ in $Y$.  Hence $w_i\ne u$ by
(9).  Minimality of $t$ says that $v$ is absent from every
$A_{i-1}$ for $i\le t$, so $w_i\ne v$ as well.  All three vertices lie
in $U=X\cup Y$.  Elementariness (3), applied first to the missing colors
$\alpha$ at $u$ and $c_i$ at $w_i$, gives $c_i\ne\alpha$; applied to
$\beta$ at $v$ and $c_i$ at $w_i$, it gives $c_i\ne\beta$.

Now unfold \noderef{TwoColorComponentSwap} on the exact identity $g_i$.
Because $c_i$ is neither switch color, (15) gives
\[
                         \sigma'_n(g_i)=c_i.                 \tag{17}
\]
For every exact edge identity $f$ incident with $w_i$, the same component
swap formula gives
\[
       \sigma'_n(f)=c_i\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
       \sigma_n(f)=c_i,                                      \tag{18}
\]
because the swap exchanges only $\alpha$ and $\beta$ and fixes every
other color and the uncolored value.  By \noderef{PresentColors}, (16)
says that no incident $f$ satisfies the right side of (18); hence none
satisfies the left side.  The complement definition
\noderef{MissingColors} now says that $c_i$ remains missing at $w_i$ under
$\sigma'_n$, and \noderef{TreeMissingColors} puts it in
$\overline\sigma'_n(A_{i-1})$.  Equations (17)--(18) verify the color and
missing-witness clauses of the same one-step
\noderef{TashkinovAugmentation}; its length and exact initial-segment
clauses are independent of the coloring.  Induction on
$i=1,\ldots,t$ therefore copies all of (14), including the arrow which
adjoins $v$, into a
$\sigma'_n$-\noderef{TashkinovAugmentationSequence} from $A$ to $S$.

By \noderef{TashkinovClosureExists}, choose a $\sigma'_n$-closure
$R'_n$ of $S$.  Its augmentation sequence retains $S$ as an exact ordered
initial segment, so $v\in V(S)\subseteq V(R'_n)$.  Concatenate that finite
sequence with the copied sequence from $A$ to $S$.  Transitivity of the
reflexive-transitive relation in
\noderef{TashkinovAugmentationSequence} gives a sequence from $A$ to
$R'_n$, and the final closedness clause is the one in the chosen closure
of $S$.  These are exactly the two clauses of
\noderef{TashkinovClosure}; therefore $R'_n$ is a closure of the original
seed $A=T_n(v_n)$ and contains $v$.

Construct the exact ordered union $T_n\vee R'_n$ by starting with all
entries of $T_n$ and then appending, in their $R'_n$-order, precisely
the entries whose new vertex is not already in $T_n$.  Every appended
edge attaches its new vertex to $T_n$ or to an earlier appended vertex,
because $R'_n$ is an augmentation sequence from a prefix of $T_n$.
Its new vertex and edge identity have not appeared earlier, by the
distinct-entry and attachment clauses of \noderef{TreeSequence} for
$T_n$ and $R'_n$.  Thus this is a
tree-sequence.  Directly from the construction, $T_n$ is its ordered
initial segment, its vertex set is the union, every new entry is the
same exact entry of $R'_n$, every required right-hand entry occurs, and
their relative order is retained.  These are all five fields of
\noderef{OrderedTreeUnion}.

The stable vertex clause identifies
the terminal missing-color set with that under $\varphi_n$; together
with the copied support, closure, and ordered union, this is a hierarchy
base under $\sigma'_n$.  Now all actual hypotheses of
\noderef{ParallelClosureUnionElementary} have been discharged: the
original ETT and terminal PE, positivity and maximum property, the full
arbitrary-input lower package, properness, exact one-uncoloredness,
$(T_n,D_n,\varphi_n)$-stability, and the newly constructed base.  Hence
$V(T_n\vee R'_n)$ is elementary under $\sigma'_n$.

The left initial-segment field of the new ordered union puts $u\in X$ in
$V(T_n\vee R'_n)$, while the vertex-union field and the copied final step
put $v\in V(R'_n)$ there.  They are distinct by (9), and (12) says that
both miss $\alpha$ under $\sigma'_n$.  This contradicts the elementarity
just obtained, after unfolding
\noderef{ColoredVertexSetElementary}.  Therefore two distinct path
components cannot meet $U$, which is precisely conclusion (iv).

Every use of a boundary, augmentation, component swap, or ordered union
above carries elements of the original edge type $E$; consequently no
step identifies distinct parallel edges having the same endpoints.
\end{proof}

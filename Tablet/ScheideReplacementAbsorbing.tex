\begin{lemma}[Scheide replacement vertices are absorbing (15)]
For every defective color $\delta$, both vertices of $U_\delta$ belong to
the absorbing set $A(T,e_0,\varphi)$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Write
\[
 X=V(T),\qquad \gamma=\gamma(\delta),\qquad
 U_\gamma=\{u^1_\gamma,u^2_\gamma\},\qquad
 Z=X\setminus U_\gamma,
\]
and put $U_\delta=\{u^1_\delta,u^2_\delta\}$,
$T_\delta$ for the replacement tree, $X_\delta=V(T_\delta)$, and
$\varphi_\delta$ for the defective-color switch.  Unpacking
\noderef{ScheideReplacementData}, the two named $\delta$-colored edge
identities $f^1_\delta,f^2_\delta$ have respective endpoint pairs
$\{v^1_\delta,u^1_\delta\}$ and
$\{v^2_\delta,u^2_\delta\}$, where $v^1_\delta,v^2_\delta\in Z$ and
$u^1_\delta,u^2_\delta\notin X$.  The same definition gives
\[
 X_\delta=Z\cup U_\delta.                                  \tag{1}
\]
The exact ordered layout in the underlying
\noderef{ScheideReducedConfiguration} and
\noderef{ScheideStructuralConfiguration} puts both vertices of
$U_\gamma$ in $X$; hence the definition of $Z$ also gives
\[
 X=Z\cup U_\gamma.                                         \tag{2}
\]
In particular, both vertices whose absorption is asserted already satisfy
the outside-$X$ conjunct in \noderef{AbsorbingVertex}.

We record the two maximum-tree facts before choosing colors.  The reduced
configuration extends a structural configuration, whose globally maximum
field is a \noderef{GlobalMaximumTriple}; its underlying
\noderef{KTriple} says in particular that $\varphi$ is proper in the sense
of \noderef{IsProperEdgeColoring}.  Applying
\noderef{ScheideGlobalMaximumOrder} to this original maximum triple gives
\[
 X\text{ is elementary and closed with respect to }\varphi. \tag{3}
\]
On the other hand, \noderef{ScheideReplacementMaximum} says both that
$\varphi_\delta$ is proper and that $(T_\delta,e_0,\varphi_\delta)$ is
globally maximum.  A second application of
\noderef{ScheideGlobalMaximumOrder} therefore gives
\[
 X_\delta\text{ is elementary and closed with respect to }
 \varphi_\delta.                                            \tag{4}
\]
Here ``elementary'' and ``closed'' always have exactly the meanings in
\noderef{ColoredVertexSetElementary} and
\noderef{ColoredVertexSetClosed}.

Now fix an arbitrary $u\in U_\delta$, an arbitrary
$\alpha\in\overline\varphi(u)$, and an arbitrary
$\beta\in\mathcal F(T,e_0,\varphi)$ with $\beta\ne\alpha$.  The appropriate
one of the two $\delta$-identities in
\noderef{ScheideReplacementData} is incident with $u$, so $\delta$ is
present at $u$ by \noderef{Incident} and \noderef{PresentColors}.
Moreover, the second conclusion of
\noderef{ScheideSymmetricDefectiveInternal} supplies a
$\gamma$-colored edge identity internal to $U_\delta$.  Its endpoints are
distinct by \noderef{LooplessMultigraph}; since they both lie in the
two-element set $U_\delta$, this identity is incident with the arbitrary
member $u$.  Since \noderef{MissingColors} is the complement of the
present colors, the assumption that $\alpha$ is missing at $u$ gives
\[
 \alpha\ne\delta,\qquad \alpha\ne\gamma.                   \tag{5}
\]

By \noderef{TreeFreeColors}, $\beta$ is missing at some vertex of $X$ and
does not belong to \noderef{TreeUsedColors}$(\varphi,T)$.  The exact block
layout and block-color clause of
\noderef{ScheideStructuralConfiguration} put $\gamma$ on each of the two
colored $\gamma$-block tree edges, so $\gamma$ is used and
$\beta\ne\gamma$.  Also, defectiveness of $\delta$ in
\noderef{DefectiveColors}, expanded using
\noderef{BoundaryColorEdges}, supplies a $\delta$-colored identity in
$\partial_G(X)$.  If $\beta=\delta$, the missing occurrence of $\beta$ in
$X$ would make this identity a forbidden boundary identity by (3) and
\noderef{ColoredVertexSetClosed}.  Thus
\[
 \beta\ne\delta,\qquad \beta\ne\gamma.                     \tag{6}
\]

The coloring $\varphi_\delta$ in
\noderef{ScheideDefectiveSwitchEffect} is exactly the
$(\gamma,\delta)$ component swap.  By the defining formula in
\noderef{TwoColorComponentSwap}, (5)--(6) imply, for every edge identity
$q$,
\[
 \varphi(q)=\alpha\Longleftrightarrow
 \varphi_\delta(q)=\alpha,
 \qquad
 \varphi(q)=\beta\Longleftrightarrow
 \varphi_\delta(q)=\beta.                                  \tag{7}
\]
Consequently \noderef{TwoColorIncidenceGraph} gives literally the same
bipartite $(\alpha,\beta)$ incidence graph for the two colorings, and
\noderef{TwoColorComponent} gives the same full component through every
node.  In particular, by \noderef{Incident},
\noderef{PresentColors}, and \noderef{MissingColors}, the present and
missing status of each of $\alpha,\beta$ at every vertex is also unchanged.

Choose $v\in X$ at which $\beta$ is missing; its existence is the first
clause of \noderef{TreeFreeColors}.  It is unique in $X$: two distinct such
vertices would contradict (3) and
\noderef{ColoredVertexSetElementary}.  By (2), either $v\in Z$ or
$v\in U_\gamma$.

First suppose $v\in Z$.  Then (1) puts $u,v$ in $X_\delta$, and they are
distinct because $u\notin X$ while $v\in X$.  By (7), $\alpha$ is missing
at $u$ and $\beta$ is missing at $v$ under $\varphi_\delta$.  Elementarity
in (4), read through \noderef{ColoredVertexSetElementary}, says that
$\beta$ is present at $u$ and $\alpha$ is present at $v$: otherwise the
two distinct vertices would share a missing color.  The complementary
definitions \noderef{MissingColors} and \noderef{PresentColors} therefore
show that the two corresponding vertex-nodes have degree one in the
incidence graph.  Indeed, $u$ has no incident $\alpha$-identity and has an
incident $\beta$-identity, while $v$ has no incident $\beta$-identity and
has an incident $\alpha$-identity; in each case that present identity is
unique because two distinct incident identities of the same color are
adjacent by \noderef{AdjacentEdges} and contradict properness in
\noderef{IsProperEdgeColoring}.

Every other vertex $x\in X_\delta\setminus\{u,v\}$ has both colors
present: missing $\alpha$ would contradict elementarity with $u$, and
missing $\beta$ would contradict elementarity with $v$.  Again using
\noderef{PresentColors}, \noderef{MissingColors},
\noderef{AdjacentEdges}, and properness, there is exactly one incident
identity of each color at $x$; the two identities are distinct because
$\alpha\ne\beta$.  Hence every such vertex-node has degree two.  Each
edge-node of the two-color incidence graph also has degree two: by
\noderef{TwoColorIncidenceGraph} its neighbors are exactly its incident
endpoint vertices, and \noderef{Incident} together with the loopless law
in \noderef{LooplessMultigraph} says that these are two distinct vertices.
This also checks explicitly both cases behind
\noderef{TwoColorIncidenceDegreeAtMostTwo}.

Because $\alpha$ and $\beta$ are missing in $X_\delta$, closedness in (4)
and \noderef{ColoredVertexSetClosed} forbid every
$\alpha$- or $\beta$-colored identity in the boundary
\noderef{BoundaryEdges}$(G,X_\delta)$.  Thus the full component through
$u$ is trapped in the finite incidence graph over $X_\delta$.  Starting
at the degree-one node $u$, follow at each subsequent degree-two node the
unique incidence other than the one just used.  This walk cannot branch or
stop at an internal node.  It cannot repeat a node: at a first repetition,
the two incidences used on the first visit together with the new entering
incidence would violate the degree-two bound (and returning to $u$ would
violate its degree-one computation).  Finiteness therefore forces the walk
to end at another degree-one node.  The degree computations show that the
only possibility is $v$.  Hence the full $(\alpha,\beta)$ component is a
path with vertex ends $u,v$.

It remains to suppose $v\in U_\gamma$.  The internal identities supplied
below and \noderef{LooplessMultigraph} show that the two vertices in each
pair are distinct, so we may write
\[
 U_\delta=\{u,u'\},\qquad U_\gamma=\{v,v'\}.
\]
Use \noderef{ScheideSymmetricMissingInternal} in its second direction on
the color $\beta$ missing at $v\in U_\gamma$: it gives a
$\beta$-colored edge identity $b_\delta$ with endpoints $u,u'$.  Use its
first direction on the color $\alpha$ missing at $u\in U_\delta$: it gives
an $\alpha$-colored identity $a_\gamma$ with endpoints $v,v'$.  By (7),
these are the same colored identities in the common incidence graph.

We next force the two remaining identities.  In $X_\delta$, the distinct
vertices $u,u'$ cannot both miss $\alpha$ by (4) and
\noderef{ColoredVertexSetElementary}; hence $\alpha$ is present at $u'$ by
\noderef{MissingColors} and \noderef{PresentColors}.  Choose an incident
$\alpha$-identity $a_Z$.  It is unique by
\noderef{AdjacentEdges} and properness in
\noderef{IsProperEdgeColoring}.  Its other endpoint exists by
\noderef{Incident} and differs from $u'$ by
\noderef{LooplessMultigraph}.  Since $\alpha$ is missing at $u$, this
endpoint is not $u$.  Since $\alpha$ is missing in $X_\delta$, (4) and
\noderef{ColoredVertexSetClosed} exclude an $\alpha$-colored identity in
\noderef{BoundaryEdges}$(G,X_\delta)$.  Decomposition (1) therefore forces
the other endpoint of $a_Z$ to lie in $Z$.

Similarly, in the original set $X$, elementarity in (3) forces $\beta$ to
be present at $v'$, because it is missing at the distinct vertex $v$.
By \noderef{MissingColors}, \noderef{PresentColors},
\noderef{AdjacentEdges}, and properness, there is a unique incident
$\beta$-identity $b_Z$ at $v'$.  The loopless law gives a distinct other
endpoint, missingness at $v$ excludes $v$, and original closedness in (3)
excludes a $\beta$-colored member of
\noderef{BoundaryEdges}$(G,X)$.  Decomposition (2) therefore forces the
other endpoint of $b_Z$ to lie in $Z$.

We claim that $a_Z$ and $b_Z$ are exactly the two boundary identities of
$Z$ in the common $(\alpha,\beta)$ incidence graph.  First, every
$z\in Z$ has both colors present.  Under $\varphi_\delta$, it cannot miss
$\alpha$, because $u,z$ are distinct members of the elementary set
$X_\delta$ and $u$ misses $\alpha$.  Under $\varphi$, it cannot miss
$\beta$, because $v,z$ are distinct members of the elementary set $X$ and
$v$ misses $\beta$.  By (7) these assertions concern the same graph.  The
definitions \noderef{MissingColors} and \noderef{PresentColors}, followed
by \noderef{AdjacentEdges} and properness, give exactly one incident
identity of each color and thus degree two at every such vertex-node.

Now let $q$ be any $\alpha$-colored boundary identity of $Z$.  Replacement
closedness, using the missing occurrence of $\alpha$ at $u$, forbids $q$
from leaving $X_\delta$ by
\noderef{ColoredVertexSetClosed} and \noderef{BoundaryEdges}.  Its endpoint
outside $Z$ therefore lies in $U_\delta$ by (1); it cannot be $u$ by
\noderef{MissingColors}, so it is $u'$, and the uniqueness just proved from
\noderef{IsProperEdgeColoring} makes $q=a_Z$.  Conversely $a_Z$ is such a
boundary identity by its two endpoints.  If $q$ is instead
$\beta$-colored, original closedness and the missing occurrence at $v$
force its endpoint outside $Z$ into $U_\gamma$ by (2); it cannot be $v$,
so it is $v'$, and properness makes $q=b_Z$.  Conversely $b_Z$ is a
$\beta$-boundary identity.  This proves the exactness claim.

Finally consider the alternating walk that starts
\[
 u,\ b_\delta,\ u',\ a_Z
\]
and has now entered $Z$.  At $u$ there is no $\alpha$ incidence and the
$\beta$ incidence $b_\delta$ is unique; at $u'$ the displayed identities
are the unique incidences of their respective colors.  Inside $Z$, every
vertex-node has degree two by the preceding paragraph.  Every edge-node
encountered has exactly its two distinct endpoint neighbors by
\noderef{TwoColorIncidenceGraph}, \noderef{Incident}, and
\noderef{LooplessMultigraph}; in particular this realizes the bound in
\noderef{TwoColorIncidenceDegreeAtMostTwo} on the edge-node side as well.
Thus, after entering through $a_Z$, the finite non-backtracking walk has a
unique continuation, cannot branch, and cannot stop internally.  Nor can
it close or repeat before leaving $Z$: a first repeated node would require
a third incidence besides its entering and leaving incidences, while
returning through $a_Z$ would repeat that boundary edge-node.  It must
therefore leave $Z$, and the exact boundary calculation says that its only
unused exit is $b_Z$.  From there the unique continuation is
\[
 b_Z,\ v',\ a_\gamma,\ v,
\]
where it stops because $\beta$ is missing at $v$ and the displayed
$\alpha$-identity is unique by properness.  Hence $u$ and the unique
original tree vertex $v$ missing $\beta$ lie in the same full
$(\alpha,\beta)$ component.

Both cases produce the witness required by \noderef{AbsorbingVertex} for
the arbitrary choices of $u$, $\alpha$, and free $\beta\ne\alpha$.
Together with the outside-$X$ fact from
\noderef{ScheideReplacementData}, this makes each of
$u^1_\delta,u^2_\delta$ absorbing.  By the definition
\noderef{AbsorbingVertices}, $U_\delta\subseteq A(T,e_0,\varphi)$, as
claimed.
\end{proof}

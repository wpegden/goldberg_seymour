\begin{lemma}[Scheide replacement tree is globally maximum (9)]
For each defective color $\delta$, the switched coloring
$\varphi_\delta$ is proper and leaves exactly $e_0$ uncolored, and the
replacement tree $T_\delta$ in the chosen replacement data forms a globally
maximum triple $(T_\delta,e_0,\varphi_\delta)$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Properness and the unique-uncolored-edge assertion are the first two exact
conclusions of \noderef{ScheideDefectiveSwitchEffect}.  It remains to check
the ordered replacement tree.

Delete from $T$ the two named edges and vertices in the
$\gamma(\delta)$ block.  The other block edges attach directly to the
base, by \noderef{ScheideReducedConfiguration}, so deleting this pair does
not invalidate any later attachment.  By the switch-effect statement, no
remaining edge identity incident with $V(T)$ changes color and every
missing-color witness away from $v^0_\delta$ remains valid.  At
$v^0_\delta$, $\delta$ becomes missing.  By
\noderef{ScheideReplacementPairFixed}, the two named replacement edges
retain color $\delta$.  Appending
$f^1_\delta,u^1_\delta,f^2_\delta,u^2_\delta$ therefore satisfies the
Tashkinov condition twice, using the witness $v^0_\delta$.

The vertex-set equation in \noderef{ScheideReplacementData} deletes two
tree vertices and adds two outside vertices.  Hence
$|V(T_\delta)|=|V(T)|=t(G)$.  Together with the already checked properness,
unique uncolored edge, critical and numerical clauses inherited from the
original globally maximum triple, this is exactly
\noderef{GlobalMaximumTriple} for
$(T_\delta,e_0,\varphi_\delta)$.
\end{proof}

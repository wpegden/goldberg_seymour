\begin{helper}[Second normal form in the last-index case]
Let \((\varphi,T)\) be a minimum bad pair with
\(2<\rho(T)=|E(T)|=n\).  Suppose a color \(\beta\), unused on the
displayed colored tree edges, is missing at \(y_n\) and at some
\(y_j\) with \(j\le n-2\).  Then there are an explicit replacement
coloring \(\varphi_2\), the exact last-edge and penultimate-vertex
indices \(r,p\) (so \(r+1=p+1=n\)), and a color \(\gamma\) such that
\((\varphi_2,T)\) is minimum bad, \(\varphi_2(e_r)=\gamma\), and
\(\gamma\) is present at \(y_p\).
\end{helper}
\begin{proof}
Unfold \noderef{TashTreeMinimumBadPair} through
\noderef{TashTreeBadPair} and \noderef{KTriple}.  The clause
\noderef{IsOneEdgeUncolored} says that the exact first edge \(e_1=e_0\)
is the unique uncolored edge.  Since \(n>2\), exact-edge injectivity in
\noderef{TreeSequence} makes the last edge distinct from \(e_1\), so it
is colored; call its color \(\gamma\), and take \(r=n-1\) and
\(p=n-1\) in the formal zero-based indexing.

If \(\gamma\) is present at \(y_p=y_{n-1}\), take
\(\varphi_2=\varphi\).  Suppose instead that it is missing there.  The
condition \noderef{TashkinovTree} for the last edge gives a
missing-\(\gamma\) witness in the prefix through \(y_{n-1}\).  That
prefix is the proper initial segment from
\noderef{TreeSequencePrefix}, and it is elementary by
\noderef{TashTreeProperPrefixElementary}.  Both
the witness and \(y_{n-1}\) miss \(\gamma\) in the sense of
\noderef{MissingColors}; hence \noderef{ColoredVertexSetElementary} and
vertex injectivity from \noderef{TreeSequence} force the witness to be
\(y_{n-1}\) itself.  The same facts show that \(\gamma\) is unused on all
earlier colored tree edges.  Indeed, an earlier tree edge colored
\(\gamma\) would, by \noderef{TashkinovTree}, have a
missing-\(\gamma\) witness at a vertex preceding that edge.  This vertex
is strictly earlier than \(y_{n-1}\), so \noderef{MissingColors} and
\noderef{ColoredVertexSetElementary} would contradict elementariness of
the prefix.

The given \(\beta\) is unused on every colored tree edge, in particular
through \(y_{n-1}\).  The last vertex \(y_n\) misses \(\beta\) and is
incident with the \(\gamma\)-colored last edge by
\noderef{TreeSequence}.  Thus \noderef{MissingColors} gives
\(\beta\ne\gamma\).  Properness, which is needed below, follows by
unfolding \noderef{TashTreeMinimumBadPair} through
\noderef{TashTreeBadPair} and then using \noderef{KTriple}.
Apply \noderef{TashTreeControlledInterchange} to
\(\beta,\gamma\) at \(y_j,y_{n-1}\).  The theorem returns an explicit
component-swap coloring and a new minimum-bad certificate.  Its
component \(Q\), understood as the connected support in
\noderef{TwoColorComponent}, is a path whose two controlled ends are
\(y_j,y_{n-1}\).  At \(y_n\), all premises of
\noderef{MissingColorIncidenceEndpoint} hold: the affected colors are
distinct, \(\varphi\) is proper by \noderef{KTriple}, and \(\beta\) is
missing there.  Hence the vertex-node \(y_n\) has degree at most one in
the \((\beta,\gamma)\)-incidence graph.  On the other hand, the last edge
is incident with \(y_n\) and colored \(\gamma\), so
\noderef{TwoColorIncidenceGraph} joins its edge-node to \(y_n\); the
degree is therefore exactly one.  If \(y_n\) belonged to \(Q\), the
spanning path from \noderef{TwoColorComponentSpanningPath} would make it
an end.  This would be a third end distinct from \(y_j,y_{n-1}\), with
distinctness following from \(j\le n-2\) and
\noderef{TreeSequence}.  Thus \(y_n\notin Q\).  If the last edge-node
belonged to \(Q\), its adjacency to \(y_n\) in
\noderef{TwoColorIncidenceGraph} and connected-component closure in
\noderef{TwoColorComponent} would put \(y_n\) in \(Q\).  Therefore the
last edge-node is outside \(Q\) as well.

Since its node is outside \(Q\), the defining outside case of
\noderef{TwoColorComponentSwap} fixes the last edge's color
\(\gamma\).  At the controlled end \(y_{n-1}\), the nontrivial path from
the distinct vertex \(y_j\) has an incident edge-node which, by
\noderef{TwoColorIncidenceGraph}, has color \(\beta\) or \(\gamma\).  This edge
cannot have color \(\gamma\), because \(\gamma\) is missing at
\(y_{n-1}\) by \noderef{MissingColors}; its old color is therefore
\(\beta\).  Its edge-node lies in \(Q\), so the inside case of
\noderef{TwoColorComponentSwap} changes its exact value from \(\beta\)
to \(\gamma\).  The same edge remains incident with \(y_{n-1}\), and
therefore witnesses \(\gamma\in\noderef{PresentColors}(\varphi_2,y_{n-1})\).
The replacement coloring and its
minimum-bad certificate supplied by
\noderef{TashTreeControlledInterchange}, together with the chosen exact
indices, give every asserted conjunct.
\end{proof}

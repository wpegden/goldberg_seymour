\begin{lemma}[Situation 7.2, Subcase 1.1 outside the stored exception]
In the setting of a
\noderef{HierarchySituationTwoCaseOneData} \(D\), write
\[
 q=C.\mathit{hierarchy}.\mathit{dividerCount},\qquad
 D_{n,q}=\noderef{HierarchyStoredColorsAt}(C.\mathit{hierarchy},q),
 \qquad
 \Gamma^q=\bigcup_{\eta\in D_{n,q}}\Gamma^q_\eta .
\]
Suppose either \(\theta\notin\Gamma^q\), or there is
\(\eta\in D_{n,q}\) such that
\(\theta\in\Gamma^q_\eta\) and \(\eta\) is not missing at
\(y_{p-1}\).  Then a contradiction follows.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Put \(H=C.\mathit{hierarchy}\),
\(A=\noderef{HierarchyStarredStage}(H,q)\),
\(\ell=\noderef{HierarchyFullTailLength}(C)\), and
\[
 B=\noderef{HierarchyFullTailPrefix}(C,\ell-2),\qquad
 B^+=\noderef{HierarchyFullTailPrefix}(C,\ell-1).
\]
Apply \noderef{HierarchyCounterexamplePenultimateElementary} to the
properness, exact one-uncoloredness, lower-rung, fewer-divider,
lexicographic-minimality, and large-set fields of
\noderef{HierarchyMinimumCounterexampleContext}.  It says exactly that
\[
                  V(B^+)\text{ is elementary under }\varphi_n.       \tag{1}
\]
The Case 1 datum makes \(\alpha\) missing at \(y_{p-1}\), whereas
\(\alpha\in D_{n,q}\).  Unfolding
\noderef{HierarchyStoredColorsAt} shows that \(\alpha\) is not missing
on \(A\).  Since \(A\preccurlyeq B\preccurlyeq B^+\) by
\noderef{TashkinovHierarchy} and
\noderef{HierarchyFullTailPrefix}, the vertex \(y_{p-1}\) belongs to
\(B^+\) and is distinct from every earlier displayed vertex.  If any
vertex of \(B\) missed \(\alpha\), it and \(y_{p-1}\) would violate
elementarity (1).  Thus \(y_{p-1}\) is the first displayed vertex
missing \(\alpha\).

The equality
\(\Gamma^q_\alpha=\{\gamma_1,\gamma_2\}\), the avoidance field of
\noderef{GoodHierarchy}, and the preceding first-position calculation
give, for \(j=1,2\),
\[
 \gamma_j\in\overline\varphi_n(A),\qquad
 \gamma_j\notin
 \varphi_n\langle B^+-A\rangle .                              \tag{2}
\]
The tail-exclusion argument includes the divider-zero exception:
if \(A=T_n\) and the first tail edge is the exact SE/RE seed, its color
lies in the certified stored set, while each \(\gamma_j\) is
stage-missing and hence is not in \(D_{n,q}\).  Thus that individual
seed cannot be a \(\gamma_j\)-edge.  No assertion about a parallel edge
with the same endpoints is used.

Apply \noderef{ChangeLemma} to the ordered pairs
\((\gamma_j,\alpha)\).  The two colors are distinct because
\(\gamma_j\) is missing on \(A\), while \(\alpha\) is not.  They are
both missing on \(B^+\), witnessed in \(A\) and at \(y_{p-1}\).
When \(q>0\), the stage-missing statement in (2) is missingness on
\(T_{n,q}\), and supplies the first alternative of the Change Lemma;
when \(q=0\), it is missingness on \(T_n\), and supplies the second.
Because \(\alpha\) is not missing on \(A\), the conditional order and
unused premise is active: \(v_{\gamma_j}\in V(A)\) occurs before
\(y_{p-1}\), and its exact unused interval is the second statement in
(2).  All minimum-context hypotheses are the displayed fields already
used above, and (1) is the required derived (6.6) premise.
It follows that the full two-color components satisfy
\[
 K_{\alpha,\gamma_j}(v_{\gamma_j})
      =K_{\alpha,\gamma_j}(y_{p-1})\qquad(j=1,2),              \tag{3}
\]
where \(v_{\gamma_j}\in V(A)\) is the first
\(\gamma_j\)-missing vertex.

Invoke \noderef{HierarchySituationTwoReorderedTreesCertified} on the
underlying \noderef{HierarchySituationTwoTailData}.  Retain the exact
trees \(T^-,T^*\), their PE or exact-seed SE/RE augmentation routes,
their actual \noderef{CorrespondingExtendedTashkinovTree}
certificates, and maximum property for both replacement ETTs.  Also
retain the certified bases \(\mathcal B^-,\mathcal B^*\) and copied
hierarchies \(H^-,H^*\).  Their cores and closures equal those of the
original base, their support positions have the same numerical value,
their divider count is \(q\), their stages through index \(q\) equal the
old stages in order, and their divider positions have the old numerical
values.  In particular,
\[
 V(T^-)=V(T)\setminus\{y_{p-1}\},\qquad V(T^*)=V(T).          \tag{4}
\]

We next construct actual \noderef{GoodHierarchy} witnesses
\(Q^-\) on \(H^-\) and \(Q^*\) on \(H^*\).  Transport the old total
Gamma function across the certified equality of divider counts.  The
retained stages and exact raw stored-set agreement in the corresponding
series show directly from \noderef{HierarchyStoredColorsAt} that every
\(D_{n,j}\) is unchanged.  The stage-missing sets are unchanged as well.
Consequently the two-color cardinalities and pairwise disjointness are
the old fields.  Every refresh clause uses only stages through
\(T_{n,q}\), and every minus-closed clause uses a stage of index at most
\(q\); these are retained ordered trees, so those fields are old fields
after transport.  The parallel-base clause is also unchanged because
the certificate gives literal equality of the core and closure, while
correspondence gives the same terminal ladder tree and kind.  It remains
only to prove avoidance.

For \(j<q\), both the starred stage and the next stage are retained, so
the defining edge interval in \noderef{HierarchySegmentColors} is
exactly the old interval, as is the relevant
\noderef{HierarchyStageMissingColors} set in the
\noderef{GoodHierarchy} avoidance clause.
Fix the terminal index \(j=q\), a stored color \(\zeta\in D_{n,q}\),
and \(c\in\Gamma^q_\zeta\).  All entries of \(B\) have the old order.
If \(\zeta\) is missing at a vertex of \(B\), its first missing position
is an unchanged position of \(B\), so both new segment intervals are
contained in that same old interval.  Suppose it is not missing on
\(B\).  Then every old and new cutoff is one of the positions of
\(y_{p-1}\), \(y_p\), or the last-position fallback.  The tree \(T^-\)
deletes \(e_{p-1}\).  In \(T^*\), if the moved-later edge
\(e_{p-1}\) occurs before the new cutoff, then \(\zeta\) is present at
the earlier vertex \(y_p\), so in the old order \(e_{p-1}\) already
occurred before either the cutoff at \(y_{p-1}\) or the later fallback.
Thus an occurrence on \(e_{p-1}\) was already prohibited by the old
avoidance field.  Every edge of \(B\) is unchanged.  The only remaining
edge moved to an earlier position is \(e_p\), and its color is
\(\theta\).  It follows in both cases that every \(c\ne\theta\) excluded
from the old segment remains excluded from both new segments.  Thus the
only possible new avoidance failure has \(c=\theta\).

If \(\theta\notin\Gamma^q\), this possible failure cannot occur for any
\(\zeta\).  Suppose instead that
\(\theta\in\Gamma^q_\eta\) for the selected
\(\eta\in D_{n,q}\), with
\(\eta\notin\overline\varphi_n(y_{p-1})\).  Membership in
\noderef{HierarchyStoredColorsAt} says that \(\eta\) is not missing on
the last starred stage \(A\).  In the old display, \(e_p\) has edge
position \(m-1\), while \(|A|\leq m-2\).  Since the old avoidance field
excludes its color \(\theta\) from the segment preceding the first
\(\eta\)-missing vertex, that first missing position is at most
\(m-1\).  It is not \(m-1\), because that vertex is
\(y_{p-1}\) and \(\eta\) is present there.  Hence it is at most
\(m-2\), so the first \(\eta\)-missing vertex is already in \(B\).
That vertex and every earlier entry are unchanged in both reorderings.
The moved edge \(e_p\), now at position \(m-2\), is therefore not
strictly before the \(\eta\)-cutoff, as required by the definition of
the segment-color set.  Finally, if \(\zeta\ne\eta\), pairwise
disjointness of the old Gamma pairs and
\(\theta\in\Gamma^q_\eta\) give
\(\theta\notin\Gamma^q_\zeta\).  Thus every stored color and every
Gamma color satisfies terminal avoidance.  We have now filled all
fields of the two witnesses \(Q^-\) and \(Q^*\), rather than assuming
that goodness follows from correspondence.

We now derive the contradiction in the two possibilities for
\(\gamma_1\) at \(y_p\).  If
\(\gamma_1\in\overline\varphi_n(y_p)\), then
\(v_{\gamma_1}\in V(A)\subseteq V(T^-)\) and \(y_p\in V(T^-)\) are
distinct by vertex injectivity in \noderef{TreeSequence}.  They both
miss \(\gamma_1\), so \(V(T^-)\) is not elementary.  The corresponding
ETT, the certified base \(\mathcal B^-\), the actual hierarchy \(H^-\),
its maximum property, and \(Q^-\) construct a
\noderef{HierarchyCounterexample} \(C^-\).  Correspondence gives the
same rung, and the certificate gives divider count \(q\).  Its last
starred stage is the old \(A\): for \(q=0\) this follows from equality of
the bases' cores, and for \(q>0\) from equality of the retained stage at
index \(q\).  The append and length equations give full-tail length
\(\ell-1\).  Unfolding \noderef{HierarchyPathNumber}, either its finite
candidate set is empty and the value is that full-tail length, or its
minimum belongs to the interval ending there.  Hence its path number is
at most \(\ell-1\).  The datum says that the old path number is
\(\ell\).  Therefore the first coordinate of
\noderef{HierarchyCounterexampleMeasure} is strictly smaller, and
\noderef{HierarchyCounterexampleLess}(C^-,C)\) contradicts the
lexicographic-minimality field of the context.

It remains that \(\gamma_1\) is present at \(y_p\).  Work in \(T^*\).
By (4), its vertex set is the same as that of \(T\), so the original
non-elementarity witness, the corresponding ETT, the certified base
\(\mathcal B^*\), its maximum property, the actual hierarchy \(H^*\),
and \(Q^*\) form a counterexample \(C^*\).  The retained last starred
stage and \(|T^*|=|T|\) make its full-tail length \(\ell\).  As above,
its path number is at most \(\ell\).  If it is smaller, the first
coordinate contradicts minimality.  We may therefore assume it equals
the old value \(\ell\).  The last ordinary hierarchy stage at index
\(q\) is also retained, and \(|T^*|=|T|\), so the second measure
coordinate is unchanged.  Thus
\(\mathfrak m(C^*)=\mathfrak m(C)\).

Construct an actual minimum context \(\mathcal X^*\) for \(C^*\).
Properness and exact one-uncoloredness are the same statements about
\(\varphi_n\).  For the lower-rung field, correspondence gives
\(\mathcal E^*.\mathit{rounds}=\mathcal E.\mathit{rounds}\), so the
same strict lower-rung inequalities invoke the old complete package.
For the fewer-divider field, a same-rung maximum ETT with fewer dividers
than \(H^*\) has fewer than \(q\) dividers and the same rung as the old
ETT, so the original fewer-divider field applies.  For lexicographic
minimality, a counterexample smaller than \(C^*\) is smaller than \(C\)
because their two measures are equal, contradicting the old field.
Finally, correspondence identifies the terminal ladder tree and stored
series data, while its rung agreement identifies \(2n+11\); hence the
old large missing-set inequality is exactly the required one.  These
are all five fields of
\noderef{HierarchyMinimumCounterexampleContext}, so \(\mathcal X^*\)
is not merely an appeal to the old context on a different ETT.

Now apply \noderef{HierarchyCounterexamplePenultimateElementary} anew
to \(C^*\) and the five fields of \(\mathcal X^*\).  Its last starred
stage is \(A\), its full-tail length is \(\ell\), and its penultimate
full-tail prefix has length \(|A|+\ell-1=|T|-1\).  The exact relation
\(T^-+(e_{p-1},y_{p-1})=T^*\) in
\noderef{HierarchySituationTwoReorderedTrees} says precisely that the
first \(|T|-1\) edge entries and first \(|T|\) vertex entries of \(T^*\)
are \(T^-\).  Therefore the newly derived conclusion is
\[
                  V(T^-)\text{ is elementary under }\varphi_n.       \tag{5}
\]
This is not the original elementarity statement (1), whose tree is
\(B^+\).

The display of \(T^*\) reaches \(y_p\) before \(y_{p-1}\).  Since
\(\alpha\) is not missing on \(A\), and \(y_{p-1}\) is an
\(\alpha\)-missing vertex of the elementary set \(V(B^+)\), equation
(1) says that \(\alpha\) is not missing at any vertex of \(B\).  Since
it is missing at \(y_p\), its first missing vertex in \(T^*\) is
\(y_p\).  The first \(\gamma_1\)-missing vertex remains
\(v_{\gamma_1}\in V(A)\).  Equation (2) excludes \(\gamma_1\) from the
old segment through \(B\).  The newly inserted earlier edge has color
\(\theta\), and \(\theta\ne\gamma_1\): this is immediate in the first
nonexceptional alternative, while in the second the selected
\(\eta\ne\alpha\) because \(\alpha\) is missing and \(\eta\) is present
at \(y_{p-1}\), so disjointness of
\(\Gamma^q_\eta\) and \(\Gamma^q_\alpha\) proves it.  Hence
\(\gamma_1\) is unused on the exact \(T^*\)-interval from \(A\) to
\(y_p\).  The two colors are missing on the new penultimate prefix
\(T^-\), by their witnesses \(v_{\gamma_1}\) and \(y_p\).  Together
with (5), the actual counterexample \(C^*\), and the actual minimum
context \(\mathcal X^*\), these facts verify every premise of a second
application of \noderef{ChangeLemma} with ordered pair
\((\gamma_1,\alpha)\).  It gives
\[
 K_{\alpha,\gamma_1}(v_{\gamma_1})
      =K_{\alpha,\gamma_1}(y_p).                              \tag{6}
\]
Together with (3), this puts the three distinct vertices
\(v_{\gamma_1},y_{p-1},y_p\) in one two-color component, with
\(\gamma_1\) missing at the first and \(\alpha\) missing at the other
two.  They are distinct because \(v_{\gamma_1}\) lies in
\(V(A)\subseteq V(B)\), whereas \(y_{p-1}\) and \(y_p\) occupy the two
successive positions after \(B\), and the vertex map of
\noderef{TreeSequence} is injective.  At a vertex missing
\(\gamma_1\), no incident edge has color \(\gamma_1\), and properness
in \noderef{IsProperEdgeColoring} permits at most one incident
\(\alpha\)-edge.  Thus \(v_{\gamma_1}\) has two-color incidence degree
at most one.  The same argument with the colors reversed applies at
each of \(y_{p-1},y_p\), which miss \(\alpha\).  The component has a
spanning path by \noderef{TwoColorComponentSpanningPath}; all three
vertices must be endpoints of that path, but a finite path has at most
two endpoints.  This contradiction completes the nonexceptional branch.
\end{proof}

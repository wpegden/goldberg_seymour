\begin{helper}[The second recoloring has the full replacement suffix]
Fix a defective color $\delta$ and a
\noderef{ScheideDefectiveReturnData} $R$.  Write
$\gamma=\gamma_{\gammaIndex(\delta)}$, $\eta=\gamma_{R.j}$, and set
\[
 \psi_1=\noderef{OutsideTreeColorSwap}(T,\varphi,\gamma,\eta),
 \qquad
 K=\noderef{TwoColorComponent}
      (G,\psi_1,\eta,\delta,\operatorname{inl}R.v_1),
\]
\[
 \psi_2=\noderef{TwoColorComponentSwap}
      (G,\psi_1,\eta,\delta,\operatorname{inl}R.v_1).
\]
Then $\psi_1$ is proper, leaves exactly $e_0$ uncolored, is
$(T,\varnothing,\varphi)$-stable, and fixes every edge identity incident
with $T$.  The set $K$ is
exactly the set of nodes of the recorded replacement path at or after
the stored position of $R.v_1$; it contains $R.f_1$, meets $V(T)$ only
at $R.v_1$, and contains no displayed edge identity of $T$.  Moreover
$\psi_1(R.f_1)=\delta$.

The coloring $\psi_2$ is proper and leaves exactly $e_0$ uncolored;
$\delta$ is missing at $R.v_1$, every other vertex of $V(T)$ has its
original missing-color set, and
$\psi_2(f^0_\delta)=\delta$.
\end{helper}
\begin{proof}
Put $X=\noderef{TreeSequenceVertexSet}(T)$.  The return datum says that
$\gamma$ and $\eta$ are distinct and missing on $X$, at
$v^0_\delta$ and $R.v_1$ respectively.  Expanding the globally maximum
triple from \noderef{ScheideReducedConfiguration}, its
\noderef{KTriple} makes $\varphi$ proper and leaves exactly $e_0$
uncolored, while \noderef{ScheideGlobalMaximumOrder} makes $X$ closed.
Therefore \noderef{OutsideTreeColorSwapStable}, with empty protected
color set, proves all four asserted properties of $\psi_1$.  In
particular $R.f_1$, being incident with $R.v_1\in X$, is fixed and
retains color $\delta$.

Let $Q$ be the suffix of the recorded replacement path beginning at the
stored occurrence of $R.v_1$.  The last-return field says
$V(Q)\cap X=\{R.v_1\}$ and identifies its first later edge and vertex as
$R.f_1,R.u_1$.  Every later edge of $Q$ has both endpoints outside
$X$.  Its old color is $\gamma$ or $\delta$ by
\noderef{TwoColorIncidenceGraph}; along the simple proper path these
colors alternate.  Hence the outside swap changes precisely the later
$\gamma$-edges to $\eta$ and fixes the $\delta$-edges.  Thus $Q$ is an
$(\eta,\delta)$ walk under $\psi_1$.

We check that no incidence adjacency leaves this walk.  At $R.v_1$ the
color $\eta$ is missing and $R.f_1$ is the unique incident
$\delta$-edge.  The predecessor edge on the old path has color
$\gamma$; it is incident with $X$, remains $\gamma$, and so is absent
from the new two-color graph.  At every internal vertex of $Q$, its two
path edges have distinct colors $\eta,\delta$, and properness excludes a
third such edge.  An internal edge-node has only its two endpoint
adjacencies by \noderef{TwoColorIncidenceGraph}.

For the terminal endpoint, the old full replacement path from
$v^0_\delta$ is the component support by
\noderef{ScheideDefectiveChainPath}.  Its initial vertex misses
$\gamma$.  Since $\delta$ belongs to \noderef{DefectiveColors}, there is
a $\delta$-colored boundary identity of $X$.  Closedness therefore makes
$\delta$ present at every vertex of $X$ by \noderef{MissingColors} and
\noderef{PresentColors}; properness makes its incident identity unique.
Thus the initial vertex has degree one in the old two-color graph.  The degree bound
\noderef{TwoColorIncidenceDegreeAtMostTwo}, simplicity, connectedness,
and finiteness therefore make the other endpoint degree one as well.
On edges wholly outside $X$, the new $\eta,\delta$ status is exactly the
old $\gamma,\delta$ status.  On a boundary edge, closedness excludes
both $\gamma$ and $\eta$, so the outside swap fixes the relevant status.
The terminal endpoint consequently remains degree one.  Both endpoints
and all internal nodes have now been checked, proving that the support
of $Q$ is exactly $K$.  This also proves the asserted intersection with
$X$ and the absence of displayed tree-edge nodes.

The colors $\eta$ and $\delta$ are distinct, so
\noderef{TwoColorComponentSwapProper} makes $\psi_2$ proper.  Directly
from \noderef{TwoColorComponentSwap}, uncolored values remain uncolored
and colored values remain colored, so the exact-one-uncolored property
is preserved.  At $R.v_1$ the component switch changes the unique
incident identity $R.f_1$ from $\delta$ to $\eta$ and fixes all other
incident identities.  Thus $\delta$ becomes missing there.  For every
other vertex of $X$, neither its vertex-node nor an incident edge-node
is in $K$; \noderef{TwoColorComponentSwapMissingBehavior} and the
stability of the first swap give its original missing set.

Finally $f^0_\delta$ is incident with $v^0_\delta\in X$, so the outside
swap fixes it.  If its edge-node belonged to $K$, its incidence with
$v^0_\delta$ would put that vertex-node in $K$, contrary to
$K\cap X=\{R.v_1\}$ and $R.v_1\ne v^0_\delta$.  The component switch
therefore fixes this exact identity too, and
\noderef{ScheideDefectiveSetup} gives
$\psi_2(f^0_\delta)=\delta$.
\end{proof}

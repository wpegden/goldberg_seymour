\begin{lemma}[Defective-chain structure, part (c)]
Let $(T,e_0,\varphi)$ be a globally maximum triple for $G$.  Let
$\alpha\in\mathcal D(V(T),\varphi)$ be defective, let
$\gamma\in\mathcal F(T,e_0,\varphi)$ be free and missing at a vertex
$u\in V(T)$, and let $z\notin V(T)$.  If the supplied incidence walk $P$
from $u$ to $z$ is a full defective-chain path for
$(T,\varphi,\alpha,\gamma,u,z)$, then there exist incidence indices
$i_1<i_2$ and vertices $v_1,v_2$ satisfying the
first-departure/last-return predicate for
$(T,\varphi,\alpha,\gamma,u,z,P)$.  Equivalently, $v_1$ is the first
vertex of $P$ outside $V(T)$ and $v_2$ is the last vertex of $P$ in
$V(T)$ after that first departure.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
By the supplied hypothesis and \noderef{ScheideDefectiveChainPath}, the
particular walk $P$ is a finite simple incidence path, starts at the tree
vertex $u$, ends at the outside vertex $z$, and spans the full two-color
component through $u$.  Hence its finite ordered list of vertex-nodes has
a first member outside $V(T)$; call it $v_1$.  The corresponding incidence
index is the first-departure index $i_1$.

By \noderef{ScheideGlobalMaximumBoundaryFacts}, the defective color
$\alpha$ has at least three distinct boundary edge identities, whereas no
$\gamma$-edge leaves $V(T)$.  By
\noderef{ScheideDefectiveChainBoundaryEdges}, all of those
$\alpha$-boundary edge-nodes occur on $P$.  Starting inside and crossing
the boundary at least three times before ending outside forces a return to
$V(T)$ after the first departure.  Hence there is a tree vertex after
$v_1$.  Among the finitely many such later vertex-nodes choose the last
one and call it $v_2$.  The first and last choices give exactly the index
conditions in \noderef{ScheideDepartureReturn}; its full-path conjunct is
the supplied hypothesis.  Notice that the indices are incidence-walk
indices: boundary edge-nodes occur between consecutive multigraph
vertex-nodes, so no crossing is lost by parallelism.
\end{proof}

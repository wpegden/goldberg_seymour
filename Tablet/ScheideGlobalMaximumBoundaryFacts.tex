\begin{lemma}[Scheide maximum-triple boundary facts (c)--(e)]
Let $(T,e_0,\varphi)$ be globally maximum.  If it has no defective color,
then $V(T)$ is strongly closed.  Every color missing on $V(T)$ has no
boundary edge of that color.  For every defective color $\alpha$, the
number $|\partial_{\varphi,\alpha}(V(T))|$ is odd and at least three.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
By \noderef{ScheideGlobalMaximumOrder}, $X=V(T)$ is elementary and closed,
and $|X|$ is odd.

If the defective-color set from \noderef{DefectiveColors} is empty, no two
distinct boundary edge identities share a color: any shared color would
have at least two members in the exact boundary-color set of
\noderef{BoundaryColorEdges} and would therefore be defective.  Together
with closure, this is precisely strong closure as defined in
\noderef{ColoredVertexSetStronglyClosed}.

If $\alpha$ is missing on $X$, closure forbids every $\alpha$-colored
boundary edge identity.  This gives
$\partial_{\varphi,\alpha}(X)=\varnothing$ directly from
\noderef{TreeMissingColors}, \noderef{ColoredVertexSetClosed}, and
\noderef{BoundaryColorEdges}.

Finally fix $\alpha\in\noderef{DefectiveColors}(G,\varphi,X)$.  By the
definitions of \noderef{DefectiveColors} and \noderef{DefectiveColor},
$|\partial_{\varphi,\alpha}(X)|\geq2$, so this boundary-color set is
nonempty.  Choose $b\in\partial_{\varphi,\alpha}(X)$.  The definition in
\noderef{BoundaryColorEdges} says that $b\in\noderef{BoundaryEdges}(G,X)$
and $\varphi(b)=\alpha$.  Since $X$ is closed, for every $x\in X$ the
definition in \noderef{ColoredVertexSetClosed}, applied to this same edge
$b$, gives $\alpha\notin\noderef{MissingColors}(G,\varphi,x)$.  By
\noderef{MissingColors}, the missing colors are the complement of
\noderef{PresentColors}; hence $\alpha$ lies in
$\operatorname{PresentColors}(G,\varphi,x)$.  The definition in
\noderef{PresentColors} therefore supplies an edge identity $f$ with
\noderef{Incident}$(G,x,f)$ and $\varphi(f)=\alpha$.

This incident $\alpha$-edge is unique at $x$.  Indeed, global maximality in
\noderef{GlobalMaximumTriple} includes the $k$-triple condition
\noderef{KTriple}, and that condition says that $\varphi$ is proper in the
sense of \noderef{IsProperEdgeColoring}.  If two distinct $\alpha$-colored
edge identities $f$ and $g$ were both incident with $x$, then $x$ would
witness \noderef{AdjacentEdges}$(G,f,g)$, contradicting properness.

Define the finite incidence set and the internal $\alpha$-edge set by
\[
 I_\alpha=\{(x,f)\in X\times E(G):
      \noderef{Incident}(G,x,f)\ \hbox{and}\ \varphi(f)=\alpha\},
 \qquad
 E^{\mathrm{int}}_\alpha(X)=
 \{f\in\noderef{InternalEdges}(G,X):\varphi(f)=\alpha\}.
\]
For each $x\in X$, let $q_x$ be the unique incident $\alpha$-edge just
proved to exist.  The map $x\mapsto(x,q_x)$ is a bijection from $X$ to
$I_\alpha$, with inverse the first-coordinate projection.  Consequently,
\[
 |I_\alpha|=|X|. \tag{1}
\]

We now count $I_\alpha$ by its edge coordinate.  Fix an edge identity $f$
appearing in some pair $(x,f)\in I_\alpha$.  By \noderef{Incident}, $x$ is
one of the two entries of the unordered endpoint pair of $f$.  Write the
other entry as $y$.  The loopless condition in
\noderef{LooplessMultigraph} gives $y\ne x$.  If $y\in X$, then both
distinct endpoints lie in $X$, so \noderef{InternalEdges} puts $f$ in
$E^{\mathrm{int}}_\alpha(X)$, and the fiber of $I_\alpha$ over $f$ is
exactly $\{x,y\}$ and has size two.  If $y\notin X$, then
\noderef{BoundaryEdges} puts $f$ in the boundary, and
\noderef{BoundaryColorEdges}, together with $\varphi(f)=\alpha$, puts it
in $\partial_{\varphi,\alpha}(X)$; its fiber is exactly $\{x\}$ and has
size one.  Conversely, the definitions of \noderef{InternalEdges} and
\noderef{Incident} show that every edge in
$E^{\mathrm{int}}_\alpha(X)$ has exactly its two distinct endpoints in its
fiber, while \noderef{BoundaryEdges} says that every edge in
$\partial_{\varphi,\alpha}(X)$ has exactly its unique endpoint in $X$ in
its fiber.  Thus these are all nonempty edge fibers, and they are disjoint
by edge identity.  Summing the fiber sizes gives
\[
 |I_\alpha|
   =2|E^{\mathrm{int}}_\alpha(X)|
      +|\partial_{\varphi,\alpha}(X)|. \tag{2}
\]

By the first paragraph, $|X|$ is odd.  Equations (1) and (2) express this
odd number as an even number plus
$|\partial_{\varphi,\alpha}(X)|$, so the latter cardinality is odd.  Its
membership in \noderef{DefectiveColors}, through
\noderef{DefectiveColor}, already gives cardinality at least two.  An odd
natural number at least two is at least three, as required.
\end{proof}

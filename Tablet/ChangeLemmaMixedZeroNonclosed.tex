\begin{lemma}[Change lemma, mixed location: zero-divider nonclosed case]
Fix a finite loopless multigraph with its exact edge set, a palette of
$k$ colors, and a distinguished edge $e_0$.  Let $(T,\varphi_n)$ be a
positive-rung extended Tashkinov tree.  Let $\mathcal B$ be a hierarchy
base and let
\[
 T_n=T_{n,0}\subset T_{n,1}\subset\cdots\subset
 T_{n,q}\subset T_{n,q+1}=T
\]
be a good hierarchy under $\varphi_n$, where $q$ is its last divider
index and
\[
 A=T_{n,q}^{*}=\noderef{HierarchyStarredStage}(H,q).
\]
Assume that $T$ has maximum property, that $V(T)$ is not elementary,
that $\varphi_n$ is proper and leaves exactly $e_0$ uncolored, and that
the rung number is positive.  These data form a
\noderef{HierarchyCounterexample} $C$.

Assume the six lower-rung assertions of the technical theorem, uniformly
for every positive-rung maximum ETT on the same graph, palette, and
distinguished edge, with arbitrary certified input and generating
colorings and with rung number smaller than that of $T$: lower-rung
elementarity, terminal interchangeability, the two PE stability and
stored-pair assertions, the terminal PE component assertion, the
defective-endpoint order assertion, and the mod-coloring assertion.
Assume also the same-rung induction hypothesis that every maximum ETT
with a good hierarchy having fewer than $q$ dividers is elementary.
Finally assume the large-color estimate
\[
 2n+11\leq |\overline\varphi_n(T_n)|.
\]

Put $\ell=|T|-|A|$, the number of exact tail additions above $A$, and let
\[
 T(y_{\ell-1})=T\big|_{\min\{|A|+(\ell-1),|T|\}}
\]
be the exact ordered prefix supplied by \noderef{TreeSequencePrefix}.
Assume (6.6): $V(T(y_{\ell-1}))$ is elementary under $\varphi_n$.
Assume moreover that $C$ is lexicographically minimal among all
\noderef{HierarchyCounterexample}s on the fixed graph, palette, and
$e_0$ whose ETTs have the same rung number as $T$ and which have exactly
$q$ dividers, allowing their certified input colorings, generating
colorings, bases, ETTs, and good hierarchies to vary.  Explicitly, no
such same-rung counterexample $C'$ satisfies
\[
 \noderef{HierarchyCounterexampleLess}
  \bigl(\noderef{HierarchyCounterexampleMeasure}(C'),
        \noderef{HierarchyCounterexampleMeasure}(C)\bigr).
\]

Let $\alpha\ne\beta$ be colors missing on $T(y_{\ell-1})$.  Write
\[
 i_c=\noderef{HierarchyFirstMissingPosition}(\varphi_n,T,c),
 \qquad v_c=T.\mathit{vertex}(i_c).
\]
If at least one of $\alpha,\beta$ is not missing on $A$, assume both
\[
 v_\alpha\prec v_\beta                                      \tag{a}
\]
and the exact edge-identity exclusion
\[
 \alpha\notin
 \left\{c:\exists t\in\operatorname{Fin}|T|,
       |A|\leq t<i_\beta,
       \ \varphi_n(T.\mathit{edge}(t))=c\right\}.             \tag{b}
\]
Thus (b) is precisely
$\alpha\notin\varphi_n\langle T(v_\beta)-A\rangle$; it is not
an endpoint-only abbreviation.


Assume in addition that the first $\alpha$-missing vertex lies in the
last starred stage and the first $\beta$-missing vertex lies outside it:
\[
        v_\alpha\in V(A),\qquad v_\beta\notin V(A).
\]
Assume that $q=0$, that $\{\alpha\}$ is not closed on $A$, and
that
\[
 \alpha\in\overline\varphi_n(T_n)
 \quad\hbox{or}\quad
 \{\alpha,\beta\}\cap D_n=\varnothing.
\]
Then
\[
 \noderef{TwoColorComponent}(G,\varphi_n,\alpha,\beta,
       \operatorname{inl}v_\alpha)
 =
 \noderef{TwoColorComponent}(G,\varphi_n,\alpha,\beta,
       \operatorname{inl}v_\beta).
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Write $\mathcal E$ for the given ETT, put $H=C.\mathit{hierarchy}$, and use
the notation of the statement.  Thus $q=0$,
$A=\noderef{HierarchyStarredStage}(H,0)$, and, writing $n$ for the positive
rung number,
\[
 T_n=\mathcal E.\mathit{series}.\mathit{tree}(n),\qquad
 D_n=\noderef{TashkinovStoredColors}(\mathcal E.\mathit{series},n).
\]
For a vertex $x$, write
\[
 K_x=\noderef{TwoColorComponent}
       (G,\varphi_n,\alpha,\beta,\operatorname{inl}x).
\]

We first record the exact prefix facts.  The nestedness and terminal fields
of \noderef{TashkinovHierarchy}, together with the zero-index case of
\noderef{HierarchyStarredStage}, make $A$ an exact ordered initial segment
of $T$.  If $\ell=|T|-|A|$ were zero, equality of lengths and
\noderef{TreeSequenceInitialSegment} would identify $A=T$ entry by entry.
The full-length instance of \noderef{TreeSequencePrefix} would then identify
$T(y_{\ell-1})$ with $T$, contrary to the `notElementary' field of
\noderef{HierarchyCounterexample}.  Hence $\ell>0$, and $A$ is an exact
initial segment of the assumed elementary prefix $T(y_{\ell-1})$.
Restriction of \noderef{ColoredVertexSetElementary} makes $A$ elementary;
every vertex of either PE constituent below is in $A$, by
\noderef{OrderedTreeUnion}, so each constituent is elementary as well.

Because both colors are missing on $T(y_{\ell-1})$, the nonempty-candidate
case of \noderef{HierarchyFirstMissingPosition} applies.  It says that
$v_\alpha$ misses $\alpha$, $v_\beta$ misses $\beta$, and no earlier
displayed vertex misses the corresponding color.  Their stated locations
make them distinct.  Elementarity of the prefix says that $v_\alpha$ does
not miss $\beta$ and $v_\beta$ does not miss $\alpha$.  By
\noderef{MissingColors}, \noderef{PresentColors}, properness, and
\noderef{TwoColorIncidenceDegreeAtMostTwo}, both $K_{v_\alpha}$ and
$K_{v_\beta}$ are edge-bearing path components in the sense of
\noderef{TwoColorNontrivialPathComponent} and
\noderef{TwoColorComponentSpanningPath}.

Suppose for contradiction that $K_{v_\alpha}\ne K_{v_\beta}$.  The
component dichotomy \noderef{TwoColorComponentsDisjoint} makes their full
incidence supports disjoint.  Define
\[
                 \sigma_n=\varphi_n/K_{v_\beta}
\]
by \noderef{TwoColorComponentSwap}.  The theorem
\noderef{TwoColorComponentSwapProper} makes $\sigma_n$ proper.  The switch
fixes \textsf{none} and exchanges only the two displayed nonempty colors,
so the definition \noderef{IsOneEdgeUncolored} shows that exactly $e_0$
remains uncolored.  The disjoint component through $v_\alpha$ is untouched,
so $v_\alpha$ still misses $\alpha$; at $v_\beta$ the switch replaces the
missing color $\beta$ by $\alpha$.

The branch disjunction also gives $\alpha\notin D_n$.  If its first
alternative holds, this is immediate from
\noderef{TashkinovStoredColors}, because $D_n$ subtracts every color missing
on $T_n$ under $\varphi_n$.  If its second alternative holds, it follows
from the asserted disjointness of $\{\alpha,\beta\}$ and $D_n$.

We next eliminate the two nonparallel terminal constructors explicitly.
The `generatingColoring' field of \noderef{ExtendedTashkinovTree} identifies
$\varphi_n$ with the coloring at the last positive slot.  Eliminate the
certificate at that slot from \noderef{TashkinovSeries}.  Positivity rules
out the initial constructor, so the actual certificate is SE, PE, or RE as
listed in \noderef{TashkinovSeriesStep}.

In the SE case, write $\varphi_{n-1}$ for the series coloring at the
predecessor index and $\pi$ for the selected coloring in its
\noderef{MaximumDefectiveChoice}.  The
constructor's `previousClosed' field says that $T_n$ is closed under
$\varphi_{n-1}$, and its `currentColoring' equation, together with the ETT
generating equation, identifies $\pi=\varphi_n$.  The fields
`selectedProper', `selectedOneUncolored', and `selectedStable' say that
$\pi$ is proper, leaves exactly $e_0$ uncolored, and is
$(T_n,D_{n-1},\varphi_{n-1})$-stable.  The predecessor properness and
one-uncoloredness are separate fields of the SE constructor.  Consequently
symmetry in \noderef{sc2} gives the reverse relation saying that
$\varphi_{n-1}$ is $(T_n,D_{n-1},\pi)$-stable.

Let $c$ be missing at a vertex of $T_n$ under $\pi$, and suppose that an
exact boundary identity $f$ of $T_n$ has $\pi(f)=c$.  The vertex clause of
the reverse \noderef{StableColoring} makes $c$ missing at that same vertex
under $\varphi_{n-1}$.  Since $f$ is incident with $T_n$ and its
$\pi$-color belongs to the $\pi$-tree-missing set, the incident-edge clause
of the reverse relation gives $\varphi_{n-1}(f)=\pi(f)=c$.  This contradicts
`previousClosed', after expanding \noderef{ColoredVertexSetClosed} and
\noderef{BoundaryColorEdges}.  Thus every color missing on $T_n$ under
$\pi=\varphi_n$ has empty singleton boundary.  The same SE constructor's
`closedExtension' is also retained: by \noderef{TashkinovClosure} it is a
fixed-$\varphi_n$ \noderef{TashkinovAugmentationSequence} from the exact
one-edge SE seed to the next ladder tree, whose final vertex set is closed.
Together with `orderedExtension', this confirms that the $T_n$ just used is
the exact terminal predecessor in this certified series, rather than an
unrelated tree.

In the RE case, `previousClosed' again closes $T_n$ under
$\varphi_{n-1}$, while `currentColoring' is the literal equality
$\varphi_n=\varphi_{n-1}$.  Hence every $\varphi_n$-missing color is
singleton-closed on $T_n$ directly.  Its `closedExtension' field expands by
\noderef{TashkinovClosure} to the fixed-$\varphi_n$ augmentation chain from
the exact RE one-edge seed to the next ladder tree, and its
`orderedExtension' again identifies the same $T_n$ as the terminal
predecessor.

For either non-PE kind, the `nonparallel' field of
\noderef{HierarchyBase} gives $A=T_n$.  Since $v_\alpha\in V(A)$,
$\alpha$ is missing on this $T_n$, and the preceding SE or RE argument
makes $\{\alpha\}$ closed on $A$.  This contradicts the branch hypothesis.
The actual last constructor is therefore PE.  Its parallel base clause and
\noderef{OrderedTreeUnion} give the exact ordered identity
\[
                         A=T_n\vee R_n,                         \tag{1}
\]
where $R_n$ is the recorded closure of the supporting prefix of $T_n$.

We now construct, rather than assume, the predecessor elementarity needed
for the PE argument.  If $n>1$, apply
\noderef{CertifiedETTRestriction} with $r=n-1$.  It returns an actual
$\mathcal E^{-}$ on the original input, with rung exactly $n-1$,
displayed terminal tree exactly the old slot $T_n$, generating coloring
exactly $\varphi_{n-1}$, and inherited maximum property.  Its pointwise
equalities preserve every retained tree, coloring, raw stored set, exact
connecting-edge set, kind, and witness slot; its additional equalities
preserve each computed $D_i$ and transport each dependent step
certificate.  Lower assertion (i) therefore applies to this certified
maximum ETT and makes $V(T_n)$ elementary under $\varphi_{n-1}$.

If $n=1$, no zero-rung ETT is introduced.  Slot zero of the given series is
the initial tuple, $T_n=T_1$, and the first conclusion of
\noderef{TechnicalTheoremBase}, instantiated directly on that certified
series, makes $V(T_1)$ elementary under $\varphi_0=\varphi_{n-1}$.  Hence in
both cases the explicit predecessor-elementarity premise of
\noderef{ParallelCommonMissingClosed} holds.  That lemma says that every
color missing on both $T_n$ and $R_n$ under $\varphi_n$ is singleton-closed
on their exact union (1).

If $v_\alpha$ belonged to $V(T_n)\cap V(R_n)$, then its missing color
$\alpha$ would be missing on both constituents and hence closed on $A$,
contrary to the branch hypothesis.  Since (1) and $v_\alpha\in V(A)$ place
it in their union, exactly the following alternatives remain:
\[
 v_\alpha\in V(T_n)\setminus V(R_n)
 \quad\hbox{or}\quad
 v_\alpha\in V(R_n)\setminus V(T_n).                           \tag{2}
\]

Before switching, apply \noderef{interchange} at the identity coloring
$\varphi_n$.  The identity relation satisfies both clauses of
\noderef{StableColoring} on $T_n$ with control $D_n$ and on $R_n$ with
empty control, hence satisfies \noderef{CombinedStableColoring}.  The other
inputs are the stated maximum property, positivity, lower package,
properness, one-uncoloredness, terminal-PE equation, and the base in (1).
Thus conclusion (ii) says that any color missing on $T_n$ is
$T_n$-interchangeable with the other color, and conclusion (i) gives the
analogous assertion on $R_n$.

Suppose first that $v_\alpha\in V(T_n)\setminus V(R_n)$.  Then $\alpha$ is
missing on $T_n$, so identity-coloring conclusion (ii),
\noderef{TreeInterchangeable}, and the path certificates above imply that
the distinct component $K_{v_\beta}$ contains no vertex of $T_n$.  It also
contains no $\alpha$- or $\beta$-edge incident with $T_n$: such an edge-node
is adjacent in the incidence graph to its endpoint in $T_n$, which would
put that vertex-node in the same component.  Therefore every edge incident
with $T_n$ is either outside the switched component or has a color outside
$\{\alpha,\beta\}$, and is fixed whenever the incident-edge clause of
\noderef{StableColoring} asks it to be fixed.  Every incident edge at every
$T_n$ vertex is unchanged, so every vertex missing-color set is unchanged.
This proves that $\sigma_n$ is $(T_n,D_n,\varphi_n)$-stable, checking the
two clauses separately.

On $R_n$, neither affected color is missing.  Indeed, $\alpha$ is missing
at $v_\alpha\in A$ and $v_\alpha\notin V(R_n)$, so elementarity of $A$
excludes an $\alpha$-missing vertex of $R_n$; and $\beta$ is first missing
at $v_\beta\notin A$, so it is missing nowhere on the prefix $R_n\subseteq
A$.  At any $R_n$ vertex in the switched component both colors are present,
and the complete component switch merely exchanges its unique incident
$\alpha$- and $\beta$-edges; outside that component nothing changes.  Thus
the vertex missing-color set is unchanged.  If an incident edge is
protected by the empty-control stable relation, its old color is missing on
$R_n$, hence is neither $\alpha$ nor $\beta$, and
\noderef{TwoColorComponentSwap} fixes it.  These are respectively the
vertex and incident-edge clauses proving
$(R_n,\varnothing,\varphi_n)$-stability.

Suppose instead that $v_\alpha\in V(R_n)\setminus V(T_n)$.  Identity-coloring
conclusion (i) similarly makes $K_{v_\beta}$ disjoint from $R_n$, and the
same incidence argument proves both clauses of
$(R_n,\varnothing,\varphi_n)$-stability.  Here $\alpha$ is not missing on
$T_n$: otherwise elementarity of $A$ would give a second vertex of $A$
missing $\alpha$.  Hence the branch disjunction cannot hold through
$\alpha\in\overline\varphi_n(T_n)$ and forces
\[
                     \{\alpha,\beta\}\cap D_n=\varnothing.    \tag{3}
\]
The first-missing location $v_\beta\notin A$ also says that $\beta$ is not
missing on $T_n$.  Consequently both colors are present at every $T_n$
vertex.  A vertex on the switched component keeps one incident edge of each
color after their exchange, while a vertex off it is unchanged, so every
vertex missing-color set is fixed.  Finally, let $f$ be any incident edge
protected by $(T_n,D_n,\varphi_n)$-stability.  Its old color belongs to
$\overline\varphi_n(T_n)\cup D_n$; neither $\alpha$ nor $\beta$ belongs to
that union by the preceding facts and (3).  Thus the color of $f$ is outside
the switched pair and the exact identity $f$ is fixed.  This verifies every
protected incident edge and proves the first stable relation.

Both alternatives in (2) have therefore established
\[
 \noderef{CombinedStableColoring}(T_n,R_n,D_n,\varphi_n,\sigma_n).
                                                                  \tag{4}
\]

Let $B=T(v_\beta)$ be the exact ordered prefix from
\noderef{TreeSequencePrefix} whose terminal vertex is the displayed entry
$v_\beta$.  Because $v_\beta\notin A$ but lies in
$T(y_{\ell-1})$, if $s=|B|-|A|$ then
\[
                         1\leq s\leq\ell-1<\ell.               \tag{5}
\]
In particular $A\prec B\preccurlyeq T(y_{\ell-1})\preccurlyeq T$, with
all comparisons exact.  Before changing any ETT data,
apply \noderef{GoodHierarchyTerminalPrefixRepackage} to this exact prefix.
It gives an old-color ETT $\mathcal E_B$ on $B$ with the same complete
certified series and \noderef{MaximumProperty}, a copied base, a
zero-divider hierarchy $H_B$ whose terminal stage is $B$, and a genuine
old-color \noderef{GoodHierarchy} $\mathcal G_B$.  It also gives the exact
old computed stored-set, stage-missing-set, and Gamma equalities.  Its
segment conclusion is only the fallback-sensitive containment needed for
the old truncation; no equality of full segment-color functions is used.

We next build the required fixed-$\sigma_n$ route to $B$ step by step.  In
the actual PE constructor isolated above, `closedExtension' is a
\noderef{TashkinovClosure} from the exact seed $T_n$ to the certified next
ladder tree $T_{n+1}$.  Unfolding it first, before changing colorings, gives
a fixed-$\varphi_n$ \noderef{TashkinovAugmentationSequence}
\[
                              T_n\longrightarrow T_{n+1}.       \tag{6}
\]
The left-segment field of the \noderef{OrderedTreeUnion} in (1), followed
by $A\prec B$, gives $T_n\preccurlyeq B$.  We also have
$B\preccurlyeq T$ and the `belowFinal' field of
\noderef{ExtendedTashkinovTree} gives $T\preccurlyeq T_{n+1}$.  All three
relations are exact \noderef{TreeSequenceInitialSegment}s and retain the
individual edge entries.  Applying
\noderef{TashkinovAugmentationSequenceRestrict} to (6), still only at the
old coloring $\varphi_n$, produces
\[
             T_n=X_0\longrightarrow X_1\longrightarrow\cdots
                 \longrightarrow X_m=B                         \tag{7}
\]
as a $\varphi_n$-\noderef{TashkinovAugmentationSequence}.

We induct over the reflexive-transitive chain (7).  The seed identities in
$T_n$ are fixed by \noderef{CombinedStableClosurePreserved}, applied to
(4); that result also fixes every displayed identity of $R_n$, transports
the same $R_n$ closure to $\sigma_n$, and therefore fixes every displayed
identity of their ordered union $A$ by \noderef{OrderedTreeUnion}.

For one old step $X_i\longrightarrow X_{i+1}$, expand
\noderef{TashkinovAugmentation}.  It supplies its exact new last edge $f$,
a color $c$ with $\varphi_n(f)=c$, and a vertex $w\in V(X_i)$ at which $c$
is missing under $\varphi_n$.  If the new position is below $|A|$, then
$f$ is an exact displayed identity of $A$ and $w\in V(A)$.  The preceding
paragraph gives $\sigma_n(f)=\varphi_n(f)=c$, while the two vertex clauses
in (4), together with the vertex-set union (1), give equality of the
missing-color set at $w$.  Thus $c$ is still missing there.

If the new position is at least $|A|$, it is below the terminal position
$i_\beta=|B|$.  Since $\beta$ is not missing on $A$, the conditional
ordered-unused hypothesis of the statement applies and excludes $\alpha$
from every exact edge of this interval.  Hence $c\ne\alpha$.  If
$c=\beta$, the augmentation witness $w$ occurs in the preceding prefix and
therefore strictly before $v_\beta$; this contradicts the least-candidate
property of \noderef{HierarchyFirstMissingPosition}.  Thus
$c\notin\{\alpha,\beta\}$.  The definition
\noderef{TwoColorComponentSwap} fixes $f$ and every incident $c$-edge at
$w$, so $\sigma_n(f)=c$ and $c$ remains missing at $w$.

In both positional cases the length, attachment, and ordered-prefix fields
are coloring-independent.  Hence the same exact step is a
$\sigma_n$-\noderef{TashkinovAugmentation}.  Induction proves a
$\sigma_n$-augmentation sequence from $T_n$ to $B$.  Simultaneously, the
seed check and the new-edge check at every induction step prove
\[
 \sigma_n(f)=\varphi_n(f)
 \quad\text{for every displayed edge identity }f\text{ of }B. \tag{8}
\]
This transport used the augmentation's actual old-prefix missing witness at
every step; it did not infer a new chain merely from equality of final
vertex sets.

Apply \noderef{HierarchySplitter} to $\mathcal E_B$, its maximum property,
the copied old-color base and $H_B$, the terminal-PE equation, (4), and the
verified $\sigma_n$-augmentation sequence.  Its hypotheses also include
the stated lower-rung package and the proper and one-uncolored facts already
proved for $\sigma_n$.  It returns an actual
\noderef{CorrespondingExtendedTashkinovTree} $\mathcal E'_B$ on the same
exact terminal tree $B$, a transported maximum-property witness, a base
under $\sigma_n$, and a hierarchy $K$ with the same core, PE closure,
zero divider count, ordered stages, and divider data as $H_B$.  At this
point no goodness under $\sigma_n$ has been assumed.

We now construct that zero-divider goodness field by field.  Copy the Gamma
function of $\mathcal G_B$ to $K$.  First compute its actual index set.
The `storedColorsAgree' fields of
\noderef{CorrespondingExtendedTashkinovTree} identify every raw certified
$S_i$ set.  The first vertex clause of (4) identifies the missing-color
sets on the common ladder tree $T_n$.  Unfolding
\noderef{TashkinovStoredColors}, namely raw stored union minus the current
$T_n$-missing set, therefore gives equality of the old and replacement
terminal sets $D_n$.

Every vertex of the common core $A=T_n\vee R_n$ lies in $T_n$ or $R_n$ by
\noderef{OrderedTreeUnion}; the two vertex clauses in (4) identify its
missing set under $\varphi_n$ and $\sigma_n$, with tree missingness taken as
the vertexwise union in \noderef{TreeMissingColors}.  Now unfold
\noderef{HierarchyStoredColorsAt} at the sole index zero.  It subtracts the
missing-color set of this starred core $A$ from the just-identified terminal
$D_n$, so
\[
       \noderef{HierarchyStoredColorsAt}(K,0)
       =\noderef{HierarchyStoredColorsAt}(H_B,0).               \tag{9}
\]
Likewise \noderef{HierarchyStageMissingColors} at index zero is exactly the
missing-color set of $T_n$, so the old and new stage-missing sets agree.
In particular, since $v_\alpha\in A$ misses $\alpha$, the definition in
(9) excludes $\alpha$ from both computed stored index sets.

For the avoidance field we must compare totalized first-missing positions,
not merely displayed edge colors.  We do so for all three kinds of color.
First, no vertex before $v_\alpha$ misses $\alpha$ under $\varphi_n$.  If
such an earlier vertex became $\alpha$-missing under $\sigma_n$, then it
would lie in the switched component and would have been $\beta$-missing
under $\varphi_n$, contradicting that $v_\beta$ is the first such vertex;
outside the switched component nothing changed.  The vertex $v_\alpha$
lies in the disjoint component $K_{v_\alpha}$ and still misses $\alpha$.
Thus its displayed position remains the first $\alpha$-missing position in
$B$.  This case is recorded even though $\alpha$ is not an index in (9).

Second, no vertex of $B$ before $v_\beta$ misses $\beta$ under
$\sigma_n$.  Indeed, outside $K_{v_\beta}$ this follows from the old
least-candidate property.  If a vertex $x$ in $K_{v_\beta}$ became
$\beta$-missing, then $x$ was $\alpha$-missing under $\varphi_n$.  It is
not $v_\alpha$, because the two old components are disjoint, and both
vertices lie in the elementary prefix $T(y_{\ell-1})$; this would contradict
elementarity with the $\alpha$-missing vertex $v_\alpha$.  At the terminal
vertex $v_\beta$ of $B$, old elementarity says that $\alpha$ was present,
and the component switch replaces missing $\beta$ by missing $\alpha$.
Thus no vertex of $B$ is $\beta$-missing under $\sigma_n$.  The no-candidate
branch of \noderef{HierarchyFirstMissingPosition} assigns $\beta$ the last
position of $B$, which is exactly the old displayed position of
$v_\beta$.

Finally, for every $\eta\notin\{\alpha,\beta\}$,
\noderef{TwoColorComponentSwap} fixes every $\eta$-colored edge globally.
Hence the $\eta$-missing candidates in $B$ are pointwise identical under
the two colorings.  Their least positions agree when the set is nonempty,
and their last-position fallbacks agree when it is empty.

Now fix an index color
$\eta\in\noderef{HierarchyStoredColorsAt}(K,0)$ and an exact edge position
witnessing membership in the new
\noderef{HierarchySegmentColors}(K,0,\eta)$.  The case $\eta=\alpha$ is
impossible by (9).  In the case $\eta=\beta$, the preceding fallback
calculation identifies the new bound with the old terminal position of
$v_\beta$; for every other $\eta$, the pointwise candidate calculation
identifies the two bounds.  Same hierarchy stages identify the core and
terminal bounds and the exact edge at this position, while (8) identifies
its color.  Therefore this very position witnesses membership of the same
color in the old segment set.  This proves only the pointwise containment
needed at relevant stored indices; it does not assert equality of the full
\noderef{HierarchySegmentColors} functions.  The old avoidance field of
$\mathcal G_B$, the stage-missing equality, and the copied Gamma pair now
prove the new avoidance field.

The `twoColors' and `disjointPairs' fields of
\noderef{GoodHierarchy} copy literally from $\mathcal G_B$ using (9).
There is no positive divider, so `uniqueRefresh' and `minusClosed' are
vacuous.  For the cardinality half of `parallelBase', (4) identifies the
missing-color sets on both $T_n$ and $R_n$; equation (9) and the copied
Gamma pairs identify the excluded reserved union.  Hence the old cardinality
inequality is exactly the new one.

It remains to prove the non-minus-closed half.  Expand the old witness from
$\mathcal G_B$ using \noderef{TreeColorSetMinusClosed},
\noderef{TreeColorSetClosed}, and \noderef{BoundaryColorEdges}.  It gives a
color
\[
 c\in\overline\varphi_n(A)\setminus\Gamma^0
\]
and an individual boundary identity $f$ of $A$ with
$\varphi_n(f)=c$.  If $f$ is fixed by the switch, the same vertexwise
missing equality on $A$, the same reserved union, and the same exact
boundary edge make it a witness under $\sigma_n$.

If $f$ is changed, then \noderef{TwoColorComponentSwap} gives
$c\in\{\alpha,\beta\}$ and puts the edge-node of $f$ in $K_{v_\beta}$.
The color $\beta$ is missing nowhere on $A$, because its first missing
vertex is outside $A$, so $c=\alpha$; the old witness also says
$\alpha\notin\Gamma^0$.  Suppose $A$ nevertheless became
$(\Gamma^0)^-$-closed under $\sigma_n$.  The vertex $v_\alpha$ still makes
$\alpha$ missing on $A$, so the definition
\noderef{TreeColorSetMinusClosed} makes the singleton $\{\alpha\}$ closed
on $A$ under $\sigma_n$.  Apply conclusion (iii) of
\noderef{interchange} with the already verified combined stability (4): it
makes $\alpha,\beta$ interchangeable on $A$ under $\sigma_n$.

Switching a complete two-color component does not change the underlying
$\{\alpha,\beta\}$ incidence graph, only the labels of its edge colors.
Thus the old distinct components $K_{v_\alpha}$ and $K_{v_\beta}$ are still
two distinct path components under $\sigma_n$.  The first meets $A$ at
$v_\alpha$.  The changed boundary edge $f$ is incident with an inside
vertex of $A$, so its edge-node and that inside vertex-node put the second
component in contact with $A$.  This contradicts
\noderef{TreeInterchangeable}.  Therefore $A$ remains non-minus-closed.

We have now checked `twoColors', `avoidance', `disjointPairs', vacuous
`uniqueRefresh', both parts of `parallelBase', and vacuous `minusClosed'.
These fields construct a complete good witness $\mathcal G'_B$ under
$\sigma_n$.  Only after this construction do we invoke
\noderef{SameGoodHierarchy}: the same-stage certificate from the splitter,
(9), and the copied Gamma values give precisely its required data.

Both $v_\alpha$ and $v_\beta$ belong to $B$, are distinct, and miss
$\alpha$ under $\sigma_n$.  Hence $B$ is not elementary.  The replacement
ETT, transported maximum property, base, hierarchy, and the newly constructed
good witness form a \noderef{HierarchyCounterexample} $C'$ with the same
zero divider count.  The explicit rounds equality returned by
\noderef{GoodHierarchyTerminalPrefixRepackage} identifies the rung of
$\mathcal E_B$ with that of the original ETT.  The `rungAgreement' field
of the \noderef{CorrespondingExtendedTashkinovTree} certificate identifies
the rung of $\mathcal E'_B$ with that of $\mathcal E_B$.  Hence the ETT
underlying $C'$ has the same rung as the ETT underlying $C$.

Finally compare the exact measures.  Put
$r_0=\noderef{HierarchyPathNumber}(H)$ and let $r'_0$ be the path number of
$K$.  If $s<r_0$, the tail-length fallback in the path-number definition
gives $r'_0\leq s<r_0$.  If $r_0\leq s$, the old path tail beginning at
$r_0$ restricts to the exact prefix $B$, with its singleton terminal case
still counted as a path, so $r'_0\leq r_0$.  In the
equality case, the second coordinate of
\noderef{HierarchyCounterexampleMeasure} uses the ordinary zero stage
$T_{n,0}=T_n$ on both hierarchies and drops from
$|T|-|T_n|$ to $|B|-|T_n|$ by (5).  Therefore
\noderef{HierarchyCounterexampleLess} holds from $C'$ to $C$.  The
same-rung equality just proved and the zero-divider equality make $C'$
eligible for the stated minimality, a contradiction.  The supposition
that the two components were
distinct was false, proving the required equality.
\end{proof}

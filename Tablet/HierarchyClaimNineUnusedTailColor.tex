\begin{lemma}[Claim 9: a color unused on the tail]
Fix the Section 7 data, a
\noderef{HierarchyReplacementPresentation} \(P\), and a color \(\alpha\)
missing on its last starred stage and at the final vertex \(y_\ell\).
Assume the paper's normalization: when the divider count is positive,
\(\alpha\) is missing on \(T_{n,q}\), and when it is zero, \(\alpha\) is
missing on \(T_n\).  Assume no index \(1\leq i\leq\ell-1\) is already
common-missing with \(y_\ell\), and assume that no exact displayed tail
edge at or above the starred stage has color \(\alpha\).  Also assume every
proper exactly-one-uncolored replacement coloring has a missing color at
every vertex.

Then \noderef{HierarchyEligibleCommonMissingIndices} is nonempty.
More precisely, the construction produces a replacement presentation in
which \(y_{\ell-1}\) and \(y_\ell\) have a common missing color.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Let \(\mu_n\), \(H\), and \(A\) denote the coloring, hierarchy, and last
starred stage of \(P\).  Its transported minimum context and
\noderef{HierarchyCounterexampleTailAtLeastTwo} give \(2\leq\ell\).
Put \(B=\noderef{HierarchyFullTailPrefix}(C,\ell-1)\) and let
\(y_{\ell-1}\) be its exact last vertex.  The explicit palette-surplus
hypothesis, applied to the proper exactly-one-uncolored coloring \(\mu_n\)
stored in \(P\), supplies a color
\(\beta\in\overline\mu_n(y_{\ell-1})\).
The no-common hypothesis implies \(\beta\notin\overline\mu_n(y_\ell)\),
whereas \(\alpha\in\overline\mu_n(y_\ell)\); hence \(\alpha\ne\beta\).

The penultimate-prefix theorem
\noderef{HierarchyCounterexamplePenultimateElementary}, applied to the
transported context, says that \(V(B)\) is elementary.  Since \(\alpha\)
is already missing on \(A\preccurlyeq B\), it is not missing at
\(y_{\ell-1}\).  Thus the first \(\alpha\)-missing component meeting \(A\)
and the first \(\beta\)-missing component through \(y_{\ell-1}\) are the
same by \noderef{ChangeLemma}: its positive-divider alternative uses the
given \(\alpha\in\overline\mu_n(T_{n,q})\), and its zero-divider alternative
uses \(\alpha\in\overline\mu_n(T_n)\).  The exact unused-edge premise of
that lemma follows from the present hypothesis.  Consequently the
\((\alpha,\beta)\)-component through \(y_\ell\) is disjoint from this
component, exactly as in paper Case 1.

Let \(\nu_n\) be the component swap through \(y_\ell\).  We check the
full-tail lift's three pointwise premises.  No \(\alpha\)-colored displayed
tail identity exists by hypothesis.  If a \(\beta\)-colored edge occurred
before \(y_{\ell-1}\), \noderef{HierarchyTailMissingColorExclusion} would
make it the unique zero-divider SE/RE first seed, colored by a member of
\(D_n\).  The component equality just obtained puts that seed in the
component meeting \(A\), not in the disjoint component through \(y_\ell\).
Every later \(\beta\)-edge is likewise in the component ending at
\(y_{\ell-1}\).  Therefore the selected component contains no displayed
tail edge, including the exceptional individual seed identity, and the
swap fixes every tail-edge value.

The same component separation shows that the selected component is outside
\(B\).  Hence vertexwise missing-color sets agree on \(B\).  For the
first-position condition, unaffected colors are immediate; \(\alpha\) has
an unchanged earlier witness in \(A\), and \(\beta\) has the unchanged
witness \(y_{\ell-1}\).  Thus
\noderef{HierarchyFirstMissingPosition} is unchanged for every color in
the last \noderef{HierarchyStoredColorsAt}.  Notice that this argument does
not require or claim equality at the terminal vertex.

The component through \(y_\ell\) is a
\noderef{TwoColorPathComponent}, because \(y_\ell\) is missing \(\alpha\)
and properness leaves it at most one incident \(\beta\)-edge in the
two-color incidence graph.  We may now apply
\noderef{StableChangeCasesOneTwoFullTail}.  The normalization hypothesis
is exactly its positive- or zero-divider Cases 1/2 alternative.  The result
supplies the actual corresponding full-tree ETT, maximum property, rebuilt
base, good hierarchy, and \noderef{SameGoodHierarchy} data.

To construct the required minimum context, use rung agreement to rewrite
every strict lower-rung bound and every same-rung premise.  The
\noderef{SameGoodHierarchy} field gives equal divider counts and equal
stages, hence equal \noderef{HierarchyCounterexampleMeasure}; the old
minimality field therefore excludes every smaller replacement measure.
Corresponding ladder-tree equality and terminal stability identify the
two terminal-ladder missing-color sets and transfer the \(2n+11\)
inequality.  The corresponding certificate itself supplies properness and
exact one-uncoloredness.  The fewer-divider and full lower-rung functions
are then the old functions after rewriting the common rung and divider
numbers.  These are all fields of
\noderef{HierarchyMinimumCounterexampleContext}.  Since the
swap makes \(\beta\) missing at \(y_\ell\) while preserving its missingness
at \(y_{\ell-1}\), the full tree is non-elementary.  These facts construct
the replacement counterexample, its transported minimum context, and hence
a replacement presentation \(P'\).

Taking \(i=\ell-1\), \(P'\), and \(\beta\) satisfies both membership tests
in the classical filter defining
\noderef{HierarchyEligibleCommonMissingIndices}.  The bounds
\(1\leq\ell-1\leq\ell-1\) follow from \(2\leq\ell\), so the finite set is
nonempty.
\end{proof}

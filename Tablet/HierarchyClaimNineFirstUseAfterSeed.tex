\begin{lemma}[Claim 9: first tail use after the seed]
Fix the Section 7 minimum context and a
\noderef{HierarchyReplacementPresentation} \(P\).  Let \(\alpha\) be
missing on \(P\)'s last starred stage \(A\) and at \(y_\ell\).  Assume
the normalized alternative that \(\alpha\) is missing on \(T_{n,q}\)
when \(q>0\), and on \(T_n\) when \(q=0\).  Suppose no
\(y_i\), \(1\leq i\leq\ell-1\), has a color common-missing with
\(y_\ell\) under \(P\)'s coloring.  Assume every proper
exactly-one-uncolored replacement coloring has a missing color at every
vertex.

Suppose the first exact displayed tail edge colored \(\alpha\) is
\(e_j\), where \(2\leq j\leq\ell\).  The edge is supplied as an element
of the original edge type at exact position \(|A|+j-1\), and every
earlier exact tail position is explicitly required not to carry
\(\alpha\).  Then
\noderef{HierarchyEligibleCommonMissingIndices} is nonempty.
The construction yields a replacement presentation for which
\(y_{j-1}\) and \(y_\ell\) have a common missing color.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Write \(\mu_n,H,A\) for the data of the supplied
\noderef{HierarchyReplacementPresentation}.  Since \(j\geq2\), the exact
prefix \(T(y_{j-1})=\noderef{HierarchyFullTailPrefix}(C,j-1)\) contains
the vertex \(y_{j-1}\) strictly above \(A\).  Choose
\(\beta\in\overline\mu_n(y_{j-1})\) using the stated palette-surplus
hypothesis and properness from the transported
\noderef{HierarchyMinimumCounterexampleContext}.  The no-common premise
gives \(\beta\notin\overline\mu_n(y_\ell)\), while \(\alpha\) is missing
there, so \(\alpha\ne\beta\).

Apply \noderef{HierarchyCounterexamplePenultimateElementary} to the
transported context.  Both \(A\) and \(y_{j-1}\) lie in its exact
penultimate prefix.  Its elementarity, together with the normalized
missing witness for \(\alpha\), shows that \(\alpha\) is not missing at
\(y_{j-1}\).  The first-use equation and the exact earlier-edge exclusions
are precisely the hypotheses used in paper Subcase 2.1.

The positive-divider or zero-divider alternative of
\noderef{ChangeLemma} applies to \(\alpha,\beta\).  In the latter case,
\noderef{HierarchyTailMissingColorExclusion} records the sole possible
exception: \(e_1=f_n\) in an actual SE/RE step, with its exact edge identity
and color in \(D_n\).  Since \(j\geq2\), this exceptional identity precedes
\(e_j\); the first-use hypothesis says it is not \(\alpha\)-colored, and if
it is \(\beta\)-colored it belongs to the component ending at
\(y_{j-1}\).  Thus the change lemma identifies the component through the
first \(\alpha\)-missing vertex with that through \(y_{j-1}\), and the
component through \(y_\ell\) is disjoint from it.

Let \(\nu_n\) be the \((\alpha,\beta)\)-swap on the latter component.  We
verify the hypotheses of \noderef{StableChangeCasesOneTwoFullTail}.
Before \(e_j\), the first-use premise excludes \(\alpha\), and the tail
exclusion lemma controls every possible \(\beta\)-edge, including the exact
zero-divider seed.  At and after \(e_j\), an affected displayed edge would
join the component whose endpoint is \(y_{j-1}\); it therefore cannot lie
in the disjoint component through \(y_\ell\).  Hence every exact displayed
tail edge keeps its value.  The same argument at vertices shows that the
selected component is outside the penultimate prefix, so all missing sets
there agree.  Finally, every unaffected color has the same first missing
candidate; \(\alpha\) has an unchanged witness in \(A\), and \(\beta\) has
the unchanged earlier witness \(y_{j-1}\).  Thus the required
\noderef{HierarchyFirstMissingPosition} equality holds for every member of
the last \noderef{HierarchyStoredColorsAt}.

Because \(y_\ell\) is missing \(\alpha\), properness makes its full
incidence component a possibly singleton
\noderef{TwoColorPathComponent}.  All conditions of the full-tail lift are
therefore proved, including its exact normalization alternative.  Applying
\noderef{StableChangeCasesOneTwoFullTail} constructs the corresponding
full-tree ETT, transports maximum property, and rebuilds the full good
hierarchy with \noderef{SameGoodHierarchy} data.

The swap leaves \(\beta\) missing at \(y_{j-1}\) and makes it missing at
\(y_\ell\).  Hence the replacement full tree is non-elementary.  Rung
agreement rewrites the lower-rung and fewer-divider functions.
\noderef{SameGoodHierarchy} gives equal divider counts and stages and hence
equal \noderef{HierarchyCounterexampleMeasure}, so the old minimality field
is the required replacement minimality field.  Corresponding ladder-tree
agreement and terminal stability identify the terminal-ladder missing sets
and transfer the \(2n+11\) estimate; the corresponding certificate supplies
properness and exact one-uncoloredness.  These data construct every field
of the replacement \noderef{HierarchyMinimumCounterexampleContext}, yielding
a replacement presentation \(P'\).  Since
\(1\leq j-1\leq\ell-1\), the witnesses \(j-1,P',\beta\) satisfy the filter
defining \noderef{HierarchyEligibleCommonMissingIndices}; the latter is
nonempty.
\end{proof}

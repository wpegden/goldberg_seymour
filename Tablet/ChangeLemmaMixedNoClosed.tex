\begin{lemma}[Change lemma, mixed location: no closed starred stage]
Fix a finite loopless multigraph with its exact edge set, a palette of
$k$ colors, and a distinguished edge $e_0$.  Let $(T,\varphi_n)$ be a
positive-rung extended Tashkinov tree.  Let $\mathcal B$ be a hierarchy
base and let
\[
 T_n=T_{n,0}\subset T_{n,1}\subset\cdots\subset
 T_{n,q}\subset T_{n,q+1}=T
\]
be a good hierarchy under $\varphi_n$, where $q$ is its last divider
index and
\[
 A=T_{n,q}^{*}=\noderef{HierarchyStarredStage}(H,q).
\]
Assume that $T$ has maximum property, that $V(T)$ is not elementary,
that $\varphi_n$ is proper and leaves exactly $e_0$ uncolored, and that
the rung number is positive.  These data form a
\noderef{HierarchyCounterexample} $C$.

Assume the six lower-rung assertions of the technical theorem, uniformly
for every positive-rung maximum ETT on the same graph, palette, and
distinguished edge, with arbitrary certified input and generating
colorings and with rung number smaller than that of $T$: lower-rung
elementarity, terminal interchangeability, the two PE stability and
stored-pair assertions, the terminal PE component assertion, the
defective-endpoint order assertion, and the mod-coloring assertion.
Assume also the same-rung induction hypothesis that every maximum ETT
with a good hierarchy having fewer than $q$ dividers is elementary.
Finally assume the large-color estimate
\[
 2n+11\leq |\overline\varphi_n(T_n)|.
\]

Put $\ell=|T|-|A|$, the number of exact tail additions above $A$, and let
\[
 T(y_{\ell-1})=T\big|_{\min\{|A|+(\ell-1),|T|\}}
\]
be the exact ordered prefix supplied by \noderef{TreeSequencePrefix}.
Assume (6.6): $V(T(y_{\ell-1}))$ is elementary under $\varphi_n$.
Assume moreover that $C$ is lexicographically minimal among all
\noderef{HierarchyCounterexample}s on the fixed graph, palette, and
$e_0$ which have exactly $q$ dividers, allowing their certified input
colorings, generating colorings, bases, ETTs, and good hierarchies to
vary.  Explicitly, no such counterexample $C'$ satisfies
\[
 \noderef{HierarchyCounterexampleLess}
  \bigl(\noderef{HierarchyCounterexampleMeasure}(C'),
        \noderef{HierarchyCounterexampleMeasure}(C)\bigr).
\]

Let $\alpha\ne\beta$ be colors missing on $T(y_{\ell-1})$.  Write
\[
 i_c=\noderef{HierarchyFirstMissingPosition}(\varphi_n,T,c),
 \qquad v_c=T.\mathit{vertex}(i_c).
\]
If at least one of $\alpha,\beta$ is not missing on $A$, assume both
\[
 v_\alpha\prec v_\beta                                      \tag{a}
\]
and the exact edge-identity exclusion
\[
 \alpha\notin
 \left\{c:\exists t\in\operatorname{Fin}|T|,
       |A|\leq t<i_\beta,
       \ \varphi_n(T.\mathit{edge}(t))=c\right\}.             \tag{b}
\]
Thus (b) is precisely
$\alpha\notin\varphi_n\langle T(v_\beta)-A\rangle$; it is not
an endpoint-only abbreviation.


Assume in addition that the first $\alpha$-missing vertex lies in the
last starred stage and the first $\beta$-missing vertex lies outside it:
\[
        v_\alpha\in V(A),\qquad v_\beta\notin V(A).
\]
Assume that $q>0$ and that, for every $s$ with
$0\leq s\leq q$, the singleton $\{\alpha\}$ is not closed on
$T_{n,s}^{*}$.  Then
\[
 \noderef{TwoColorComponent}(G,\varphi_n,\alpha,\beta,
       \operatorname{inl}v_\alpha)
 =
 \noderef{TwoColorComponent}(G,\varphi_n,\alpha,\beta,
       \operatorname{inl}v_\beta).
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Write $\mathcal E$ for the given ETT, put
$H=C.\mathit{hierarchy}$ and $q=H.\mathit{dividerCount}$, and retain the
notation $A,\ell,T(y_{\ell-1}),i_\alpha,i_\beta,v_\alpha,v_\beta$ from
the statement.  For a vertex $x$ put
\[
 K_x=\noderef{TwoColorComponent}
       (G,\varphi_n,\alpha,\beta,\operatorname{inl}x).
\]
All component supports below are supports in the incidence graph, so their
edge-nodes are the individual multigraph edge identities.

We first record the exact prefix and path facts.  Since $q>0$, the
positive-index clause of \noderef{HierarchyStarredStage} identifies
$A=T_{n,q}$.  The nestedness fields of \noderef{TashkinovHierarchy} make
every ordinary and starred hierarchy stage an exact ordered initial segment
of $A$, and make $A$ a proper initial segment of $T$.  Hence
$\ell=|T|-|A|>0$.  The exact-prefix property of
\noderef{TreeSequencePrefix}, together with
\noderef{TreeSequenceInitialSegment}, puts $A$ and every earlier stage
inside $T(y_{\ell-1})$ entry by entry.  Restricting the pairwise
disjointness condition in \noderef{ColoredVertexSetElementary} therefore
makes every such stage elementary under $\varphi_n$.

The assumed membership of both colors in
\noderef{TreeMissingColors} of $T(y_{\ell-1})$ ensures that the
nonempty-candidate clause of \noderef{HierarchyFirstMissingPosition}
applies.  Thus $v_\alpha$ misses $\alpha$, $v_\beta$ misses $\beta$, and
no earlier displayed vertex misses the corresponding color.  Their mixed
locations make them distinct.  Elementarity of $T(y_{\ell-1})$ says that
$v_\alpha$ does not miss $\beta$ and $v_\beta$ does not miss $\alpha$.
Expanding \noderef{MissingColors} and \noderef{PresentColors}, there is
therefore a $\beta$-colored edge incident with $v_\alpha$ and an
$\alpha$-colored edge incident with $v_\beta$, while no $\alpha$-edge is
incident with $v_\alpha$ and no $\beta$-edge is incident with $v_\beta$.
Properness makes the displayed incident edge unique at each endpoint.
Equivalently, each endpoint vertex-node has degree exactly one in its
two-color incidence component; the global degree bound is also the
conclusion of \noderef{TwoColorIncidenceDegreeAtMostTwo}.  Hence each
component satisfies the leaf condition of
\noderef{TwoColorPathComponent}.  Its displayed incident edge-node makes
it edge-bearing, so both components satisfy
\noderef{TwoColorNontrivialPathComponent}.  Finally
\noderef{TwoColorComponentSpanningPath} supplies a spanning simple path
whose node set is the whole component.  These are the two paper path
components to which \noderef{TreeInterchangeable} applies.

We next construct the predecessor data required by \noderef{rutcor}.
Write $n>0$ for the rung number and $\varphi_{n-1}$ for the coloring in
the slot preceding the generating coloring $\varphi_n$.  The generating
field of \noderef{ExtendedTashkinovTree} and the certified array in
\noderef{TashkinovSeries} identify these exact slots.  Eliminate its last
positive certificate; \noderef{TashkinovSeriesStep} says that it is one of
the actual SE, PE, or RE constructors and that its current coloring is
$\varphi_n$.

If $n>1$, restrict every array in the certified series to the slots through
$n-1$, retaining tree slots through $T_n$.  Every retained step has the same
constructor and witnesses, since it refers only to retained entries.  The
last retained noninitial certificate gives the strict exact inclusion from
$T_{n-1}$ into $T_n$.  Use $T_n$ as the displayed terminal tree; the upper
initial-segment relation into that retained output is reflexive.  These data
construct an actual \noderef{ExtendedTashkinovTree} $\mathcal E^{-}$ of
rung $n-1$, terminal tree exactly $T_n$, and generating coloring exactly
$\varphi_{n-1}$.  Every field of \noderef{MaximumProperty} restricts:
the global-order equality for $T_1$ is unchanged, and every indexed stable
coloring, exact seed, closure, and cardinality bound below $n-1$ is literally
the corresponding old field at the same index below $n$.  Thus
$\mathcal E^{-}$ has maximum property.  Lower assertion (i), applied to
this actual positive-rung ETT, gives
\[
             V(T_n)\text{ is elementary under }\varphi_{n-1}. \tag{1}
\]
If $n=1$, there is no predecessor ETT.  Slot zero of this same certified
series is its actual initial tuple, its coloring is
$\varphi_0=\varphi_{n-1}$, and its certified closure is exactly
$T_1=T_n$.  The elementarity conclusion of
\noderef{TechnicalTheoremBase}, instantiated on that tuple, gives (1).
No zero-rung ETT or zero-rung maximum-property claim is introduced.

Because $v_\alpha\in A=T_{n,q}$, the color $\alpha$ is missing on the
last ordinary stage.  Apply the no-closed-stage alternative of
\noderef{rutcor} using (1), full-tail elementarity (6.6), maximum property,
the stated good hierarchy, $q>0$, and the hypothesis that no starred stage
is $\alpha$-closed.  Retain all four parts of its conclusion:
\[
\begin{split}
 &\alpha\in
   \bigcup_{\eta\in D_{n,j}}\Gamma^{j-1}_\eta
   \quad\hbox{and}\quad
   \alpha\in\bigcup_{\eta\in D_{n,j-1}}\Gamma^{j-1}_\eta
   =\Gamma^{j-1} \quad(1\leq j\leq q),                         \tag{2}\\
 &\Theta_n=PE,                                                   \tag{3}\\
 &v_\alpha\in V(T_n)\setminus V(R_n),                           \tag{4}\\
 &\alpha\notin
      \varphi_n\langle T_{n,q}-T_n\rangle.                      \tag{5}
\end{split}
\]
In particular, (2) is the stage-by-stage reserved membership, and (5)
is the exact unused-color interval, not merely a statement about its
endpoints.

The vertex $v_\beta$ lies outside $A$, so the least-candidate property
says that $\beta$ is missing nowhere on $A$.  The premise of the
conditional ordered-unused hypothesis is therefore true.  Retain both its
order conclusion $v_\alpha\prec v_\beta$ and its exact exclusion
\[
       \alpha\notin\varphi_n\langle T(v_\beta)-A\rangle.        \tag{6}
\]

We now obtain the interchangeability used by the switch.  At the identity
coloring $\varphi_n$, reflexivity verifies both clauses of
\noderef{StableColoring} on $T_n$ with control $D_n$ and on $R_n$ with
empty control, hence verifies
\noderef{CombinedStableColoring}.  Together with (3), the given maximum
property, properness, one-uncoloredness, base, and lower package, conclusion
(ii) of \noderef{interchange} says that $\alpha$ and $\beta$ are
$T_n$-interchangeable, because (4) makes $\alpha$ missing on $T_n$.

Suppose for contradiction that $K_{v_\alpha}\ne K_{v_\beta}$.  The first
certified path component meets $T_n$ at $v_\alpha$.  If the second met
$T_n$, \noderef{TreeInterchangeable} would identify them.  Thus
$K_{v_\beta}$ contains no vertex-node of $T_n$.  It also contains no
$\alpha$- or $\beta$-colored edge-node incident with $T_n$: such an
edge-node is adjacent in the incidence graph to its endpoint vertex-node
in $T_n$, placing that vertex-node in the same component.  The component
dichotomy \noderef{TwoColorComponentsDisjoint} also makes it disjoint from
$K_{v_\alpha}$.

Define
\[
                 \sigma_n=\varphi_n/K_{v_\beta}
\]
by \noderef{TwoColorComponentSwap}.  The theorem
\noderef{TwoColorComponentSwapProper} makes $\sigma_n$ proper.  The switch
fixes \textsf{none} and only exchanges the two nonempty colors, so expanding
\noderef{IsOneEdgeUncolored} shows that exactly $e_0$ remains uncolored.
The disjoint component through $v_\alpha$ is unchanged, so $v_\alpha$
still misses $\alpha$; at $v_\beta$ the switch replaces missing $\beta$
by missing $\alpha$.

We verify combined stability point by point.  On $T_n$, the incidence
argument above says that every incident edge with color $\alpha$ or
$\beta$ lies outside the switched component; every incident edge of any
other color is fixed by definition.  Hence every incident edge value and
every vertex missing-color set is unchanged.  These facts give separately
the protected-edge and vertex clauses of
$(T_n,D_n,\varphi_n)$-\noderef{StableColoring}.

On $R_n$, neither affected color is missing.  The vertex-union clause of
\noderef{OrderedTreeUnion} places $R_n$ inside the starred hierarchy core
and hence inside $A$.  For $\alpha$, a missing vertex of $R_n$ together with the distinct vertex
$v_\alpha\in T_n\setminus R_n$ would give two vertices of the elementary
prefix $T(y_{\ell-1})$ missing $\alpha$.  For $\beta$, its first missing vertex is the later
vertex $v_\beta\notin A$.  Thus at an $R_n$ vertex in the switched
component, properness gives one incident edge of each affected color and
the complete switch exchanges them; off the component nothing changes.
Every vertex missing set on $R_n$ is therefore unchanged.  If the
empty-control stable relation protects an incident edge, its old color is
in the $R_n$-missing set and consequently is neither $\alpha$ nor $\beta$;
\noderef{TwoColorComponentSwap} fixes its exact identity.  This checks both
clauses of $(R_n,\varnothing,\varphi_n)$-stability.  Together the two
relations are exactly
\[
 \noderef{CombinedStableColoring}(T_n,R_n,D_n,\varphi_n,\sigma_n).
                                                                  \tag{7}
\]
Applying \noderef{CombinedStableClosurePreserved} to (7) fixes every
displayed edge identity of $T_n$ and $R_n$ and makes the same ordered
$R_n$ the required closure under $\sigma_n$.

Let $B=T(v_\beta)$ be the exact ordered prefix ending at the displayed
vertex $v_\beta$.  Since $v_\beta\in T(y_{\ell-1})-A$, if
$s_B=|B|-|A|$, then
\[
                       1\leq s_B\leq\ell-1<\ell.                \tag{8}
\]
Thus $A\prec B\preccurlyeq T(y_{\ell-1})\preccurlyeq T$.  Both
distinguished vertices lie in $B$ and miss $\alpha$ under $\sigma_n$, so
$B$ is not elementary.

Before changing the hierarchy coloring, apply
\noderef{GoodHierarchyTerminalPrefixRepackage} to the exact prefix
$A\prec B\preccurlyeq T$.  It gives an old-color ETT $\mathcal E_B$ on
the actual tree $B$, with the same complete certified series and maximum
property, a copied base $\mathcal B_B$, a same-divider hierarchy $H_B$
whose nonterminal and starred stages are the old ones, and a genuine
old-color good witness $\mathcal G_B$.  It also gives equality of every old
computed stored set, stage-missing set, and Gamma pair.  Its segment
conclusion is the fallback-sensitive containment for truncation, not an
equality of full \noderef{HierarchySegmentColors} functions.

We now extract and transport the terminal augmentation chain.  Destruct the
actual terminal PE constructor singled out in (3).  Its `closedExtension'
field is a \noderef{TashkinovClosure} from the exact seed $T_n$ to the
certified final closure $T_{n+1}$.  Unfolding that field first, while the
coloring is still fixed at $\varphi_n$, supplies a
\noderef{TashkinovAugmentationSequence}
\[
                         T_n\longrightarrow T_{n+1}.            \tag{9}
\]
The PE base and hierarchy inclusions give
$T_n\preccurlyeq A\prec B$; the definition of $B$ gives
$B\preccurlyeq T$; and the `belowFinal' field of
\noderef{ExtendedTashkinovTree} gives $T\preccurlyeq T_{n+1}$.  These are
exact \noderef{TreeSequenceInitialSegment} relations.  Hence
\noderef{TashkinovAugmentationSequenceRestrict}, applied only to the
fixed-$\varphi_n$ chain (9), gives
\[
       T_n=X_0\longrightarrow X_1\longrightarrow\cdots
             \longrightarrow X_m=B                             \tag{10}
\]
as a $\varphi_n$-augmentation sequence.

Equations (5) and (6), with $A=T_{n,q}$, partition the exact interval
$B-T_n$ and show that no displayed edge in it has old color $\alpha$.
We now induct over the reflexive-transitive chain (10).  The seed identities
of $T_n$ and all its vertex missing sets are fixed by (7) and
\noderef{CombinedStableClosurePreserved}.  For one old step
$X_i\longrightarrow X_{i+1}$, unfold
\noderef{TashkinovAugmentation}.  It gives the exact new last edge $f$, its
old color $c=\varphi_n(f)$, and an old-prefix vertex $w\in V(X_i)$ at which
$c$ is missing under $\varphi_n$.  The exact interval exclusion just proved
gives $c\ne\alpha$.  If $c=\beta$, then $w$ occurs strictly before the
terminal vertex $v_\beta$ of $B$, contradicting the least-candidate clause
of \noderef{HierarchyFirstMissingPosition}.  Therefore
$c\notin\{\alpha,\beta\}$.  The component swap fixes $f$ and every incident
$c$-colored edge at $w$, so $\sigma_n(f)=c$ and $c$ remains missing at $w$.
The length, attachment, and exact-prefix clauses of the augmentation are
coloring-independent.  Thus this very step is a
$\sigma_n$-\noderef{TashkinovAugmentation}.  Induction transports every
indexed step of (10) and proves a fixed-$\sigma_n$ augmentation sequence
from $T_n$ to $B$.  Simultaneously the seed and step checks prove
\[
 \sigma_n(f)=\varphi_n(f)
 \quad\text{for every displayed edge identity }f\text{ of }B.  \tag{11}
\]
In particular, the missing witness required by each new augmentation was
verified at the precise old prefix where it is used.

The $T_n$ clause of (7), the stated lower package, properness, and
one-uncoloredness allow \noderef{Td} to supply the corresponding
\noderef{ModColoring}.  More directly, all hypotheses in the statement of
\noderef{HierarchySplitter} are now present: the old maximum prefix ETT,
terminal PE, (7), the lower package, and the just-constructed
$\sigma_n$-augmentation route.  It returns an actual
\noderef{CorrespondingExtendedTashkinovTree} $\mathcal E'_B$ on the exact
tree $B$, transported maximum property, a base $\mathcal B'_B$ under
$\sigma_n$, and a hierarchy $K$ satisfying
\noderef{SameHierarchyStages} with $H_B$.  In particular, the splitter has
not supplied a good hierarchy under $\sigma_n$.

We construct that goodness now.  First identify every set on which the old
Gamma data are defined.  The `storedColorsAgree' clauses of
\noderef{CorrespondingExtendedTashkinovTree} identify each raw certified
$S_i$ set.  The first vertex clause of (7) identifies missing colors on the
common terminal ladder tree $T_n$.  Unfold
\noderef{TashkinovStoredColors}: it is the raw stored union minus that
terminal missing set.  Hence the old and replacement terminal $D_n$ sets
are equal.

Every ordinary and starred hierarchy stage lies in $A$.  At a stage vertex
outside $K_{v_\beta}$, all incident $\alpha$- and $\beta$-edge identities
are outside that component and are fixed.  At a stage vertex inside the
component, $\beta$ was present because its first missing vertex is the later
$v_\beta$, and $\alpha$ was present because elementarity of
$T(y_{\ell-1})$ already has the distinct $\alpha$-missing vertex
$v_\alpha$ in the other component.  Properness gives one incident edge of
each color, and the complete component switch exchanges them, so both
colors remain present.  Every other color is fixed globally.  Thus every
vertex missing-color set is unchanged on every ordinary and starred stage;
after taking the vertexwise union in \noderef{TreeMissingColors}, all the
corresponding tree-missing sets agree.

Now unfold \noderef{HierarchyStoredColorsAt}.  It subtracts the missing
set of the relevant starred stage from the terminal $D_n$ just identified.
Therefore, for every $0\leq j\leq q$,
\[
             D^K_{n,j}=D^{H_B}_{n,j}.                           \tag{12}
\]
Similarly, \noderef{HierarchyStageMissingColors} is the $T_n$-missing set
at index zero and the ordinary-stage missing set at every positive index.
The preceding vertexwise calculation identifies all of these sets.  Only
now transport the Gamma function of $\mathcal G_B$ along the divider-count
equality and copy its pair at every common index in (12).

We also record that $\alpha$ is not in any set in (12).  At $j=0$, the
$j=1$ instance of (2) puts $\alpha$ in an old Gamma pair selected from the
stage-zero missing set, so the `twoColors' clause of the old
\noderef{GoodHierarchy} makes $\alpha$ missing on $T_n$, hence on the
starred core; \noderef{HierarchyStoredColorsAt} subtracts it.  At
$1\leq j\leq q$, the first membership in (2) puts $\alpha$ in a pair
$\Gamma^{j-1}_\eta$.  The old `twoColors' clause makes that pair missing
on $T_n$ when $j=1$ and on $T_{n,j-1}$ when $j>1$.  Nestedness carries the
witness into $T_{n,j}=T_{n,j}^{*}$, so the same subtraction excludes
$\alpha$ from $D^{H_B}_{n,j}$.  Equation (12) transfers all these exclusions
to $K$.

Next compute the totalized first-missing cutoffs in $B$ separately for the
three possible color types.  First, $v_\alpha$ remains the first
$\alpha$-missing vertex.  No earlier vertex missed $\alpha$ under
$\varphi_n$.  If an earlier vertex became $\alpha$-missing under
$\sigma_n$, it would lie in the switched component and would have been
$\beta$-missing under $\varphi_n$, contradicting
$v_\alpha\prec v_\beta$ and the minimality of $i_\beta$.  Outside the
component nothing changed, and $v_\alpha$ lies in the disjoint component
and still misses $\alpha$.

Second, no vertex of $B$ misses $\beta$ under $\sigma_n$.  A vertex
outside $K_{v_\beta}$ is unchanged and, before the terminal vertex
$v_\beta$, did not miss $\beta$.  If a vertex inside that component became
$\beta$-missing, then it was $\alpha$-missing under $\varphi_n$.
Elementarity of $B\preccurlyeq T(y_{\ell-1})$ forces it to be
$v_\alpha$, but $v_\alpha$ lies in the other component.  At the terminal
vertex $v_\beta$, the old $\alpha$-edge whose existence was proved at the
start is switched to a $\beta$-edge.  Thus the candidate set is empty, and
the no-candidate clause of \noderef{HierarchyFirstMissingPosition} assigns
$\beta$ the last position of $B$, exactly its old position $i_\beta$.

Finally, for every $\eta\notin\{\alpha,\beta\}$,
\noderef{TwoColorComponentSwap} fixes every incident $\eta$-edge globally.
The set of $\eta$-missing candidates in $B$ is pointwise identical under
the two colorings.  Hence the least candidates agree when nonempty and the
last-position fallbacks agree when empty.

We can now verify each field of \noderef{GoodHierarchy}.  For `avoidance',
fix $j\leq q$, a relevant $\eta\in D^K_{n,j}$, and one exact edge position
witnessing a color in the new
\noderef{HierarchySegmentColors}(K,j,\eta)$.  The case
$\eta=\alpha$ is impossible by the exclusions above.  If $\eta=\beta$,
the fallback computation identifies the new upper cutoff with the old
terminal position $i_\beta$; for every other $\eta$, the candidate-set
calculation identifies the two cutoffs.  Same hierarchy stages identify
the exact lower stage bound, next-stage bound, and displayed edge at the
chosen position, and (11) identifies its color.  Thus this individual new
edge witnesses membership of its color in the corresponding old segment.
The old avoidance field of $\mathcal G_B$, the stage-missing equality, and
the copied Gamma pair prove the new avoidance clause.  This is a pointwise
new-to-old containment at the relevant stored indices; no equality of full
segment-color functions is asserted.

The `twoColors' and `disjointPairs' fields copy from $\mathcal G_B$ using
(12) and the stage-missing equalities.  For `uniqueRefresh', same stages
identify each exact stage difference, (12) identifies the stored index
sets, the vertexwise computation identifies the missing-color unions on
the difference, and the Gamma pairs agree.  Therefore both the old refresh
witness and its uniqueness proof transfer at every positive index.

For the cardinality half of the PE `parallelBase' field, the two vertex
clauses in (7) identify missing-color sets separately on $T_n$ and $R_n$.
Equation (12) at zero and Gamma equality identify the excluded reserved
union, so the old common-missing cardinality inequality is the new one.
For non-minus-closedness, unfold the old witness from $\mathcal G_B$ through
\noderef{TreeColorSetMinusClosed}, \noderef{TreeColorSetClosed}, and
\noderef{BoundaryColorEdges}.  It supplies a color
\[
 c\in\overline\varphi_n(T_n\vee R_n)\setminus\Gamma^0
\]
and an individual exact edge identity
$f\in\noderef{BoundaryEdges}(V(T_n\vee R_n))$ with
$\varphi_n(f)=c$.  The $j=1$ instance of (2) gives
$\alpha\in\Gamma^0$.  The first $\beta$-missing vertex lies outside $A$,
while the core is an exact prefix of $A$, so $\beta$ is not missing on the
core.  Hence the witness color $c$ is neither $\alpha$ nor $\beta$.
The component switch fixes the exact identity $f$ and its color.  The
vertex clauses in (7), the exact union in \noderef{HierarchyBase}, the
vertex-union clause of \noderef{OrderedTreeUnion}, and
\noderef{TreeMissingColors} identify the core missing-color set, while the
graph-theoretic boundary is unchanged.  Thus the same $c,f$ witness failure
of minus-closedness under $\sigma_n$.  In particular, the boundary witness
has not been replaced by an endpoint-only assertion; by
\noderef{TreeIncidentEdge} it remains the same edge incident with the core.

It remains to verify `minusClosed' at every positive stage.  Fix
$1\leq j\leq q$, take a color $c$ missing under $\sigma_n$ on $T_{n,j}$
but outside the common reserved union
\[
                 \bigcup_{\eta\in D_{n,j}}\Gamma^{j-1}_\eta,
\]
and suppose an exact boundary identity $f$ has $\sigma_n(f)=c$.
The stage-missing equality makes $c$ old-missing.  If $c=\alpha$, the
first membership in (2) contradicts that it lies outside the reserved
union.  If $c=\beta$, the fact that $v_\beta$ is the first
$\beta$-missing vertex and lies outside $A$ contradicts its membership in
the common stage-missing set.  Otherwise
$c\notin\{\alpha,\beta\}$, so \noderef{TwoColorComponentSwap} fixes the
exact boundary identity and gives $\varphi_n(f)=c$.  Expanding
\noderef{TreeColorSetMinusClosed}, \noderef{TreeColorSetClosed}, and
\noderef{BoundaryColorEdges}, this is an old counterexample to the old
`minusClosed' field.  These three cases prove the replacement field for
every positive stage.

We have now checked `twoColors', `avoidance', `disjointPairs',
`uniqueRefresh', both `parallelBase' clauses, and `minusClosed'.  They
construct a complete good witness $\mathcal G'_B$ under $\sigma_n$.  Only
after this construction apply \noderef{SameGoodHierarchy}: same hierarchy
stages, (12), and the transported Gamma values supply exactly its pointwise
data.

The corresponding ETT on $B$, transported maximum property, base,
hierarchy, newly constructed good witness, and the two distinct vertices
$v_\alpha,v_\beta$ missing $\alpha$ form a
\noderef{HierarchyCounterexample} $C'$ with the same divider count $q$.
It remains to compare the actual measures.  Put
$p=\noderef{HierarchyPathNumber}(H)$ and let $p'$ be the path number of
$K$.  If $s_B<p$, the fallback bound in the path-number definition gives
$p'\leq s_B<p$.  If $p\leq s_B$, the old path tail beginning at $p$
restricts to the exact prefix $B$, with its singleton terminal case still
counted as a path, so $p'\leq p$.  If equality holds, the second coordinate
of \noderef{HierarchyCounterexampleMeasure} strictly drops.  Since
$q>0$ gives $A=T_{n,q}$, equation (8) yields
\[
       |B|-|T_{n,q}|=s_B<\ell=|T|-|T_{n,q}|.
\]
Therefore \noderef{HierarchyCounterexampleLess} holds from $C'$ to $C$,
contrary to the stated minimality.  The supposition that the two path
components were distinct is false, and hence
$K_{v_\alpha}=K_{v_\beta}$ as required.
\end{proof}

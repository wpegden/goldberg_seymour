\begin{lemma}[Exit vertices miss no free color; Scheide Lemma 3.8]
For every globally maximum triple $(T,e_0,\varphi)$,
\[
 \overline\varphi(F(T,e_0,\varphi))
 \cap\mathcal F(T,e_0,\varphi)=\varnothing.
\]
Equivalently, no Scheide exit vertex misses a free color.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Fix $v\in F(T,e_0,\varphi)$ and suppose, toward a contradiction, that a
color $\alpha$ belongs both to
$\noderef{MissingColors}(G,\varphi,v)$ and to
$\noderef{TreeFreeColors}(\varphi,T)$.  By the definition
\noderef{ScheideExitVertices}, membership of $v$ in this finite set
unfolds to the predicate \noderef{ScheideExitVertex}.  Unpack that
predicate.  It supplies a vertex $u\in V(T)$, a color $\gamma$ missing at
$u$, a defective color
$\delta\in\noderef{DefectiveColors}(G,\varphi,V(T))$, an outside vertex
$z$, and an incidence walk $P$ from $u$ to $z$.  The walk satisfies the
full-path predicate \noderef{ScheideDefectiveChainPath} for
$(T,\varphi,\delta,\gamma,u,z)$, and a specified occurrence of $v$ on
$P$ lies in $V(T)$ while every later vertex-node of $P$ lies outside
$V(T)$.  In particular, $P$ is simple, $z\notin V(T)$, and its support is
exactly the $(\delta,\gamma)$-component through $u$.

First suppose that $\alpha=\gamma$.  The maximum-order facts
\noderef{ScheideGlobalMaximumOrder} say that $V(T)$ is elementary.  If
$u\ne v$, then the common color $\gamma=\alpha$ lies in the missing-color
sets at the two distinct tree vertices $u$ and $v$, contrary to the
definition \noderef{ColoredVertexSetElementary}.  Hence $u=v$.
Furthermore, $\gamma=\alpha$ is free and missing at $u$, $\delta$ is
defective, and $P$ has the required full-path property.  The lemma
\noderef{ScheideDefectiveChainDepartureReturn} therefore gives a first
outside occurrence and a later last returning tree vertex $v_2$, with
indices satisfying \noderef{ScheideDepartureReturn}.  The exit witness
for $v$ says that no tree vertex occurs later than its specified
occurrence, while the last-return predicate says the same of the
specified occurrence of $v_2$.  Comparing the two indices, either strict
inequality would put one of $v,v_2$ later than the other's last-tree
occurrence.  Thus the indices are equal, and the values of $P$ there give
$v_2=v$.  Apply
\noderef{ScheideDefectiveChainLastReturnNoFree} to these exact data.  It
says that $v_2=v$ misses no free color, contradicting that the free color
$\alpha$ is missing at $v$.

It remains to treat $\alpha\ne\gamma$.  Put
\[
 X=\noderef{TreeSequenceVertexSet}(T).
\]
Since $\alpha$ is free, the definitions
\noderef{TreeFreeColors} and \noderef{TreeMissingColors} give a vertex
$x_\alpha\in X$ at which $\alpha$ is missing.  The exit data give
$u\in X$ with $\gamma$ missing at $u$.  By
\noderef{ScheideGlobalMaximumOrder}, $X$ is closed; expanding this with
\noderef{ColoredVertexSetClosed}, and using the exact boundary identities
from \noderef{BoundaryEdges} and \noderef{BoundaryColorEdges}, shows that
no boundary identity has color $\alpha$ or $\gamma$.  This is also the
missing-color boundary clause of
\noderef{ScheideGlobalMaximumBoundaryFacts}.  Thus
\[
 \noderef{BoundaryColorEdges}(G,\varphi,X,\alpha)=\varnothing,
 \qquad
 \noderef{BoundaryColorEdges}(G,\varphi,X,\gamma)=\varnothing. \tag{1}
\]

The membership of $\delta$ in \noderef{DefectiveColors}, together with
the defective-color clause of
\noderef{ScheideGlobalMaximumBoundaryFacts}, gives at least three
distinct $\delta$-colored identities in $\partial_G(X)$; in particular,
there is one.  Closedness now implies that $\delta$ is missing at no
vertex of $X$: if it were missing at $x\in X$, the definition
\noderef{ColoredVertexSetClosed} would forbid that chosen
$\delta$-colored boundary identity.  Since \noderef{MissingColors} is the
complement of \noderef{PresentColors}, $\delta$ is present at every
vertex of $X$.  It is therefore distinct from $\gamma$, which is missing
at $u$, and from $\alpha$, which is missing at $x_\alpha$.  Together with
the case assumption, the three colors $\alpha,\gamma,\delta$ are
pairwise distinct.

Define
\[
 \varphi'=\noderef{OutsideTreeColorSwap}(T,\varphi,\alpha,\gamma).
\]
Thus every identity incident with $X$ is fixed, and on every other
identity the two colors $\alpha$ and $\gamma$ are transposed; every other
value, including the uncolored value, is fixed.  We next verify, rather
than assume, that $(T,e_0,\varphi')$ is again globally maximum.

The definition \noderef{GlobalMaximumTriple} supplies a
$\noderef{KTriple}(G,k,e_0,\varphi)$, hence properness of $\varphi$ in the
sense of \noderef{IsProperEdgeColoring}.  Let $f,g$ be distinct adjacent
colored edge identities under $\varphi'$.  By
\noderef{AdjacentEdges}, they share a vertex $w$ in the incidence sense
of \noderef{Incident}.  There are three cases.  If both are incident with
$X$, both values are fixed, so equality of their new colors would be
equality of their old colors.  If neither is incident with $X$, the same
transposition of $\alpha$ and $\gamma$ is applied to both, and its
injectivity again turns equality of new colors into equality of old
colors.  Finally suppose exactly one, say $f$, is incident with $X$.
Then $w\notin X$, since otherwise $g$ would also be incident with $X$.
The identity $f$ has one end in $X$ and the shared end $w$ outside $X$,
so \noderef{BoundaryEdges} puts $f$ in $\partial_G(X)$.  By (1), the
fixed color of $f$ is neither $\alpha$ nor $\gamma$.  The transposition
on $g$ can equal this fixed color only if $g$ already had that same
color.  Each case contradicts the old properness, proving that
$\varphi'$ is proper.

The same $k$-triple also supplies
$\noderef{IsOneEdgeUncolored}(\varphi,e_0)$.  The outside swap fixes an
uncolored value and sends every colored value to a colored value.
Checking each edge identity gives
\[
 \noderef{IsOneEdgeUncolored}(\varphi',e_0).                    \tag{2}
\]
For every $x\in X$, each edge incident with $x$ satisfies
\noderef{TreeIncidentEdge} and hence is fixed.  The witness definition
\noderef{PresentColors} therefore gives
\[
 \noderef{PresentColors}(G,\varphi',x)
   =\noderef{PresentColors}(G,\varphi,x),
\]
and taking complements by \noderef{MissingColors} gives equality of the
missing-color sets at $x$.  Every boundary edge is incident with $X$, so
for every color $c$,
\[
 \noderef{BoundaryColorEdges}(G,\varphi',X,c)
   =\noderef{BoundaryColorEdges}(G,\varphi,X,c).                \tag{3}
\]
Consequently \noderef{DefectiveColors} is unchanged.  Taking the union
of the unchanged vertex missing sets shows via
\noderef{TreeMissingColors} that the tree-missing set is unchanged.
Every displayed edge of $T$ joins a displayed vertex to an earlier one by
\noderef{TreeSequence}; hence it is incident with $X$ by
\noderef{TreeIncidentEdge} and is fixed.  Thus
\noderef{TreeUsedColors} is unchanged, and so is its difference from the
tree-missing set, namely \noderef{TreeFreeColors}.

We also verify the Tashkinov condition witness by witness.  The old
globally maximum triple supplies
$\noderef{TashkinovTree}(G,e_0,\varphi,T)$.  For every displayed edge
after the first, retain its old earlier vertex and old missing-color
witness.  The displayed edge keeps its color, and the earlier vertex has
the same missing-color set, so every witness remains valid for
$\varphi'$.  Hence $T$ is a Tashkinov tree for $(e_0,\varphi')$.  The
criticality, chromatic-index equality, and degree inequality in
\noderef{KTriple} depend only on $G,k,e_0$; combining them with the
properness just proved and (2) rebuilds the same $k$-triple.  The tree
$T$ itself and therefore $|X|$ are unchanged.  All three clauses of
\noderef{GlobalMaximumTriple} now give
\[
 (T,e_0,\varphi')\text{ globally maximum}.                     \tag{4}
\]

Let $R=vPz$ be the terminal incidence subwalk beginning at the specified
exit occurrence of $v$.  The last-tree-vertex clause in
\noderef{ScheideExitVertex} says that the only vertex-node of $R$ in $X$
is its initial node $v$.  Since its terminal node $z$ is outside $X$, the
first edge-node of $R$ joins $v$ to an outside vertex: the adjacency rule
in \noderef{TwoColorIncidenceGraph}, together with
\noderef{Incident} and \noderef{BoundaryEdges}, makes its individual
edge identity a boundary identity.  If a later edge-node of $R$ were
another boundary identity, its two neighboring vertex-nodes on the
incidence walk would be its two ends, one in $X$ and one outside $X$.
That would put a later tree vertex on $R$, contradicting the exit clause.
Thus
\[
 V(R)\cap X=\{v\},\qquad
 \bigl|E(R)\cap\partial_G(X)\bigr|=1.                           \tag{5}
\]
Both assertions concern actual edge-nodes and hence actual parallel-edge
identities.

The first boundary edge-node of $R$ lies on the old
$(\delta,\gamma)$-walk.  By \noderef{TwoColorIncidenceGraph} its old
color is $\delta$ or $\gamma$, and (1) excludes $\gamma$ on a boundary
identity.  It therefore has old color $\delta$ and, being incident with
$X$, remains $\delta$ under $\varphi'$.  Every later edge of $R$ has both
ends outside $X$ by (5).  Consecutive old edge identities on $P$ are
distinct and adjacent; because $\delta\ne\gamma$ and $\varphi$ is
proper, their old colors alternate between $\delta$ and $\gamma$.
The outside swap fixes $\delta$ and changes each of these outside
$\gamma$-edges to $\alpha$.  Thus the same ordered incidence sequence
$R$ is a $(\delta,\alpha)$-walk under $\varphi'$.

We prove that this is the complete new component, not merely a walk in
it.  At $v$, the color $\alpha$ remains missing by the equality of
tree-vertex missing sets, while the first edge of $R$ is the unique
incident $\delta$-edge by properness of $\varphi'$.  If the chosen
occurrence of $v$ has a predecessor edge on $P$, that edge is distinct
from the first edge of $R$; both old colors lie in
$\{\delta,\gamma\}$, so old properness and the first edge's color
$\delta$ make the predecessor's color $\gamma$.  It is incident with
$v\in X$ and hence stays $\gamma$, which is outside
$\{\delta,\alpha\}$.  If there is no predecessor, the occurrence is the
initial vertex $u$ of $P$ and there is no prefix to consider.  In either
case there is no new two-color incidence before $v$, and missingness of
$\alpha$ together with uniqueness of the incident $\delta$-edge excludes
every extra incidence there.

At every outside internal vertex-node of $R$, the two incident edges of
$R$ have the two distinct colors $\delta$ and $\alpha$.  Properness of
$\varphi'$ permits no additional incident edge of either color.  At an
edge-node, \noderef{TwoColorIncidenceGraph} gives only the incidences with
its two endpoint vertices, and both are already the neighboring nodes of
$R$.  Hence no incidence leaves $R$ at an internal node.

It remains to check the terminal vertex $z$.  In the old incidence graph,
$u$ has degree one: $\gamma$ is missing there, while $\delta$ is present
there and its incident $\delta$-edge is unique by properness.  The
support equality in \noderef{ScheideDefectiveChainPath} says that the
simple path $P$ spans the whole old $(\delta,\gamma)$-component.  The
degree bound \noderef{TwoColorIncidenceDegreeAtMostTwo} applies because
$\varphi$ is proper.  Every internal node of the simple path already uses
two distinct path incidences, and $u$ has only its one path incidence.
If $z$ had a second incidence, its other neighbor would still belong to
the same component and hence to the support of $P$; but every edge-node
in that support is internal on this vertex-to-vertex path and already
uses its two path incidences.  Therefore $z$ is the other degree-one
endpoint of the old full component.

Now let an edge identity incident with $z$ have new color $\delta$ or
$\alpha$.  If it is not incident with $X$, reversing the outside
transposition shows that its old color was respectively $\delta$ or
$\gamma$.  If it is incident with $X$, then $z\notin X$ makes it a
boundary identity by \noderef{TreeIncidentEdge} and
\noderef{BoundaryEdges}; it is fixed, and (1) excludes both old
$\alpha$ and old $\gamma$ on such an identity.  Thus in this case a new
two-color edge must already have had old color $\delta$.  Every new
$(\delta,\alpha)$-incidence at $z$ therefore comes from an old
$(\delta,\gamma)$-incidence at $z$.  The old degree-one conclusion, and
the last edge of $R$ which realizes that one incidence, show that $z$ has
degree one in the new two-color graph as well.

We have proved that the support of $R$ is connected and that no incidence
of the new two-color graph leaves it at either endpoint, at an internal
vertex-node, or at an edge-node.  By the connected-component definition
\noderef{TwoColorComponent},
\[
 \operatorname{supp}(R)=
 \noderef{TwoColorComponent}
   (G,\varphi',\delta,\alpha,\operatorname{inl}v).              \tag{6}
\]
The terminal subwalk of the simple path $P$ is simple, its initial vertex
$v$ lies in $X$, and its terminal vertex $z$ lies outside $X$.  Equation
(6) supplies the remaining support clause.  Consequently $R$ satisfies
every clause of \noderef{ScheideDefectiveChainPath} for
$(T,\varphi',\delta,\alpha,v,z)$.

Apply \noderef{ScheideDefectiveChainBoundaryEdges} to the rebuilt
globally maximum triple (4), the unchanged defective color $\delta$, the
unchanged free color $\alpha$ still missing at $v$, and this exact full
component $R$.  Its conclusion puts every $\delta$-colored boundary edge
identity under $\varphi'$ on $R$.  By (5), $R$ contains exactly one
boundary identity.  On the other hand,
\noderef{ScheideGlobalMaximumBoundaryFacts} gives at least three distinct
$\delta$-colored boundary identities under $\varphi$, and (3) says that
this precise set of identities is unchanged under $\varphi'$.  This is a
contradiction.

Thus for the arbitrary $v\in F(T,e_0,\varphi)$ there is no color in both
$\noderef{MissingColors}(G,\varphi,v)$ and
$\noderef{TreeFreeColors}(\varphi,T)$.  The two finite color sets are
disjoint.  Since $v$ was arbitrary, the asserted disjointness holds for
every Scheide exit vertex.
\end{proof}

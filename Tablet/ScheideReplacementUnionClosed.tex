\begin{lemma}[Scheide one-pair unions are closed (17)]
For every defective color $\delta$, the set
$X_\delta=V(T)\cup U_\delta$ is closed with respect to $\varphi$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Let $\alpha$ be missing at a vertex of $X_\delta$.  First suppose it is
missing on $V(T)$.  Original closedness, from
\noderef{ScheideGlobalMaximumOrder}, excludes an $\alpha$-edge from
$V(T)$ to its exterior.  The two vertices of $U_\delta$ have an internal
$\alpha$-edge in every case.  If $\alpha=\gamma(\delta)$ use assertion
(12) in \noderef{ScheideSymmetricDefectiveInternal}.  If the missing tree
vertex lies in $Z_{\gamma(\delta)}$ and
$\alpha\ne\gamma(\delta)$, use
\noderef{ScheideReplacementInternalMissing}.  If it lies in the deleted
pair, use assertion (14) in
\noderef{ScheideSymmetricMissingInternal}.  Therefore no $\alpha$ edge can
leave through the replacement pair either.

Now suppose $\alpha$ is missing at a vertex of $U_\delta$.  The incident
$\delta$ edge and internal $\gamma(\delta)$ edge show that it is neither
of those colors.  Consequently $\alpha$ is unchanged by the defective
switch and is missing on the closed globally maximum replacement tree from
\noderef{ScheideReplacementMaximum}.  No $\alpha$-edge can leave
$Z_{\gamma(\delta)}\cup U_\delta$.  Assertion (13) in
\noderef{ScheideSymmetricMissingInternal} supplies an internal
$\alpha$-edge of the omitted pair $U_{\gamma(\delta)}$, so no such edge
leaves that pair either.  These two cases cover every possible missing
vertex in $X_\delta$ and prove closure as defined in
\noderef{ColoredVertexSetClosed}.
\end{proof}

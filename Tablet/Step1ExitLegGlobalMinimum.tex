\begin{lemma}[Step 1 exit-leg global minimum]
Let $T$ be a positive-rung ETT of rung number $n$, constructed from an
arbitrary certified input coloring and with generating coloring
$\varphi_n$.  Assume that $T$ has maximum property, that $\varphi_n$ is
proper and leaves exactly the distinguished edge $e_0$ uncolored, and
fix a hierarchy base.  Let
\[
 T_n=T_{n,0}\subset T_{n,1}\subset\cdots\subset
 T_{n,q}\subset T_{n,q+1}=T
\]
be a good hierarchy $H$ over that base, with its actual family of
two-color sets $\Gamma^j_\eta$.

Assume, uniformly over every certified input coloring and generating
coloring, that every positive-rung ETT $U$ of rung number $s<n$ with
maximum property satisfies all six lower-rung assertions:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $V(U)$ is elementary under its generating coloring;
\item under every proper coloring leaving exactly $e_0$ uncolored and
stable on the final ladder closure, that closure is interchangeable for
two colors whenever at least one is missing there;
\item stability descends unconditionally at every PE support to the prefix before
that support and, when its first-appearance index is greater than one, also
to the preceding ladder stage; the stored pairs at
distinct PE slots with distinct supports and the same first-appearance
index are disjoint;
\item at a terminal PE, the stored two-color component through the
support meets the terminal ladder tree only at the support;
\item every nonminimum endpoint of a defective boundary color precedes
each later PE support or SE extension vertex allowed by its
first-appearance index; and
\item every proper terminal stable coloring leaving exactly $e_0$
uncolored is a mod-coloring of $U$.
\end{enumerate}

Assume also the induction hypothesis (6.1) in the following explicit
form.  For every arbitrary certified input coloring and generating
coloring $\psi$, every ETT $U$ of the same rung number $n$ as $T$ which
has maximum property, and every good hierarchy $K$ of $U$ with strictly
fewer than $q$ dividers, $V(U)$ is elementary under $\psi$.  Finally
assume the large-branch estimate
\[
              |\overline{\varphi_n}(T_n)|\geq 2n+11.       \tag{L}
\]

Let $1\leq p\leq q$, let
$S=T_{n,p}$ (formally, for $i=p-1$ this is
`$H.\mathit{stage}(i.\mathit{succ}.\mathit{castSucc})$'), and let
$\alpha,\beta\in[k]$ be distinct.  Assume
$\alpha\in\overline{\varphi_n}(S)$, the singleton $\{\alpha\}$ is closed
on $S$ under $\varphi_n$, and $\alpha,\beta$ are not
$S$-interchangeable.

Then there exist an arbitrary certified input, a proper
generating coloring $\psi$ leaving exactly $e_0$ uncolored, and a
same-rung ETT $U$ on an exact terminal tree $T'$ with maximum property,
together with a base and a good hierarchy $K$, such that
$d=K.\mathit{dividerCount}<q$.  This presentation has its own large
estimate, its separate terminal minus-closed certificate, and
$V(T')$ is elementary under $\psi$.

There exist distinct colors $a,b$, a color $\theta$, two exact
support-disjoint $(T',\psi,\{a,b\})$-exit paths $P_1,P_2$ with distinct
outside and tree ends $a_1,b_1,a_2,b_2$ ordered by $b_1\prec b_2$, and
individual $b$-colored boundary-edge identities $f_1,f_2$ on the paths
incident with $b_1,b_2$.  The color $a$ is missing and singleton-closed
on $T'$.  Writing $A=K.\mathit{starred}(d)$, the color $\theta$ is
different from $a,b$, is missing and singleton-closed on $A$, and is
singleton-closed on $T'$.

This presentation is primary-minimal over the full replacement family:
under every proper exactly-one-uncolored stable coloring, no
corresponding same-rung maximum ETT with its own large estimate,
terminal certificate, and good hierarchy with fewer than $d$ dividers
admits two such exit legs.  Moreover, either $b$ is missing on $T'$, or
$b$ is not missing and there is a third exact exit path $P_3$, pairwise
support-disjoint from the first two, with a third outside and tree end,
$b_2\prec b_3$, and a retained $b$-colored boundary identity $f_3$ on
$P_3$.  In the latter alternative the chosen triple is secondary-minimal
over all corresponding same-divider presentations: none has second
color missing, and none whose second color is present has a qualifying
triple with total exact walk length smaller than
$|P_1|+|P_2|+|P_3|$.  Every comparison ranges over arbitrary certified
replacement inputs and retains individual parallel-edge identities.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Put $n=\mathcal E.\mathit{rounds}$ and $q=H.\mathit{dividerCount}$,
where $\mathcal E$ denotes the ETT in the statement.  We first record two
elementary consequences of the definitions that will be used for every
comparison presentation.

Let $\mathcal U$ be a same-rung maximum ETT with generating coloring
$\psi$, base $B$, good hierarchy $K$, terminal tree $U$, and
$d=K.\mathit{dividerCount}<q$.  Write
\[
 X=\mathcal U.\mathit{series.tree}(n),\qquad
 A=K.\mathit{starred}(d),
\]
where the first expression means the terminal ladder tree.  The last
starred stage is an initial segment of $U$.  Indeed, if $d>0$, then
\noderef{HierarchyStarredStage} identifies $A$ with $K.\mathit{stage}(d)$,
and the last `nested' and `terminalStage' fields of
\noderef{TashkinovHierarchy} give
$K.\mathit{stage}(d)\preccurlyeq K.\mathit{stage}(d+1)=U$.  If $d=0$,
then $A=B.\mathit{core}$, and the `coreInsideFirst' and `terminalStage'
fields give $A\preccurlyeq K.\mathit{stage}(1)=U$.  Thus in both cases
\[
                       A\preccurlyeq U.                       \tag{1}
\]
Unfolding \noderef{TreeSequenceInitialSegment} in (1) shows entry by
entry that $V(A)\subseteq V(U)$, using
\noderef{TreeSequenceVertexSet}.  Taking the unions of vertex-missing
sets in \noderef{TreeMissingColors} therefore gives
\[
                 \overline\psi(A)\subseteq\overline\psi(U).
\]
Subtract this inclusion from the common raw set
$\noderef{TashkinovStoredColors}(\mathcal U.\mathit{series},n)$.
The definitions \noderef{HierarchyTerminalStoredColors} and
\noderef{HierarchyStoredColorsAt} then give the required direction
\[
 D^K_{n,d+1}=\mathit{HierarchyTerminalStoredColors}(K)
       \subseteq D^K_{n,d}
       =\mathit{HierarchyStoredColorsAt}(K,d).                 \tag{2}
\]
In particular, the terminal set in (2), rather than an additional
internal-stage value, is the index set in the certificate
\[
 U\text{ is }
 \left(\bigcup_{\eta\in D^K_{n,d+1}}\Gamma^d_\eta\right)^-
 \text{-closed under }\psi.                                  \tag{C}
\]

We also make explicit how two exit legs supply the exact edge data used
below.  Suppose $c$ is missing and singleton-closed on $U$, and $Q_1,Q_2$
are support-disjoint $c,d'$ exit paths with distinct outside ends.  By
\noderef{TreeExitPath}, the final vertex-node of $Q_j$ is in $V(U)$, all
earlier vertex-nodes are outside, and the walk has positive incidence
length.  Hence the edge-node immediately before its final vertex exists.
Call its exact edge identity $g_j$.  The two adjacencies encoded by
\noderef{TwoColorIncidenceGraph}, interpreted through
\noderef{Incident}, show that $g_j$ has an end in $V(U)$ and an end
outside; these are distinct ends, consistently with
\noderef{LooplessMultigraph}.  Thus
$g_j\in\noderef{BoundaryEdges}(V(U))$.  Its color is $c$
or $d'$.  The first possibility is excluded by singleton closedness,
unfolded through \noderef{TreeColorSetClosed} and
\noderef{BoundaryColorEdges}; consequently $g_j$ is an exact
$d'$-colored boundary identity, lies on $Q_j$, and is incident with its
tree end.  Support disjointness makes the two tree ends distinct.  The
pairwise-distinct vertex list in \noderef{TreeSequence}, together with
\noderef{TreeVertexOrder}, then orders those two tree ends strictly; if
necessary we exchange all subscripts, walks, and edge identities.

Define an \emph{admissible presentation} to consist of the following
data, and no induction hypotheses.  It has an arbitrary certified input,
a generating coloring $\psi$, a tree $U$, a same-rung ETT $\mathcal U$
on $U$ with maximum property, a base $B$, and a good hierarchy $K$ with
$d<q$.  It requires that $\psi$ be proper and leave exactly $e_0$
uncolored, that its own terminal-ladder bound be
\[
             2n+11\leq |\overline\psi(X)|,                    \tag{3}
\]
and that (C) hold with the genuine terminal set of (2).  Finally it has
distinct colors $a,b$, with $a$ missing and singleton-closed on $U$, and
two exact, support-disjoint \noderef{TreeExitPath}s with distinct outside
ends.  We include the distinct tree ends, their strict order, and the two
individual $b$-colored boundary identities just derived.  The hypotheses
`hlower' and `hfewer' of the statement are fixed ambient hypotheses, not
fields of an admissible presentation.

Every admissible presentation has elementary terminal vertex set.  Its
rung number is $n$, it has maximum property, its hierarchy is good, and
$d<q$, so the hypothesis `hfewer' applies literally and yields
\[
                    V(U)\text{ is elementary under }\psi.      \tag{4}
\]
If $b\notin\overline\psi(U)$, apply
\noderef{ElementaryClosedExitPairExtendsTriple} to (4), properness, and
the two stored legs.  It returns a third exit path support-disjoint from
both, third outside and tree ends distinct from the preceding ends, and
an exact $b$-colored boundary identity on that path.  Thus the set of
qualifying triples of exit paths is nonempty.  For an admissible
presentation define its secondary number to be $0$ if
$b\in\overline\psi(U)$; otherwise define it to be one plus the least
total walk length among all qualifying triples for the same structural
presentation and colors.  The least length exists by well-ordering of
$\mathbb N$.  Give the presentation the lexicographic coordinate
\[
                      (d,\text{secondary number}).             \tag{5}
\]

We next prove that the class just defined is nonempty.  Put
$r=i+1$, so $1\leq r\leq q$, and apply
\noderef{GoodHierarchyPrefixRepackage} at $r$.  It gives an ETT
$\mathcal U_0$ on the exact ordered tree
$U_0=H.\mathit{stage}(r)$, with the same entire certified series and rung
number as $\mathcal E$, maximum property, a base $B_0$, and a good
hierarchy $K_0$ having $r-1<q$ dividers.  Its exact retained stages,
stored sets, and Gamma values are those of $H$.  The equality of entire
series transports (3) from the assumed large bound.

The separate last conclusion of
\noderef{GoodHierarchyPrefixRepackage} says that $U_0$ is
\[
 \left(\bigcup_{\eta\in D^H_{n,r}}\Gamma^{r-1}_\eta\right)^-
 \text{-closed}.                                              \tag{6}
\]
This is exactly (C) for $K_0$, rather than an internal closure field of
$K_0$.  To check this assertion, unfold
\noderef{HierarchyTerminalStoredColors}: its raw set is the same because
the two ETTs have the same certified series, and its subtracted missing
set is $\overline\varphi(U_0)$.  Since $r>0$,
\noderef{HierarchyStarredStage} identifies the tree subtracted in
$D^H_{n,r}$ with $H.\mathit{stage}(r)=U_0$.  Hence
$\mathit{HierarchyTerminalStoredColors}(K_0)=D^H_{n,r}$.  The retained
Gamma equality at the last index is
$\Gamma^{K_0,r-1}_\eta=\Gamma^{H,r-1}_\eta$, so (6) is precisely (C).

Apply `hfewer' to $\mathcal U_0,K_0$ to obtain elementariness.  The
original hypotheses on $H.\mathit{stage}(r)$ say that $\alpha$ is
missing and singleton-closed there and that $\alpha,\beta$ are not
interchangeable.  Therefore
\noderef{ElementaryClosedNoninterchangeableExitPair} supplies two exact
support-disjoint exit legs, distinct outside and tree ends, and their
individual $\beta$-colored boundary identities.  Order the tree ends as
above.  Properness and the exactly-one-uncolored condition are the given
ones for $\varphi$, while all remaining structural data came from the
prefix theorem.  We have therefore constructed an admissible
presentation.

Choose an admissible presentation with least coordinate (5), first
minimizing the nonempty set of divider counts and then the nonempty set of
secondary numbers at that divider count.  Denote its data by
\[
 (\chi,U,\psi,\mathcal U,B,K,\Gamma; a,b),
 \qquad d=K.\mathit{dividerCount},
\]
and retain two qualifying legs for now.  Notice that $d=0$ is allowed.
Only $G,k,e_0,n,q$ and the ambient hypotheses are fixed in this minimum;
the certified input, generating coloring, ETT, base, hierarchy, terminal
tree, colors, and paths all vary.

Before constructing the requested color $\theta$, we state the counting
fact with all indices visible.  By
\noderef{HierarchyStoredColorsCardinality},
$|D^K_{n,j}|\leq n$ at every internal index $0\leq j\leq d$.
The `twoColors' and `disjointPairs' fields of
\noderef{GoodHierarchy} therefore give
\[
 \left|\bigcup_{\eta\in D^K_{n,j}}\Gamma^j_\eta\right|
       =2|D^K_{n,j}|\leq2n.                                  \tag{7}
\]
The terminal inclusion (2) also gives, explicitly,
\[
 \bigcup_{\eta\in D^K_{n,d+1}}\Gamma^d_\eta
 \subseteq
 \bigcup_{\eta\in D^K_{n,d}}\Gamma^d_\eta.                  \tag{8}
\]
We construct $\theta$ in three cases.

Suppose first that $d=0$ and the last certified step is not PE.  The ETT
identity in \noderef{ExtendedTashkinovTree} identifies $\psi$ with the
last series coloring.  Since $n>0$, the last certificate supplied by
\noderef{TashkinovSeries} is a noninitial constructor of
\noderef{TashkinovSeriesStep}; under the present assumption it is RE or
SE.  In the RE case its `currentColoring' field identifies $\psi$ with
the preceding coloring, and its `previousClosed' field
\noderef{ColoredVertexSetClosed} says directly that every color missing
on $X$ is closed on $X$ under $\psi$.

In the SE case write $\pi$ for the preceding coloring and $\rho$ for the
selected coloring.  The `currentColoring' field gives $\psi=\rho$, while
the `selectedStable' field of
\noderef{MaximumDefectiveChoice} gives stability from $\pi$ to $\rho$ on
$X$.  Both are proper and leave exactly $e_0$ uncolored by the SE fields
and the selected-choice fields.  Symmetry in \noderef{sc2} therefore
gives stability from $\rho$ to $\pi$.  By
\noderef{StableColoring}, the two colorings have identical missing sets
at every vertex of $X$.  If a color $c\in\overline\psi(X)$ occurred on
an exact $\psi$-boundary edge $f$, symmetric stability would fix $f$
because $c$ is missing on $X$: hence $\pi(f)=\psi(f)=c$.  But $c$ is
also missing on $X$ under $\pi$, contradicting the SE `previousClosed'
field after unfolding \noderef{BoundaryColorEdges}.  Thus every color
missing on $X$ is singleton-closed there under $\psi$ in the SE case as
well.

Now (3), (7) at $j=0$, and deletion of the two colors $a,b$ leave at
least nine colors in
\[
 \overline\psi(X)\setminus
 \left(\{a,b\}\cup
   \bigcup_{\eta\in D^K_{n,0}}\Gamma^0_\eta\right).
\]
Choose one and call it $\theta$.  The `nonparallel' field of
\noderef{HierarchyBase} identifies $B.\mathit{core}=X$, and
\noderef{HierarchyStarredStage} identifies this core with $A$.  Hence
$\theta$ is missing and closed on $A$.  The terminal tree contains $X$
by \noderef{TashkinovHierarchy}, so $\theta$ is also missing on $U$.
Its avoidance of the larger internal union and (8) show that it avoids
the terminal reserved union.  Certificate (C), unfolded through
\noderef{TreeColorSetMinusClosed}, therefore closes $\theta$ on $U$.

Suppose next that $d=0$ and the last step is PE.  The `parallelBase'
field of \noderef{GoodHierarchy} gives
\[
 4\leq\left|
 (\overline\psi(X)\cap\overline\psi(B.\mathit{closure}))
 \setminus\bigcup_{\eta\in D^K_{n,0}}\Gamma^0_\eta
 \right|.                                                     \tag{9}
\]
After deleting $a,b$, choose a remaining color $\theta$; it is missing
on both $X$ and $B.\mathit{closure}$ and avoids the internal reserved
union.

We verify, rather than assume, the pre-step elementariness needed here.
If $n=1$, the first conjunct of \noderef{TechnicalTheoremBase}, applied
to the certified series of $\mathcal U$, says exactly that $V(X)$ is
elementary under the pre-step coloring.  If $n>1$, apply
\noderef{CertifiedETTPredecessor} to $\mathcal U$ and its maximum
property.  It returns a maximum ETT of rung $n-1>0$, with terminal tree
exactly $X$ and generating coloring exactly the pre-step coloring.  Its
rung is strictly below $n$, so the first conjunct of the ambient `hlower'
hypothesis gives the same elementariness conclusion.  Thus
\noderef{ParallelCommonMissingClosed} applies and closes every color in
the intersection in (9) on $B.\mathit{core}$.  The `parallel' field of
\noderef{HierarchyBase} and \noderef{OrderedTreeUnion} identify this
core with the exact ordered union $X\vee B.\mathit{closure}$, while
\noderef{HierarchyStarredStage} identifies it with $A$.  Therefore
$\theta$ is missing on $A$ (already on the initial segment $X$) and is
closed there.  As in the preceding case, (8) and (C) close it on $U$.

Finally suppose $d>0$.  Put
\[
 B_-=K.\mathit{stage}(d-1),\qquad A=K.\mathit{stage}(d),
\]
where the second equality is \noderef{HierarchyStarredStage}.  Repeated
use of the `nested' field of \noderef{TashkinovHierarchy} gives
$X\preccurlyeq B_-\preccurlyeq A\preccurlyeq U$.  Unfolding
\noderef{TreeSequenceInitialSegment} and
\noderef{TreeMissingColors} at each inclusion gives
\[
 \overline\psi(X)\subseteq\overline\psi(B_-)
 \subseteq\overline\psi(A)\subseteq\overline\psi(U).         \tag{10}
\]
There is also an initial-segment inclusion
$K.\mathit{starred}(d-1)\preccurlyeq A$: for $d>1$ it is the hierarchy
inclusion from stage $d-1$ to stage $d$, while for $d=1$ it is exactly
the `coreInsideFirst' field.  Repeating the missing-set subtraction
argument of (2), now for these two internal starred stages, gives
\[
                         D^K_{n,d}\subseteq D^K_{n,d-1}.       \tag{11}
\]
Consequently the `twoColors' and `disjointPairs' fields of
\noderef{GoodHierarchy} at index $d-1$ apply to every index in
$D^K_{n,d}$.  Together with
\noderef{HierarchyStoredColorsCardinality} at index $d$, they give the
specific bound
\[
 \left|\bigcup_{\eta\in D^K_{n,d}}\Gamma^{d-1}_\eta\right|
       =2|D^K_{n,d}|\leq2n.                                  \tag{12}
\]
Use (3), (10), (12), and deletion of $a,b$ to choose
\[
 \theta\in\overline\psi(B_-)
 \setminus\left(\{a,b\}\cup
   \bigcup_{\eta\in D^K_{n,d}}\Gamma^{d-1}_\eta\right).       \tag{13}
\]
There are again at least nine choices.  By (10), $\theta$ is missing on
$A$.  The last `minusClosed' field of \noderef{GoodHierarchy} says that
$A$ is
$(\bigcup_{\eta\in D^K_{n,d}}\Gamma^{d-1}_\eta)^-$-closed;
thus (13) and \noderef{TreeColorSetMinusClosed} close $\theta$ on $A$.

It remains to show that $\theta$ avoids the terminal union in (C), whose
Gamma superscript is $d$.  By (2), every terminal index $\eta$ lies in
$D^K_{n,d}$.  Apply the last `uniqueRefresh' field of
\noderef{GoodHierarchy}, and let $\eta_g$ be its unique refreshed index.
For $\eta\ne\eta_g$, that field gives
$\Gamma^d_\eta=\Gamma^{d-1}_\eta$, so (13) excludes $\theta$.
For $\eta=\eta_g$, every color in $\Gamma^d_{\eta_g}$ is missing at a
vertex of
\[
             V(A)\setminus V(K.\mathit{starred}(d-1)).         \tag{14}
\]
Every vertex of $B_-$ lies in $K.\mathit{starred}(d-1)$: this is equality
when $d-1>0$ by \noderef{HierarchyStarredStage}; when $d-1=0$, it follows
from the base-stage identity, the `parallel' or `nonparallel' field of
\noderef{HierarchyBase}, and the vertex-union clause of
\noderef{OrderedTreeUnion}.  If $\theta$ lay in the refreshed pair,
(13) would give a $\theta$-missing vertex in $B_-$, while (14) would give
a distinct $\theta$-missing vertex in $A$.  This contradicts (4) and the
definition \noderef{ColoredVertexSetElementary}.  Hence $\theta$ avoids
every $\Gamma^d_\eta$ indexed by the terminal set.  By (10) it is missing
on $U$, so (C) closes it on $U$.

All three cases produce
\[
 \theta\ne a,b,\qquad
 \theta\in\overline\psi(A),\qquad
 A\text{ is }\{\theta\}\text{-closed},\qquad
 U\text{ is }\{\theta\}\text{-closed}.                       \tag{15}
\]

We now derive the primary universal clause.  Take data quantified there
and suppose, toward a contradiction, that its two exit legs exist.
Unfold \noderef{CorrespondingExtendedTashkinovTree}.  The object
`corr' contains its actual `replacementInput', actual certified
replacement series, and `replacementETT' on the displayed tree; its
generating-coloring field is the quantified coloring $\sigma$, and its
`rungAgreement' gives
\[
 \mathit{corr.replacementETT.rounds}=\mathcal U.\mathit{rounds}=n.
\]
The quantified hypotheses give properness, exact one-uncoloredness,
maximum property, the base and good hierarchy $L$, its own large bound,
and its terminal certificate with
\noderef{HierarchyTerminalStoredColors}.  The stable-coloring premise is
part of the certified replacement-family comparison; it is not confused
with the globally fixed lower package.  Since
\[
 L.\mathit{dividerCount}<d<q,
\]
`hfewer', applied using `rungAgreement', also gives elementariness of the
replacement terminal tree.  From the two quantified exit paths,
support-disjointness, distinct outside ends, and singleton closedness,
the explicit final-edge argument preceding the definition of admissible
presentations supplies the distinct tree ends, their order, and exact
second-color boundary identities.  Thus these data are literally an
admissible presentation with primary coordinate
$L.\mathit{dividerCount}<d$, contradicting the choice of (5).  This is
exactly the asserted primary `False' conclusion.

If $b\in\overline\psi(U)$, retain the chosen pair, ordered tree ends, and
boundary identities; the first alternative of the theorem holds.  Now
suppose $b\notin\overline\psi(U)$.  Among all qualifying triples used to
define the selected presentation's secondary number, choose one of least
total length.  Pairwise support disjointness makes its three tree ends
distinct.  The linear order of their three unique positions in
\noderef{TreeSequence} lets us relabel the complete path-and-end packages
so that $b_1\prec b_2\prec b_3$ in
\noderef{TreeVertexOrder}.  Apply the explicit final-edge argument to
each path to obtain the retained exact $b$-colored boundary identities
$f_1,f_2,f_3$.  Relabeling preserves total walk length, all three support
disjointness statements, and all outside-end inequalities.  This gives
the stated three-leg alternative.

For its secondary universal clause, take the quantified comparison data
and again unfold \noderef{CorrespondingExtendedTashkinovTree}.  Its
replacement input, replacement ETT, rung agreement, certified series,
and generating-coloring data are genuine fields.  The assumed properness,
one-uncoloredness, maximum property, same-divider good hierarchy, large
bound, terminal certificate, and two exact exit legs provide every field
of an admissible presentation; the missing exact boundary identities,
tree-end distinction, and order follow from the same final-edge argument.
Moreover,
$L.\mathit{dividerCount}=d<q$, so `hfewer' applies and supplies the
elementariness used by the comparison class.  If the comparison color is
missing, this admissible presentation has secondary number $0$, smaller
than the selected positive number.  If it is present and the displayed
third leg makes a qualifying triple of total length smaller than
$|P_1|+|P_2|+|P_3|$, its least triple length is no larger than that
displayed length, again giving a smaller secondary number.  Either case
contradicts lexicographic minimality and proves the quantified `False'.

Finally use the selected certified input, $U,\psi,\mathcal U,B,K,\Gamma$,
the ordered pair (or the first two paths of the ordered minimal triple),
their exact edge identities, and the color $\theta$ from (15) as the
witnesses.  Admissibility supplies the same rung, maximum property,
$d<q$, properness, exact one-uncoloredness, (3), and (C); (4) supplies
elementariness; the two support lemmas and the final-edge argument supply
all exact exit-leg fields; and the last two paragraphs supply the two
universal minimality clauses.  Every field of the asserted witness package
has therefore been proved, including the formerly omitted $d=0$ cases.
\end{proof}

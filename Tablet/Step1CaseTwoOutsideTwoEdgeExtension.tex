\begin{lemma}[Step 1, Subcase 2.1: adjoining the two retained edges]
Let $\mu$ be a mod-coloring of a positive-rung maximum ETT $\mathcal E$,
and let
$C$ carry a corresponding ETT $\mathcal E_C$ with maximum property, an
actual hierarchy base, and a good hierarchy $K_C$.  Assume explicitly
that the terminal PE augmentation sequence to $C$, or the exact terminal
SE/RE seed followed by its augmentation sequence to $C$, is available.
Assume the replacement large missing-color estimate and suppose colors
$\gamma,\beta$ are both absent from the last indexed Gamma union of
$K_C$.

Let $b_1,b_2,b_3\in V(C)$, with $\beta$ missing at $b_3$.  Let $f_1,f_2$
be retained individual $\beta$-colored boundary-edge identities of $C$,
incident with $b_1,b_2$ respectively, and suppose their outside endpoints
are distinct.  Then there are exact tree-sequences
\[
                         J_1=C+f_1,\qquad J=J_1+f_2,
\]
a corresponding ETT $\mathcal E_J$ under $\mu$, an actual hierarchy
base, and a good hierarchy $K_J$ on $J$.  The replacement has maximum
property and the same divider count and large estimate as the $C$-level
package; $\gamma,\beta$ remain absent from its last indexed Gamma union.
Both endpoints of each exact identity $f_1,f_2$, and all three vertices
$b_1,b_2,b_3$, belong to $V(J)$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Expand \noderef{BoundaryColorEdges} and \noderef{BoundaryEdges}.  For
$i=1,2$, there are vertices $x_i\in V(C)$ and $u_i\notin V(C)$ such that
$\operatorname{ends}(f_i)=\{x_i,u_i\}$ and $\mu(f_i)=\beta$.  By
\noderef{Incident}, the assumption that $b_i$ is incident with $f_i$ says
that $b_i$ is one of these two ends.  Since $b_i\in V(C)$ whereas
$u_i\notin V(C)$, it follows that $b_i=x_i$.  Thus
$\operatorname{ends}(f_i)=\{b_i,u_i\}$.  The inside/outside memberships
give $b_i\ne u_i$; in particular the endpoint pair is nondegenerate, as
required by the looplessness axiom in \noderef{LooplessMultigraph}.  The
outside-endpoint hypothesis now gives
$u_1\ne u_2$ by taking these particular incidences and outside
certificates.

Put $m=\operatorname{length}(C)$.  Using the record in
\noderef{TreeSequence}, form $J_1$ with length $m+1$, with all vertex and
edge entries through $C$ unchanged, and with last vertex $u_1$ and last
edge $f_1$.  Form $J$ similarly from $J_1$ by adjoining last vertex $u_2$
and last edge $f_2$.  Vertex injectivity holds because $u_1\notin V(C)$,
$u_2\notin V(C)$, and $u_1\ne u_2$, where the vertex sets here are the
images specified by \noderef{TreeSequenceVertexSet}.  For edge
injectivity, each displayed edge of $C$ has both ends in $V(C)$ by the
attachment field of \noderef{TreeSequence}, whereas each $f_i$ has an
outside end.  Hence neither $f_i$ is an old displayed edge.  Moreover
$f_1\ne f_2$, since equality of the exact identities would give
$\{b_1,u_1\}=\{b_2,u_2\}$.  An inside vertex cannot equal an outside
vertex, so this equality forces $b_1=b_2$ and $u_1=u_2$, contrary to the
distinctness of the outside ends.  The
new attachment witnesses are respectively the old vertices $b_1,b_2$;
$b_2$ remains an old vertex at the second append.  Positivity, the first
edge, and all old attachment witnesses are inherited from $C$.  Thus
$J_1$ and $J$ satisfy every field of \noderef{TreeSequence}.

Their lengths, entrywise agreement, and last edge identities give the two
ordered initial-segment relations of
\noderef{TreeSequenceInitialSegment} and hence every field of
\noderef{TreeSequenceAdjoinEdge}:
\[
                         J_1=C+f_1,\qquad J=J_1+f_2.           \tag{1}
\]
In particular, transitivity of entrywise agreement gives $C\preccurlyeq
J$, and the displayed lengths give
$\operatorname{length}(J)=\operatorname{length}(C)+2$.

The color $\beta$ is missing at $b_3\in V(C)$, hence belongs to
\noderef{TreeMissingColors} of $C$.  Entrywise vertex transport in
\noderef{TreeSequenceInitialSegment}, followed by the image definition in
\noderef{TreeSequenceVertexSet}, puts the same vertex $b_3$ in $V(J_1)$;
so $\beta$ also belongs to the tree-missing set of $J_1$.  The two new
exact identities have color $\beta$.  Combining these facts with (1)
verifies every field of
\[
   \noderef{TashkinovAugmentation}(\mu,C,J_1),\qquad
   \noderef{TashkinovAugmentation}(\mu,J_1,J).                \tag{2}
\]
Append the two steps (2) to the stated $C$-level route using the
reflexive-transitive closure in
\noderef{TashkinovAugmentationSequence}.  In the PE case this gives a
route from the last ladder tree $T_n$ to $J$.  In the SE/RE case it gives
a route from the same exact seed $T_n+f_n$ to $J$, retaining both the
individual identity $f_n$ and the equality $F_n=\{f_n\}$.  Since the
replacement ETT on $C$ is an \noderef{ExtendedTashkinovTree}, its
above-penultimate and strict-above-penultimate fields give
$T_n\preccurlyeq C$ and
$\operatorname{length}(T_n)<\operatorname{length}(C)$.  Composing the
first relation with $C\preccurlyeq J$ using
\noderef{TreeSequenceInitialSegment}, and composing the strict length
inequality with $\operatorname{length}(C)<\operatorname{length}(J)$,
shows that $J$ strictly extends $T_n$.  These are exactly the eligibility
alternatives in \noderef{ModColoring}.

Apply the universal clause of the given mod-coloring to $J$.  It supplies
an actual \noderef{CorrespondingExtendedTashkinovTree} certificate
$\mathcal C_J$ under $\mu$.  The rung is the same as that of the original
ETT, and \noderef{MaximumPropertyTransport} transfers the original
maximum property
to its replacement ETT $\mathcal E_J$.

We next construct every field of a \noderef{HierarchyBase} $B_J$ for
$\mathcal E_J$.  The two corresponding certificates identify their last
ladder trees, raw stored sets, exact connecting-edge sets, and extension
kinds with the same data of the original ETT.  In particular their last
ladder tree is the same ordered tree $T_n$.  If the common terminal kind
is not PE, set the new core equal to the old core, hence to $T_n$ by the
nonparallel field of the $C$-level base, and copy its support position and
unused closure.  The support remains positive.  The terminal missing-set
compatibility is unchanged because both the base coloring and displayed
generating coloring are $\mu$ on the same exact $T_n$.

Suppose the terminal kind is PE.  Extract separately the terminal PE
constructors of $\mathcal E_C$ and $\mathcal E_J$.  Their connecting-edge
sets each agree, by their respective
\noderef{CorrespondingExtendedTashkinovTree} certificates, with the same
exact connecting-edge set of the original ETT.  The singleton equations
in \noderef{TashkinovSeriesStep} therefore identify the two PE edge
identities.  The endpoint equations in
\noderef{MaximumDefectiveChoice}, the inside assertions in
\noderef{DefectiveVertex}, and the outside-end assertions orient the
common unordered pair exactly as in the $C$-level construction.  Hence
the actual support vertex and its positive position agree.

In the PE case, copy the core and closure from the $C$-level base.  Its PE
field already says that this closure is a genuine
\noderef{TashkinovClosure} under the same coloring $\mu$, and its
\noderef{OrderedTreeUnion} witness is coloring-free.  Thus no new prefix
assertion about the closure is used: the copied closure certificate, the
support identity just proved, and the ordered union verify the PE field.
Together with the common terminal missing-set compatibility and the
positive copied support position, this finishes every field of $B_J$.

We now construct a \noderef{TashkinovHierarchy} $K_J$ field by field.  Put
$d=K_C.\operatorname{dividerCount}$ and give $K_J$ the same divider count.
For stage indices $r=0,\ldots,d$, set
$K_J.\operatorname{stage}(r)=K_C.\operatorname{stage}(r)$, and set only
the terminal stage $K_J.\operatorname{stage}(d+1)$ equal to $J$.  The
base-stage equation follows from the old equation and the equality of the
two terminal ladder trees $T_n$; the terminal-stage equation is immediate.

Consider a transition from stage $r$ to stage $r+1$.  If $r<d$, both
stages are unchanged, so copy the old nesting and strictness fields.  If
$r=d$, the old fields give
\[
 K_C.\operatorname{stage}(d)\preccurlyeq C,\qquad
 \operatorname{length}(K_C.\operatorname{stage}(d))
       <\operatorname{length}(C).
\]
Compose the first relation with $C\preccurlyeq J$ using the entrywise
definition in \noderef{TreeSequenceInitialSegment}.  Combine the second
inequality with
$\operatorname{length}(C)<\operatorname{length}(J)$ from (1).  This gives
the required nesting and strictness for the last transition.  Hence all
stage-chain fields hold, including when $d=0$.

For every old divider position
$p=K_C.\operatorname{dividerPosition}(i)\in
\operatorname{Fin}(\operatorname{length}(C)+1)$, define
\[
 \widehat p\in\operatorname{Fin}(\operatorname{length}(J)+1),
 \qquad \widehat p.\operatorname{val}=p.\operatorname{val}.
\]
The new bound follows from $p.\operatorname{val}<
\operatorname{length}(C)+1$ and the length inequality supplied by
$C\preccurlyeq J$.  Thus the divider-position field is well typed.
Positivity and strict increasingness copy verbatim because natural values
were not changed.

It remains to check the divider-stage equations.  Fix $i<d$ and put
$p=K_C.\operatorname{dividerPosition}(i)$.  By
\noderef{TreeSequencePrefix}, both
$\operatorname{TreeSequencePrefix}(J,p)$ and
$\operatorname{TreeSequencePrefix}(C,p)$ have length $p$.  For every
vertex index at most $p$ their vertex entries agree because
$C\preccurlyeq J$, and for every edge index below $p$ their edge entries
agree for the same reason.  Their inherited positivity, injectivity, first
edge, and attachment fields are propositions, so proof irrelevance and
these entrywise equalities give
\[
 \operatorname{TreeSequencePrefix}(J,p)
   =\operatorname{TreeSequencePrefix}(C,p).
\]
The stage $i+1$ was left unchanged.  Substituting the old
$K_C.\operatorname{dividerStage}(i)$ equation therefore proves the new
divider-stage equation.

Finally check the core-inside-first field.  If $d>0$, stage $1$ is among
the unchanged stages $0,\ldots,d$, and $B_J.\operatorname{core}$ is the
copied $B_C.\operatorname{core}$, so the old field applies directly.  If
$d=0$, the new first stage is the terminal stage $J$.  The old
core-inside-first field gives
$B_C.\operatorname{core}\preccurlyeq C$; composing it with
$C\preccurlyeq J$ gives
$B_J.\operatorname{core}\preccurlyeq J$.  Thus every hierarchy field has
been proved before any good-hierarchy data are transported.

Only now define the Gamma family of $K_J$ to equal that of $K_C$.  By
\noderef{HierarchyStarredStage}, for each good-hierarchy index
$0\leq j\leq d$, the starred stage is unchanged: at $j=0$ it is the copied
base core, and at $j>0$ it is one of the copied stages through index $d$.
This same zero-versus-positive case split supports below the equality of
the indexed stored sets, the unique-refresh predicates, and the
starred-stage and segment sources used in the avoidance argument.  The
stage-missing set is also unchanged: at
$j=0$ it uses the common last ladder tree $T_n$, and at $j>0$ it uses the
unchanged stage $j$.  The raw stored sets and coloring are common by the
corresponding certificates.  Expanding
\noderef{HierarchyStoredColorsAt} therefore identifies every indexed
stored set.  Consequently the two-color cardinalities and pairwise
disjointness copy.  In every unique-refresh clause, the stage and starred
stage involved have index at most $d$, so their vertex sets agree by
\noderef{TreeSequenceVertexSet}; the unique witness and all unchanged-pair
equalities copy.  The parallel-base clause uses only the common terminal
kind and ladder tree and the copied base core, closure, stored set, and
Gamma pair, so it copies.  Each internal minus-closed clause also concerns
only a stage through $d$ and therefore copies from the given
\noderef{GoodHierarchy} on $K_C$.

It remains only to verify avoidance.  Fix $j<d$.  Its starred and next
stages are unchanged.  If an indexed color $\eta$ is missing in $C$, its
first missing position is unchanged in $J$.  If it is not missing in $C$,
its old totalized position is $\operatorname{length}(C)$ and its new
position is larger, while strictness of the old hierarchy gives
$\operatorname{length}(K_C.\operatorname{stage}(j+1))<
\operatorname{length}(C)$.  In both cases, taking the smaller of the
first-missing position and the unchanged next-stage length selects exactly
the same displayed edge indices.  Thus the segment-color set at $j<d$ is
unchanged and the old avoidance clause applies.

Now let $j=d$ and fix an indexed stored color $\eta$.  Expand the totalized definition
\noderef{HierarchyFirstMissingPosition}.  If $\eta$ is missing at some
vertex of $C$, the least such index is the same in $J$: all vertices of
$C$ occur first and entrywise in $J$, and their missing sets, as defined
by \noderef{MissingColors}, depend only on $G$ and $\mu$.  Thus the old
least witness remains least.  If $\eta$ is
missing at no vertex of $C$, its old totalized position is the last vertex
of $C$, while its new first-missing position is either one of the two new
vertex positions or, if neither is missing $\eta$, the last vertex of
$J$; in every case the new position is strictly beyond the old last
vertex.  By \noderef{HierarchySegmentColors}, the enlarged last segment
therefore contains the old segment and possibly only the two newly
displayed edges $f_1,f_2$.  Both have color $\beta$.  Since $\beta$ is
absent from the last indexed Gamma union, it is absent from the particular
pair $\Gamma^d_\eta$.  The old avoidance clause excludes all old segment
colors, and this exclusion of $\beta$ excludes both possible new edge
colors.  The source stage-missing set at index $d$ was already shown
unchanged.  Hence the avoidance clause holds at $d$ as well.  All fields
of \noderef{GoodHierarchy} are now verified, giving the required
$\mathcal G_J$.

The indexed stored sets and Gamma pairs were copied, so the given
exclusions of $\gamma$ and $\beta$ transfer directly.  Both
$\mathcal E_C$ and $\mathcal E_J$ correspond to the same original ETT;
their rung numbers and exact last ladder trees agree, and their displayed
generating coloring is the same $\mu$.  The large estimate for
$\mathcal E_C$ is consequently the required estimate for
$\mathcal E_J$.

Finally, (1) and \noderef{TreeSequenceInitialSegment} transport every
vertex of $C$ into $J$, and the construction adds $u_1,u_2$.  If a vertex
$v$ is incident with $f_i$, then \noderef{Incident} and
$\operatorname{ends}(f_i)=\{b_i,u_i\}$ give $v=b_i$ or $v=u_i$; both are
in $V(J)$ by \noderef{TreeSequenceVertexSet}.  Thus every endpoint of each
exact identity $f_i$ is in $V(J)$.  The same prefix transport puts
$b_1,b_2,b_3$ in $V(J)$.  This proves all asserted conclusions.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Situation 7.1: advance a stored common color]
Fix a replacement presentation \(P\) and
\(1\leq i<\ell-1\).  Suppose
\(\alpha\in D_{n,q}\) is missing under its coloring at both exact vertices
\(y_i\) and \(y_\ell\).  Under
\(\noderef{HierarchyPathNumber}=1\) and the explicit palette-surplus
hypothesis, one has
\[
 i+1\in\noderef{HierarchyEligibleCommonMissingIndices}.
\]
The conclusion exposes the replacement presentation obtained by the
paper's two consecutive Kempe changes.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Write \(\mu\) for \(P.\mathit{coloring}\), \(H\) for its hierarchy, and
\(q\) for the last stored index.  Put
\[
 A=\noderef{HierarchyStarredStage}(H,q),\quad
 \ell=\noderef{HierarchyFullTailLength}(C),\quad
 B=T(y_{\ell-1}),\quad X=T(y_{i+1}),\quad X^-=T(y_i),
\]
where the last three trees are the corresponding
\noderef{HierarchyFullTailPrefix}es.  Unpacking
\(P.\mathit{sameGood}\) in
\noderef{HierarchyReplacementPresentation} and
\noderef{SameGoodHierarchy} identifies the
original and replacement base cores, divider counts, stages, and exact
last starred stage.  Hence it also identifies their full-tail lengths and
all these exact prefixes.  The inequalities on \(i\) give
\[
 A\prec X^-\prec X\preccurlyeq B\prec T,
 \qquad |X|=|X^-|+1.                                      \tag{1}
\]
All relations in (1) are entrywise
\noderef{TreeSequenceInitialSegment}s, so they retain the individual
displayed vertices and edges.  The same stage identities transport the
given path-number-one condition to \(H\); thus every edge after the first
tail edge has the consecutive endpoints prescribed by
\noderef{HierarchyPathNumber}.

Apply \noderef{HierarchyCounterexamplePenultimateElementary} to
\(P.\mathit{minimumContext}\).  This gives
\noderef{ColoredVertexSetElementary} for
\(\noderef{TreeSequenceVertexSet}(B)\) under \(\mu\).  The same context
also supplies \noderef{IsProperEdgeColoring} and
\noderef{IsOneEdgeUncolored} for \(e_0\).  Since
\(\alpha\in D_{n,q}=\noderef{HierarchyStoredColorsAt}(H,q)\),
the definition of that set says \(\alpha\) is not missing on \(A\).
Elementarity and the given missing witness at \(y_i\) consequently show
that \(y_i\) is the first displayed \(\alpha\)-missing vertex in the
sense of \noderef{HierarchyFirstMissingPosition}.

The two-color and disjoint-pairs fields of \noderef{GoodHierarchy} give
two distinct colors
\(H.\mathit{good}.\Gamma^q_\alpha=\{\gamma_1,\gamma_2\}\).
The exact edge \(e_{i+1}=T.\mathit{edge}(|X^-|)\) has at most one color,
so relabel the pair so that
\[
                         \mu(e_{i+1})\ne\gamma_1.             \tag{2}
\]
Avoidance, whose forbidden interval is defined by
\noderef{HierarchySegmentColors}, puts \(\gamma_1\) in
\noderef{HierarchyStageMissingColors}\((H,q)\) and excludes it from every
exact segment between \(A\) and the first \(\alpha\)-missing position;
(2) extends that exclusion through \(X\).  Apply
\noderef{HierarchyTailMissingColorExclusion} to the \(\alpha\)-missing
vertex \(y_i\).  It excludes \(\alpha\) from the same displayed part
through \(X^-\), except possibly the exact first edge when \(q=0\),
\(A=T_n\), and that edge is the certified SE/RE connecting seed carrying
\(\alpha\in D_n=\noderef{TashkinovStoredColors}(\mathcal E,n)\).
The one remaining edge of \(X-X^-\) is \(e_{i+1}\); path number one makes
it incident with \(y_i\), so the definition of
\noderef{MissingColors} forbids it from having color \(\alpha\).  Thus
\[
 \gamma_1\notin\mu\langle X-A\rangle,
 \quad
 \alpha\notin\mu\langle X-A\rangle
 \text{ apart from that named seed}.                         \tag{3}
\]

Let \(v_\gamma\) be the first displayed vertex missing \(\gamma_1\).
It lies in the last ordinary stage, hence in \(A\).  The order
\(|v_\gamma|<|y_i|\), the two missing witnesses, (3), and the appropriate
positive- or zero-divider alternative are exactly the applicable
hypotheses of \noderef{ChangeLemma}.  It follows that
\[
 \noderef{TwoColorComponent}(G,\mu,\gamma_1,\alpha,
          \operatorname{inl}v_\gamma)
 =
 \noderef{TwoColorComponent}(G,\mu,\gamma_1,\alpha,
          \operatorname{inl}y_i).                            \tag{4}
\]
Let \(Q\) be the component through \(y_\ell\).  The three vertices
\(v_\gamma,y_i,y_\ell\) are distinct by (1), by the fact that
\(\alpha\) is not missing on \(A\), and by vertex injectivity in
\noderef{TreeSequence}.  Each has degree at most one in the affected
incidence graph, by \noderef{MissingColorIncidenceEndpoint}; reverse the
color order for the vertices missing \(\alpha\) and use the literal
symmetry of \noderef{TwoColorIncidenceGraph}.  If \(Q\) equalled (4), it
would contain three distinct degree-at-most-one nodes.  But
\noderef{TwoColorComponentSpanningPath} spans a proper-coloring component
by a simple path, while \noderef{TwoColorIncidenceDegreeAtMostTwo} bounds
the full degree by two, so only the two path ends can have degree at most
one.  Hence the components differ, and
\noderef{TwoColorComponentsDisjoint} gives their disjointness.  The same
endpoint lemma shows that \(Q\) is a possibly singleton
\noderef{TwoColorPathComponent}.

Define
\[
 \nu=\noderef{TwoColorComponentSwap}
       (G,\mu,\gamma_1,\alpha,\operatorname{inl}y_\ell).      \tag{5}
\]
The properness theorem \noderef{TwoColorComponentSwapProper} and the fact
that the swap fixes \(\mathsf{none}\) show that \(\nu\) is proper and
still leaves exactly \(e_0\) uncolored.  Apply
\noderef{StableChangeCasesOneTwo} with ordered colors
\((\gamma_1,\alpha)\), the two witnesses in \(B\), component data (4)--(5),
and the stage-missing Cases 1/2 alternative for \(\gamma_1\).  It gives
\[
 \noderef{HierarchyWeaklyStable}(H,q,\nu)
 \quad\text{and}\quad \noderef{ModColoring}(P.\mathit{corresponding}.
 \mathit{replacementETT},\nu).                              \tag{6}
\]

We now verify the split-tail inputs, without asserting equality of the
whole displayed tail.  First,
\[
 \nu(T.\mathit{edge}(r))=\mu(T.\mathit{edge}(r))
       \quad(|A|\le r<|X|).                                  \tag{7}
\]
For an affected edge, (3) leaves only the named zero-divider seed.  Its
actual \noderef{TashkinovSeriesStep} is SE or RE.  In SE,
\noderef{MaximumDefectiveChoice} makes that exact edge incident with its
selected vertex of \(T_n\); in RE,
\noderef{RevisitingExtensionCertificate} makes it incident with its
inside endpoint.  Thus it is a \noderef{TreeIncidentEdge} of \(T_n\),
and the protected incident-edge clause of the
\noderef{StableColoring} furnished by (6) fixes its value.  Every
unaffected value is fixed directly by (5), proving (7).

Second, for every \(v\in V(B)\),
\[
              \noderef{MissingColors}(G,\nu,v)
              =\noderef{MissingColors}(G,\mu,v).              \tag{8}
\]
If \(\operatorname{inl}v\notin Q\), no incident edge-node lies in \(Q\),
so no incident value changes.  If \(\operatorname{inl}v\in Q\),
disjointness from (4) makes \(v\ne v_\gamma,y_i\).  Elementarity of
\(B\), applied separately to the missing witnesses at those two distinct
vertices, says that both \(\gamma_1\) and \(\alpha\) are present at
\(v\).  Expanding \noderef{PresentColors}, choose their incident edge
identities; properness makes each identity unique.  Both edge-nodes are
in \(Q\), so (5) transposes the two values and keeps both colors present.
All other colors are fixed.  This proves (8), including internal vertices
of \(Q\).

We extract the old route from the actual last certificate in the
\noderef{TashkinovSeries}.  The PE constructor of
\noderef{TashkinovSeriesStep} supplies a
\noderef{TashkinovClosure}, hence a
\(\mu\)-\noderef{TashkinovAugmentationSequence} from \(T_n\); the SE/RE
constructors supply the exact singleton connecting edge, exact
\noderef{TreeSequenceAdjoinEdge} seed \(T_n+f_n\), and the route from
that seed.  The terminal exact-prefix field of
\noderef{ExtendedTashkinovTree} and two applications of
\noderef{TashkinovAugmentationSequenceRestrict} restrict this route first
to \(T\) and then to \(X\).  The required source inclusions are exact:
in PE, \(T_n\preccurlyeq A\preccurlyeq X\); in SE/RE with \(q=0\), the
seed is the first tail edge and \(i\ge1\) puts it in \(X\); and with
\(q>0\), the strict first hierarchy stage contains that one-edge seed and
precedes \(A\).  Since every
\noderef{TashkinovAugmentation} increases length by one, induction on the
same finite chain locates the unique member of length \(|X|\), identifies
it with \(X\) by exact-prefix antisymmetry, and factors off an old-color
suffix
\[
                         X\longrightarrow T.                 \tag{9}
\]

Let \(S\) be \(T_n\) in PE and the exact seed in SE/RE.  We apply
\noderef{TashkinovAugmentationSequenceColoringTransport} to the restricted
old route \(S\to X\), with exact penultimate prefix \(X^-\).  Its
missing-set premise follows from (8) and \(X^-\preccurlyeq B\).  For its
edge premise, route-added edges at or beyond \(A\) are fixed by (7).  If
\(q>0\), the \noderef{TreeColoringInvariant} clause of (6) fixes all
earlier displayed edges in \(A\).  If \(q=0\) and the kind is PE, (6)
unfolds to \noderef{CombinedStableColoring};
\noderef{CombinedStableClosurePreserved} fixes the displayed edges of
\(T_n\) and its PE closure, and the ordered-union description of \(A\)
therefore fixes every earlier route-added edge.  If \(q=0\) and the kind
is SE/RE, then \(A=T_n\) and \(S\) is already the one-edge seed, so there
is no earlier route-added edge.  The transport lemma now gives the exact
new-color route
\[
 \begin{cases}T_n\longrightarrow X,&\text{PE},\\
 T_n+f_n\longrightarrow X,&\text{SE/RE},\end{cases}
 \quad\text{under }\nu,                                     \tag{10}
\]
retaining the singleton connecting-edge equality and seed identity.

Both affected colors remain in \noderef{TreeMissingColors} of \(X\):
their old witnesses
\(v_\gamma,y_i\) lie there, and their common component (4) is disjoint
from the changed component.  This also follows pointwise from (8).  For
stored first positions, \(\gamma_1\) is not stored because it is missing
on the last ordinary stage contained in \(A\); \(\alpha\)'s first
position is \(y_i\) and is preserved by (8); all other stored colors are
unaffected.  Finally \(\alpha\) belongs to no Gamma pair because it is
not missing on \(A\), whereas every Gamma color is stage-missing.  If
\(\gamma_1\in\Gamma^q_\eta\), then \(\eta=\alpha\) by pairwise
disjointness, and the first \(\alpha\)-missing position is
\(|X^-|\le|X|\).  These facts give respectively the stored-position and
affected-Gamma cutoff premises.

Apply \noderef{StableChangeCasesOneTwoSplitTail} to the current ETT,
base, counterexample, and minimum context, with split \(X\).  Equations
(1), (4)--(10), the two split missing witnesses, and the preceding
stored-position and cutoff checks supply every hypothesis.  Denote the
returned correspondence relative to the current ETT by \(\mathcal C_1\),
and retain its maximum property, rebuilt base, hierarchy, good hierarchy,
and same-good data.  The swap makes \(\gamma_1\) missing at \(y_\ell\):
if it was already missing this is immediate, and otherwise its unique
incident \(\gamma_1\)-edge belongs to \(Q\) and changes to \(\alpha\).
It remains missing at \(v_\gamma\) by (8).  These distinct witnesses make
the returned terminal tree non-elementary, so the returned hierarchy data
form a new \noderef{HierarchyCounterexample}.

We spell out how this output becomes a replacement presentation relative
to the original fixed ETT.  Let \(\mathcal C_0=P.\mathit{corresponding}\).
Construct a \noderef{CorrespondingExtendedTashkinovTree} \(\mathcal C_{10}\)
by taking the replacement input, final series, and ETT from
\(\mathcal C_1\), casting its round parameter along
\(\mathcal C_0.\mathit{rungAgreement}\).  Compose the two rung
equalities.  At every positive ladder index, compose
\(\mathcal C_1.\mathit{ladderTreesAgree}\), the transported
\(\mathcal C_0.\mathit{seriesPresentation}\), and
\(\mathcal C_0.\mathit{ladderTreesAgree}\).  At every round-plus-one
index do the same for stored sets, connecting-edge sets, and extension
kinds.  Take final coloring, properness, and one-uncoloredness from
\(\mathcal C_1\) after these casts.

For replacement stability, compose the original-to-current and
current-to-new \noderef{StableColoring} relations at each positive ladder
index.  Their tree and protected sets agree by the ladder/raw-set
equalities and the definition of \noderef{TashkinovStoredColors}; the
properness and one-uncoloredness needed by the transitivity theorem
\noderef{sc2} come from the two correspondence certificates and from the
appropriate positive \noderef{TashkinovSeriesStep} constructor.  This
completes \(\mathcal C_{10}\).  Its fields transport the lower-rung and
large-missing-set parts of the minimum context.  The returned same-good
relation, composed field by field with \(P.\mathit{sameGood}\), transports
divider counts, stages, stored sets, Gamma pairs, the fewer-divider
function, and \noderef{HierarchyCounterexampleMeasure}; hence it transports
lexicographic minimality.  Together with the new properness and
one-uncoloredness fields, this reconstructs every field of
\noderef{HierarchyMinimumCounterexampleContext}.  Thus these data form a
genuine original-relative replacement presentation \(P_1\).

We perform the second paper swap with the same exact split.  By the
palette-surplus hypothesis applied to \(P_1\)'s proper exactly-one-uncolored
coloring \(\nu\), choose
\[
                    \theta\in\noderef{MissingColors}(G,\nu,y_{i+1}). \tag{11}
\]
The transported elementarity of \(B\) and the
\(\gamma_1\)-missing witness in its earlier stage imply
\(\theta\ne\gamma_1\), show that \(y_{i+1}\) is the first
\(\theta\)-missing vertex, and exclude \(\theta\) from every last Gamma
pair.  Apply \noderef{HierarchyTailMissingColorExclusion} at \(y_{i+1}\):
\(\theta\) occurs on no exact edge of \(X-A\), except possibly the same
certified zero-divider seed, which is protected if it carries \(\theta\).
The old avoidance equation for \(\gamma_1\) is unchanged under the first
swap through \(X\), by (7).

Apply \noderef{ChangeLemma} under \(\nu\) to
\((\gamma_1,\theta)\).  It identifies the component through the earlier
stage witness with the component through \(y_{i+1}\).  The component
through \(y_\ell\), where \(\gamma_1\) is missing, is disjoint: otherwise
the three distinct missing endpoints in the elementary prefix and at the
terminal vertex would again contradict the spanning-path and degree-two
lemmas used above.  Swap that terminal component and call the coloring
\(\rho\).  Apply \noderef{StableChangeCasesOneTwo} first, with
\(\gamma_1\) as the stage-missing color.

For this second swap the preceding arguments give, one by one: fixed
edge values only for \(|A|\le r<|X|\), using Gamma avoidance,
\noderef{HierarchyTailMissingColorExclusion}, and the protected exact
seed; equality of missing sets on every vertex of \(B\), by treating
vertices inside and outside the selected component; an old \(\nu\)-route
to \(X\) and suffix \(X\to T\), by restricting and factoring the actual
terminal certificate; and a \(\rho\)-route to \(X\), by applying
\noderef{TashkinovAugmentationSequenceColoringTransport} with penultimate
prefix \(X^-\).  Both \(\gamma_1\) and \(\theta\) remain missing on
\(X\).  The only possibly affected stored first position is that of
\(\theta\), and its exact first witness \(y_{i+1}\) is preserved by the
vertexwise equality.  The Gamma cutoff holds because \(\theta\) belongs
to no Gamma pair and \(\gamma_1\in\Gamma^q_\alpha\) has the earlier
\(\alpha\)-missing position \(y_i\).

A second application of
\noderef{StableChangeCasesOneTwoSplitTail} therefore constructs the full
tree under \(\rho\), rather than pretending that edges after \(X\) were
fixed.  The swap makes \(\theta\) missing at \(y_\ell\) and preserves its
missingness at \(y_{i+1}\), so these two distinct vertices certify the
new counterexample.  Apply the field-by-field correspondence composition,
same-good composition, measure transport, and minimum-context
reconstruction just established, now to \(P_1\) and the second split-tail
output.  Composing once more with \(P_1\)'s original-relative certificate
produces a genuine replacement presentation \(P_2\) relative to the
original ETT.

Finally \(1\le i+1\le\ell-1\) by the hypotheses, and under \(P_2\) the
color \(\theta\) is missing at both \(y_{i+1}\) and \(y_\ell\).
Consequently \(i+1,P_2,\theta\) satisfy the defining filter in
\noderef{HierarchyEligibleCommonMissingIndices}, proving the assertion.
\end{proof}

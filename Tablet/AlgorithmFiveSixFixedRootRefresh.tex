\begin{theorem}[One fixed-root Algorithm 5.6 refresh]
Fix a finite loopless multigraph $G$ with individual edge type $E$, a
palette $[k]$, and a distinguished edge $e_0$.  Let $\mathcal E$ be a
positive-rung ETT of rung $n$, generating coloring $\varphi$, maximum
property, terminal ladder tree $T_n$, and hierarchy base $B$.  Assume
$\Delta(G)+1\leq k$, $11\leq t(G)$, and a
\noderef{TechnicalInductionContext} at bound $n$.  Assume also that, for
every arbitrary-input maximum ETT $\mathcal L$ of rung $n$ carrying a
base and a good hierarchy, its terminal ladder tree $L_n$ satisfies
\[
                 2n+11\leq |\overline\psi(L_n)|,
\]
where $\psi$ is the generating coloring of $\mathcal L$.

Let $U$ carry a corresponding ETT certificate $C_U$ relative to the
fixed original $\mathcal E$ and the same coloring $\varphi$, maximum
property for $C_U.\mathit{replacementETT}$, and a base $B_U$ whose core,
closure, and numerical support position equal those of $B$.  Let $H$ be
a hierarchy on $U$ over $B_U$, with $d$ dividers and good witness
$\mathcal G$.  Put
\[
 q=\operatorname{last}(d),\qquad
 A=H.\mathit{starred}(q),\qquad
 D^+=\noderef{HierarchyTerminalStoredColors}(H),\qquad
 \Gamma^- =\bigcup_{\theta\in D^+}\mathcal G.\Gamma^q_\theta.
\]
Assume that $U$ is $(\Gamma^-)^-$-closed but is not fully closed under
$\varphi$.

Finally retain an exact anchor tree $J$ and an augmentation sequence
$J\longrightarrow U$.  The anchor data are one of the following two
alternatives.
\begin{enumerate}
\item The terminal kind of $\mathcal E$ is PE, $J=B.\mathit{core}$,
and there is an exact $\varphi$-augmentation sequence
$T_n\longrightarrow J$; in this alternative
$|B.\mathit{core}|<|U|$.
\item The terminal kind is SE or RE, and there is an individual edge
$f_n$ such that the original connecting-edge set is exactly $\{f_n\}$
and $J=T_n+f_n$ as an exact one-edge tree-sequence; in these alternatives
$|T_n|<|U|$.
\end{enumerate}

Then there exist colors
$\alpha,\beta,\eta,\gamma_1,\gamma_2\in[k]$, an exact edge $f$, and a
tree-sequence $W$ with all of the following properties.
\begin{enumerate}
\item Both $V(A)$ and $V(U)$ are elementary under $\varphi$,
$|A|<|U|$, and both vertex sets have odd cardinality.  Moreover
$\alpha\in\overline\varphi(U)-\Gamma^-$ and $U$ is
$\alpha$-closed.
\item The color $\beta$ is missing on $U$, belongs to $\Gamma^-$, and
has a nonempty exact colored boundary.  The color $\eta$ belongs to
$D^+$, $\beta\in\mathcal G.\Gamma^q_\eta$, and $\eta$ is the unique
member of $D^+$ whose old pair contains $\beta$.  The chosen edge $f$
belongs to that exact $\beta$-boundary and satisfies
$\varphi(f)=\beta$.
\item The tree $W$ is the exact one-edge extension $U+f$, and
$U\longrightarrow W$ is a Tashkinov augmentation under $\varphi$.
The colors $\gamma_1\ne\gamma_2$ are each missing at a vertex of
$V(U)-V(A)$, the pair $\{\gamma_1,\gamma_2\}$ is disjoint from
$\Gamma^-$, and in particular it does not contain $\beta$.
\item The tree $W$ carries a corresponding certificate $C_W$ relative
to the fixed original $\mathcal E$, maximum property, and a base $B_W$
whose core, closure, and numerical support position equal those of $B$.
There is a hierarchy $H_W$ on $W$ with $d+1$ dividers and a good witness
$\mathcal G_W$.  Under the canonical inclusions of finite indices,
all stages $0,\ldots,d+1$, all old divider positions, all indexed stored
sets $0,\ldots,d$, and every old Gamma pair agree exactly with those of
$H$ and $\mathcal G$.  The new preceding stage and starred stage are
$U$, the new terminal stage is $W$, and the new divider position is
$|U|$.  At the new last indexed level the stored set is exactly $D^+$,
\[
 \mathcal G_W.\Gamma^{d+1}_\eta=\{\gamma_1,\gamma_2\},\qquad
 \mathcal G_W.\Gamma^{d+1}_\theta=
     \mathcal G.\Gamma^q_\theta
       \quad(\theta\in D^+,\ \theta\ne\eta).
\]
There is an exact augmentation sequence $J\longrightarrow W$.
\item There are a terminal tree $V$, a corresponding certificate $C_V$
relative to the same fixed original $\mathcal E$, maximum property, and
a base $B_V$ whose core, closure, and numerical support position equal
those of $B$, such that $|U|<|V|$ and $J\longrightarrow V$ is an exact
augmentation sequence.  The tree $V$ carries a hierarchy $K$ with
$d+1$ dividers and a good witness $\mathcal G_V$.  Its old stages,
divider positions, indexed stored sets, and Gamma pairs agree with those
of $H$ exactly as for $H_W$; its new preceding and starred stage are
$U$, its terminal stage is $V$, its new divider position is $|U|$, its
new indexed stored set is $D^+$, and its last Gamma family has exactly
the displayed replacement at $\eta$.  Finally
\[
 V\text{ is }
 \left(\bigcup_{\theta\in
   \noderef{HierarchyTerminalStoredColors}(K)}
   \mathcal G_V.\Gamma^{d+1}_\theta\right)^-\text{-closed under }\varphi.
\]
\end{enumerate}
Every equality of stages above is equality of complete ordered
edge-valued tree-sequences.  In particular, $D^+$ is the old terminal
stored set, not the old last indexed stored set.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
We first recover the fixed-original mod-coloring.  The lower six fields of
the assumed \noderef{TechnicalInductionContext}, conjoined in their
listed order, have exactly the arbitrary-input scope and the strict
lower-rung bounds required below.  Its four current fields similarly give
assertions (iii)--(vi) for every arbitrary-input maximum ETT of rung $n$.
Because $n>0$, the terminal certificate of the original
\noderef{TashkinovSeries} is one of the positive SE, PE, or RE constructors
of \noderef{TashkinovSeriesStep}.  In each case its current coloring is
proper and leaves exactly $e_0$ uncolored.  The generating-color equation
in \noderef{ExtendedTashkinovTree} identifies that coloring with
$\varphi$.  Reflexivity in \noderef{sc2} makes $\varphi$ stable relative
to itself on $T_n$ with the terminal stored set.  Applying the
`currentSix' field to the original ETT, its maximum certificate, and these
three facts gives
\[
                         \varphi\text{ is a }
             \noderef{ModColoring}\text{ of }\mathcal E.       \tag{1}
\]
No mod-coloring is being assumed independently.

We next prove elementarity of the current terminal tree.  Apply
\noderef{GoodHierarchyElementaryLarge} to
$C_U.\mathit{replacementETT}$, its maximum certificate, $B_U,H$, and
$\mathcal G$.  The rung agreement in
\noderef{CorrespondingExtendedTashkinovTree} identifies its rung with
$n$.  Thus a strict lower-rung inequality for this replacement is a
strict inequality below $n$, so the six lower projections of the context
assemble the required lower conjunction.  The same rung agreement lets
the four current projections assemble the current conjunction.  The
candidate-uniform missing-color hypothesis applies to this very
replacement ETT and this actual good hierarchy, rather than importing a
bound from the original presentation.  Together with $11\leq t(G)$ these
are all hypotheses of the cited theorem, and hence
\[
                         V(U)\text{ is elementary}.             \tag{2}
\]

We prove separately that the preceding starred stage $A$ is elementary.
First suppose $d=0$.  By the definition of
\noderef{HierarchyStarredStage}, $A=B_U.\mathit{core}$.

If the terminal kind is PE, use the six lower context projections in
\noderef{ParallelClosureUnionElementary}, with the original ETT,
original maximum property, terminal PE equation, coloring $\varphi$, and
base $B$.  Properness and unique uncoloredness are the terminal facts
used above, and the required stability is again reflexivity from
\noderef{sc2}.  The conclusion makes $B.\mathit{core}$ elementary.
The assumed equality of the two cores therefore gives elementarity of
$A$.

Suppose instead that the terminal kind is SE or RE.  Correspondence
preserves the terminal kind and the exact terminal ladder tree.  The
nonparallel clause of \noderef{HierarchyBase} consequently identifies
$B_U.\mathit{core}$, and hence $A$, with that exact $T_n$.
If $n=1$, the first conclusion of \noderef{TechnicalTheoremBase} makes
$T_n$ elementary under the slot-zero coloring.  If $n>1$, apply
\noderef{CertifiedETTPredecessor} to the original ETT.  It returns an
actual positive-rung maximum ETT of rung $n-1$, with terminal displayed
tree exactly $T_n$ and generating coloring the slot-$(n-1)$ coloring;
the `lowerOne' field of the context makes this $T_n$ elementary.  In the
SE constructor the selected terminal coloring is stable on $T_n$
relative to the preceding coloring, and in the RE constructor the two
colorings are equal.  In the $n=1$ and $n>1$ cases alike, the vertex
clause of \noderef{StableColoring} identifies every missing-color set on
$T_n$ before and after the terminal step.  Elementarity therefore
transports to $\varphi$, proving it for $A$ without ever introducing a
zero-rung ETT.

It remains to handle $d>0$.  Take the positive last indexed level of
$H$.  The theorem \noderef{GoodHierarchyPrefixRepackage} returns a
same-rung maximum ETT whose terminal displayed tree is exactly $A$, with
a transported base, a truncated hierarchy, and a good witness.  Apply
\noderef{GoodHierarchyElementaryLarge} to that package.  As in the proof
of (2), rung equality reconstructs its lower and current conjunctions
from the context, and the universal missing bound applies to this actual
prefix presentation and good hierarchy.  Hence $V(A)$ is elementary in
the positive-divider case as well.  We have proved
\[
                         V(A)\text{ is elementary}              \tag{3}
\]
in all three required cases.

We also record that $A$ is a proper ordered segment of $U$.  When $d>0$,
this is the last strict nesting assertion in
\noderef{TashkinovHierarchy}.  When $d=0$ and the kind is PE, it is the
assumed strict inequality $|B.\mathit{core}|<|U|$ transported through
$B_U.\mathit{core}=B.\mathit{core}$.  When $d=0$ and the kind is SE or
RE, $A=T_n$ as above and the second retained strict inequality applies.
In either zero-divider case, `coreInsideFirst' from
\noderef{TashkinovHierarchy}, followed by its terminal-stage equation,
supplies the exact ordered initial-segment relation; in the
positive-divider case that relation is the last `nested' field.
Thus
\[
                    A\preccurlyeq U,\qquad |A|<|U|.             \tag{4}
\]

We now obtain the closed missing colors needed for parity.  By
\noderef{TashkinovStoredColorsCardinality}, the raw terminal stored set
has at most $n$ members; $D^+$ is a subset of it by the definition of
\noderef{HierarchyTerminalStoredColors}.  The terminal set $D^+$ is also
a subset of the old last indexed set: (4) makes every color missing on
$A$ missing on $U$, so subtracting the larger missing set can only remove
more raw stored colors.  For every such owner, the `twoColors' field of
\noderef{GoodHierarchy} says that its old Gamma set has cardinality two.
The elementary finite-union estimate therefore gives
\[
                            |\Gamma^-|\leq2n.                  \tag{5}
\]
The candidate-uniform bound applied to $C_U,H,\mathcal G$ gives at least
$2n+11$ colors missing on its terminal ladder tree.  That ladder tree is
an exact initial segment of $U$ by
\noderef{ExtendedTashkinovTree}, and the union definition
\noderef{TreeMissingColors} carries each missing witness into $U$.
Together with (5), this leaves a color
\[
                         \alpha\in\overline\varphi(U)-\Gamma^-.
                                                                    \tag{6}
\]
The assumed $(\Gamma^-)^-$-closedness is exactly
\noderef{TreeColorSetMinusClosed}; applying it to (6) says that $U$ is
$\alpha$-closed.

For $A$ we make the analogous choice in the case appropriate to its
construction.  If $d>0$, use the last internal `minusClosed' field of
\noderef{GoodHierarchy}.  The same stored-set bound, the same two-color
union estimate, and the terminal-ladder missing colors contained in $A$
leave a missing color outside that preceding Gamma union; the
`minusClosed' field makes it closed on $A$.  If $d=0$ and the kind is PE,
the `parallelBase' field gives at least four colors in
\[
 (\overline\varphi(T_n)\cap
   \overline\varphi(B_U.\mathit{closure}))-
   \bigcup_{\theta\in D_{n,0}}\mathcal G.\Gamma^0_\theta.
\]
To see that these colors are closed on $A$, first obtain elementarity of
the pre-step $T_n$ from \noderef{TechnicalTheoremBase} when $n=1$ and
from the actual \noderef{CertifiedETTPredecessor} plus `lowerOne' when
$n>1$.  Then \noderef{ParallelCommonMissingClosed}, with the exact PE
base and the transported core and closure equalities, makes the entire
displayed common-missing set closed on $A$.  Choose any one of the four
colors.  Finally, if $d=0$ and the kind is SE or RE, the positive
constructor of \noderef{TashkinovSeriesStep} says, in the sense of
\noderef{ColoredVertexSetClosed}, that $T_n$ was closed under the
preceding coloring.  The terminal stable relation in the SE
case, or coloring equality in the RE case, preserves every missing set
on $T_n$ and, by the symmetric incident-edge criterion in
\noderef{sc2}, preserves the absence of a boundary in a missing color.
The degree argument below supplies at least one missing color.  Hence in
every case there is a color missing on and closed at $A$.               \tag{7}

For completeness, we spell out the parity consequence rather than assume
it.  Let $X$ be either $V(A)$ with the color from (7), or $V(U)$ with
$\alpha$.  By (2) or (3), the chosen color is missing at exactly one
vertex of $X$: existence is its selection, and two distinct missing
vertices would contradict \noderef{ColoredVertexSetElementary}.  At
every other vertex it is present by the complementary definitions
\noderef{MissingColors} and \noderef{PresentColors}.
\noderef{IsProperEdgeColoring} gives exactly one incident edge of that
color, while
\noderef{TreeColorSetClosed} forces the other endpoint of each such edge
to remain in $X$.  Looplessness and properness therefore partition all
vertices except the unique missing vertex into two-element endpoint
pairs.  Thus
\[
                |V(A)|\text{ and }|V(U)|\text{ are odd}.       \tag{8}
\]

The injective vertex array in \noderef{TreeSequence} and the definition
of \noderef{TreeSequenceVertexSet} give
$|V(S)|=|S|+1$ for every tree-sequence $S$.  By (4) and (8), the positive
difference $|V(U)|-|V(A)|$ is even, hence is at least two.  Choose two
distinct vertices $x,y\in V(U)-V(A)$.  The definitions \noderef{Degree}
and \noderef{MaximumDegree}, the inequality $\Delta(G)+1\leq k$, and
\noderef{IsProperEdgeColoring} show that every vertex misses a color:
otherwise all $k$ colors would be represented by distinct incident edges,
giving $k\leq\deg_G(v)\leq\Delta(G)<k$.  Choose
$\gamma_1\in\overline\varphi(x)$ and
$\gamma_2\in\overline\varphi(y)$.  Elementarity of $U$ makes these
colors distinct.

Every old Gamma color belonging to an owner in $D^+$ is missing at a
vertex of $A$ by \noderef{GoodHierarchyReservedDisjoint}.  Neither
$\gamma_1$ nor $\gamma_2$ can equal such a color, since it is missing at
$x$ or $y$ outside $A$ and (2) forbids a repeated missing color in $U$.
Consequently
\[
               \{\gamma_1,\gamma_2\}\cap\Gamma^-=\varnothing.
                                                                    \tag{9}
\]

We next choose the exact Step 1 edge.  Expanding the failure of
\noderef{TreeColorSetClosed} gives a color $\beta$ which is missing on
$U$ and has a nonempty $\beta$-boundary.  If
$\beta\notin\Gamma^-$, the assumed
\noderef{TreeColorSetMinusClosed} assertion would make that boundary
empty.  Therefore $\beta\in\Gamma^-$.  Expand the finite biunion to
choose $\eta\in D^+$ with
$\beta\in\mathcal G.\Gamma^q_\eta$.  Since $D^+$ is contained in the
last indexed stored set, `disjointPairs' in
\noderef{GoodHierarchy} shows that no distinct owner in $D^+$ has a pair
containing $\beta$.  This proves the asserted uniqueness.  Choose an
individual identity $f$ in the nonempty boundary.  By
\noderef{BoundaryColorEdges}, it is an edge in
\noderef{BoundaryEdges}$(G,V(U))$ and
\[
                              \varphi(f)=\beta.                \tag{10}
\]
Equation (9), together with $\beta\in\Gamma^-$, also proves that the new
pair excludes $\beta$.

We construct $W=U+f$ as an exact ordered tree, not merely as a vertex-set
extension.  The boundary definition supplies endpoints $u\in V(U)$ and
$w\notin V(U)$ with ends $\{u,w\}$.  Give $W$ length $|U|+1$, copy all
old vertex and edge entries, and use $w$ and $f$ as the new last entries.
Vertex injectivity follows from $w\notin V(U)$.  Edge injectivity follows
because every old displayed edge has both endpoints in $V(U)$ by the
attachment field of \noderef{TreeSequence}, whereas $f$ has endpoint
$w$ outside.  The old attachment equations copy, and the new one uses
the old position of $u$.  These checks construct a genuine tree-sequence
and give \noderef{TreeSequenceAdjoinEdge}$(U,f,W)$.  Equations (10) and
$\beta\in\overline\varphi(U)$ are then precisely the color and missing
witness required by \noderef{TashkinovAugmentation}; hence
\[
                              U\longrightarrow W.              \tag{11}
\]

We certify $W$ relative to the fixed original before constructing its
dependent hierarchy.  Using the reflexive-transitive-chain definition
\noderef{TashkinovAugmentationSequence}, concatenate the retained
$J\longrightarrow U$ sequence with (11).  In the PE anchor case,
concatenate first the retained
$T_n\longrightarrow J$ sequence; in the SE/RE case retain the exact
original connecting-edge identity and the exact equation $J=T_n+f_n$.
The strict inequalities assumed for the two anchor alternatives, followed
by $|U|<|W|$, give $|T_n|<|W|$.  By
\noderef{TashkinovAugmentationSequenceInitialSegment}, $T_n$ is an exact
initial segment of $W$.  These are precisely the PE or SE/RE eligibility
clauses in (1).  Apply that fixed-original
\noderef{ModColoring} directly to obtain
\[
 C_W:\noderef{CorrespondingExtendedTashkinovTree}
        (\mathcal E,\varphi,W).                                \tag{12}
\]
Thus (12) is relative to $\mathcal E$, not to $C_U$'s replacement ETT.
The theorem \noderef{MaximumPropertyTransport} transfers the original
maximum property to $C_W.\mathit{replacementETT}$, and
\noderef{HierarchyBaseCorrespondingTransport} transports the original
base $B$ itself, giving $B_W$ with the three stated equalities.

We now build the one-divider hierarchy $H_W$.  Set its divider count to
$d+1$.  Copy the $d+2$ old stages of $H$ in order and append $W$ as the
new terminal stage.  Copy the $d$ old divider positions and use $|U|$ as
the new one.  All old nesting, strictness, divider-stage, and base-core
fields copy from \noderef{TashkinovHierarchy}; the final nesting and
strictness come from (11), and exact prefix equality in
\noderef{TreeSequenceAdjoinEdge} identifies the prefix of $W$ of length
$|U|$ with the complete ordered tree $U$.  This proves all hierarchy
fields and the claimed equalities of stages and positions.

For its indexed stored sets, the old starred stages are unchanged, so
the definition \noderef{HierarchyStoredColorsAt} gives the old
equalities.  The new starred stage is $U$.  Both $C_U$ and the certificate
to be constructed on $W$ preserve the fixed original raw stored sets;
therefore at the new indexed level the definition is
\[
 D_n-\overline\varphi(U)=
       \noderef{HierarchyTerminalStoredColors}(H)=D^+.         \tag{13}
\]

Define the new total Gamma family by copying every old indexed family;
at the new level put $\{\gamma_1,\gamma_2\}$ at $\eta$ and copy the old
last pair for every other color.  We verify every field of
\noderef{GoodHierarchy}.  Old pair cardinalities and disjointness copy.
At the new level, cardinality two follows from
$\gamma_1\ne\gamma_2$; disjointness between the new pair and every copied
pair is (9), and disjointness among copied pairs is the old field.

For avoidance, the two new colors are missing on $U$.  The only edge of
$W-U$ is $f$, whose color is $\beta$, and (9) excludes $\beta$ from the
new pair.  Thus the new pair avoids the exact segment before the first
$\eta$-missing position.  If $\theta\ne\eta$ belongs to $D^+$, its copied
pair is missing on $A$ by
\noderef{GoodHierarchyReservedDisjoint}, hence also missing on $U$; old
pair disjointness says it does not contain $\beta$, so it too avoids the
only new segment edge.  At every old level, the stage and starred stage
are unchanged.  If the relevant stored color was already missing on $U$,
its first missing position is unchanged on passing to $W$; otherwise its
old fallback position and its new first-or-fallback position both lie
after that old stage.  The cutoff in
\noderef{HierarchySegmentColors} is therefore unchanged, so all old
avoidance fields copy exactly.

Every old unique-refresh clause also copies.  At the new divider,
$\eta$ has its new pair missing on $V(U)-V(A)$, and every other member of
$D^+$ retains its old last pair.  The owner is unique: a copied old pair
is missing on $A$, while every color of the new pair is missing at one of
$x,y\notin V(A)$; elementarity of $U$ and nonemptiness of every old
two-color pair prevent a copied pair from being contained in the new
difference-missing set.  The PE base clause is unchanged.  Finally, the
new internal minus-closed clause is exactly the assumed
$(\Gamma^-)^-$-closedness of $U$, using (13), while all earlier clauses
copy.  This completes the good witness $\mathcal G_W$ with exactly the
stated unique replacement.  Equation (13) follows from the raw stored-set
agreement in (12).

We may now run Step 2 without losing the fixed root.  Apply
\noderef{GoodHierarchyRestrictedAugmentation} with the
original ETT $\mathcal E$, the fixed-original mod-coloring (1), original
maximum property, $W,C_W,B_W,H_W$, and $\mathcal G_W$.  It returns a
finite exact augmentation chain $W\longrightarrow V$, a certificate
$C_V$ already relative to $\mathcal E$, maximum property, a transported
base, a hierarchy $K$ with the same $d+1$ dividers, and a good witness
whose stages, starred stages, indexed stored sets, divider positions, and
Gamma pairs agree with those of $H_W$.  It also gives
\[
 V\text{ is }
 \left(\bigcup_{\theta\in
   \noderef{HierarchyTerminalStoredColors}(K)}
   \mathcal G_V.\Gamma^{d+1}_\theta\right)^-\text{-closed}.    \tag{14}
\]
Compose its exact equalities with those just proved between $H_W$ and
$H$; this gives every displayed old-stage, position, stored-set, and
Gamma equality in the conclusion, as well as the new indexed equality
(13) and the unique pair replacement.  Concatenating the three
augmentation sequences gives $J\longrightarrow V$.  Since (11) gives
$|U|<|W|$ and the returned chain has $W$ as an exact initial segment of
$V$, we have $|U|<|V|$.  Equation (14) is the requested terminal
minus-closedness.  Both (12) and $C_V$ were obtained directly from the
same fixed-original mod-coloring, so no replacement-on-replacement
certificate or uncomposed positive-stage stability relation remains.
This proves every assertion.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Extension-base Part I(2): delta-color exclusion]
Let \(G\) be a finite loopless multigraph, let \(e_0\) be its
distinguished edge, and fix \(k\) and an initial partial \(k\)-edge-coloring.
Let \(T\), with generating coloring, be an ETT whose certified series has
\(n\) noninitial rounds.  Choose the last noninitial Lean index \(q\), so
\(q+1=n\), and assume that the positive slot \(q.\mathsf{succ}\) is a PE
whose exact chosen vertex is \(v_n\in V(T)\).  Put \(j=m(v_n)\).  Choose a
Lean index \(jSlot\) with
\[
                         jSlot+1=m(v_n),
\]
and write \(T_j\) and \(\varphi_{j-1}\) for the tree and coloring in the
certified series at that index.  Define, locally,
\[
 L=\{s:j\leq s\leq n,\ \Theta_s=\mathrm{PE},\
                         m(v_s)\leq j\};                       \tag{A}
\]
membership here includes the exact equality saying that the chosen vertex
at slot \(s\) is \(v_s\), viewed as a vertex of \(T\).

Assume the following two ladder-stage hypotheses.

First, for every Lean index \(r<q\), the vertex set of the displayed tree
\(T_{r+1}\) is elementary under its displayed coloring \(\varphi_r\).

Second, for all noninitial Lean indices \(a<r\), every proper coloring
\(\pi\) leaving exactly \(e_0\) uncolored that is
\((T_{r+1},D_r,\varphi_r)\)-stable, every defective color \(\delta\) of
\(T_{r+1}\) under \(\pi\), and every \(\delta\)-boundary vertex \(x\)
which has an earlier \(\delta\)-boundary vertex in the tree order, the
following holds.  If \(x\in V(T)\), \(m(x)\leq a+1\), and slot
\(a.\mathsf{succ}\) is a PE or SE with exact chosen vertex \(v_{a+1}\), then
\[
         x=v_{a+1}\quad\hbox{or}\quad x\prec_{T_{r+1}}v_{a+1}. \tag{B}
\]
This is the precise lower-rung maximum-defective order hypothesis quantified
in the Lean statement; in particular, (A) and (B) are assumptions on the
present ETT data, not hypotheses borrowed from another theorem.

Then, for all \(s,t\in L\) with \(s\leq t\), and for the exact certified
colors \(\delta_t\) and \(\gamma_s\) in their PE slots,
\[
 \delta_t\notin\overline\varphi_{s-1}(T_s),
 \qquad \delta_t\ne\gamma_s.                                  \tag{1}
\]
Moreover, for every (t\in L),
\[
                  \delta_t\notin\overline\varphi_{j-1}(T_j).  \tag{2}
\]
The gamma and delta colors in Lean are tied to the exact optional fields
of their PE slots; neither conclusion is an additional hypothesis.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
We use throughout the exact edge identities stored by the series; in
particular, two parallel edges remain distinct elements of the edge type.

We first prove a fact valid at every PE slot \(r\):
\[
                  \delta_r\notin
                  \overline\varphi_{r-1}(T_r).                 \tag{3}
\]
Eliminate the certificate at \(r\), using \noderef{TashkinovSeries} and
the equality \(\Theta_r=\mathrm{PE}\).  The PE constructor in
\noderef{TashkinovSeriesStep} supplies the previous proper coloring
\(\varphi_{r-1}\), for which \(T_r\) is closed, and an admissible choice
\(\pi_{r-1}\) from \noderef{MaximumDefectiveChoice}.  The latter is
proper, leaves exactly \(e_0\) uncolored, and is
\((T_r,D_{r-1},\varphi_{r-1})\)-stable.  Its selected edge \(f_r\) is
incident with its selected vertex in \(T_r\), is a defective boundary-edge
identity, and satisfies \(\pi_{r-1}(f_r)=\delta_r\).  Expanding
\noderef{DefectiveEdge} and then \noderef{BoundaryColorEdges} shows, by
comparison with this exact color equation, that
\(f_r\in\partial_{\pi_{r-1},\delta_r}(T_r)\); in particular
\(f_r\in\partial_G(T_r)\) by \noderef{BoundaryEdges}.

Suppose contrary to (3) that
\(\delta_r\in\overline\varphi_{r-1}(T_r)\).  By the union definition
\noderef{TreeMissingColors}, choose \(z\in V(T_r)\) with
\(\delta_r\in\overline\varphi_{r-1}(z)\).  The vertex clause of
\noderef{StableColoring} gives
\(\overline\pi_{r-1}(z)=\overline\varphi_{r-1}(z)\), so
\(\delta_r\in\overline\pi_{r-1}(z)\) and, again by
\noderef{TreeMissingColors},
\(\delta_r\in\overline\pi_{r-1}(T_r)\).  Apply the symmetry part of
\noderef{sc2} to the two proper colorings, both leaving exactly \(e_0\)
uncolored.  It makes \(\varphi_{r-1}\)
\((T_r,D_{r-1},\pi_{r-1})\)-stable.  The edge \(f_r\) is incident with
the tree in the sense of \noderef{TreeIncidentEdge}, and its reference
color under \(\pi_{r-1}\) is the tree-missing color \(\delta_r\).
The edge clause of \noderef{StableColoring} therefore gives
\(\varphi_{r-1}(f_r)=\pi_{r-1}(f_r)=\delta_r\).  Now
\(f_r\in\partial_G(T_r)\), \(z\in V(T_r)\), and \(\delta_r\) is missing
at \(z\), contradicting the closedness condition
\noderef{ColoredVertexSetClosed}.  This proves (3).

Fix \(s,t\in L\) with \(s\leq t\).  If \(s=t\), (3) is the first
assertion of (1).  At slot \(s\), \noderef{ParallelExtensionColor} says
that \(\gamma_s\) is missing at \(v_s\) under the selected coloring.
The selected vertex belongs to \(V(T_s)\) by the defective-vertex field of
\noderef{MaximumDefectiveChoice}, and the vertex clause of
\noderef{StableColoring} transfers this membership to
\(\overline\varphi_{s-1}(v_s)\).  The definition
\noderef{TreeMissingColors} then puts
\(\gamma_s\in\overline\varphi_{s-1}(T_s)\).  In view of (3),
\(\delta_s\ne\gamma_s\).

Suppose henceforth that \(s<t\), and assume for a contradiction that
\(\delta_t\in\overline\varphi_{s-1}(T_s)\).  Unfolding
\noderef{TreeMissingColors}, choose \(u\in V(T_s)\) such that
\(\delta_t\in\overline\varphi_{s-1}(u)\).  Since \(s<t\leq n\), the
explicit lower-rung hypothesis makes \(V(T_s)\) elementary under
\(\varphi_{s-1}\).  Thus \noderef{ColoredVertexSetElementary} says that
no \(x\in V(T_s)\setminus\{u\}\) also misses \(\delta_t\).  For each
such \(x\), the complement definition \noderef{MissingColors} first puts
\(\delta_t\) in the set of colors present at \(x\); unfolding
\noderef{PresentColors} then supplies a particular edge identity \(f_x\)
incident with \(x\), in the sense of \noderef{Incident}, such that
\[
                        \varphi_{s-1}(f_x)=\delta_t.             \tag{4}
\]

The PE certificate at \(s\) states that \(T_s\) is closed under
\(\varphi_{s-1}\).  If \(f_x\) were in the exact boundary set
\(\partial_G(T_s)\) of \noderef{BoundaryEdges}, then (4), the vertex
\(u\in V(T_s)\), and the fact that \(\delta_t\) is missing at \(u\)
would contradict \noderef{ColoredVertexSetClosed}.  Hence \(f_x\) is not
a boundary edge.  Since it is incident with the inside vertex \(x\), the
two-endpoint definitions \noderef{Incident} and \noderef{BoundaryEdges}
imply that every endpoint incident with this
same edge identity is inside \(V(T_s)\).  Thus
\(f_x\in E(G[T_s])\) by \noderef{InternalEdges}.

Apply \noderef{SameColorSeriesPreserved} to the nonempty interval from
slot \(s\) through the slot immediately before \(t\).  Its internal-edge
clause and (4) give
\[
                        \varphi_{t-1}(f_x)=\delta_t.             \tag{5}
\]
The original assumption puts \(\delta_t\) in
\(\overline\varphi_{s-1}(T_s)\cup D_{s-1}\).  Iterating the two
inclusions of \noderef{HkuMissingStoredMonotone} through the same stage
interval yields
\[
       \delta_t\in\overline\varphi_{t-1}(T_t)\cup D_{t-1}.      \tag{6}
\]

At the PE slot \(t\), write \(\pi_{t-1}\) for the selected coloring and
\(\rho=\pi'_{t-1}\) for the second coloring.  The first is
\((T_t,D_{t-1},\varphi_{t-1})\)-stable.  The ordered-extension fields
of \noderef{TashkinovSeriesStep} and
\noderef{TreeSequenceInitialSegment} put every vertex of \(T_s\) in
\(T_t\).  Hence the internal edge \(f_x\) is incident with \(T_t\), and
(5), (6), and the edge clause of
\noderef{StableColoring} give
\(\pi_{t-1}(f_x)=\varphi_{t-1}(f_x)=\delta_t\).  The PE certificate
makes \(\rho\)
\((T_t,D_{t-1}\cup\{\delta_t\},\pi_{t-1})\)-stable.  Because
\(\delta_t\) lies in this enlarged controlling set, a second use of the
same edge clause gives
\[
                              \rho(f_x)=\delta_t.                \tag{7}
\]

We record explicitly why (7) excludes \(x\) from the
\(\delta_t\)-boundary vertices of \(T_t\).  If it did not, the definition
\noderef{BoundaryColorVertex} would supply an exact edge identity \(g\)
incident with \(x\) and belonging to
\(\partial_{\rho,\delta_t}(T_t)\).  By
\noderef{BoundaryColorEdges}, \(\rho(g)=\delta_t\), and by
\noderef{BoundaryEdges}, \(g\) is a boundary edge of \(T_t\).  The edge
\(f_x\), being internal to \(T_s\), has both endpoints in \(T_s\), and
the same initial-segment inclusion puts both endpoints in \(T_t\).
Consequently it is not a boundary edge of \(T_t\), so \(g\ne f_x\).
The common incident
vertex \(x\) makes these two distinct edge identities adjacent by
\noderef{AdjacentEdges}.  Their common color in (7) contradicts the
properness condition \noderef{IsProperEdgeColoring} for \(\rho\).  We
have proved
\[
 x\in V(T_s)\setminus\{u\}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 x\notin I[\partial_{\rho,\delta_t}(T_t)].                       \tag{8}
\]
This argument concerns exact edge identities, so it remains valid when
\(f_x\) and \(g\) are parallel.

We next put \(\rho\) into the precise admissible class required by the
maximum-defective choice.  Its enlarged-set stability implies
\((T_t,D_{t-1},\pi_{t-1})\)-stability: the vertexwise equality is
unchanged, and every color in
\(\overline\pi_{t-1}(T_t)\cup D_{t-1}\) also belongs to
\(\overline\pi_{t-1}(T_t)\cup(D_{t-1}\cup\{\delta_t\})\), so the edge
clause of \noderef{StableColoring} restricts directly.  The PE constructor
and \noderef{MaximumDefectiveChoice} say that
\(\varphi_{t-1},\pi_{t-1},\rho\) are proper and leave exactly \(e_0\)
uncolored.  The transitivity part of \noderef{sc2}, applied at the
smaller set \(D_{t-1}\), now composes this restricted stability with the
selected coloring's
\((T_t,D_{t-1},\varphi_{t-1})\)-stability.  Consequently
\[
                 \rho\text{ is }(T_t,D_{t-1},\varphi_{t-1})
                 \text{-stable}.                              \tag{9}
\]

It remains to verify defectiveness under \(\rho\) without collapsing
parallel edges.  The selected edge \(f_t\) from
\noderef{MaximumDefectiveChoice} is defective under \(\pi_{t-1}\).
Unfold \noderef{DefectiveEdge}: for some color \(\alpha\), it belongs to
\(\partial_{\pi_{t-1},\alpha}(T_t)\), and \(\alpha\) is defective.
The definition \noderef{BoundaryColorEdges} and the stored equation
\(\pi_{t-1}(f_t)=\delta_t\) force \(\alpha=\delta_t\).  Hence
\noderef{DefectiveColor} gives at least two distinct edge identities in
\(\partial_{\pi_{t-1},\delta_t}(T_t)\), one of which is \(f_t\).

The exact \(\delta_t\)-boundary-edge set is unchanged on passing from
\(\pi_{t-1}\) to \(\rho\).  Indeed, membership in
\noderef{BoundaryEdges} is independent of the coloring, and every
boundary edge is incident with its inside tree endpoint, hence with the
tree by \noderef{TreeIncidentEdge}.  If such an edge has color
\(\delta_t\) under \(\pi_{t-1}\), the edge clause of the enlarged-set
stability gives the same color under \(\rho\).  Conversely, symmetry in
\noderef{sc2} makes \(\pi_{t-1}\) stable relative to \(\rho\) for the
same enlarged controlling set, and its edge clause gives the reverse
implication.  Unfolding \noderef{BoundaryColorEdges} proves equality of
the two finite sets.  Therefore \(\delta_t\) is defective under \(\rho\),
and \(f_t\) remains one of its boundary edges.

Choose another exact edge identity
\(g\in\partial_{\rho,\delta_t}(T_t)\) with \(g\ne f_t\), possible by
\noderef{DefectiveColor}.  By \noderef{BoundaryEdges}, choose its endpoint
\(y\in V(T_t)\); its endpoint equation makes it incident with \(g\) by
\noderef{Incident}, and then \noderef{BoundaryColorVertex} makes \(y\) a
\(\delta_t\)-boundary vertex.  The stored edge \(f_t\) is incident with
\(v_t\in V(T_t)\), so \(v_t\) is also such a boundary vertex.  These two
vertices are distinct: if \(y=v_t\), then the distinct identities \(g\)
and \(f_t\) are adjacent by \noderef{AdjacentEdges} and both have color
\(\delta_t\), contrary to \noderef{IsProperEdgeColoring}.  Since
\(\delta_t\) is defective under \(\rho\), the edge \(g\) is a defective
edge by \noderef{DefectiveEdge}, and \(y\) is a defective vertex by
\noderef{DefectiveVertex}.

Apply the maximality field of \noderef{MaximumDefectiveChoice} to
\(\rho,y\), using properness, the one-uncolored condition, (9), and the
defective-vertex fact just proved.  It gives \(y=v_t\) or
\(y\prec_{T_t}v_t\).  The first alternative was excluded, so
\[
                              y\prec_{T_t}v_t.                  \tag{10}
\]

By \noderef{ExtensionBaseSupportOrder}, applied with the explicit
lower-rung assertion (v) from the present statement,
\(v_t\preceq_Tv_s\).  We now justify the required stage containment.
Iterate the ordered-extension fields in \noderef{TashkinovSeriesStep}
and finish with the terminal segment field in
\noderef{ExtendedTashkinovTree}.  Expanding
\noderef{TreeSequenceInitialSegment} proves that both \(T_s\) and \(T_t\)
are ordered initial segments of \(T\).  Such an embedding preserves
\noderef{TreeVertexOrder}: the same two vertex positions witness the
strict order after applying the entrywise prefix equations.  Thus (10)
gives \(y\prec_Tv_t\).

We also need downward closure, not merely order preservation.  Let \(A\)
be an ordered initial segment of \(B\), let \(b\in V(A)\), and suppose
\(a\prec_B b\).  By \noderef{TreeSequenceVertexSet}, \(b\) has a position
\(i_b\) in \(A\); the entrywise equality in
\noderef{TreeSequenceInitialSegment} puts it at the same position in
\(B\).  The uniqueness of vertex positions from the injectivity field of
\noderef{TreeSequence} and the definition \noderef{TreeVertexOrder} show
that the position of \(a\) in \(B\) is smaller than \(i_b\), hence still
lies in the prefix \(A\).  The corresponding prefix equation then puts
\(a\in V(A)\).  Apply this observation to \(A=T_s\), \(B=T\), and
\(b=v_s\in V(T_s)\).  From \(v_t\preceq_Tv_s\) we get
\(v_t\in V(T_s)\).  If \(v_t=v_s\), then (10) gives \(y\prec_Tv_s\);
if \(v_t\prec_Tv_s\), transitivity of the numerical position order in
\noderef{TreeVertexOrder} gives the same conclusion.  Thus
\(y\in V(T_s)\) as well.

If \(u=v_t\), then the boundary vertex \(y\) belongs to
\(V(T_s)\setminus\{u\}\), contradicting (8).  If \(u\ne v_t\), the
boundary vertex \(v_t\) itself belongs to that difference, again
contradicting (8).  Therefore
\(\delta_t\notin\overline\varphi_{s-1}(T_s)\).

Finally, \noderef{ParallelExtensionColor} makes \(\gamma_s\) missing at
\(v_s\) under the selected coloring at \(s\).  The vertex clause of
\noderef{StableColoring} makes it missing there under
\(\varphi_{s-1}\), and \noderef{TreeMissingColors} then gives
\(\gamma_s\in\overline\varphi_{s-1}(T_s)\).  The exclusion just proved
implies \(\delta_t\ne\gamma_s\), completing (1).

For (2), the finite set \(L\) is nonempty.  At the terminal PE slot, the
chosen vertex is \(v_n\), and its defective-vertex field in
\noderef{MaximumDefectiveChoice} puts it in \(V(T_n)\).  Thus \(T_n\) is
a candidate containing \(v_n\) in the minimum
\noderef{ETTFirstAppearanceIndex}, so \(j=m(v_n)\leq n\).  The terminal
index consequently satisfies all three defining clauses in
\noderef{ExtensionBaseActiveIndices}: \(j\leq n\), its kind is PE, and
its exact chosen vertex has first-appearance index \(j\leq j\).

Let \(s_0\) be the least manuscript subscript represented in \(L\).
For every \(t\in L\), we have \(s_0\leq t\), so (1) gives
\[
        \delta_t\notin\overline\varphi_{s_0-1}(T_{s_0}).        \tag{11}
\]

We split the index transport into its two possible cases.  If \(s_0=j\),
then the relation \(jSlot+1=j\) identifies the Lean slot for \(T_j\) with
the slot for \(T_{s_0}\).  Both the tree and coloring are therefore the
same indexed entries, so
\[
 \overline\varphi_{j-1}(T_j)
   =\overline\varphi_{s_0-1}(T_{s_0})
\]
by reflexivity.  The desired exclusion follows immediately from (11).

Assume instead that \(s_0>j\).  Fix \(x\in V(T_j)\).  The stage \(T_j\)
is an ordered initial segment of the final ETT, so \(x\in V(T)\).  In the
finite minimum defining \noderef{ETTFirstAppearanceIndex}, the ladder
stage \(T_j\) itself is a candidate containing \(x\); hence
\[
                              m(x)\leq j.                        \tag{12}
\]
There is no PE iteration \(h\) with \(j\leq h\leq s_0-1\) whose exact
chosen vertex is \(x\).  Otherwise (12), the PE equality, and \(j\leq h\)
would put \(h\) in the set \noderef{ExtensionBaseActiveIndices}, while
\(h<s_0\), contradicting the least choice of \(s_0\).

The interval \(j,j+1,\ldots,s_0-1\) is nonempty in the present case.
Apply \noderef{HkuUnsupportedVertexColors} to \(x\) over precisely that
interval.  Its conclusion is
\[
             \overline\varphi_{j-1}(x)
                 =\overline\varphi_{s_0-1}(x).                 \tag{13}
\]
This holds for every \(x\in V(T_j)\).  Taking the vertexwise union in
\noderef{TreeMissingColors} gives the first equality below.  Iterating
the certified initial segments from \noderef{TashkinovSeriesStep} puts
every vertex of \(T_j\) in \(T_{s_0}\), so the same union definition gives
the inclusion:
\[
 \overline\varphi_{j-1}(T_j)
   =\overline\varphi_{s_0-1}(T_j)
   \subseteq\overline\varphi_{s_0-1}(T_{s_0}).
\]
Combining this with (11) proves (2) also when \(s_0>j\).
\end{proof}

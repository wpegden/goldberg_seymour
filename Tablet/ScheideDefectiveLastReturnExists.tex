\begin{helper}[A second tree vertex determines last-return data]
Fix a defective color $\delta$.  If $w\in V(T)$, $w\ne v^0_\delta$, and
the vertex-node $w$ belongs to the full
$(\gamma(\delta),\delta)$ component through $v^0_\delta$, then there
exists a \noderef{ScheideDefectiveReturnData} for $\delta$.
\end{helper}
\begin{proof}
Write $X=\noderef{TreeSequenceVertexSet}(T)$,
$i=\gammaIndex(\delta)$, $\gamma=\gamma_i$, and let $P'$ be the
replacement path supplied by \noderef{ScheideDefectiveSetup}.  By
\noderef{ScheideDefectiveChainPath}, $P'$ is simple and its support is
exactly the full $(\gamma,\delta)$ component through $v^0_\delta$.
Thus the assumed vertex-node $w$ occurs on $P'$.  Among all occurrences
after the initial $v^0_\delta$ whose vertex lies in $X$, choose the last
one and call its vertex $v_1$.  It is distinct from $v^0_\delta$ by
simplicity.  The terminal vertex of $P'$ lies outside $X$, so the next
two nodes after $v_1$ exist; bipartiteness of
\noderef{TwoColorIncidenceGraph} makes them an edge-node $f_1$ and a
vertex-node $u_1$.  Lastness gives $u_1\notin X$ and gives the required
universal assertion about every later vertex-node.  Adjacency in the
incidence graph and \noderef{Incident} give
$\operatorname{ends}(f_1)=\{v_1,u_1\}$.

The suffix of $P'$ beginning at $v_1$ is a two-color path whose last tree
vertex is $v_1$.  The incidence graph does not depend on the order of its
two named colors, $\delta$ is defective, and $\gamma$ is missing at the
initial tree vertex $v^0_\delta$.  Hence this suffix satisfies the
definition \noderef{ScheideExitVertex}, so
$v_1\in\noderef{ScheideExitVertices}(T,\varphi)$.  The inclusion
\noderef{ScheideStructuralExitsInBase} places $v_1$ in the base image
$Y$.

The edge $f_1$ has color $\gamma$ or $\delta$ by
\noderef{TwoColorIncidenceGraph}.  Its endpoints show that it is a
boundary identity of $X$ in the sense of \noderef{BoundaryEdges}.
Expanding the \noderef{GlobalMaximumTriple} in
\noderef{ScheideReducedConfiguration},
\noderef{ScheideGlobalMaximumOrder} says that $X$ is closed and
elementary.  Since $\gamma$ is missing at $v^0_\delta\in X$, closedness
excludes a $\gamma$-colored boundary identity.  Therefore
$\varphi(f_1)=\delta$.

The exact-one exit clause of \noderef{ScheideReducedConfiguration}
gives a unique block index $j$ for which $\gamma_j$ is missing at
$v_1$.  The colors $\gamma_i$ and $\gamma_j$ cannot be equal: they are
missing at the two distinct vertices $v^0_\delta,v_1\in X$, contrary to
elementariness.  Injectivity of the block-color map consequently gives
$j\ne i$.

It remains only to record the color inequalities required by
\noderef{ScheideDefectiveReturnData}.  The setup already gives
$\alpha _0\ne\gamma_i$ and $\alpha _0\ne\gamma_j$.  Membership of
$\delta$ in \noderef{DefectiveColors} supplies a
$\delta$-colored boundary identity of $X$.  Closedness says that its
color is missing at no vertex of $X$; by \noderef{MissingColors} and
\noderef{PresentColors}, $\delta$ is present at every vertex of $X$.
It is therefore distinct from $\alpha _0,\gamma_i,\gamma_j$, which are
missing respectively at $y_0,v^0_\delta,v_1$.  These data, with the
chosen three exact path positions, fill every field of the required
return datum.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Scheide enlarged set is closed (19)]
The enlarged set $X=V(T)\cup\bigcup_\delta U_\delta$ is closed with
respect to $\varphi$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Write $U_\delta=\{u^1_\delta,u^2_\delta\}$ and
$X_\delta=V(T)\cup U_\delta$.  If there is no defective color, the
indexed union in \noderef{ScheideEnlargedVertexSet} is empty, and hence
$X=V(T)$.  The globally maximum field in the structural data then makes
$V(T)$ closed by \noderef{ScheideGlobalMaximumOrder}.  We may therefore
assume that at least one defective color exists.

Suppose, for a contradiction, that an edge identity $f$ of color
$\alpha$ leaves $X$.  Choose its ends as $u\in X$ and $w\notin X$, and
choose $x\in X$ at which $\alpha$ is missing.  These choices are exactly
the negation of closure in \noderef{ColoredVertexSetClosed}.  If
$u\in V(T)$, choose any defective color $\delta$; otherwise the union
formula in \noderef{ScheideEnlargedVertexSet} supplies a defective
$\delta$ with $u\in U_\delta$.  Thus in both cases $u\in X_\delta$.
Since $w\notin X$ also gives $w\notin X_\delta$, the edge $f$ is a
boundary identity of $X_\delta$.  Its closedness from
\noderef{ScheideReplacementUnionClosed} and its color $\alpha$ show that
$x\notin X_\delta$.

In particular $x\notin V(T)$ and $x\notin U_\delta$.  The same union
formula now supplies a defective color $\delta'$ with
$x\in U_{\delta'}$.  By
\noderef{ScheideReplacementPairsLaminar}, the two pairs are equal or
disjoint.  They cannot be equal because $x\notin U_\delta$, so
$U_\delta$ and $U_{\delta'}$ are disjoint.  If $u$ were in $V(T)$, then
$f$ would also leave $X_{\delta'}$, while $\alpha$ is missing at
$x\in X_{\delta'}$; this contradicts the closedness of
$X_{\delta'}$ from \noderef{ScheideReplacementUnionClosed}.  Therefore
$u\notin V(T)$ and hence $u\in U_\delta$.  The disjointness just proved
then gives $u\notin X_{\delta'}$.

Let $y_0$ denote the first base vertex.  The exact position clauses of
\noderef{ScheideStructuralConfiguration}, together with the image
definition \noderef{TreeSequenceVertexSet}, put $y_0$ in $V(T)$.  The
setup \noderef{ScheideDefectiveSetup} states that $\alpha_0$ is missing
at $y_0$ and differs from every block color.  Thus $y_0,x\in X$, and
they are distinct because $x\notin V(T)$.  By
\noderef{ScheideEnlargedElementary} the set $X$ is elementary; unfolding
the pairwise-disjoint missing-set condition in
\noderef{ColoredVertexSetElementary} shows that
$\alpha_0\ne\alpha$, since otherwise the same color would be missing at
the two distinct vertices $y_0$ and $x$.

Consider the full $(\alpha_0,\alpha)$ incidence component $K$ through
$u$ in the sense of \noderef{TwoColorComponent}.  Apply closedness of
$X_{\delta'}$ separately to the two missing colors.  Because $\alpha_0$
is missing at $y_0\in X_{\delta'}$, no $\alpha_0$-colored boundary
identity of $X_{\delta'}$ exists.  Because $\alpha$ is missing at
$x\in X_{\delta'}$, no $\alpha$-colored boundary identity exists either.
The root $u$ is outside $X_{\delta'}$.  If a vertex-node of
$X_{\delta'}$ belonged to $K$, a path in the connected incidence support
from $u$ to that vertex would have a first edge identity crossing into
$X_{\delta'}$; that identity has color $\alpha_0$ or $\alpha$, contrary
to one of the two preceding closure conclusions.  Hence $K$ avoids every
vertex of $X_{\delta'}$.  Moreover, an edge-node of $K$ incident with a
vertex of $X_{\delta'}$ is adjacent to that vertex-node in the same
incidence graph, so it too is impossible.  Consequently $K$ avoids every
vertex and every incident edge identity of $X_{\delta'}$, in particular
all vertices and incident identities of $V(T)$.

We next record all color inequalities needed for the two switches.  For
each block color $\gamma_i$, \noderef{ScheideStructuralConfiguration}
provides a base vertex of $V(T)$ missing $\gamma_i$.  Since $x$ lies
outside $V(T)$ and misses $\alpha$, elementariness of $X$ implies
$\alpha\ne\gamma_i$.  The setup already gives
$\alpha_0\ne\gamma_i$ for every $i$.  The replacement data
\noderef{ScheideReplacementData} give a named edge
$f^1_\delta=v^1_\delta u^1_\delta$ of color $\delta$, with
$v^1_\delta\in V(T)$ and $u^1_\delta\in U_\delta$.  Its first end is in
$X_{\delta'}$, while its second end is outside that set because the two
pairs are disjoint and replacement-pair vertices lie outside $V(T)$.
Closedness of $X_{\delta'}$, first with the color missing at $x$ and then
with the color missing at $y_0$, therefore yields
$\alpha\ne\delta$ and $\alpha_0\ne\delta$.  Finally
\noderef{ScheideDefectiveSetup} makes $\gamma(\delta)$ missing at
$v^0_\delta$ while the incident identity $f^0_\delta$ has color
$\delta$; by the meaning of missing color in \noderef{MissingColors},
$\gamma(\delta)\ne\delta$.  Together with
$\alpha_0\ne\alpha$, these facts say that the four colors
$\alpha_0,\alpha,\gamma(\delta),\delta$ are pairwise distinct.

Put
\[
 \varphi_\delta=
 \varphi/P'_{\delta}
 =\operatorname{swap}_{\gamma(\delta),\delta}(\varphi)
 \quad\hbox{and}\quad T_\delta=R(\delta).\mathrm{replacementTree}.
\]
Here the first equality is the exact component swap specified by
\noderef{TwoColorComponentSwap}, and
\noderef{ScheideReplacementMaximum} says that $\varphi_\delta$ is proper,
leaves exactly $e_0$ uncolored, and makes
$(T_\delta,e_0,\varphi_\delta)$ globally maximum.

We now justify, edge by edge, that $K$ has the same support under
$\varphi$ and $\varphi_\delta$.  If an identity has color
$\alpha_0$ or $\alpha$ under $\varphi$, the definition of the first swap
leaves that value fixed, whether or not the identity lies in the switched
component, because neither value is $\gamma(\delta)$ or $\delta$.
Conversely, inspection of the three branches in
\noderef{TwoColorComponentSwap} shows that an output value
$\alpha_0$ or $\alpha$ can only have had that same input value: the only
values interchanged are $\gamma(\delta)$ and $\delta$, and both are
different from $\alpha_0,\alpha$.  Thus an edge identity is
$\alpha_0$- or $\alpha$-colored for one coloring exactly when it is so,
with the same color, for the other.  The two corresponding incidence
graphs are identical, so their component through $u$ is the same set $K$.

No edge identity of $T_\delta$ belongs to $K$.  Indeed, the exact edge
list in \noderef{ScheideReplacementData} says that every such identity is
either an inherited identity of $T$ other than the two deleted block
edges, or one of $f^1_\delta,f^2_\delta$.  By the attachment clause of
\noderef{TreeSequence}, every inherited tree identity is incident with a
vertex of $V(T)$ and is therefore excluded by the avoidance result above.
The two appended identities have color $\delta$ under $\varphi$, so they
are not edge-nodes in the $(\alpha_0,\alpha)$ support.  The support is the
same under $\varphi_\delta$, as just proved.

Let $\psi$ be the result of swapping $\alpha_0$ and $\alpha$ on $K$ in
$\varphi_\delta$.  We verify rather than assume that $T_\delta$ remains
Tashkinov.  Fix $q\in U_\delta$.  Since replacement-pair vertices are
outside $V(T)$, $q\ne y_0$; since $U_\delta$ and $U_{\delta'}$ are
disjoint, $q\ne x$.  Elementariness of $X$, with $\alpha_0$ missing at
$y_0$ and $\alpha$ missing at $x$, says that neither color is missing at
$q$, hence both are present there in the terminology of
\noderef{MissingColors}.  The first switch interchanges only
$\gamma(\delta),\delta$, so both colors are also present at $q$ under
$\varphi_\delta$.  If the vertex-node $q$ is outside $K$, no incident
edge-node lies in $K$ and its missing set is fixed.  If $q$ lies in $K$,
properness of $\varphi_\delta$ gives exactly one incident edge of each of
the two present colors; both edge-nodes lie in $K$, and the second switch
merely exchanges them.  All other incident values are fixed.  Thus in
either case the whole missing-color set at $q$ is unchanged.

Every vertex of $V(T)$ and every edge incident with it is avoided by
$K$, so its whole missing-color set is unchanged as well.  The exact
vertex equation in \noderef{ScheideReplacementData} is
\[
 V(T_\delta)=
 \bigl(V(T)\setminus
   \{u^1_{\gamma(\delta)},u^2_{\gamma(\delta)}\}\bigr)
 \cup U_\delta .
\]
Consequently the second switch preserves the missing-color set of every
vertex of $T_\delta$.  It also fixes every edge identity of $T_\delta$,
as proved above.  The globally maximum clause supplied by
\noderef{ScheideReplacementMaximum}, unfolded with
\noderef{GlobalMaximumTriple}, supplies
\noderef{TashkinovTree} witnesses for every noninitial edge of
$T_\delta$ under $\varphi_\delta$.  The same earlier vertex, the same
missing color, and the same edge color remain valid under $\psi$.
Therefore $T_\delta$ is a Tashkinov tree for $\psi$.

We also check every other clause of global maximality.  The colors
$\alpha_0$ and $\alpha$ are distinct, so
\noderef{TwoColorComponentSwapProper}, applied to the proper coloring
$\varphi_\delta$, makes $\psi$ proper.  By
\noderef{TwoColorComponentSwap}, the second switch sends colored values
to colored values and fixes the uncolored value.  Thus the assertion from
\noderef{ScheideReplacementMaximum} that exactly $e_0$ is uncolored is
preserved in the precise sense of \noderef{IsOneEdgeUncolored}.  The
criticality of $G$, the equality $\chi'(G)=k+1$, and the inequality
$\Delta(G)+1\leq k$ are graph-and-integer clauses and are copied unchanged
from the old \noderef{KTriple}.  These five facts reconstruct a
$k$-triple $(G,e_0,\psi)$.  The tree itself is unchanged, so its vertex
cardinality is still $t(G)$; combined with the Tashkinov conclusion of the
preceding paragraph, the three clauses of
\noderef{GlobalMaximumTriple} are all satisfied for
$(T_\delta,e_0,\psi)$.

It remains to contradict closedness of the reconstructed maximum tree.
Since
$f$ has color $\alpha$ and is incident with the root $u$, its edge-node is
adjacent to $u$ in the two-color incidence graph, and hence belongs to the
full component $K$ by \noderef{TwoColorComponent}.  The first switch fixes
its color, and the second therefore changes $f$ from $\alpha$ to
$\alpha_0$.  The first switch cannot create an $\alpha_0$-edge at $y_0$
because its two colors are disjoint from $\alpha_0$, and the second switch
fixes every edge incident with the avoided vertex $y_0$.  Hence
$\alpha_0$ remains missing at $y_0$ under $\psi$.

The displayed vertex equation puts $u\in U_\delta$ in $V(T_\delta)$.
It also puts $y_0$ there: the structural position of $y_0$ is zero,
whereas the two deleted block vertices have positions at least $p\geq2$;
vertex injectivity in \noderef{TreeSequence} makes these vertices
distinct.  On the other hand that same equation gives
$V(T_\delta)\subseteq V(T)\cup U_\delta\subseteq X$, so
$w\notin X$ implies $w\notin V(T_\delta)$.  Thus the same exact identity
$f$, with ends $u\in V(T_\delta)$ and $w\notin V(T_\delta)$, is a boundary
identity of $V(T_\delta)$ under $\psi$.  But the reconstructed global
maximality makes $V(T_\delta)$ closed under $\psi$ by
\noderef{ScheideGlobalMaximumOrder}.  Its boundary identity $f$ has color
$\alpha_0$, while $\alpha_0$ is missing at
$y_0\in V(T_\delta)$, contradicting that closedness directly.  The assumed
boundary identity $f$ therefore cannot exist, and $X$ is closed.
\end{proof}

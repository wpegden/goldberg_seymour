\begin{lemma}[Case 1: the first vertex and equations (13)--(15)]
Use manuscript stages \(j=m(v_n)\leq n\), represented in Lean by
zero-based predecessor slots \(j_{\rm slot}\leq q\), with
\(q.\mathrm{val}+1=n\) and
\[
 j_{\rm slot}.\mathrm{val}+1
   =\noderef{ETTFirstAppearanceIndex}(v_n)=j.
\]
Thus the exact reference coloring in every hypothesis below is the stored
coloring
\(\mathtt{ett.series.data.coloring\ jSlot.castSucc}), namely
\(\varphi_{j-1}\), and the corresponding stored tree is \(T_j\).
Choose the exact occurrence position of \(v_n\) in \(T_j\), require that
position to be positive, and identify the closure seed with
\noderef{TreeSequencePrefix} through that occurrence.  In other words, the
seed is exactly the ordered stage prefix \(T_j(v_n)\), not an unrelated
tree-sequence.  Let
\[
 L_1=\{1_1<\cdots<1_{p+1}=n\}
\]
be the exact class of active PE slots supported at \(v_n\), with exact
fields \(\gamma_{1_{a+1}}=\gamma_a\),
\(\delta_{1_{a+1}}=\delta_a\), and
\(S_{1_{a+1}}=\{\gamma_a,\delta_a\}\).  Assume
\[
 \gamma_{a+1}=\delta_a\quad(0\leq a<p),
 \qquad \gamma_0,\delta_0,\ldots,\delta_p
             \text{ are pairwise distinct}.                  \tag{4}
\]
Let \(T'_j\), with its displayed closure certificate from that exact seed,
be the elementary closure supplied by \noderef{ExtensionBaseClosure}.
Suppose Case 1 holds:
\[
 \overline\varrho_1(T'_j-v_n)\cap
          \bigcup_{i\in L_1}S_i\ne\varnothing.               \tag{C1}
\]
Assume also the Claim 4.3 missing-color equality on
\(T_j-v_n\), the elementariness of \(T_j\) under
\(\varphi_{j-1}\), the Part I(2) exclusions
\(\delta_a\notin\overline\varphi_{j-1}(T_j)\), and
\[
 \gamma_0\in\overline\varphi_{j-1}(v_n),\qquad
 \overline\varrho_1(v_n)\cap\bigcup_{i\in L_1}S_i
       =\{\delta_p\}.                                        \tag{U}
\]

Then there are a first vertex \(u\) in the order of \(T'_j-v_n\) and
a least position \(b\) such that
\(\gamma_b\in\overline\varrho_1(u)\).  No earlier
\(\gamma_a\), \(a<b\), is missing at \(u\), and every other vertex
of \(T'_j-v_n\) meeting the displayed support-color union is either
\(u\) or occurs after \(u\).  Moreover,
\[
                         u\in V(T'_j)-V(T_j),                 \tag{13}
\]
\[
 \overline\varrho_1(T'_j(u)-u)\cap
  \left(\bigcup_{i\in L_1}S_i-\{\delta_p\}\right)
                         =\varnothing,                         \tag{14}
\]
and, for \(L_1^*=\noderef{ExtensionBaseCaseOneIndices}\),
\[
 \delta_p\notin\bigcup_{i\in L_1^*}S_i,qquad
 \overline\varrho_1(v_n)\cap
       \bigcup_{i\in L_1^*}S_i=\varnothing.                  \tag{15}
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Put
\[
 S(L_1)=\bigcup_{i\in L_1}S_i
 =\bigcup_{a\in\operatorname{Fin}(p+1)}
       \mathtt{ett.series.data.stored}(\mathtt{indices}(a)+1).
\]
Unfold the vertex union in \noderef{TreeMissingColors} in (C1).  Membership
in the resulting biunion and intersection says exactly that there are a
vertex \(x\in V(T'_j)\setminus\{v_n\}\) and a color
\(c\in\overline\varrho_1(x)\cap S(L_1)\).  Consequently the finite set
\[
 A=\{x\in V(T'_j)\setminus\{v_n\}:
          \overline\varrho_1(x)\cap S(L_1)\ne\varnothing\}
\]
is nonempty.

We make the choice of its first member explicit.  By
\noderef{TreeSequenceVertexSet}, every member of \(A\) has a displayed
position in \(T'_j\).  Hence the nonempty finite set
\[
 I_A=\{i\in\operatorname{Fin}(T'_j.\mathtt{length}+1):
                      T'_j.\mathtt{vertex}(i)\in A\}
\]
has a least natural-valued position \(i_u\).  Set
\(u=T'_j.\mathtt{vertex}(i_u)\).  The vertex map is injective by
\noderef{TreeSequence}, so a vertex of \(V(T'_j)\) has only one displayed
position.  Thus, if \(w\in A\) has position \(i_w\), minimality gives
\(i_u\leq i_w\); equality gives \(w=u\), and strict inequality gives
\(u\prec_{T'_j}w\) by \noderef{TreeVertexOrder}.  This proves
\(u\in V(T'_j)\), \(u\ne v_n\), and the universal order-minimality
clause in the conclusion.

Before applying elementariness, we verify that \(v_n\) belongs to the
closure.  Write \(T_j\) for the stored tree at \(j_{\rm slot}\).  In the
prefix from \noderef{TreeSequencePrefix}, the position whose value is
\(\mathtt{terminalPosition.val}\) is a valid last position because that
prefix has length \(\mathtt{terminalPosition.val}\).  The defining
entrywise equality for this prefix, together with \(\mathtt{hseed}\) and
\(\mathtt{hterminalAt}\), therefore gives a position of the seed whose
vertex is exactly \(v_n\).  Unfolding \noderef{TreeSequenceVertexSet}
shows
\[
                         v_n\in V(\mathtt{seed}).             \tag{16}
\]
Now unfold \noderef{TashkinovClosure} and then
\noderef{TashkinovAugmentationSequence}.  The first closure conjunct is a
reflexive-transitive chain of one-step augmentations from the seed to
\(T'_j\).  At one such step, \noderef{TashkinovAugmentation} supplies
\noderef{TreeSequenceInitialSegment} from the old sequence to the new one.
Its entrywise vertex equality, together with
\noderef{TreeSequenceVertexSet}, sends every old displayed vertex at
position \(i\) to the new displayed vertex at the cast of the same
position.  Thus one augmentation gives inclusion of vertex sets.  Induction
along the reflexive-transitive chain, using equality in the reflexive case
and composing these inclusions in the transitive case, yields
\[
                    V(\mathtt{seed})\subseteq V(T'_j).
\]
Combining this with (16) proves \(v_n\in V(T'_j)\).

Equation (U) now puts \(\delta_p\) both in
\(\overline\varrho_1(v_n)\) and in \(S(L_1)\).  Since \(u\ne v_n\) and
both vertices have just been proved to lie in \(V(T'_j)\), the definition
\noderef{ColoredVertexSetElementary} makes their missing-color sets
disjoint.  In particular,
\[
                       \delta_p\notin\overline\varrho_1(u).  \tag{17}
\]
Choose \(c\in\overline\varrho_1(u)\cap S(L_1)\), which is possible since
\(u\in A\).  Unfolding the finite biunion and using the exact stored-pair
identities shows that \(c=\gamma_a\) or \(c=\delta_a\) for some
\(a\in\operatorname{Fin}(p+1)\).  In the second alternative, either
\(a=\operatorname{Fin.last}(p)\), contrary to (17), or there is a unique
\(t\in\operatorname{Fin}(p)\) with \(a=t.\mathtt{castSucc}\).  In the
latter case the chain equality in (4) gives
\(c=\delta_{t.\mathtt{castSucc}}=\gamma_{t.\mathtt{succ}}\).
Therefore the set of positions \(a\) for which
\(\gamma_a\in\overline\varrho_1(u)\) is nonempty.  Choose its least
member \(b\).  Then \(\gamma_b\in\overline\varrho_1(u)\), while no
\(\gamma_a\) with \(a<b\) is missing at \(u\).

We prove (13).  The equality \(\mathtt{hterminalAt}\), unfolded through
\noderef{TreeSequenceVertexSet}, also gives \(v_n\in V(T_j)\).  Suppose,
for a contradiction, that \(u\in V(T_j)\).  Since \(u\ne v_n\), this puts
\(u\) in the erased set on which the Claim 4.3 agreement hypothesis
applies.  If \(b=0\), then \(\gamma_b=\gamma_0\) is missing at \(v_n\)
under \(\varphi_{j-1}\) by hypothesis.  The elementariness of \(V(T_j)\)
under that coloring and the distinctness of \(u,v_n\) imply that
\(\gamma_0\) is not missing at \(u\) under \(\varphi_{j-1}\).  Agreement
then says it is not missing at \(u\) under \(\varrho_1\), contrary to the
choice of \(b\).  If \(b>0\), write \(b=t.\mathtt{succ}\) for the unique
\(t\in\operatorname{Fin}(p)\).  Equation (4) gives
\(\gamma_b=\delta_{t.\mathtt{castSucc}}\).  The Part I(2) hypothesis
excludes this delta color from \noderef{TreeMissingColors} of \(T_j\), so
unfolding that vertex biunion shows that it is not missing at \(u\) under
\(\varphi_{j-1}\).  Agreement again contradicts the choice of \(b\).
Thus \(u\notin V(T_j)\), which is (13).

For (14), suppose that a color \(d\) belongs to its left-hand
intersection.  Unfolding \noderef{TreeVerticesBefore} gives a vertex
\(x\in V(T'_j)\) with \(x\prec_{T'_j}u\),
\(d\in\overline\varrho_1(x)\), and
\(d\in S(L_1)\setminus\{\delta_p\}\).  If \(x=v_n\), equation (U) forces
\(d=\delta_p\), contradicting the last membership.  If \(x\ne v_n\),
then \(x\in A\).  The minimality already proved for \(u\) says that either
\(x=u\) or \(u\prec_{T'_j}x\).  The first possibility is incompatible
with \(x\prec_{T'_j}u\), and the second gives positions simultaneously
strictly increasing in both directions.  These contradictions follow
directly by unfolding \noderef{TreeVertexOrder} and using the position
injectivity in \noderef{TreeSequence}.  No such \(d\) exists, so the
intersection is empty and (14) follows.

It remains to prove (15).  Unfold
\noderef{ExtensionBaseCaseOneIndices}.  Membership in its filtered image
says exactly that a slot is \(\mathtt{indices}(a)\) for a position
\(a\) with \(a.\mathtt{val}<b.\mathtt{val}\).  Hence, using
\(\mathtt{hstored}\), every color in its stored-color biunion is either
\(\gamma_a\) or \(\delta_a\) for such an \(a\).

We check that neither possibility can equal \(\delta_p\).  The injective
map in (4),
\[
 \operatorname{Fin.cons}(\gamma_0,\delta),
\]
lists \(\gamma_0\) at position zero and \(\delta_a\) at successor
position \(a+1\).  Since \(a<b\leq p\), the position \(a+1\) is strictly
below the position \(p+1\) of \(\delta_p\); injectivity therefore gives
\(\delta_a\ne\delta_p\).  For \(\gamma_a\), if \(a=0\), its position
zero is distinct from \(p+1\).  If \(a>0\), write
\(a=t.\mathtt{succ}\); (4) identifies \(\gamma_a\) with
\(\delta_{t.\mathtt{castSucc}}\), whose position in the same injective
map is \(a<p+1\).  Thus \(\gamma_a\ne\delta_p\) as well.  This proves
\[
          \delta_p\notin\bigcup_{i\in L_1^*}S_i.
\]
The filtered image is contained in the full image, so its stored-color
biunion is contained in \(S(L_1)\).  A color in
\(\overline\varrho_1(v_n)\cap\bigcup_{i\in L_1^*}S_i\) would therefore
belong to \(\overline\varrho_1(v_n)\cap S(L_1)\), which is
\(\{\delta_p\}\) by (U).  It would have to equal \(\delta_p\), contrary
to the exclusion just proved.  The intersection is empty.  This is the
second assertion of (15), and completes all the Case 1 selection
properties.
\end{proof}

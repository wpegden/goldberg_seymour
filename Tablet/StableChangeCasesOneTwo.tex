\begin{lemma}[Stable change, cases 1 and 2]
Fix a finite loopless multigraph with its exact edge set, a palette of
$k$ colors, and a distinguished edge $e_0$.  Let $(T,\varphi_n)$ be a
positive-rung extended Tashkinov tree.  Let $\mathcal B$ be a hierarchy
base and let
\[
 T_n=T_{n,0}\subset T_{n,1}\subset\cdots\subset
 T_{n,q}\subset T_{n,q+1}=T
\]
be a good hierarchy $H$ under $\varphi_n$.  Put
\[
 A=T_{n,q}^{*}=\noderef{HierarchyStarredStage}(H,q),\qquad
 D_n=\noderef{TashkinovStoredColors}(\mathcal E,q_n),
 \qquad D_{n,q}=\noderef{HierarchyStoredColorsAt}(H,q).
\]
Assume that $T$ has maximum property, that $V(T)$ is not elementary,
that $\varphi_n$ is proper and leaves exactly $e_0$ uncolored, and that
these data form a \noderef{HierarchyCounterexample} $C$.

Assume, uniformly for every positive-rung maximum ETT on the same graph,
palette, and distinguished edge, with arbitrary certified input and
generating colorings and with rung number smaller than that of $T$, the
six lower-rung assertions of the technical theorem: elementarity,
terminal interchangeability, the unconditional PE stability-before-support
assertion, the predecessor-ladder stability assertion conditional on
first-appearance index greater than one, and the unconditional stored-pair
disjointness assertion, the terminal PE component assertion, the defective-endpoint
order assertion, and the mod-coloring assertion.  Assume also that every
same-rung maximum ETT with a good hierarchy having fewer than $q$
dividers is elementary.  Assume that $C$ is lexicographically minimal
among same-rung hierarchy counterexamples with exactly $q$ dividers, in
the sense of \noderef{HierarchyCounterexampleLess} and
\noderef{HierarchyCounterexampleMeasure}, and assume
\[
                 2n+11\leq |\overline\varphi_n(T_n)|.          \tag{L}
\]

Put $\ell=|T|-|A|$, the number of exact tail additions above $A$, and
\[
 T(y_{\ell-1})=T\big|_{\min\{|A|+(\ell-1),|T|\}}.
\]
Assume (6.6), namely that $V(T(y_{\ell-1}))$ is elementary under
$\varphi_n$.  Let $\alpha\ne\beta$ be colors missing on
$T(y_{\ell-1})$, and put
\[
 i_c=\noderef{HierarchyFirstMissingPosition}(\varphi_n,T,c),
 \qquad v_c=T.\mathit{vertex}(i_c).
\]

Let $z$ certify a possibly singleton
\noderef{TwoColorPathComponent} $Q$ of the full
$(\alpha,\beta)$-component through $z$, and suppose that the full node
support of $Q$ is disjoint from the full component through
$\operatorname{inl}v_\alpha$.  Thus $Q$ is not required to contain an
edge, and no path hypothesis is imposed on the component through
$v_\alpha$.  Put
\[
 \sigma_n=\noderef{TwoColorComponentSwap}
       (G,\varphi_n,\alpha,\beta,z).
\]
Suppose exactly one of the following two paper cases holds:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $q\geq1$ and $\alpha\in\overline\varphi_n(T_{n,q})$;
\item $q=0$ and either
$\alpha\in\overline\varphi_n(T_n)$, or
$\alpha\in\overline\varphi_n(A)$ with
$\alpha,\beta\notin D_n$.
\end{enumerate}
Then all of the following hold.
\begin{enumerate}
\item The coloring $\sigma_n$ is
$(T_{n,q}^{*},D_n,\varphi_n)$-weakly stable in the exact sense of
\noderef{HierarchyWeaklyStable}.
\item The coloring $\sigma_n$ is a
$\varphi_n\bmod T_n$ coloring in the sense of \noderef{ModColoring}.
Either $A=T_n$, the non-strict endpoint which the tablet's positive-rung
ETT type intentionally does not repackage, or $A$ has an actual maximum
ETT presentation under $\sigma_n$.
\item If $q\geq1$, the same ordered base core and closure and the stages
\[
 T_n=T_{n,0}\subset\cdots\subset T_{n,q}=A
\]
form a good hierarchy of an actual maximum ETT on $A$ under
$\sigma_n$.  The terminal extension kind of this prefix ETT is exactly
the terminal extension kind of the original ETT on $T$.  Its divider
count is $q-1$; at every retained index the sets $D_{n,j}$ agree exactly,
and
$\Gamma^j_\eta$ agrees exactly for every $\eta\in D_{n,j}$.  In addition,
\[
 T_{n,q}\text{ is }
 \left(\bigcup_{\eta\in D_{n,q}}\Gamma^{q-1}_\eta\right)^{-}
 \text{-closed under }\sigma_n.                                \tag{M}
\]
\end{enumerate}
No corresponding ETT or good-hierarchy assertion for the full tree $T$
is claimed in these two cases.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Unpack $C$ using \noderef{HierarchyCounterexample}.  Its fields supply
positivity, maximum property, the displayed
\noderef{TashkinovHierarchy}, its \noderef{GoodHierarchy} witness, and
non-elementarity.  Every component below is a
\noderef{TwoColorComponent} in the vertex--edge incidence graph, so its
edge nodes retain individual parallel-edge identities.

We first dispose of the zero-tail endpoint.  The ordered
initial-segment fields of the hierarchy put $A$ inside $T$, so
$|A|\leq|T|$.  If $\ell=0$, the definition $\ell=|T|-|A|$ forces equal
lengths, and the ordered vertex and exact-edge equalities give $A=T$
entry by entry.  Truncated subtraction makes the prefix in (6.6) the full-length
\noderef{TreeSequencePrefix} $T$, contradicting the non-elementarity
field of $C$.  Hence $\ell\geq1$.  It follows from the exact prefix and
hierarchy inclusions, including the vertex-union field of
\noderef{OrderedTreeUnion} for the PE core, that $A$, every displayed
stage, the PE closure, and the PE core all have vertex sets contained in
$T(y_{\ell-1})$.  Restricting
\noderef{ColoredVertexSetElementary} makes each of these sets elementary.
By \noderef{HierarchyFirstMissingPosition}, $v_\alpha$ and $v_\beta$ are
therefore actual first missing witnesses, and a color missing on a displayed
stage has a unique missing vertex there.

Properness and the fact that $\alpha$ is missing at $v_\alpha$ give the
path certificate which was deliberately not assumed in the statement.
At the vertex-node $\operatorname{inl}v_\alpha$ there is no
$\alpha$-edge-node and, by \noderef{IsProperEdgeColoring}, at most one
$\beta$-edge-node.  Hence its neighbor set in
\noderef{TwoColorIncidenceGraph} is a subsingleton.  The vertex-node itself
belongs to its full component, so it witnesses
\noderef{TwoColorPathComponent} for the component rooted at
$v_\alpha$.  This argument includes the case in which both colors are
missing there and the component is the isolated singleton.

We shall use the following consequence repeatedly.  Let $U$ be a
displayed tree containing $v_\alpha$, and suppose $U$ is
$\alpha,\beta$-interchangeable.  If a vertex-node of $Q$ belonged to
$V(U)$, then the assumed path certificate for $z$, the just-derived path
certificate at $v_\alpha$, and \noderef{TreeInterchangeable} would make
their two full components equal.  Both contain their roots, so equality
contradicts the assumed disjointness.  Thus every vertex-node of $Q$ is
outside $U$.  Taking the singleton root set $\{z\}$ and expanding
\noderef{TwoColorComponentSwap} verifies every clause of
\noderef{OutsideKempeRecoloring} for $U$.  Consequently
\noderef{OutsideKempeRecoloringStable} says that $\sigma_n$ is proper,
leaves exactly $e_0$ uncolored, preserves every missing-color set on
$U$, and fixes every exact edge identity incident with $U$.              \tag{1}

Independently of $U$, properness of $\sigma_n$ also follows from
\noderef{TwoColorComponentSwapProper}.  The swap fixes
$\mathsf{none}$ and exchanges only the two displayed nonempty values, so
\noderef{IsOneEdgeUncolored} again shows that exactly $e_0$ is uncolored.

We need one standard predecessor instance for the uses of
\noderef{rutcor}.  If $n=1$, the first conclusion of
\noderef{TechnicalTheoremBase} gives elementarity of $T_n$ under the
pre-step coloring.  If $n>1$, apply
\noderef{CertifiedETTRestriction} with target $r=n-1$.  It returns an
actual ETT on the original input, with rung exactly $n-1$, displayed tree
exactly $T_n$, generating coloring exactly $\varphi_{n-1}$, inherited
maximum property, full pointwise equality of retained raw and computed
data, and heterogeneous equality of retained certificates.  Lower
assertion (i) on that certified ETT gives the same elementarity.  Denote
this established pre-step fact
by (2).  The other nonstructural premise of every use of
\noderef{rutcor} below is exactly the ambient (6.6): the corrected lemma
uses the same last starred stage $A$, the same full-tail length
$\ell=|T|-|A|$, and the same prefix
$T(y_{\ell-1})$.  No comparison with the hierarchy path number is
needed.

We now treat the two cases.

\textbf{Case 1: $q\geq1$ and
$\alpha\in\overline\varphi_n(T_{n,q})$.}
Here $A=T_{n,q}$ by \noderef{HierarchyStarredStage}; in particular
$v_\alpha\in V(A)$.  Split according as $v_\beta$ belongs to $A$.

Suppose first that $v_\beta\in V(A)$.  In this subcase both colors are
missing on $A$.  The conditional ordering premise of
\noderef{ChangeLemma} is vacuous, so all its remaining hypotheses are
exactly the ambient lower-rung, fewer-divider, same-rung minimality, prefix, and
large-color hypotheses here.  Its case-1 conclusion gives
\[
 K_{v_\alpha}=K_{v_\beta}.                                    \tag{3}
\]
Thus the disjointness hypothesis also keeps $Q$ disjoint from the full
component rooted at $v_\beta$; neither first-missing vertex lies in $Q$.

Assume that one of $\alpha,\beta$ is singleton-closed on some starred
stage, and choose the largest index $r$ at which either singleton is
closed.  We do not infer missingness from closedness.  Instead,
missingness of both colors descends from $A$ to this stage.  Indeed, by
the least-witness definition in \noderef{HierarchyFirstMissingPosition},
if one of their first missing vertices occurred for the first time at a
later positive stage, the stage elementarity proved above and
\noderef{GoodHierarchyNewMissingClosed} would make that color closed at
that later stage, contradicting maximality.  Hence
\[
 v_\alpha,v_\beta\in V(T_{n,r}^{*}).                           \tag{4}
\]

Apply \noderef{rutcor}, with (6.6) and (2), separately to each affected color.  For
a color closed at $r$, its maximal-closed conclusion excludes that color
from every exact displayed edge after $T_{n,r}^{*}$ through $A$.  For the
other color, choose its own largest singleton-closed stage if one exists
and first apply the same missingness descent to it; that stage is no later
than $r$, and the same conclusion applies.  If no such stage exists,
the no-closed conclusion of \noderef{rutcor} excludes the color from all
displayed edges after $T_n$, hence in particular after
$T_{n,r}^{*}$.  Therefore
\[
 \alpha,\beta\notin
 \varphi_n\langle A-T_{n,r}^{*}\rangle.                        \tag{5}
\]

Place in the first color slot a color which is missing and closed at $r$.
If $r>0$, the hypotheses just established, the lower package,
fewer-divider induction, maximum property, and (L) are exactly those of
\noderef{step1}.  If $r=0$ and the terminal kind is PE, conclusion (iii)
of \noderef{interchange} applies to the exact core, with the reflexive
\noderef{CombinedStableColoring}.

Suppose $r=0$ and the terminal kind is SE or RE.  The nonparallel field of
\noderef{HierarchyBase} identifies the exact core with $T_n$.  Eliminate
the actual positive terminal constructor of
\noderef{TashkinovSeriesStep}, and write $\varphi_{n-1}$ and $D_{n-1}$ for
the predecessor coloring and stored set exposed by that constructor.  In
the SE constructor, its choice is a
\noderef{MaximumDefectiveChoice}.  The fields `selectedProper',
`selectedOneUncolored', and `selectedStable' say respectively that the
selected coloring is proper, leaves exactly $e_0$ uncolored, and is
$(T_n,D_{n-1},\varphi_{n-1})$-stable.  The constructor's
`currentColoring' equation identifies this selected coloring with
$\varphi_n$, so all three assertions transport to $\varphi_n$.  In the RE
constructor, `previousProper' and `previousOneUncolored' give the first
two assertions, while `currentColoring' is the literal equality
$\varphi_n=\varphi_{n-1}$.  After this equality, the two clauses of
\noderef{StableColoring} are reflexive: every controlled incident edge
value and every vertex missing-color set equals itself.  Thus RE supplies
the same stable-coloring premise.

If $n=1$, these are exactly the properness, one-uncoloredness, and
stability inputs to the interchangeability conclusion of
\noderef{TechnicalTheoremBase} on the initial closure $T_n$.  If $n>1$,
use the certified rung-$(n-1)$ ETT supplied by
\noderef{CertifiedETTRestriction} in (2); lower assertion (ii) applies
to its inherited maximum property and the same three
inputs.  The chosen first-slot color is missing on $T_n$, so the remaining
disjunctive missing-color premise is satisfied.  Hence $T_n$ is
interchangeable also in the non-PE case.  If the closed color was $\beta$,
these conclusions
are initially written with the displayed color order reversed; the
definitions of \noderef{TwoColorComponent},
\noderef{TwoColorPathComponent}, and \noderef{TreeInterchangeable} are
symmetric in the two colors, so they give the stated $\alpha,\beta$ order.
Thus $T_{n,r}^{*}$ is interchangeable in all cases.
Together with (4), observation (1) makes the swap an outside recoloring of
this exact controlled stage.                                           \tag{6}

Now suppose instead that neither affected singleton is closed on any
starred stage.  The no-closed conclusion of \noderef{rutcor}, applied
to both colors, gives
\[
 \Theta_n=PE,\qquad
 v_\alpha,v_\beta\in V(T_n)-V(R_n),                            \tag{7}
\]
puts each affected color in every appropriate reserved Gamma union, and
excludes both colors from the exact displayed edges of $A-T_n$.
Conclusion (ii) of \noderef{interchange}, with reflexive combined
stability, makes $T_n$ interchangeable because $\alpha$ is missing there.
Observation (1) therefore makes $Q$ outside $T_n$.  Elementarity of the
PE core and (7) say that neither affected color is missing on $R_n$.
Every exact edge added in the Tashkinov closure of the support prefix to
$R_n$ has, by \noderef{TashkinovAugmentation}, a color missing on its
preceding subtree; consequently no such edge has color $\alpha$ or
$\beta$.  The support prefix lies in $T_n$ and is fixed by (1), so all
displayed edge values of $R_n$ are preserved.  More directly, neither
affected color is missing at any vertex of $R_n$.  Since the swap changes
only $\alpha$ and $\beta$, every $R_n$ vertex has the same missing-color
set.  If an edge identity incident with $R_n$ has old color in
$\overline\varphi_n(R_n)$, that color is not $\alpha$ or $\beta$, so the
swap fixes the edge.  These are precisely the vertex and incident-edge
clauses of \noderef{StableColoring} for
$(R_n,\varnothing,\varphi_n)$.  Together with the
$(T_n,D_n,\varphi_n)$-stable relation supplied by (1), this is the second
conjunct required by \noderef{CombinedStableColoring}.                  \tag{8}

Return to the alternative $v_\beta\notin V(A)$.  Now $\beta$ is missing
at no vertex of $A$.  If some starred stage is singleton-closed for
$\alpha$, choose the largest such index $r$.  The same descent,
\noderef{rutcor}, and interchangeability argument used for (4)--(6) gives
$v_\alpha\in T_{n,r}^{*}$, makes $Q$ outside that stage, and excludes
$\alpha$ from the exact tail to $A$.  Every ordinary edge of that tail is
introduced by \noderef{TashkinovAugmentation}; its color is missing on an
earlier prefix.  Since $v_\beta$ is the first $\beta$-missing vertex in the
sense of \noderef{HierarchyFirstMissingPosition} and lies after $A$, none
of those ordinary identities has color $\beta$.
There is exactly one possible exception: $r=0$, the core is $T_n$, and
the first displayed edge is the SE/RE connecting seed $f_n$ with
$\beta\in D_n$.  Eliminate the actual SE or RE constructor of
\noderef{TashkinovSeriesStep}.  In SE, the singleton connecting-edge
equation identifies $f_n$ with the exact edge of its
\noderef{MaximumDefectiveChoice}.  The field `vertexDefective.1' puts the
chosen vertex in $V(T_n)$, and `edgeIncident' says that this exact edge is
incident with that vertex.  In RE, the singleton equation identifies
$f_n$ with the edge of the
\noderef{RevisitingExtensionCertificate}; `insideInCurrent' puts its
inside endpoint in $V(T_n)$, and `edgeEnds' says that this exact edge is
incident with that endpoint.  Thus in either constructor the singleton
equation first identifies the stored seed and the constructor payload then
proves that this same identity is incident with $T_n$.  Only now does (1)
fix its value.  This is precisely the
stored-seed exception, not a claim that every later segment color is
unchanged.                                                              \tag{9}

If no starred stage is singleton-closed for $\alpha$, apply the
no-closed conclusion of \noderef{rutcor}.  It gives the PE kind,
$v_\alpha\in V(T_n)-V(R_n)$, membership of $\alpha$ in every later
reserved Gamma union, and absence of $\alpha$ from $A-T_n$.
Again \noderef{interchange}(ii) makes $T_n$ interchangeable and (1) makes
$Q$ outside it.  The color $\beta$ is missing nowhere on $A$, so neither
it nor $\alpha$ is missing at any vertex of $R_n$.  Therefore the swap
preserves every vertex missing-color set on $R_n$, and every edge incident
with $R_n$ whose old color lies in $\overline\varphi_n(R_n)$ has an
unaffected color and is fixed.  By the two clauses of
\noderef{StableColoring}, this is
$(R_n,\varnothing,\varphi_n)$-stability; together with the relation from
(1), \noderef{CombinedStableColoring} preserves the PE base.  Tashkinov
augmentation excludes $\beta$ from the displayed edges after the base
because it is missing nowhere on the preceding prefixes.               \tag{10}

We summarize what (5)--(10) prove in every subcase of Case 1.  Every exact
displayed edge of $A$ has the same value under $\varphi_n$ and $\sigma_n$.
At $v_\alpha$, and also at $v_\beta$ when it lies in $A$, the swapped
component is disjoint by hypothesis and (3).  At every other vertex of
the elementary tree $A$, both affected colors are present; if its
vertex-node lies in $Q$, the two incident affected edges are exchanged
together, and otherwise neither is changed.  All other colors are fixed.
Thus
\[
 \sigma_n(A.\mathit{edge}(i))=\varphi_n(A.\mathit{edge}(i)),
 \qquad
 \overline\sigma_n(v)=\overline\varphi_n(v)\qquad(v\in V(A)). \tag{11}
\]
The base stability in (6), or the combined stability in (8) and (10),
together with the tree invariant (11), is exactly
\noderef{HierarchyWeaklyStable} at the positive last index $q$.

\textbf{Case 2: $q=0$.}
Now $A=T_{n,0}^{*}$ is the exact hierarchy core.  The case hypothesis
always makes $\alpha$ missing on $A$: either it is already missing on the
initial segment $T_n$, or this is explicit.  As in Case 1, if
$v_\beta\in A$, the conditional premise of \noderef{ChangeLemma} is
vacuous and its case-2 conclusion identifies the components rooted at
$v_\alpha$ and $v_\beta$; hence $Q$ contains neither root.              \tag{12}

First suppose that one of $\alpha,\beta$ is both missing and
singleton-closed on $A$.  This explicit conjunction is the formal meaning
of choosing a closed tree-missing color; mere boundary emptiness is not
used as missingness.  If the terminal kind is PE, apply conclusion (iii)
of \noderef{interchange}, putting that missing closed color first and
using reflexive combined stability.

Suppose instead that the terminal kind is SE or RE.
\noderef{HierarchyBase} identifies $A=T_n$.  Eliminate the actual terminal
\noderef{TashkinovSeriesStep}.  In SE, the terminal
\noderef{MaximumDefectiveChoice} supplies `selectedProper',
`selectedOneUncolored', and `selectedStable', and `currentColoring'
transports all three fields to $\varphi_n$; hence $\varphi_n$ is proper,
leaves exactly $e_0$ uncolored, and is
$(T_n,D_{n-1},\varphi_{n-1})$-stable.  In RE, `previousProper' and
`previousOneUncolored' supply the first two facts, `currentColoring'
identifies $\varphi_n$ with $\varphi_{n-1}$, and reflexivity of the
incident-edge and vertex clauses in \noderef{StableColoring} supplies the
stable relation.  For $n=1$, the interchangeability conclusion of
\noderef{TechnicalTheoremBase} applies to these exact inputs.  For $n>1$,
the certified predecessor ETT from \noderef{CertifiedETTRestriction}
has maximum property and lower assertion (ii) applies.  In both cases the chosen closed color is also missing on
$T_n$, discharging the last premise and giving the same interchangeability.
When the missing closed color is $\beta$, symmetry of
\noderef{TwoColorComponent}, \noderef{TwoColorPathComponent}, and
\noderef{TreeInterchangeable} restores the displayed $\alpha,\beta$ order.
Observation (1) makes $Q$ an outside recoloring of all of $A$.  In the
non-PE case $A=T_n$, so \noderef{OutsideKempeRecoloringStable}, read with
control set $D_n$, gives the required
$(T_n,D_n,\varphi_n)$-relation in the sense of
\noderef{StableColoring}.  In PE, both $T_n$ and $R_n$ lie in the core
$A$; applying the same outside-stability conclusion to $T_n$ with control
set $D_n$ and to $R_n$ with the empty control set gives the two conjuncts
of \noderef{CombinedStableColoring}.                                    \tag{13}

It remains that neither affected color which is missing on $A$ is closed
there.  We show in detail that the terminal kind cannot be SE or RE.  In
either event the nonparallel field of \noderef{HierarchyBase} gives
$A=T_n$.  Eliminate the actual non-PE constructor in
\noderef{TashkinovSeriesStep}.  In RE, `previousClosed' says that
$V(T_n)$ is \noderef{ColoredVertexSetClosed} under $\varphi_{n-1}$, and
`currentColoring' is the equality $\varphi_n=\varphi_{n-1}$, so the same
closedness holds under $\varphi_n$.  By the boundary-edge meaning of this
definition, every color missing at a vertex of $T_n$ has empty singleton
boundary, which is exactly singleton closedness in
\noderef{TreeColorSetClosed}.

In SE, write $\pi$ for the selected coloring.  The predecessor is proper
and leaves exactly $e_0$ uncolored by the constructor, while
\noderef{MaximumDefectiveChoice} says that $\pi$ is proper, leaves exactly
$e_0$ uncolored, and is $(T_n,D_{n-1},\varphi_{n-1})$-stable.  Symmetry in
\noderef{sc2} gives the reverse relation: $\varphi_{n-1}$ is
$(T_n,D_{n-1},\pi)$-stable.  Suppose a color $c$ is missing on $T_n$ under
$\pi$ and an exact boundary identity has color $c$ under $\pi$.  The
vertex clause of \noderef{StableColoring} makes $c$ missing at the same
vertex under $\varphi_{n-1}$; the boundary edge is incident with $T_n$, so
the incident-edge clause of the reverse stable relation fixes its value.
This contradicts `previousClosed' after expanding
\noderef{ColoredVertexSetClosed}.  Thus every $\pi$-missing color is
singleton-closed.  The `currentColoring' equation identifies
$\pi=\varphi_n$.  In both non-PE constructors, therefore, $\alpha$, which
is missing on $A=T_n$, is closed there, contradicting the present branch.
Thus
$\Theta_n=PE$.  The common-missing closure conclusion of
\noderef{ParallelCommonMissingClosed}, with predecessor elementarity (2),
shows that an actual first missing witness, as specified by
\noderef{HierarchyFirstMissingPosition}, lying in
$V(T_n)\cap V(R_n)$ would make its color closed on $A=T_n\vee R_n$.
Therefore
\[
 v_\alpha\notin V(T_n)\cap V(R_n),
\]
and the same holds for $v_\beta$ whenever $v_\beta\in A$.              \tag{14}

The vertex-set equality in \noderef{OrderedTreeUnion} makes the following
constituent-location alternatives exhaustive.

If the two first-missing vertices lie in opposite constituents, conclusion
(iv) of \noderef{interchange}, with the color names ordered according to
the two locations, makes the whole core $A$ interchangeable.  Hence $Q$
is outside $A$ by (1).  Apply \noderef{OutsideKempeRecoloringStable} to
the two subtrees of $A$: with control set $D_n$ on $T_n$ and the empty set
on $R_n$, its two \noderef{StableColoring} conclusions are exactly
\noderef{CombinedStableColoring}.                                      \tag{15}

Suppose next that $v_\alpha\in V(T_n)-V(R_n)$.  If $v_\beta\in A$, then
(12), (14), and the same-constituent alternative put it in
$V(T_n)-V(R_n)$; if it is outside $A$, it is missing nowhere on $R_n$.
Conclusion (ii) of \noderef{interchange} makes $T_n$ interchangeable, so
$Q$ is outside $T_n$ and (1) gives $(T_n,D_n,\varphi_n)$-stability.
Elementarity of the core says that neither affected color is missing on
$R_n$.  Thus every $R_n$ vertex retains its missing set under the swap.
Moreover, any edge incident with $R_n$ whose old color is missing on
$R_n$ has a color different from $\alpha,\beta$ and is fixed.  This is
precisely the incident-edge clause of \noderef{StableColoring}, while the
preceding sentence is its vertex clause.  Thus
$(R_n,\varnothing,\varphi_n)$-stability, together with the stable relation
on $T_n$, gives \noderef{CombinedStableColoring}.                       \tag{16}

Finally suppose that $v_\alpha\in V(R_n)-V(T_n)$.  If $v_\beta\in A$ it
lies in the same exclusive constituent, and otherwise it lies outside the
core.  Conclusion (i) of \noderef{interchange} makes $R_n$
interchangeable, so $Q$ is outside $R_n$.  Here $\alpha$ is not missing on
$T_n$; hence the case-2 disjunction forces the second alternative and
gives $\alpha,\beta\notin D_n$.  Neither color is missing on $T_n$, by
elementarity and the locations.  The statement
\noderef{HkuTreeColorsControlled} excludes both colors from every exact
displayed edge of $T_n$, since a used tree color lies in
$\overline\varphi_n(T_n)\cup D_n$.  Any incident edge whose color belongs
to $\overline\varphi_n(T_n)\cup D_n$ likewise has an unaffected color and
is fixed.  Missing sets on $T_n$ are preserved because both affected
colors are present at every one of its vertices.  These are exactly the
incident-edge and vertex clauses of \noderef{StableColoring}, and give the
first stable relation.  Outside stability on $R_n$ gives the second, so
\noderef{CombinedStableColoring} holds again.                            \tag{17}

Equations (13), (15), (16), and (17) prove
\noderef{HierarchyWeaklyStable} at the zero index.  They also prove the
analogue of (11) for the exact core $A$.  In (16), every edge added in the
closure $R_n$ has a color missing on its preceding subtree and hence is
neither $\alpha$ nor $\beta$; in (17), the $R_n$ edges are fixed by
outside stability, hence by \noderef{StableColoring}, and the $T_n$ edges
are fixed by \noderef{HkuTreeColorsControlled}.  The vertex clauses of the
same stable relations give equality of all missing sets.  Thus in every
Case-2 branch all displayed edge identities of $A$ and all vertexwise
missing sets agree.                                                     \tag{18}

We now establish the ETT and mod-coloring conclusions uniformly.  Inspect
the terminal positive \noderef{TashkinovSeriesStep}.  In PE it supplies a
\noderef{TashkinovClosure}, hence a
\noderef{TashkinovAugmentationSequence}, from $T_n$ to the certified final
closure.  In SE or RE it supplies an individual connecting edge $f_n$, the
exact singleton equation $F_n=\{f_n\}$, a
\noderef{TreeSequenceAdjoinEdge} seed $T_n+f_n$, and an augmentation
sequence from that seed to the same final closure.  The hierarchy makes
$A$ an exact ordered initial segment of $T$, and
\noderef{ExtendedTashkinovTree} makes $T$ an exact ordered initial segment
of that closure.  Both are entrywise prefix relations in the sense of
\noderef{TreeSequenceInitialSegment}.  In the non-PE case with $q=0$,
$A=T_n$ and no strict route is required.  In every other case the relevant
seed is an initial segment of $A$; for the SE/RE seed this follows from its
one-edge length, the strict hierarchy stage, and equality of entries in the
common certified closure.  Apply
\noderef{TashkinovAugmentationSequenceRestrict} to obtain the exact old
route to $A$.                                                           \tag{19}

At each step of (19), the new edge is the exact next displayed identity of
$A$, and its color is missing at a vertex of the preceding prefix by
\noderef{TashkinovAugmentation}.  Equations (11) and (18) preserve both
the edge value and that missing-color witness.  Length, attachment, and
ordered-prefix clauses do not depend on the coloring.  Hence the same
route is a $\sigma_n$-augmentation sequence, retaining the exact SE/RE
seed identity when present.                                             \tag{20}

Weak stability contains $(T_n,D_n,\varphi_n)$-stability in both terminal
kind branches.  Apply \noderef{Td}, with the given lower package, original
maximum property, positivity, and the proved properness and unique
uncolored edge.  It yields
\[
                         \noderef{ModColoring}(\mathcal E,\sigma_n).     \tag{21}
\]
If $A=T_n$, this is the stated endpoint.  Otherwise (20) is exactly the PE
eligibility clause, or the exact SE/RE seed clause, in (21).  Its universal
property gives a \noderef{CorrespondingExtendedTashkinovTree} on $A$, and
\noderef{MaximumPropertyTransport} gives maximum property.  This proves
conclusions 1 and 2 in both paper cases.

Assume finally that $q>0$.  Apply
\noderef{GoodHierarchyPrefixRepackage} to the original ETT, maximum
witness, base, hierarchy, and good witness at the positive internal index
$q$.  It returns an old-color maximum ETT on the exact ordered tree
$A=T_{n,q}$ with the same certified series, a base with the same core and
closure, and a good hierarchy $H_A$ of divider count $q-1$ having the
exact retained stages, stored sets, and total Gamma values.  It also
returns the old terminal minus-closed assertion corresponding to (M).   \tag{22}

If the terminal kind is PE, apply \noderef{HierarchySplitter} to this
prefix ETT.  Its combined-stability premise is the PE projection of the
proved weak stability, and its build premise is the PE route in (20).  It
returns a corresponding maximum ETT under $\sigma_n$, the same core and
closure, and the same retained ordered stages and dividers.  In the SE/RE
case use the corresponding ETT already obtained from (21), rebuild the
base with the common exact $T_n$ as core and the unused closure field, and
copy the retained stages and dividers from $H_A$.  The equal series,
ladder trees, stored sets, connecting edges, and terminal non-PE kind are
the statement-level fields of
\noderef{CorrespondingExtendedTashkinovTree}; every hierarchy field then
has exactly its old ordered-tree proof.  Denote the resulting hierarchy
under $\sigma_n$ by $K$.                                                \tag{23}

We also record the terminal extension kind of the ETT underlying $K$.
In the PE branch, the correspondence returned by
\noderef{HierarchySplitter} identifies its replacement kind with the
terminal kind of the old-color prefix ETT.  The latter prefix ETT has,
by \noderef{GoodHierarchyPrefixRepackage}, the same entire certified
series as the original ETT, so equality transport identifies its terminal
kind with the original terminal kind.  Transitivity gives the asserted
equality.  In the SE/RE branch, the correspondence obtained directly from
(21) identifies the replacement kind with the original kind at every
stored index, in particular at the terminal index.  Thus the same equality
holds in both branches.                                                 \tag{23a}

Copy every Gamma pair from (22).  Equations (11) give equality of all
stage and starred-stage missing sets.  Correspondence gives equality of
the raw stored sets, so the definition
\noderef{HierarchyStoredColorsAt} gives equality of every retained
$D_{n,j}$.  We check the five fields of \noderef{GoodHierarchy}.
Pair cardinality and pairwise disjointness are literal.  For avoidance,
fix a retained $j,\eta$, a copied Gamma color $c$, and an exact segment
edge position.  Missing-set equality makes the first $\eta$-missing
position the same, and (11) fixes the value of that individual displayed
edge.  Thus a new occurrence of $c$ would be the same old occurrence,
contrary to old avoidance.  This is a pointwise argument; it does not
assert a generic equality of \noderef{HierarchySegmentColors} under a
two-color swap.  Unique refresh copies because both exact stage
differences, all their vertex missing sets, the stored sets, and Gamma
pairs agree.                                                            \tag{24}

For the PE base field, the common-missing cardinality is unchanged by
combined stability.  Consider an old exact boundary witness to failure of
minus-closedness.  More explicitly,
\noderef{TreeColorSetMinusClosed} expands the old failure into failure of
\noderef{TreeColorSetClosed}: there is an old-missing color outside the
stage-zero reserved union and an exact boundary identity having that old
color.  In a maximal-closed branch the base core lies inside
the controlled outside stage, so (1) fixes that boundary identity.  In a
no-closed Case-1 branch, \noderef{rutcor} puts every affected color which
is missing on the core into the stage-zero reserved Gamma union; hence a
witness color outside that union is not $\alpha$ or $\beta$ and its exact
edge value is fixed.  Combined stability makes the witness color
new-missing as well, and boundary membership depends only on its endpoints.
Thus the same color and edge witness failure of the new
\noderef{TreeColorSetClosed} predicate, hence the new non-minus-closed
clause.  The non-PE implication is vacuous.                              \tag{25}

For an internal minus-closed field, expand
\noderef{TreeColorSetMinusClosed} through
\noderef{TreeColorSetClosed}: let $c$ be missing on the retained stage but
outside its reserved union, and suppose an exact boundary identity has new
color $c$.  At or below the controlled outside stage, (1) fixes the
identity.  Above it, maximality says no affected color is singleton-closed.
If an affected color is missing there, the old minus-closed field of
\noderef{GoodHierarchy}, read as the corresponding
\noderef{TreeColorSetClosed} assertion, forces it into the relevant
reserved union; an
affected color not missing there cannot be $c$.  Therefore $c$ is
unaffected, the swap fixes the boundary identity, and (11) makes $c$
old-missing as well.  The exact identity is therefore an old
$c$-colored boundary edge, contradicting the old closedness of the color
set.  This proves every internal field, including its last instance.    \tag{26}

Equations (22)--(26) construct the required good hierarchy on $A$, with
the exact retained base, stages, stored sets, and paper Gamma pairs.  The
separately returned last field from
\noderef{GoodHierarchyPrefixRepackage}, transported by the same boundary
argument (26), is exactly
\[
 T_{n,q}\text{ is }
 \left(\bigcup_{\eta\in D_{n,q}}\Gamma^{q-1}_\eta\right)^-
 \text{-closed under }\sigma_n.
\]
This is (M) and completes conclusion 3.  No step constructed or used a
replacement hierarchy on the full tree $T$, as required for cases 1 and 2.
\end{proof}

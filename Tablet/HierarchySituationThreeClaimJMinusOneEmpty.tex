\begin{lemma}[Situation 7.3, Claim \(j-1\): empty case]
Fix a rung-local minimum hierarchy counterexample \(C\), set

\[
 j=\noderef{HierarchyPathNumber}(C.\mathit{hierarchy}),\qquad
 \ell=\noderef{HierarchyFullTailLength}(C),
\]

and assume \(2\le j<\ell\).  Assume every vertex has a missing color
under every proper exactly-one-uncolored replacement coloring.  If
\(\noderef{HierarchyEligibleCommonMissingIndices}\) is empty, then there
nevertheless are an actual
\noderef{HierarchyReplacementPresentation} \(P\) and a color \(\alpha\)
missing under \(P.\mathit{coloring}\) both at the terminal vertex and
somewhere in the exact prefix \(T(y_{j-2})\), such that, with the notation
\(D=D_{n,q}\), \(\Gamma^q=\bigcup_{\eta\in D}\Gamma^q_\eta\), and
\(M_0=\overline\varphi(P.\mathit{replacementBase}.\mathit{core}-T_n)\),
either

\[
                       \alpha\notin\Gamma^q\cup M_0,          \tag{A}
\]

or \(\alpha\in\Gamma^q_\eta\) for some \(\eta\in D\) whose first
missing vertex occurs no later than \(y_{j-2}\).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Put \(j=\noderef{HierarchyPathNumber}(H)\) and
\(\ell=\noderef{HierarchyFullTailLength}(C)\), where
\(H=C.\mathit{hierarchy}\), and let \(q\) be the last stored index.
Write
\[
\begin{gathered}
 A=\noderef{HierarchyStarredStage}(H,q),\qquad
 B=T(y_{j-2}),\qquad B^+=T(y_{j-1}),\\
 B_{\rm pen}=T(y_{\ell-1}),\qquad
 D=\noderef{HierarchyStoredColorsAt}(H,q),\\
 T_n=\mathcal E.\mathit{series.data.tree}(n),\qquad
 D_n=\noderef{TashkinovStoredColors}(\mathcal E.\mathit{series.data},n),\\
 M_0=\bigcup_{v\in V(\mathit{base.core})\setminus V(T_n)}
                  \noderef{MissingColors}(G,\varphi,v),\\
 \Gamma^q=\bigcup_{\eta\in D}C.\mathit{good}.\Gamma^q_\eta .
                                                               \tag{1}
\end{gathered}
\]
Here \(V(X)\) means \(\noderef{TreeSequenceVertexSet}(X)\).  Every
\(T(y_s)\) is the exact \noderef{HierarchyFullTailPrefix}; the fields of
\noderef{TashkinovHierarchy} and the entrywise relation
\noderef{TreeSequenceInitialSegment}, together with \(2\le j<\ell\), give
the exact
prefix chain
\[
              A\preccurlyeq B\prec B^+\preccurlyeq
              B_{\rm pen}\prec T.                              \tag{2}
\]

\textbf{The identity presentation and the initial color.}
We first enumerate the fields of an identity
\noderef{HierarchyReplacementPresentation} \(P_0\).  Its
\texttt{coloring} is \(\varphi\); its \texttt{corresponding} field is the
reflexive \noderef{CorrespondingExtendedTashkinovTree}, with replacement
input, series, ETT, rung, ladder trees, raw stored sets, connecting sets,
extension kinds, proper colorings, one-uncolored colorings, and positive
stable relations all chosen identically; its \texttt{maximum} field is
\(C.\mathit{maximum}\); its \texttt{replacementBase} is
\(\mathit{base}\); its \texttt{counterexample} is \(C\); its
\texttt{sameGood} field is reflexive
\noderef{SameGoodHierarchy}; and its \texttt{minimumContext} is the
supplied \noderef{HierarchyMinimumCounterexampleContext}.  Thus all seven
fields, including the context rather
than merely its conclusion, have been given.

Apply \noderef{HierarchyCounterexamplePenultimateElementary} to, in
order,
\[
 \mathit{ctx.proper},\quad\mathit{ctx.oneEdgeUncolored},\quad
 \mathit{ctx.lowerRung},\quad\mathit{ctx.fewerDividers},\quad
 \mathit{ctx.lexicographicMinimality},\quad
 \mathit{ctx.largeMissingSet}.
\]
It gives
\[
        V(B_{\rm pen})\text{ is elementary under }\varphi.      \tag{3}
\]
By \noderef{ColoredVertexSetElementary}, this means precisely that the
missing-color sets of two distinct displayed vertices are disjoint.
Apply \noderef{HierarchyCounterexampleTerminalCommonMissing} to the same
six fields and choose
\[
 \alpha\in\noderef{TreeMissingColors}(\varphi,B_{\rm pen})
       \cap\noderef{MissingColors}(G,\varphi,y_\ell).           \tag{4}
\]
If an \(\alpha\)-missing witness in \(B_{\rm pen}\) were a tail vertex
\(y_s\), \(1\le s\le\ell-1\), then the defining filter of
\noderef{HierarchyEligibleCommonMissingIndices} would contain
\((s,P_0,\alpha)\), contrary to the assumed emptiness.  The entrywise
description in \noderef{TreeSequence} says that the remaining vertices
of \(B_{\rm pen}\) are exactly those of \(A\).  Hence
\[
       \alpha\in\noderef{TreeMissingColors}(\varphi,A)
               \subseteq\noderef{TreeMissingColors}(\varphi,B). \tag{5}
\]
If \(\alpha\notin\Gamma^q\cup M_0\), equations (4)--(5) make
\((P_0,\alpha)\) the required witness.  Otherwise either
\(\alpha\in M_0\setminus\Gamma^q\), or
\(\alpha\in C.\mathit{good}.\Gamma^q_{\eta_m}\) for some
\(\eta_m\in D\).  We treat those alternatives separately.

\textbf{Two facts used in every switch.}
First we prove, universally, that no stored color belongs to any last
Gamma pair.  Fix \(\rho,\eta\in D\) and
\(c\in C.\mathit{good}.\Gamma^q_\eta\).  The avoidance field of
\noderef{GoodHierarchy} puts \(c\) in
\noderef{HierarchyStageMissingColors}(H,q)\).  If \(q>0\), this is the
set missing on the last starred stage \(A\).  If \(q=0\), it is the set
missing on \(T_n\), and \(T_n\preccurlyeq A\) by the parallel
\noderef{HierarchyBase} union or by the nonparallel core equality.
Thus in either case \(c\in\overline\varphi(A)\).  On the other hand,
unfolding \noderef{HierarchyStoredColorsAt} gives
\[
                   D=D_n\setminus\overline\varphi(A),           \tag{6}
\]
so \(\rho\notin\overline\varphi(A)\), and therefore \(\rho\ne c\).
Since \(\rho,\eta\) were arbitrary,
\[
                  D\cap\Gamma^q=\varnothing.                   \tag{7}
\]
Together with the \texttt{twoColors} and \texttt{disjointPairs} fields
of \noderef{GoodHierarchy}, (7) also says that the three-color blocks
\[
                  Q_\eta=C.\mathit{good}.\Gamma^q_\eta\cup\{\eta\}
                                                                  \tag{8}
\]
are pairwise disjoint.

Second, suppose distinct colors \(c,d\) are missing on \(B_{\rm pen}\),
\noderef{ChangeLemma} has identified their components through their
first missing vertices, and \(c\) is missing at \(y_\ell\).  If the two
first missing vertices coincide, that common vertex misses both colors,
so its vertex-node has degree zero in
\noderef{TwoColorIncidenceGraph}; its
\noderef{TwoColorComponent} is the isolated singleton and cannot contain
the distinct terminal vertex.  If the witnesses differ, the two
witnesses and \(y_\ell\) are three distinct vertex-nodes of degree at
most one by \noderef{MissingColorIncidenceEndpoint}.  Were the terminal
component the identified witness component,
\noderef{TwoColorComponentSpanningPath} would give a simple path
spanning it, but a path in the degree-at-most-two graph of
\noderef{TwoColorIncidenceDegreeAtMostTwo} has at most two such ends.
This is impossible.  In both cases
\noderef{TwoColorComponentsDisjoint} makes the terminal component
disjoint from the first-witness component.  The same endpoint and
spanning-path facts give a possibly singleton
\noderef{TwoColorPathComponent} certificate for the terminal component.
After switching this terminal component, (d) is missing at
(y_\ell).  Indeed, if the component is the isolated terminal vertex,
then both affected colors were already missing there.  Otherwise the
terminal vertex has degree one: its unique affected incident edge has
color (d), since (c) is missing, and the swap changes that edge to
color (c).  Thus no (d)-edge remains incident with (y_\ell).

For any such disjoint terminal-component swap \(\psi\mapsto\psi'\), we
will use the following pointwise observation:
\[
 \noderef{MissingColors}(G,\psi',v)=
 \noderef{MissingColors}(G,\psi,v)
                \qquad(v\in V(B_{\rm pen})).                   \tag{9}
\]
If the vertex-node \(v\) is outside the switched component, adjacency in
\noderef{TwoColorIncidenceGraph} keeps every affected incident edge
outside it.  If it is inside, disjointness keeps it away from both first
missing witnesses.  Elementarity (3) then makes both affected colors
present at \(v\).  Expanding \noderef{PresentColors} and using
\noderef{IsProperEdgeColoring}
gives exactly one incident edge of each affected color; connectedness
puts both edge-nodes in the switched component, and
\noderef{TwoColorComponentSwap} exchanges them.  Every other incident
color is fixed.  This proves (9), including when the terminal component
meets the prefix internally; it proves no displayed-edge equality.
Every switch below is proper by
\noderef{TwoColorComponentSwapProper}, and it leaves exactly \(e_0\)
uncolored in the sense of \noderef{IsOneEdgeUncolored}, because the
transposition fixes the value \(\mathsf{none}\).

\textbf{Repackaging rule.}
We record explicitly how each full-tree output below becomes a member of
the original replacement family.  Suppose a provider returns a
corresponding ETT \(\mathcal C'\) on the same exact \(T\), maximum
property \(M'\), a base \(\mathcal B'\), a hierarchy \(K'\), goodness
\(g'\), and
\(\noderef{SameGoodHierarchy}(C.\mathit{good},g')\), and suppose an
affected color is missing at \(y_\ell\) and at a vertex of the strict
prefix \(B\).  Define a \noderef{HierarchyCounterexample} \(C'\) field by
field: positivity is \(C.\mathit{positive}\) transported by
\(\mathcal C'.\mathit{rungAgreement}\), the hierarchy is \(K'\), goodness
is \(g'\), maximum property is \(M'\), and non-elementarity follows from
the two distinct missing vertices.

Now enumerate the seven replacement-presentation fields:
\[
 \texttt{coloring}=\psi',\quad
 \texttt{corresponding}=\mathcal C',\quad
 \texttt{maximum}=M',\quad
 \texttt{replacementBase}=\mathcal B',\quad
 \texttt{counterexample}=C',\quad
 \texttt{sameGood}=\noderef{SameGoodHierarchy}(C.\mathit{good},g'),
                                                                  \tag{10}
\]
and let \texttt{minimumContext} be the following structure, whose six
fields are also explicit.
\begin{enumerate}
\item \texttt{proper} and \texttt{oneEdgeUncolored} are the terminal
properness and exact one-uncoloredness fields of \(\mathcal C'\), also
verified directly above for the component swap.
\item \texttt{lowerRung} is \(\mathit{ctx.lowerRung}\): rewrite the
strict rung comparison by \(\mathcal C'.\mathit{rungAgreement}\).
\item \texttt{fewerDividers} is \(\mathit{ctx.fewerDividers}\): rewrite
the same-rung premise by rung agreement and the divider inequality by
the divider equality in \noderef{SameGoodHierarchy}.
\item \texttt{lexicographicMinimality} is
\(\mathit{ctx.lexicographicMinimality}\).  The same-good stage and
divider equalities make the path number and terminal-stage excess equal,
so both coordinates of
\noderef{HierarchyCounterexampleMeasure} for \(C'\) and \(C\) agree.
\item \texttt{largeMissingSet} follows from rung and terminal-ladder
agreement in \(\mathcal C'\), together with (9) on
\(T_n\preccurlyeq A\preccurlyeq B_{\rm pen}\).
\end{enumerate}
Thus (10), with this sixth field, is an original-relative
\noderef{HierarchyReplacementPresentation}; none of the six minimum
conditions is inferred merely from the replacement label.

\textbf{The base-extra alternative.}
Assume
\[
                   \alpha\in M_0\setminus\Gamma^q.             \tag{11}
\]
By \noderef{HierarchyStoredColorsCardinality},
\[
                             |D|\le n.                          \tag{12}
\]
The two-color and disjoint-pair clauses of
\noderef{GoodHierarchy} give
\[
              |\Gamma^q|=\sum_{\eta\in D}
                    |C.\mathit{good}.\Gamma^q_\eta|
                         =2|D|\le2n.                            \tag{13}
\]
We also need the deletion of \(M_0\), rather than hiding it in this
count.  If a color belonged both to
\(\overline\varphi(T_n)\) and to \(M_0\), it would be missing at one
vertex of \(T_n\) and at a different vertex of
\(V(\mathit{base.core})\setminus V(T_n)\).  Both vertices lie in
\(B_{\rm pen}\), contradicting elementarity (3).  Hence
\[
       \overline\varphi(T_n)\cap M_0=\varnothing.               \tag{14}
\]
The large-set field of the context, (13), and (14) now give
\[
 \left|\overline\varphi(T_n)\setminus(\Gamma^q\cup M_0)\right|
 \ge (2n+11)-2n=11.
\]
Choose
\[
 \beta\in\overline\varphi(T_n)\setminus(\Gamma^q\cup M_0).
                                                                  \tag{15}
\]
Since \(T_n\preccurlyeq A\), this is in particular a
\(\beta\)-missing witness on \(A\).  Equations (11) and (15) give
\(\beta\ne\alpha\).

Apply \noderef{ChangeLemma} with the ordered color pair
\((\beta,\alpha)\).  At positive divider count the distinguished color
\(\beta\) is missing on \(A=T_{n,q}\).  At divider zero, (15) already
gives \(\beta\in\overline\varphi(T_n)\).  Equivalently, if one starts
only from a witness in the parallel core, exclusion from \(M_0\) forces
that witness into \(T_n\): a witness outside \(T_n\) would, by the
biunion definition in (1), put \(\beta\) in \(M_0\).  These are exactly
Cases 1 and 2 of the Change Lemma and of
\noderef{StableChangeCasesOneTwo}; no assertion about
\(\alpha\notin D_n\) is used.  The Change Lemma identifies the first
\(\beta\)- and \(\alpha\)-witness components.  Apply the preceding
coincident/distinct-witness argument and switch the disjoint terminal
component through \(y_\ell\), calling the result \(\sigma\).

Equation (9) shows that both \(\beta\) and \(\alpha\) remain missing on
\(A\) under \(\sigma\).  Both lie outside \(\Gamma^q\) by
(11) and (15).  For every stored \(\eta\in D\), (6) excludes both
affected colors from \(D\); hence the swap fixes the missing predicate
for \(\eta\) at every vertex.  Unfolding the totalized least-position
definition \noderef{HierarchyFirstMissingPosition}, including its
terminal fallback, gives
\[
 i_\eta(\sigma)=i_\eta(\varphi)\qquad(\eta\in D).               \tag{16}
\]
Thus the Cases 1/2 data, component disjointness, starred-stage
missingness, Gamma exclusion, (9), and (16) are precisely the hypotheses
of \noderef{StableChangeCasesOneTwoStarredTail}, with
\(\beta\) in its first color slot and \(\alpha\) in its second.
That lemma uses the actual old suffix \(A\to T\); the reason no
displayed-edge fixation is needed is exactly
\noderef{TashkinovAugmentationSequenceComponentSwap}: both affected
colors are missing on its source \(A\), so the same exact augmentation
suffix survives.

The starred-tail provider returns the full corresponding ETT, maximum
property, rebuilt base, hierarchy, goodness, and same-good data.
The swap makes \(\beta\) missing at \(y_\ell\), while (9) preserves its
witness on \(A\subseteq B\).  Apply the repackaging rule to obtain
\(P_1\).  Same-good equality identifies its \(\Gamma^q\) with (1), and
(9) on the base core and \(T_n\) identifies its base-extra set with
\(M_0\).  Therefore (15) says that \(P_1,\beta\) satisfy alternative
(A), completing the base-extra case.

\textbf{A Gamma owner not missing on the penultimate prefix.}
It remains that
\[
 \eta_m\in D,\qquad
 \alpha\in C.\mathit{good}.\Gamma^q_{\eta_m}.                  \tag{17}
\]
First assume
\[
                 \eta_m\notin\overline\varphi(B_{\rm pen}).    \tag{18}
\]
The avoidance clause of \noderef{GoodHierarchy}, read with the
totalized first position
\noderef{HierarchyFirstMissingPosition}, excludes \(\alpha\) from every
exact displayed edge of \(T-A\): under (18), the first \(\eta_m\)-missing
position is no earlier than the terminal position, including the fallback
case.

Apply \noderef{NineN} at \(i=\ell-2\), which is legal since
\(2\le j<\ell\).  Let \(X=T(y_{\ell-2})\), let \(U\) be the exact colors
used from \(A\) to \(X\), and let
\[
 \mathcal A=(\overline\varphi(X)\setminus M_0)\setminus U
\]
be its available set.  NineN gives
\(|\mathcal A|\ge2n+11\).  If
\(|\mathcal A\setminus(\Gamma^q\cup D)|\ge5\), delete the colors of the
two exact suffix edges \(T-X\), a set of cardinality at most two, and
choose
\[
 \beta\in\mathcal A\setminus(\Gamma^q\cup D)
\]
which is different from both suffix-edge colors.  Then \(\beta\) is
missing on \(X\), lies outside \(M_0\cup\Gamma^q\cup D\), and is used on
no exact edge of \(T-A\).

Otherwise the complement has cardinality at most four, so the second
conclusion of NineN supplies an injective family
\((\eta_h)_{h\in\operatorname{Fin}7}\) in \(D\), each missing on \(X\),
whose block \(Q_{\eta_h}\) from (8) avoids \(U\).  The blocks are
pairwise disjoint by (7)--(8), including both cross terms between a
stored singleton and every Gamma pair.  Each of the two suffix-edge
colors can therefore meet at most one block.  Of seven blocks, choose one
meeting neither suffix-edge color, and put \(\beta=\eta_h\) for its
owner.  Then the whole block \(Q_\beta\), in particular \(\beta\), is
used on no exact edge of \(T-A\).  Moreover
\(\beta\notin M_0\): membership in \(M_0\) would make \(\beta\) missing
on \(A\), contrary to \(\beta\in D\) and (6).

In both alternatives,
\[
 \beta\in\overline\varphi(X),\quad
 \alpha,\beta\notin\varphi\langle T-A\rangle,\quad
 \alpha\ne\beta.                                               \tag{19}
\]
The last inequality follows from (7), because \(\alpha\in\Gamma^q\)
whereas \(\beta\) is outside \(\Gamma^q\) in both alternatives.
Apply \noderef{ChangeLemma} to \((\alpha,\beta)\).  If \(\beta\) is not
missing on \(A\), its first witness follows the first \(\alpha\)-witness,
and the exact edge exclusion required by that lemma is (19); otherwise
both witnesses lie on \(A\).  At positive divider count \(\alpha\) is
missing on \(A=T_{n,q}\).  At divider zero, avoidance puts every member
of \(\Gamma^q_{\eta_m}\), hence \(\alpha\), among the colors missing on
\(T_n\).  Thus the same ordered pair satisfies the Cases 1/2 premise.
Separate the terminal component by the coincident/distinct-witness
argument and switch it, obtaining \(\sigma\).

Because (19) excludes both affected colors from every exact tail edge,
the swap fixes every displayed edge at every position from \(|A|\) to
\(|T|-1\).  Equation (9) gives pointwise missing-set equality on
\(B_{\rm pen}\).  To check every stored first position, fix
\(\rho\in D\).  The color \(\alpha\) is not stored by (7).  In the free
alternative \(\beta\notin D\), so \(\rho\) is unaffected globally.  In
the block alternative, the only affected stored color is
\(\rho=\beta\); it has an actual first missing witness in
\(X\preccurlyeq B_{\rm pen}\), and (9) preserves missingness at that and
every earlier displayed vertex.  Hence in both alternatives, including
the fallback for every other \(\rho\),
\[
 i_\rho(\sigma)=i_\rho(\varphi)\qquad(\rho\in D).               \tag{20}
\]
All hypotheses of \noderef{StableChangeCasesOneTwoFullTail} are now
verified literally.  Invoke it, rather than a split-tail theorem.  Its
full-tree output, the retained \(\beta\)-witness in \(X\), and the new
terminal \(\beta\)-missing witness give a presentation \(P_2\) by the
repackaging rule.

Let \(v_\beta\) be the vertex at the position (20).  Its position is at
most \(y_{\ell-2}\).  If it is no later than \(y_{j-2}\), then
\(P_2,\beta\) satisfy (A): in the free alternative this is the choice of
\(\beta\); in the block alternative \(\beta\in D\), so (6)--(7) exclude
it from both the copied base-extra set and copied Gamma union.  If
\(v_\beta=y_{j-1}\), then \(j-1\), with the actual witnesses
\((P_2,\beta)\), belongs to
\noderef{HierarchyEligibleCommonMissingIndices}.  Apply
\noderef{HierarchySituationThreeClaimJMinusOneNonempty} to that nonempty
finite set; its conclusion is exactly the conclusion required here.  If
\(v_\beta=y_s\) for \(j\le s\le\ell-2\), then \(s\) is an eligible index,
whereas \noderef{HierarchySituationThreeEligibleIndexBound} says every
eligible index is strictly smaller than \(j\), a contradiction.  The
injective total display in \noderef{TreeSequence} makes these alternatives
exhaustive.

\textbf{A Gamma owner with an actual penultimate witness.}
Finally suppose
\[
                 \eta_m\in\overline\varphi(B_{\rm pen}).       \tag{21}
\]
Equation (6) says that \(\eta_m\) is not missing on \(A\).  Therefore
\noderef{HierarchyFirstMissingPosition} has an actual first tail witness
\(y_t\), with \(1\le t\le\ell-1\).  If \(t\le j-2\), then
\((P_0,\alpha)\) already satisfies the Gamma alternative in the
conclusion: (4)--(5) give its two missing witnesses, (17) gives Gamma
membership, and the exact prefix length gives \(i_{\eta_m}\le|B|\).
Assume henceforth that
\[
                         j-1\le t\le\ell-1                     \tag{22}
\]
and put \(X=T(y_t)\).  Then \(A\prec X\preccurlyeq B_{\rm pen}\).

Apply \noderef{ChangeLemma} to the ordered pair
\((\alpha,\eta_m)\).  Its conditional first-position premise holds
because \(\alpha\) is missing on \(A\), whereas \(\eta_m\) first becomes
missing at \(y_t\).  Its edge-exclusion premise follows from the
avoidance clause for (17): \(\alpha\) is absent from every exact edge
from \(A\) through that first \(\eta_m\)-position.  The positive-divider
Cases 1/2 premise is again \(\alpha\in\overline\varphi(A)\); at divider
zero avoidance gives \(\alpha\in\overline\varphi(T_n)\).  The Change
Lemma identifies the two first-witness components.  Separate the
terminal component, switch it, and call the result \(\sigma\).  Apply
\noderef{StableChangeCasesOneTwo} immediately to these same ordered
data, obtaining \noderef{HierarchyWeaklyStable} at \(A\) and a
\noderef{ModColoring}.

We verify the exact split-tail data.  Gamma avoidance fixes every
\(\alpha\)-colored edge from \(A\) through \(X\).
\noderef{HierarchyTailMissingColorExclusion}, applied to the actual first
\(\eta_m\)-missing vertex \(y_t\), excludes \(\eta_m\) from the same
edge interval with one precise possible exception:
\[
 q=0,\quad A=T_n,\quad\Theta_n\in\{SE,RE\},\quad
 t_{\rm edge}=|A|,\quad
 F_n=\{f_n\},\quad\varphi(f_n)=\eta_m\in D_n.                  \tag{23}
\]
In (23), inspect the actual final
\noderef{TashkinovSeriesStep} supplied by
\noderef{TashkinovSeries}.  Its SE
\noderef{MaximumDefectiveChoice} and
\noderef{TreeSequenceAdjoinEdge} data, or its
\noderef{RevisitingExtensionCertificate}, makes the
individual edge \(f_n\) incident with \(T_n=A\).  The divider-zero
non-PE branch of the weak stability just obtained is
\noderef{StableColoring}(T_n,D_n,\varphi,\sigma); since the old value
\(\eta_m\) lies in the protected set \(D_n\), its incident-edge clause
gives \(\sigma(f_n)=\varphi(f_n)\).  Hence every exact displayed edge
from \(A\) through \(X\), including and only specially treating (23),
keeps its value.

Next inspect that same actual terminal certificate together with
\noderef{ExtendedTashkinovTree}.\texttt{belowFinal}.  PE supplies a
\noderef{TashkinovClosure} and an old
\noderef{TashkinovAugmentationSequence} from \(T_n\); SE/RE supplies the
exact \(f_n\), singleton \(F_n=\{f_n\}\), literal adjoined seed
\(T_n+f_n\), and the old route from that seed.  Restrict at \(T\) and
then at \(X\) using
\noderef{TashkinovAugmentationSequenceRestrict}.  At positive divider
count, the hierarchy puts the source below \(A\); in non-PE use
\noderef{HierarchyNonparallelFirstEdge}.  At divider-zero PE, the
ordered union in \noderef{HierarchyBase} puts \(T_n\) below \(A\); at
divider-zero SE/RE retain the exact seed from (23).

Factor the finite chain in
\noderef{TashkinovAugmentationSequence}: every
\noderef{TashkinovAugmentation} raises length by one, so the unique chain
members of lengths \(|A|\) and \(|X|\) are the exact prefixes \(A\) and
\(X\).  Weak stability supplies
\noderef{TreeColoringInvariant} below \(A\) at positive divider count.
At divider-zero PE it supplies
\noderef{CombinedStableColoring} on \(T_n\) and the terminal closure;
\noderef{CombinedStableClosurePreserved} fixes the exact edges of both
trees, while the left-segment and new-entry-from-right fields of the
\noderef{OrderedTreeUnion} cover every edge and vertex of \(A\).
At divider-zero SE/RE there is no route below the retained exact seed.
The fixed edge values just proved and (9) on the one-edge predecessors
then let
\noderef{TashkinovAugmentationSequenceColoringTransport} transport the
route through \(X\).  Thus the route is
\[
 \begin{cases}
 T_n\longrightarrow X,&\Theta_n=PE,\\
 T_n+f_n\longrightarrow X,&\Theta_n\in\{SE,RE\},
 \end{cases}                                                   \tag{24}
\]
with the exact singleton and adjoin-edge data in the second line.
The remaining factor of the old chain is the actual
\(\varphi\)-augmentation suffix \(X\to T\).

Equation (9) preserves \(\alpha\)'s missing witness on \(A\) and
\(\eta_m\)'s witness \(y_t\), so both colors are missing on \(X\) under
\(\sigma\).  Accordingly
\noderef{TashkinovAugmentationSequenceComponentSwap} transports the old
suffix \(X\to T\) without any assertion about its displayed edge
values.  This also confirms directly that (24) and the old suffix are
the genuine route pieces required by
\noderef{StableChangeCasesOneTwoSplitTail}.

It remains to check its two quantified hierarchy conditions.  For
\(\rho\in D\), (7) excludes \(\alpha\).  If
\(\rho\ne\eta_m\), its color is globally unaffected.  For
\(\rho=\eta_m\), the actual first witness \(y_t\) and the pointwise
equality (9) at all preceding vertices give, with no fallback ambiguity,
\[
 i_\rho(\sigma)=i_\rho(\varphi).                               \tag{25}
\]
For the Gamma cutoff, fix \(\rho\in D\) whose pair contains an affected

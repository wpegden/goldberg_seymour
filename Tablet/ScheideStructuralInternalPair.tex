\begin{lemma}[Scheide internal block-pair edges (3)]
Let $\gamma$ be a block color in a reduced Scheide configuration, put
$U_\gamma=\{u^1_\gamma,u^2_\gamma\}$ and
$Z_\gamma=V(T)\setminus U_\gamma$.  If
$\alpha\ne\gamma$ is missing at a vertex of $Z_\gamma$, then there is an
individual edge identity of color $\alpha$ whose endpoint pair is exactly
$\{u^1_\gamma,u^2_\gamma\}$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
By \noderef{ScheideGlobalMaximumOrder}, $V(T)$ is elementary and closed.
Since $\alpha$ is missing at a vertex of $Z_\gamma$, elementariness makes
$\alpha$ present at both vertices of $U_\gamma$; properness makes the
incident $\alpha$-edge at either vertex unique.  Closedness prevents either
of those edges from leaving $V(T)$.

Suppose that the $\alpha$-edge at $u^1_\gamma$ ends in $Z_\gamma$.  The
$\alpha$-edge at $u^2_\gamma$ cannot end at $u^1_\gamma$, where the unique
$\alpha$ incidence is already occupied, so it too ends in
$Z_\gamma$.  Delete from the ordered tree the two named $\gamma$-edges and
their two new vertices, then append these two individual $\alpha$-edges
and $u^1_\gamma,u^2_\gamma$.  The remaining order is Tashkinov because
every deleted block attached directly to the base; the two appended edges
have the existing missing $\alpha$ witness in $Z_\gamma$.  Properness and
the unique uncolored edge are unchanged, and the new tree has exactly
$V(T)$, so the resulting triple is globally maximum.

In that replacement tree, $\gamma$ is missing but unused, hence free.
Defective colors and Scheide exits depend only on the coloring and vertex
set, both of which are unchanged.  The exit-color surjectivity in
\noderef{ScheideReducedConfiguration} supplies an exit missing $\gamma$,
contradicting \noderef{ScheideExitNoFree}.  Thus the unique
$\alpha$-edge at $u^1_\gamma$ must end at $u^2_\gamma$, giving the asserted
edge identity and endpoint equation.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}
Fix an ETT, vertices \(v\) and \(u\), a coloring \(\varrho_1\), an
arbitrary slot map
\[
 \mathtt{indices}:\operatorname{Fin}(p+1)\longrightarrow
                  \operatorname{Fin}(n),
\]
and an arbitrary position \(b\in\operatorname{Fin}(p+1)\).  An Algorithm
(B) trace consists of a natural number of steps; for every step, its
position below \(b\), its exact slot \(\mathtt{indices}(a)\), its gamma
and delta colors; and every intermediate coloring.  The image of the
listed slots is exactly
\(\noderef{ExtensionBaseCaseOneIndices}(\mathtt{indices},b)\), and the
listed slots occur in strictly decreasing numerical order.  Each listed
slot has PE kind, has exact chosen support \(v\), and has the displayed
gamma and delta as its exact certified fields.  The first coloring is
\(\varrho_1\), and each successor is obtained by switching the two-color
component through \(u\):
\[
 \varrho^{a+1}=\varrho^a/P_u
       (\gamma_{t_a},\delta_{t_a},\varrho^a).
\]
All slot equalities are equalities of the certified finite indices, not
only equalities of their numerical values.  No increasing-enumeration,
least-missing-gamma, properness, or Case 1 hypothesis is part of this
generic data structure.  In particular \(b=0\) permits the zero-step trace:
the prefix-slot set is empty and its only coloring is \(\varrho_1\).
\end{definition}

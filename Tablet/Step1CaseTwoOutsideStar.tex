\begin{lemma}[Step 1, Subcase 2.1: the third end is outside the starred predecessor]
Let $G$ be a finite loopless multigraph retaining individual parallel-edge
identities, let $e_0$ be distinguished, and let $[k]$ be the color set.
Let $T$ be a positive-rung certified ETT with generating coloring
$\varphi$, maximum property, properness, and exactly $e_0$ uncolored.
Assume the complete six-part lower-rung package.  Let $H$ be a good
hierarchy over a hierarchy base, with $d<q_0$ dividers.  Assume uniformly
that every same-rung maximum ETT built from an arbitrary certified input
and generating coloring and having a good hierarchy with fewer than
$q_0$ dividers is elementary.  Assume the large bound
$|\overline\varphi(T_n)|\geq2n+11$, the separate terminal certificate
\[
 T\text{ is }
 \left(\bigcup_{\eta\in D^H_{n,d+1}}\Gamma^d_\eta\right)^-
 \text{-closed under }\varphi,
\]
and elementarity of $V(T)$.  Put $A=H.\mathit{starred}(d)$.

Let $\alpha\ne\beta$, with $\alpha$ missing and singleton-closed on $T$
and $\beta$ not missing on $T$.  Let $P_1,P_2,P_3$ be pairwise
support-disjoint exact exit paths for $\alpha,\beta$.  Their outside ends
$a_1,a_2,a_3$ and tree ends $b_1,b_2,b_3$ are pairwise distinct and
$b_1\prec_T b_2\prec_T b_3$.  Retain, for every $j$, the exact
$\beta$-colored boundary-edge identity $f_j$ on $P_j$ incident with
$b_j$.  Suppose $b_3\notin V(A)$.

Assume the following universal secondary-minimality property, ranging
over arbitrary certified replacement inputs rather than only over paths
on the current display.  Let $\sigma$ be any proper coloring leaving
exactly $e_0$ uncolored and stable from $\varphi$ on the final ladder
tree.  Let $T'$ be the exact terminal tree of any corresponding
same-rung ETT under $\sigma$, with maximum property, a hierarchy base,
a good hierarchy $K$ with exactly $d$ dividers, its own large estimate,
and its separate terminal minus-closed certificate.  For distinct colors
$a,b$, suppose $a$ is missing and singleton-closed on $T'$ and two exact
support-disjoint $(a,b)$ exit legs have distinct outside ends.  Such a
replacement is forbidden if either
\begin{enumerate}
\item $b$ is missing on $T'$, so its secondary coordinate is zero; or
\item $b$ is not missing and there is a third exact exit leg, pairwise
support-disjoint from the first two and with a third outside end, such
that the total of the three exact walk lengths is strictly less than
$|P_1|+|P_2|+|P_3|$.
\end{enumerate}
Then the displayed Subcase 2.1 data are contradictory.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Put $D^+=D^H_{n,d+1}$ and
$C_\Gamma=\bigcup_{\eta\in D^+}\Gamma^d_\eta$.
Choose a color $\gamma$ missing at $b_3$.  The initial
\noderef{KTriple} inside \noderef{TashkinovSeriesStep} gives
$k\geq\Delta(G)+1$, while properness, \noderef{Degree}, and
\noderef{MaximumDegree} give at most $d_G(b_3)\leq\Delta(G)$ present
colors, so \noderef{MissingColors} has such an element.  For every
$\eta\in D^H_{n,d}$,
\noderef{GoodHierarchyReservedDisjoint} puts $\Gamma^d_\eta$ among the
colors missing on $A$.  Since $b_3\notin V(A)$ and $V(T)$ is elementary,
$\gamma$ belongs to none of those pairs.  Unfolding
\noderef{TashkinovStoredColors},
\noderef{HierarchyStoredColorsAt}, and
\noderef{HierarchyTerminalStoredColors} gives
$D^+\subseteq D^H_{n,d}$, so $\gamma\notin C_\Gamma$.  The terminal
\noderef{TreeColorSetMinusClosed} certificate closes $\gamma$ on $T$.
The exact boundary edge $f_3$ has color $\beta$ at $b_3$, so
$\gamma\ne\beta$.

If $\alpha=\gamma$, put $\mu_4=\varphi$ and retain the identity ETT,
hierarchy, maximum property, and terminal certificate.  Otherwise swap
$\alpha,\gamma$ on all full components outside $T$.  Their singleton
closedness proves that this is an
\noderef{OutsideKempeRecoloring};
\noderef{OutsideKempeRecoloringStable} gives properness, exact
one-uncoloredness, and stability on every displayed stage.  Then
\noderef{HierarchyLEMStable} supplies an arbitrary replacement input,
the corresponding same-rung ETT under $\mu_4$, maximum property, the same
ordered stages and individual connecting edges, the same stored sets and
Gamma pairs, all five \noderef{GoodHierarchy} fields, and the transported
terminal certificate.  In both equality branches every $P_j$ becomes a
$(\gamma,\beta)$ exit path on the same exact walk support.  Its
$\beta$-edges are fixed because $\beta$ differs from both
$\alpha$ and $\gamma$.

Swap the full $(\gamma,\beta)$ component through $b_3$ and call the
result $\mu_5$.  The vertex $b_3$ misses $\gamma$ and is incident with
the $\beta$-edge $f_3$, so it has incidence degree one.  The outside end
$a_3$ also has degree one by \noderef{TreeExitPath}.  The degree-two
bound \noderef{TwoColorIncidenceDegreeAtMostTwo} and the spanning path
from \noderef{TwoColorComponentSpanningPath} show that $P_3$ exhausts
the whole component: an extra attachment internally would create degree
three, and an attachment at either endpoint would destroy degree one.
Consequently the swapped component meets $T$ only at $b_3$ and is
disjoint from the exact prefix $B^-$ immediately before $b_3$.

The distinctness $\gamma\ne\beta$ permits
\noderef{TwoColorComponentSwapProper}; the swap changes only those two
nonempty colors, so \noderef{IsOneEdgeUncolored} still singles out
$e_0$.  If $A\subsetneq B^-$, repackage the $d$-divider hierarchy with
terminal $B^-$.  If $B^-=A$ and $d>0$, first use the $(d-1)$-divider
presentation ending at $A$; if $B^-=A$ and $d=0$, use the actual initial
tuple through \noderef{TechnicalTheoremBase}.  In each branch the exact
finite augmentation sequence supplied by \noderef{TashkinovClosure} and
\noderef{TashkinovAugmentationSequence}, the mod-coloring conclusion of
\noderef{Td}, and \noderef{MaximumPropertyTransport} restore the one
exact edge and vertex at $b_3$.  Thus the exact prefix $C$ through $b_3$
is the terminal tree of a corresponding same-rung maximum ETT under
$\mu_5$, with the same stages through $A$, the same stored and Gamma
data, all retained minus-closed fields, strict final containment, and
terminal avoidance checked on the restored edge.  The swap makes
$\beta$ missing at $b_3$.

The pairwise support-disjoint choice puts the exact edge-nodes $f_1,f_2$
outside the support of $P_3$, which is the full swapped component.  By
\noderef{TwoColorComponentsDisjoint}, their full components are disjoint
from that component, for equality would put the corresponding $f_i$ in
$P_3$.  Hence \noderef{TwoColorComponentSwap} fixes the exact identities
$f_1,f_2$, and they remain $\beta$-colored under $\mu_5$.  Neither is
incident with $b_3$: its inside endpoint is the distinct $b_i$, and its
other endpoint lies outside $T$, whereas $b_3\in V(T)$.

Adjoin $f_1$ and then $f_2$ to $C$, always retaining their individual
identities.  Their old endpoints $b_1,b_2$ lie in $C$, their opposite
endpoints are the next vertices of the two simple exit paths, and those
new vertices are distinct even when the two tails came from opposite
ends of one full component, because the selected supports are disjoint.
Thus each step satisfies \noderef{TreeSequenceAdjoinEdge}.  The color
$\beta$ is missing at $b_3\in C$, so the first is a
\noderef{TashkinovAugmentation}; since $f_1$ is not incident with $b_3$,
the same witness remains missing and the second step is also a Tashkinov
augmentation.  Call the result $J$.

We next construct all certified data on $J$ before using elementarity.
The second swapped component is disjoint from $B^-\supseteq T_n$, so it
is an outside $(\gamma,\beta)$ recoloring relative to $T_n$.
\noderef{OutsideKempeRecoloringStable} makes $\mu_5$ stable there
relative to $\mu_4$; composing with the first outside recoloring by
\noderef{sc2} gives exactly the $(T_n,D_n,\varphi)$-stability premise of
\noderef{Td}.  Thus $\mu_5$ is a \noderef{ModColoring} of the current
ETT.  Compose the exact augmentation sequence ending at $C$ with the two
steps for $f_1,f_2$.  The universal mod-coloring clause supplies the
\noderef{CorrespondingExtendedTashkinovTree} on $J$, and
\noderef{MaximumPropertyTransport} supplies maximum property.

Copy the base and stages through $A$ and take $J$ as terminal.  This is a
\noderef{TashkinovHierarchy} with exactly $d$ dividers: all old
nestedness and strictness fields remain, and
$A\subsetneq C\subsetneq J$ proves the last strict containment.  The
corresponding-ETT fields identify every replacement ladder tree, raw
stored set, connecting edge, and extension kind.  Choose the same Gamma
family.  Pair size, pairwise disjointness, unique refresh, parallel base,
and every internal minus-closed field are transported because the swaps
fix every edge incident with the internal stages and preserve their
missing sets.

The final avoidance field requires a direct check.  For
$\eta\notin\{\beta,\gamma\}$ both recolorings preserve its first-missing
segment through $C$.  For $\eta=\beta$, the swap makes $b_3$ the first
new missing vertex, so the tested segment only shortens.  For
$\eta=\gamma$, elementarity before the swap makes $b_3$ its old first
missing vertex; after the swap the only extra displayed edges are
$f_1,f_2$, both colored $\beta$.  The color $\gamma$ belongs to no
indexed Gamma pair by the first paragraph.  The color $\beta$ belongs to
none either: \noderef{GoodHierarchyReservedDisjoint} would otherwise
make it missing on $A$, hence on $T$, contrary to the Case 2 hypothesis.
Unfolding \noderef{HierarchySegmentColors} therefore proves final
avoidance on $J$.  All five good-hierarchy fields have now been checked.

Starting at $J$, perform the manuscript's finite restricted
augmentation.  At a current tree $U$, append an exact boundary identity
$f$ only if its $\mu_5$-color is missing on $U$ and, for every
$\eta\in D^H_{n,d}$, the appended segment before the first
$\eta$-missing vertex uses no color in $\Gamma^d_\eta$.  This is exactly
the avoidance test encoded by \noderef{HierarchySegmentColors}.  Every
step is a \noderef{TashkinovAugmentation}: it records the old endpoint,
new endpoint, exact edge identity, displayed edge color, and its missing
witness.  Since $V$ and $E$ are finite and every step strictly lengthens
the ordered vertex list, a maximal finite sequence exists.

Induct along this finite sequence.  At each step the accumulated edges
form a \noderef{TashkinovAugmentationSequence}; the mod-coloring clause
gives the corresponding ETT and \noderef{MaximumPropertyTransport} gives
maximum property.  Copy the base, dividers, and internal stages.  The
pair-size, disjointness, unique-refresh, parallel-base, and internal
minus-closed fields do not change, while the selection test is precisely
the new final avoidance field.  Thus all five good-hierarchy fields hold
at every step.  Let $T'$ be a maximal last tree.

Unfold \noderef{TashkinovStoredColors} for the corresponding ETT and
\noderef{HierarchyTerminalStoredColors} for its terminal stage.  The raw
stored sets agree with the originals, while $T_n\subseteq A\subseteq T'$.
Consequently its terminal stored set
\[
 D'_{n,d+1}=D_n^{\mu_5}-\overline{\mu_5}(T')
\]
is contained in $D^H_{n,d}$.  This is also the terminal instance of
\noderef{HierarchyStoredColorsAntitone}.  If a color missing on $T'$ but
outside
$\bigcup_{\eta\in D'_{n,d+1}}\Gamma^d_\eta$ had a boundary edge, that
edge would pass the selection rule.  Indeed, if
$\eta\notin D'_{n,d+1}$, its first missing vertex already precedes the
new edge; if $\eta\in D'_{n,d+1}$, the proposed edge color is outside its
Gamma pair and all older edges already passed.  Maximality forbids the
edge.  Hence
\[
 T'\text{ is }
 \left(\bigcup_{\eta\in D'_{n,d+1}}\Gamma^d_\eta\right)^-
 \text{-closed under }\mu_5. \tag{1}
\]
The verified hierarchy has $d<q_0$ dividers, so the same-rung hypothesis
makes $V(T')$ elementary under $\mu_5$.

No $a_j$ belongs to $T'$.  Suppose instead that some $a_j$ does.  Its
path-end property says that it misses $\gamma$ or $\beta$.  It cannot
miss $\beta$, since $b_3$ already does and $T'$ is elementary, so it
misses $\gamma$.  Both colors are outside the terminal reserved union:
this was proved above and
$D'_{n,d+1}\subseteq D^H_{n,d}$.  Equation (1), the $\gamma$-missing
witness $a_j$, and the $\beta$-missing witness $b_3$ make both colors
closed on $T'$.

Every $P_i$ meets $T'$ at $b_i\in C$.  If $a_i$ were outside $T'$, the
first edge by which $P_i$ leaves $T'$ would be a $\gamma$- or
$\beta$-colored boundary identity, contradicting the two closedness
conclusions.  Hence all three $a_i$ lie in $T'$.  None can miss
$\beta$, since $b_3$ already does and $T'$ is elementary.  Therefore all
three distinct vertices $a_i$ miss $\gamma$, contradicting elementarity
already for $a_1,a_2$.  This proves that all three $a_j$ lie outside
$T'$.

For each $j$, take the final portion $L_j$ from its last vertex in $T'$
to $a_j$.  It is an exact $T'$-exit path.  The three outside endpoints
remain distinct and the exact incidence-walk supports remain pairwise
disjoint.  Since $f_1$ was adjoined to $J\subseteq T'$, it is absent
from $L_1$, while every other deletion is nonnegative.  Hence
\[
 |L_1|+|L_2|+|L_3|<|P_1|+|P_2|+|P_3|. \tag{2}
\]
The first comparison color for this replacement is $\beta$: it is
missing at $b_3$, and it belongs to no indexed Gamma pair, so (1) makes
the singleton $\{\beta\}$ closed on $T'$.  The second comparison color
is $\gamma\ne\beta$.  Properness, exact one-uncoloredness, stability,
the arbitrary replacement input, corresponding same-rung ETT, maximum
property, exact $d$-divider good hierarchy with all five fields, large
bound, and separate terminal certificate were all verified above.

Any two $L_j$ give the required two-leg witness.  If $\gamma$ is missing
on $T'$, the replacement has secondary coordinate zero.  If it is not
missing, all three $L_j$ are admissible and (2) gives a strictly smaller
three-leg total.  These are exactly the two alternatives in the stated
universal no-better-presentation property.  Either alternative is
forbidden, so Subcase 2.1 is impossible.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Scheide maximal-tree package (f)]
Let $(G,e_0,\varphi)$ be a $k$-triple and let $T$ be a fixed-pair maximal
Tashkinov tree for $(e_0,\varphi)$.  Then the induced multigraph $G[V(T)]$
contains an odd cycle.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Let $x,y$ be the first two vertices of $T$.  By the first-edge and
attachment clauses in \noderef{TreeSequence}, they are the two distinct
ends of the uncolored edge identity $e_0$.  Each misses at least two colors:
the $k$-triple inequality in \noderef{KTriple} is
$k\geq\Delta(G)+1$, while uncoloring the incident edge $e_0$ leaves at
most $d_G(x)-1$ and $d_G(y)-1$ present colors, with degree and maximum
degree as in \noderef{Degree} and \noderef{MaximumDegree}.  The complements
are the missing-color sets of \noderef{MissingColors}.  Choose a color $\alpha$
missing at $x$ and a color $\beta$ missing at $y$.

The set $V(T)$ is elementary by
\noderef{ScheideMaximalElementaryClosed}, so $\alpha\ne\beta$.
Apply \noderef{ScheideMaximalKempePath} to $S=T$, the indices of $x,y$,
and the colors $\alpha,\beta$.  It gives an even
$(\alpha,\beta)$-path contained in $V(T)$ whose ends are $x,y$.  Because
the incidence path is simple, its multigraph vertices and edge identities
are pairwise distinct.  The edge $e_0$ is not one of its edges, since it is
uncolored by \noderef{KTriple} whereas every edge on the two-color path is
colored $\alpha$ or $\beta$.  Adding the edge identity $e_0$ closes this
even path into a cycle of odd length at least three.  All its vertices lie
in $V(T)$, and all its edge identities remain distinct.  This is precisely
an odd cycle in the sense of \noderef{OddCycleIn}.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Scheide three departures and replacement data (7)]
For every defective color $\delta$, the boundary identities of color
$\delta$ from $Z_{\gamma(\delta)}$ to the exterior of $V(T)$ are exactly
three.  They can be named $f^0_\delta,f^1_\delta,f^2_\delta$ with distinct
outside ends, and these choices, together with the exact replacement
tree-sequence obtained by deleting the $\gamma(\delta)$ block and adjoining
$f^1_\delta,f^2_\delta$, form a family of
\noderef{ScheideReplacementData}.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Fix a defective color $\delta$.  We shall construct its replacement data
with all edge identities and all positions fixed, and only then make the
choices simultaneously.  Put
\[
 X=\noderef{TreeSequenceVertexSet}(T),\qquad
 i=\gammaIndex(\delta),\qquad \gamma=\gamma_i,
\]
and abbreviate
\[
 x_0=\operatorname{blockVertex}(i,0),\quad
 x_1=\operatorname{blockVertex}(i,1),\quad
 U=\{x_0,x_1\},\quad Z=X\setminus U.                         \tag{1}
\]
Also write $v^0=v^0_\delta$, $f^0=f^0_\delta$,
$u^0=u^0_\delta$, and $\alpha=\alpha_0$.  The definition
\noderef{ScheideDefectiveSetup} gives
\[
 \alpha\in\overline\varphi(y_0),\quad
 \alpha\ne\gamma_r\ (0\le r<s),\quad
 \gamma\in\overline\varphi(v^0),\quad
 \varphi(f^0)=\delta,\quad
 \operatorname{ends}(f^0)=\{v^0,u^0\},\quad u^0\notin X.       \tag{2}
\]
It also records the full simple $(\alpha,\delta)$ incidence path $P$
from $y_0$ to an outside vertex, the exact successive occurrences
$v^0,f^0,u^0$ on $P$, the fact that $v^0$ is its last vertex in $X$,
and $v^0\in\noderef{ScheideExitVertices}(T,\varphi)$.
Here \emph{full} has the exact support meaning in
\noderef{ScheideDefectiveChainPath} and
\noderef{TwoColorComponent}; thus $P$ is the whole component through
$y_0$, including each individual edge-node.

Unpack \noderef{ScheideReducedConfiguration} and its inherited
\noderef{ScheideStructuralConfiguration}.  They give the exact ordered
layout
\[
 y_0,e_1,y_1,\ldots,e_{p-1},y_{p-1},
 \quad
 \mathop{\Vert}_{0\le r<s}
   (h^0_r,x^0_r,h^1_r,x^1_r),                                \tag{3}
\]
where $p\ge2$, $h^j_r=\operatorname{blockEdge}(r,j)$,
$x^j_r=\operatorname{blockVertex}(r,j)$, and
\[
 T.\operatorname{length}+1=p+2s.                             \tag{4}
\]
Each $h^j_r$ joins $x^j_r$ directly to a base vertex, has color
$\gamma_r$, and $\gamma_r$ is missing at a base vertex.  The block
colors are injectively indexed, and every colored nonfirst base edge has
color outside the block-color set.  The exact position equations in that
definition and vertex and edge injectivity in
\noderef{TreeSequence} retain all these vertex and parallel-edge
identities.

The globally maximum field in (3), expanded with
\noderef{GlobalMaximumTriple}, supplies the unchanged
\noderef{KTriple}, the old \noderef{TashkinovTree} certificate, and
\[
 |X|=\noderef{TashkinovOrder}(G).                             \tag{5}
\]
The $k$-triple says in particular that $\varphi$ is proper in the sense
of \noderef{IsProperEdgeColoring} and leaves exactly $e_0$ uncolored in
the sense of \noderef{IsOneEdgeUncolored}.  By
\noderef{ScheideGlobalMaximumOrder}, $X$ is elementary and closed in the
senses of \noderef{ColoredVertexSetElementary} and
\noderef{ColoredVertexSetClosed}, and $|X|$ is odd.

We first locate $v^0$ precisely.  Expanding
\noderef{ScheideExitVertices} and \noderef{ScheideExitVertex}, its exit
membership gives $v^0\in X$.  The exit-covering field in
\noderef{ScheideStructuralConfiguration} supplies an index $r$ for which
$\gamma_r$ is missing at $v^0$.  The block-missing field supplies a base
vertex $y_q$ at which the same $\gamma_r$ is missing.  The base-position
equation and \noderef{TreeSequenceVertexSet} put $y_q$ in $X$.
Elementariness of $X$ now forces $v^0=y_q$: otherwise the missing sets of
the two distinct tree vertices would meet in $\gamma_r$.  Thus $v^0$ is
a base vertex.  The same position equations put $y_0$ in the base, while
they put $x_0,x_1$ in the two distinct block positions belonging to $i$.
Vertex injectivity in \noderef{TreeSequence} therefore gives
\[
 x_0,x_1\in X,\quad x_0\ne x_1,\quad
 v^0,y_0\in Z.                                               \tag{6}
\]

We next record that $\delta$ is present exactly once at every vertex of
$X$.  The defective-color clause and
\noderef{ScheideGlobalMaximumBoundaryFacts} give at least three members
of \noderef{BoundaryColorEdges}$(G,\varphi,X,\delta)$.  Choose one such
identity $b$.  By \noderef{BoundaryEdges}, $b$ has one endpoint in $X$
and one outside, and it has color $\delta$ by
\noderef{BoundaryColorEdges}.  Closedness says that $\delta$ cannot be
missing at any vertex of $X$.  Since \noderef{MissingColors} is the
complement of \noderef{PresentColors}, for every $z\in X$ the definition
\noderef{PresentColors} supplies a $\delta$-colored identity incident
with $z$.  It is unique: two distinct such identities would be adjacent
by \noderef{AdjacentEdges} and violate properness.  In particular,
$\delta\ne\alpha$ because $\alpha$ is missing at $y_0$, and
$\delta\ne\gamma$ because $\gamma$ is missing at $v^0$.       \tag{7}

Define the finite set which occurs in the statement by
\[
 B=\{f:\ f\in\noderef{BoundaryColorEdges}(G,\varphi,Z,\delta)
       \text{ and some }w\notin X\text{ satisfies }
       \noderef{Incident}(G,w,f)\}.                           \tag{8}
\]
Equations (2) and (6) say that $f^0$ has one endpoint $v^0\in Z$ and the
other endpoint $u^0\notin X$, and has color $\delta$.  The definitions
\noderef{Incident}, \noderef{BoundaryEdges}, and
\noderef{BoundaryColorEdges} therefore give
\[
 f^0\in B.                                                   \tag{9}
\]

We now prove the parity assertion rather than merely invoking it.  Let
\[
 I=\{(z,f)\in Z\times E(G):
       \noderef{Incident}(G,z,f)\text{ and }\varphi(f)=\delta\}
\]
and let
\[
 A=\{f\in\noderef{InternalEdges}(G,Z):\varphi(f)=\delta\}.
\]
The existence and uniqueness just proved give a bijection
$z\mapsto(z,f_z)$ from $Z$ to $I$, so $|I|=|Z|$.  Count $I$ instead by
its edge coordinate.  By \noderef{LooplessMultigraph}, an identity in
$A$ has two distinct endpoints in $Z$ and contributes two incidences.
An identity in $B$ has exactly one endpoint in $Z$ and contributes one.
There are no other nonempty fibers: an edge incident with $Z$ has its
other endpoint in $Z$, in $U$, or outside $X$; the middle possibility
for a $\delta$-colored identity is excluded by
\noderef{ScheideDefectiveNoCross}, and the last possibility is precisely
(8).  Hence the disjoint edge-identity fibers give the explicit equation
\[
 |Z|=|I|=2|A|+|B|.                                           \tag{10}
\]
The two distinct members $x_0,x_1$ of $X$ are exactly what was removed
in (1), so $|Z|=|X|-2$.  Since $|X|$ is odd, (10) proves that $|B|$ is
odd.  Together with (9), $|B|\ge1$.

Suppose first that $|B|>3$.  Oddness then gives $|B|\ge5$, so choose
three distinct identities $g_1,g_2,g_3\in B\setminus\{f^0\}$.  From
(8), \noderef{BoundaryEdges}, \noderef{Incident}, and
\noderef{LooplessMultigraph},
orient their endpoint equations as
\[
 \operatorname{ends}(g_j)=\{z_j,w_j\},\qquad
 z_j\in Z,\quad w_j\notin X,\quad \varphi(g_j)=\delta
 \quad(1\le j\le3).                                        \tag{11}
\]
The outside ends $w_1,w_2,w_3$ are pairwise distinct.  If two were equal,
the corresponding distinct $\delta$-colored identities would be adjacent
there, contrary to properness and \noderef{AdjacentEdges}.

Let
\[
 \varphi_\delta=\noderef{TwoColorComponentSwap}
   (G,\varphi,\gamma,\delta,\operatorname{inl}v^0).
\]
By \noderef{ScheideDefectiveSwitchEffect}, this coloring is proper,
leaves exactly $e_0$ uncolored, fixes every edge identity incident with
$X$ other than $f^0$, and satisfies
\[
 \overline{\varphi_\delta}(v^0)=
   (\overline\varphi(v^0)\setminus\{\gamma\})\cup\{\delta\}.
\tag{12}
\]
Thus every $g_j$ retains color $\delta$, since it is incident with
$z_j\in X$ and differs from $f^0$.

For completeness, construct the forbidden ordered sequence explicitly.
Put $n=T.\operatorname{length}$ and
\[
 a=p-1+2i,                                                   \tag{13}
\]
the zero-based position of $h^0_i$.  From $p\ge2$, $i<s$, and (4),
$1\le a$, the two old edge positions $a,a+1$ and vertex positions
$a+1,a+2$ exist, and $n\ge3$.  Form $T^+$ of length $n+1$ as follows:
\[
\begin{array}{rcll}
 T^+.\operatorname{edge}(q)&=&T.\operatorname{edge}(q),
   &q<a,\\
 T^+.\operatorname{edge}(q)&=&T.\operatorname{edge}(q+2),
   &a\le q,\ q+2<n,\\
 T^+.\operatorname{edge}(n-2,n-1,n)&=&(g_1,g_2,g_3),&\\[2mm]
 T^+.\operatorname{vertex}(q)&=&T.\operatorname{vertex}(q),
   &q\le a,\\
 T^+.\operatorname{vertex}(q)&=&T.\operatorname{vertex}(q+2),
   &a<q,\ q+2<n+1,\\
 T^+.\operatorname{vertex}(n-1,n,n+1)&=&(w_1,w_2,w_3).&
\end{array}                                                  \tag{14}
\]
These formulas delete exactly $(h^0_i,x_0),(h^1_i,x_1)$, retain every
other old pair in its old relative order, and append the three pairs from
(11).

We check all fields of \noderef{TreeSequence} for (14).  Positivity and
the first-edge equation follow from $n\ge3$, $a\ge1$, and the old
first-edge equation.  The retained vertices and identities are injective
because their old positions are distinct.  Each $w_j$ is outside $X$,
and the three are pairwise distinct, so vertex injectivity also holds at
the appended positions.  Every old tree identity has both endpoints in
$X$ by the attachment field of \noderef{TreeSequence}; hence no $g_j$,
which has endpoint $w_j\notin X$, is an old tree identity.  Together with
the distinct choice of the $g_j$, this proves edge injectivity.  For
attachments before $a$, retain the old earlier-position witness.  Every
shifted old identity after $a$ belongs, by the exact layout (3), to a
later block and hence attaches directly to a retained base vertex.  Each
new $g_j$ has the retained endpoint $z_j\in Z$ and the new endpoint
$w_j$.  Thus every attachment equation holds with an earlier vertex.

The same order is a \noderef{TashkinovTree} under $\varphi_\delta$.
For a retained edge before $a$, take its old Tashkinov witness.  That
witness also occurs before $a$ and is retained.  Its edge color cannot be
$\gamma$: a nonfirst base edge avoids all block colors, and an earlier
block edge has color $\gamma_r$ with $r<i$, distinct from $\gamma_i$.
Consequently the switch-effect equalities preserve both the edge color
and its missing witness, even when that witness is $v^0$.  A shifted edge
after $a$ belongs to a block $r>i$; its color $\gamma_r$ is missing at a
retained base vertex by (3), and $\gamma_r\ne\gamma_i$, so the same
switch-effect equalities preserve this witness.  Finally all three new
edges have color $\delta$ under $\varphi_\delta$, and (12) says that
$\delta$ is missing at the retained, earlier base vertex $v^0$.

The graph-only clauses of the old \noderef{KTriple} are unchanged, while
properness and the one-uncolored clause for $\varphi_\delta$ were supplied
by the switch lemma.  Hence $(G,e_0,\varphi_\delta)$ is again a
$k$-triple.  By injectivity and (14),
\[
 |V(T^+)|=|X|-2+3=|X|+1=\noderef{TashkinovOrder}(G)+1.        \tag{15}
\]
But the definition of \noderef{TashkinovOrder}, together with the
chromatic-index equality in the new $k$-triple, bounds every such
Tashkinov tree by $t(G)$.  Equation (15) is the required contradiction.
Therefore $|B|\not>3$.

It remains to exclude $|B|<3$.  Oddness and (9) would make
\[
 B=\{f^0\}.                                                   \tag{16}
\]
We now follow the second component argument in full.  From (6),
$y_0\in Z$ and $\alpha$ is missing there; from (2),
$\alpha\ne\gamma$.  Applying
\noderef{ScheideStructuralInternalPair} to this exact witness gives an
individual identity $h_\alpha$ with
\[
 \varphi(h_\alpha)=\alpha,\qquad
 \operatorname{ends}(h_\alpha)=\{x_0,x_1\}.                 \tag{17}
\]
Properness implies that $h_\alpha$ is the unique $\alpha$-colored
identity incident with either $x_0$ or $x_1$.

We first prove that the initial component $P$ avoids $U$.  Start at
$y_0\in Z$ and consider the first step of $P$ from a vertex of $Z$ to a
vertex outside $Z$, if such a step occurs before the recorded departure
$v^0,f^0,u^0$.  The traversed edge has color $\alpha$ or $\delta$ by
\noderef{TwoColorIncidenceGraph}.  It cannot enter $U$: an
$\alpha$-edge would contradict the uniqueness following (17), and a
$\delta$-edge is forbidden by \noderef{ScheideDefectiveNoCross}.  It
cannot be an $\alpha$-edge from $Z$ to the exterior of $X$, because
$\alpha$ is missing at $y_0\in X$ and closedness, expanded through
\noderef{ColoredVertexSetClosed} and \noderef{BoundaryEdges}, forbids
every $\alpha$-colored boundary identity of $X$.  Thus such a first step
must be a $\delta$-edge from $Z$ to the exterior of $X$, hence is the
unique member $f^0$ of (16).  Simplicity of the recorded full path and
the exact successive indices from \noderef{ScheideDefectiveSetup} identify
this with its displayed occurrence $v^0,f^0,u^0$.  Before it all vertex
nodes lie in $Z$, and after $v^0$ the recorded last-tree-vertex clause
excludes every vertex of $X$.  Consequently
\[
 P\cap U=\varnothing.                                        \tag{18}
\]

Let $K$ be the $(\alpha,\delta)$ component through $x_0$ in
\noderef{TwoColorComponent}.  Equation (17) puts both $x_0$ and $x_1$
in $K$.  It is distinct from the component $P$ by (18).  We claim
\[
 K\cap Z=\varnothing.                                        \tag{19}
\]
Indeed, otherwise take a shortest incidence walk in $K$ from $U$ to a
vertex of $Z$ and inspect the last edge identity on which it enters $Z$.
A direct entry from $U$ is impossible by the uniqueness in (17) for
color $\alpha$ and by \noderef{ScheideDefectiveNoCross} for color
$\delta$.  An entry from outside $X$ cannot have color $\alpha$ by the
closedness argument above.  If it has color $\delta$, (16) forces the
identity to be $f^0$.  Then $K$ contains the edge-node $f^0$, while the
recorded setup puts the same edge-node on the full component $P$.
Connected components of the same incidence graph which meet are equal,
contradicting (18).  This proves (19).

No exact tree-edge identity belongs to $K$.  The first identity $e_0$ is
uncolored and hence is not an edge-node of the two-color graph.  Any
other tree edge has both endpoints in $X$ by
\noderef{TreeSequence}.  If its edge-node lay in $K$, both endpoint
vertex-nodes would lie there, so (19) would force both endpoints to lie
in $U$.  The exact layout (3) rules this out: a base edge joins two base
positions, while every block edge, including the two selected ones,
joins one block vertex directly to a base vertex.  Thus
\[
 K\text{ contains no edge identity displayed by }T.          \tag{20}
\]

Return to the original $\delta$-boundary of $X$.
\noderef{ScheideGlobalMaximumBoundaryFacts} gives at least three distinct
identities in it.  Exactly one of them has its endpoint in $Z$, because
such identities are exactly the members of $B$ and (16) holds.  Every
other one has its unique endpoint in $X$ in $U$.  Properness permits at
most one $\delta$-colored identity at each of the two vertices $x_0,x_1$.
Hence there are exactly two remaining boundary identities, say
\[
 \operatorname{ends}(\ell_j)=\{x_j,t_j\},\qquad
 t_j\notin X,\quad \varphi(\ell_j)=\delta
 \quad(j=0,1).                                               \tag{21}
\]
They both belong to $K$: (17) first puts each $x_j$ in $K$, and the
adjacency rule in \noderef{TwoColorIncidenceGraph} then puts its incident
$\delta$-edge-node $\ell_j$ there.

Switch $\alpha$ and $\delta$ on $K$ and denote the result by
\[
 \psi=\noderef{TwoColorComponentSwap}
   (G,\varphi,\alpha,\delta,\operatorname{inl}x_0).
\]
The colors are distinct by (7), so
\noderef{TwoColorComponentSwapProper} makes $\psi$ proper.  The explicit
definition of \noderef{TwoColorComponentSwap} fixes the uncolored value
and maps every colored value to a colored value; the old
\noderef{IsOneEdgeUncolored} clauses therefore show that $\psi$ leaves
exactly $e_0$ uncolored.  Equations (17), (21), and component membership
give
\[
 \psi(h_\alpha)=\delta,\qquad
 \psi(\ell_0)=\psi(\ell_1)=\alpha.                           \tag{22}
\]

We verify every old Tashkinov witness under $\psi$.  By (20), every
displayed tree edge keeps its old color.  No nonfirst displayed edge has
color $\delta$, because
\noderef{ScheideGlobalMaximumUnused}, expanded through
\noderef{TreeUsedColors}, says that every defective color is unused on
the colored tree edges.  If a displayed edge has color $\alpha$, use the
earlier vertex $y_0$: it is the first vertex, it misses $\alpha$, and
(19) shows that its incident color set is unchanged by the swap.  For a
displayed edge of any other color, retain its old witness from
\noderef{TashkinovTree}; swapping only $\alpha$ and $\delta$ cannot
destroy missingness of this third color, whether or not the witness lies
in $K$.  These cases exhaust the nonfirst edges, so $T$ is still a
Tashkinov tree under $\psi$.

Append $(\ell_0,t_0)$ to the old order $T$.  The new vertex is fresh by
(21), and the new identity is fresh because every old tree edge has both
ends in $X$, whereas $\ell_0$ has the outside end $t_0$.  Thus vertex
and edge injectivity, the first edge, and every old attachment remain
valid, while (21) supplies the new attachment to the earlier vertex
$x_0$.  This is a \noderef{TreeSequence}.  By (22) the appended edge has
color $\alpha$, and $\alpha$ is still missing at the earlier vertex
$y_0$, so the enlarged sequence is Tashkinov.  Properness and the
one-uncolored assertion for $\psi$, together with the graph-only clauses
of the old \noderef{KTriple}, make $(G,e_0,\psi)$ a $k$-triple.  Its
vertex set has cardinality
\[
 |X|+1=\noderef{TashkinovOrder}(G)+1,
\]
again contradicting the defining maximum.  Therefore (16) is impossible.
Combining the two excluded cases with oddness proves
\[
 |B|=3.                                                       \tag{23}
\]

We now choose and orient the data required by the conclusion.  By (9)
and (23), there are exactly two remaining identities; name them
$f^1_\delta,f^2_\delta$.  Thus, as an equality of finite sets of edge
identities,
\[
 B=\{f^0_\delta,f^1_\delta,f^2_\delta\},                     \tag{24}
\]
and the three identities are pairwise distinct.  Membership in (8),
together with \noderef{BoundaryEdges}, \noderef{Incident}, and
\noderef{LooplessMultigraph}, gives orientations
\[
 \operatorname{ends}(f^j_\delta)=\{v^j_\delta,u^j_\delta\},
 \qquad v^j_\delta\in Z,\quad u^j_\delta\notin X,\quad
 \varphi(f^j_\delta)=\delta\quad(j=1,2).                    \tag{25}
\]
Together with (2), their three outside ends
$u^0_\delta,u^1_\delta,u^2_\delta$ are pairwise distinct: equality of
two would make the corresponding distinct $\delta$-colored identities
adjacent at that common endpoint, contradicting properness.  Equations
(8) and (24) give, for every edge identity $f$, the exact biconditional
\[
 \begin{split}
 &f\in\noderef{BoundaryColorEdges}(G,\varphi,Z,\delta)
   \text{ and }(\exists w\notin X)\,
       \noderef{Incident}(G,w,f)\\
 &\hspace{35mm}\Longleftrightarrow
 f=f^0_\delta\ \text{or}\ f=f^1_\delta\ \text{or}\
 f=f^2_\delta.                                               \tag{26}
 \end{split}
\]

It remains to construct the exact ordered replacement tree required by
\noderef{ScheideReplacementData}.  Retain the notation $n,a$ from
(13).  Define $T_\delta$ to have length $n$ and the following entries:
\[
\begin{array}{rcll}
 T_\delta.\operatorname{edge}(q)&=&T.\operatorname{edge}(q),
   &q<a,\\
 T_\delta.\operatorname{edge}(q)&=&T.\operatorname{edge}(q+2),
   &a\le q,\ q+2<n,\\
 T_\delta.\operatorname{edge}(n-2)&=&f^1_\delta,&\\
 T_\delta.\operatorname{edge}(n-1)&=&f^2_\delta,&\\[1mm]
 T_\delta.\operatorname{vertex}(q)&=&T.\operatorname{vertex}(q),
   &q\le a,\\
 T_\delta.\operatorname{vertex}(q)&=&T.\operatorname{vertex}(q+2),
   &a<q,\ q+2<n+1,\\
 T_\delta.\operatorname{vertex}(n-1)&=&u^1_\delta,&\\
 T_\delta.\operatorname{vertex}(n)&=&u^2_\delta.&
\end{array}                                                   \tag{27}
\]
Thus the retained-before equations hold for every edge position $q<a$
and every vertex position $q\le a$; the retained-after equations hold
for every $q\ge a$ with $q+2<n$ for edges and every $q>a$ with
$q+2<n+1$ for vertices; and the last two edge and vertex equations are
exactly the four displayed final equations.  These are all the indexed
fields, with no suppressed reindexing.

We again verify the \noderef{TreeSequence} fields individually.
Its length is exactly $n=T.\operatorname{length}$ and is positive.
Since $a\ge1$, its first identity is the old first identity $e_0$.
The retained vertices are precisely the old vertices except the two
distinct positions $a+1,a+2$, namely $x_0,x_1$ by (3).  They are
pairwise distinct by old vertex injectivity.  The two final vertices lie
outside $X$ by (25), and they are distinct by the outside-end argument
following (25); hence the entire new vertex map is injective.  The
retained edge identities are precisely the old identities except the two
distinct positions $a,a+1$, namely $h^0_i,h^1_i$.  They are pairwise
distinct by old edge injectivity.  Neither $f^j_\delta$ is an old tree
identity, because (25) gives it an endpoint outside $X$ whereas every old
tree identity has both endpoints in $X$ by the old attachment equations.
Together with (24), this proves injectivity of the entire edge map.

For attachment, a retained edge before $a$ keeps its old later endpoint
and its old earlier witness, both before the deleted positions.  A
retained edge after $a$ is, by the exact structural layout (3), an edge
of a later block; its new vertex is the same block vertex shifted down by
two and its other endpoint is a base vertex, which is retained before it.
Finally (25) says that $f^1_\delta$ and $f^2_\delta$ join their new
vertices to $v^1_\delta,v^2_\delta\in Z$, respectively, and every vertex
of $Z$ occurs in the retained portion of (27).  Thus both final
attachments have an earlier retained witness.  This proves every
tree-sequence field.

The position formulas also give the two exact finite-set identities.
Using the image definition in \noderef{TreeSequenceVertexSet} and the
injectivity just checked,
\[
 V(T_\delta)=
   \bigl(X\setminus\{x_0,x_1\}\bigr)
       \cup\{u^1_\delta,u^2_\delta\}.                         \tag{28}
\]
Likewise, splitting the finite edge-position image into the ranges
$q<a$, $a\le q$ with $q+2<n$, and the two final positions gives
\[
 \begin{split}
 \{T_\delta.\operatorname{edge}(q):q<n\}
   ={}&\bigl((\{T.\operatorname{edge}(q):q<n\}
              \setminus\{h^0_i\})\setminus\{h^1_i\}\bigr)\\
      &\cup\{f^1_\delta,f^2_\delta\}.                        \tag{29}
 \end{split}
\]
The exact position equations in (3) identify $h^j_i$ with
$\operatorname{blockEdge}(i,j)$, so (29) is exactly the successive-erase
edge-finset field of \noderef{ScheideReplacementData}.

Equations (24)--(29), together with the pairwise identity inequalities,
the memberships and endpoint equations (25), and the proved
tree-sequence structure, fill respectively every field of
\noderef{ScheideReplacementData}: the six named identities, all three
edge inequalities, both retained-tree-end memberships, both outside-end
clauses, both endpoint and color equations, the exact-three
biconditional, the replacement tree and length, all four indexed
retention families, the four final-position equations, and the exact
vertex and edge-identity finset equations.

This construction works for each
$\delta\in\noderef{DefectiveColors}(G,\varphi,X)$.  That color set is
finite, so simultaneous finite choice selects one completed
\noderef{ScheideReplacementData} structure for every defective color.
The resulting dependent family is inhabited, which is the asserted
nonemptiness.
\end{proof}

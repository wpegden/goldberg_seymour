\begin{lemma}[Step 1, Subcase 2.1: the hierarchy on the recolored prefix]
Let $G,e_0,[k],T,\varphi,\mathcal E$ be as in the positive-rung setting,
with maximum property, and assume the complete six-part lower-rung package, whose assertion (iii) gives
unconditional stability before each PE support, predecessor-ladder stability
only when its first-appearance index is greater than one, and unconditional
equal-index stored-pair disjointness.
Let $H$ be a good hierarchy over a hierarchy base, and assume the large
estimate $|\overline\varphi(T_n)|\geq2n+11$ and elementarity of $V(T)$.

Let $\gamma\ne\beta$ and let $\mu$ be proper, leave exactly $e_0$
uncolored, and be $(T_n,D_n,\varphi)$-stable.  Suppose exact ordered
prefixes satisfy
\[
 H.\mathit{starred}(d)\preccurlyeq B^-\prec C\preccurlyeq T,
 \qquad C=B^-+g
\]
for some exact edge identity $g$.  Assume every exact edge incident with
$B^-$ has the same color under $\mu$ and $\varphi$, and every vertex of
$B^-$ has the same missing-color set under the two colorings.  At the last
vertex $b$ of $C$, assume $\gamma$ is missing under $\varphi$, $\beta$ is
missing under $\mu$, and an incident exact edge has color $\gamma$ under
$\mu$.  On every vertex of $C$, assume that membership in the missing set
is unchanged for colors other than $\gamma,\beta$.  Finally assume that
$\beta$ is not missing on $T$ under $\varphi$ and that $\gamma$ is absent
from the last indexed Gamma union of $H$.

Then $\mu$ is a \noderef{ModColoring} of $\mathcal E$, and $C$ carries a
\noderef{CorrespondingExtendedTashkinovTree} $\mathcal E_C$ under $\mu$
with maximum property, an actual \noderef{HierarchyBase}, and a genuine
good hierarchy $K_C$ with the same divider count as $H$.  The old terminal
PE route, or the exact old SE/RE seed followed by its route, transports to
a \noderef{TashkinovAugmentationSequence} ending at $C$.  The replacement
retains the large estimate, and both $\gamma$ and $\beta$ are absent from
the last indexed Gamma union of $K_C$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Write $d=H.\mathit{dividerCount}$ and
$A=H.\mathit{starred}(d)$ as in \noderef{HierarchyStarredStage}.  The
lower field of \noderef{ExtendedTashkinovTree}, the PE and non-PE clauses
of \noderef{HierarchyBase}, the left-segment field of
\noderef{OrderedTreeUnion}, and the nesting clauses of
\noderef{TashkinovHierarchy} place the last ladder tree $T_n$ below $A$,
hence below $B^-$.  Transitivity of the exact entrywise prefix relation in
\noderef{TreeSequenceInitialSegment}, using the ordered vertex and edge data
of \noderef{TreeSequence}, therefore gives
\[
                    T_n\preccurlyeq A\preccurlyeq B^-
                       \prec C\preccurlyeq T.                 \tag{1}
\]

We first construct the exact terminal augmentation route to $C$.  Inspect
the last positive certificate in \noderef{TashkinovSeries} and
\noderef{TashkinovSeriesStep}.  Positivity excludes the initial
constructor.  In PE, the certificate contains a
\noderef{TashkinovClosure} of $T_n$ under $\varphi$.  In SE or RE it
contains an individual identity $f_n$, the exact equality $F_n=\{f_n\}$,
the exact seed $T_n+f_n$, and a closure of that seed.  The upper field of
\noderef{ExtendedTashkinovTree} puts $T$ below the last tree of this
closure; prefix transitivity in \noderef{TreeSequenceInitialSegment} then
puts $C$ below that last tree as well.

In PE, truncate the finite
\noderef{TashkinovAugmentationSequence} at the first member whose length
is $|C|$.  Such a member exists because every augmentation increases the
length by one, starts at $T_n$, and ends at a tree containing $C$ as an
exact prefix.  Here the length and index arithmetic are those of
\noderef{TreeSequence}, and the containment is the entrywise relation of
\noderef{TreeSequenceInitialSegment}.  The member and $C$ are equal: both
are exact initial segments of the closure tree with equal lengths, so the
definition of \noderef{TreeSequenceInitialSegment} makes every vertex and
individual edge entry agree.  In SE/RE, prefix transitivity gives that the
seed and $C$ are exact initial segments of the same closure tree.  Relation
(1), the one-edge displayed attachment in \noderef{TreeSequence}, and the
associated length arithmetic give $|T_n|+1\leq |C|$; entrywise comparison
in \noderef{TreeSequenceInitialSegment} then makes the exact seed,
including $f_n$, an initial segment of $C$.  The same equal-length-prefix
argument then gives
\[
 \begin{cases}
 T_n\longrightarrow C,&\Theta_n=\mathrm{PE},\\
 T_n+f_n\longrightarrow C,&\Theta_n\in\{\mathrm{SE},\mathrm{RE}\},
 \end{cases}                                                  \tag{2}
\]
under $\varphi$, retaining $F_n=\{f_n\}$ and the exact one-edge seed in
the second case.

Transport (2) step by step to $\mu$.  Before the final step into $C$,
the old prefix is contained in $B^-$ by
\noderef{TreeSequenceInitialSegment}.  The attachment clause of
\noderef{TreeSequence} and the definition of \noderef{TreeIncidentEdge}
make its new displayed edge incident with that old prefix, so exact-prefix
vertex-set inclusion from \noderef{TreeSequenceVertexSet} makes it incident
with $B^-$ and the assumed incident-edge agreement fixes its color.  The
missing-color witness for the augmentation belongs to the vertex set of
the old prefix, so the same vertex-set inclusion and the assumed
vertexwise missing-set equality preserve it.  For the final step, its old
prefix is $B^-$ and its exact new edge is the identity $g$ in the stated
\noderef{TreeSequenceAdjoinEdge} certificate; the displayed-edge
attachment in \noderef{TreeSequence} and \noderef{TreeIncidentEdge} give
the required incidence, so the same two arguments apply.  Length,
attachment, and exact initial-segment fields are coloring-free by
\noderef{TreeSequence} and \noderef{TreeSequenceInitialSegment}.  These
are all fields of
\noderef{TashkinovAugmentation}; induction over the finite sequence gives
the same alternatives (2) under $\mu$.  Denote their disjunction by
$\mathsf{Elig}_C$.

Apply \noderef{Td} to the given maximum property, positivity, complete
lower-rung package, properness, exact one-uncoloredness, and terminal
stability of $\mu$.  Its conclusion is precisely
\[
                         \noderef{ModColoring}(\mathcal E,\mu).\tag{3}
\]
Relation (1) supplies the exact strict extension of $T_n$, and
$\mathsf{Elig}_C$ supplies the appropriate PE or exact SE/RE alternative
in the universal clause of (3).  That clause yields an actual
\noderef{CorrespondingExtendedTashkinovTree} certificate
$\mathcal C_C$ on $C$.  By \noderef{MaximumPropertyTransport}, its
replacement ETT $\mathcal E_C$ has maximum property and the same positive
rung number $n$.

We next build the actual hierarchy base under $\mu$.  The correspondence
certificate identifies every ladder tree, raw stored set, exact
connecting-edge set, and extension kind.  If the terminal kind is not PE,
take the replacement core to be the common $T_n$, and copy the old
positive support position and the mathematically unused closure field.
The vertexwise missing-set clause of \noderef{StableColoring}, combined
with the witness-union definition of \noderef{TreeMissingColors}, gives
the terminal missing-set compatibility; the nonparallel field of
\noderef{HierarchyBase} is then definitional.

Suppose the terminal kind is PE.  Extract the actual terminal PE
constructors of the original and replacement
\noderef{TashkinovSeriesStep}; write their maximum-defective data as
$(v,u,f,\delta_n,\gamma_n)$ and
$(v',u',f',\delta'_n,\gamma'_n)$.  Their singleton connecting-edge
equations, together with connecting-edge agreement in $\mathcal C_C$,
give
\[
                         \{f'\}=F'_n=F_n=\{f\},\qquad f'=f.    \tag{4}
\]
The endpoint fields of \noderef{MaximumDefectiveChoice} identify the
ends of this individual edge with both $\{v,u\}$ and $\{v',u'\}$.  The
two \noderef{DefectiveVertex} clauses put $v,v'$ in the common $T_n$,
while their other-end clauses put $u,u'$ outside it.  Thus the equality of
unordered endpoint pairs has only the orientation $v'=v$, $u'=u$.  The
old support position therefore records the actual replacement support.

Use the original core and closure $R_n$ for the replacement PE base.  We
do not assert that $R_n$ is an ordered prefix of the core.
The vertex-set field of \noderef{OrderedTreeUnion}, followed by the
hierarchy-core prefix, the PE clause of
\noderef{HierarchyStarredStage}, and (1), gives only the inclusions that
are needed.  The exact-prefix-to-vertex-set implication used here is the
one obtained by unfolding \noderef{TreeSequenceInitialSegment} and
\noderef{TreeSequenceVertexSet}:
\[
 V(R_n)\subseteq V(\mathit{core})\subseteq V(A)\subseteq V(B^-).\tag{5}
\]
Its left-segment field retains the common $T_n$ entries, and its
every-new-right-entry field, interpreted through the ordered entries of
\noderef{TreeSequence}, retains the exact edge and vertex identities of
the added closure portion.

Transport the closure by incidence.  Every displayed edge of $R_n$,
including every new edge in its augmentation sequence, is incident with
its displayed new vertex by the attachment clause of
\noderef{TreeSequence}; (5), \noderef{TreeSequenceVertexSet}, and
\noderef{TreeIncidentEdge} therefore make that exact identity incident
with $B^-$.  Its color is fixed.  Every augmentation color missing on
$R_n$ has a vertex witness in $V(R_n)$ by
\noderef{TreeMissingColors}, so its missing set is fixed.  Hence every
\noderef{TashkinovAugmentation} in the closure remains valid under $\mu$.
If a boundary identity of $V(R_n)$ under $\mu$ had a color missing on
$R_n$, \noderef{BoundaryEdges} gives its retained inside endpoint and
\noderef{TreeMissingColors} gives a missing vertex witness in $V(R_n)$.
By (5) and \noderef{TreeIncidentEdge}, both its exact color and that
missing witness are unchanged on $B^-$; its endpoints are coloring-free,
so it would be the same forbidden boundary identity under $\varphi$.
Thus the same $R_n$ is a genuine \noderef{TashkinovClosure} under $\mu$.
Together with (4), the retained support, and the coloring-free
\noderef{OrderedTreeUnion}, this proves every PE field of a replacement
base $B_C$.  This incidence argument, rather than a false prefix claim
about $R_n$, is the promised PE closure transport.

We construct the hierarchy on $C$.  If $A\prec B^-$, apply
\noderef{GoodHierarchyTerminalPrefixRepackage} to the original hierarchy
and the exact prefix $B^-$.  It yields the same $d$ nonterminal stages,
core, closure, stored sets, Gamma pairs, and a good hierarchy whose
terminal stage is $B^-$.  If $B^-=A$ and $d>0$, apply
\noderef{GoodHierarchyPrefixRepackage} at the last ordinary stage, which
is $A$ by the positive-index clause of \noderef{HierarchyStarredStage}.  It
returns the good $(d-1)$-divider prefix and the separate last internal
minus-closed certificate; append $A$ as the $d$-th ordinary stage below
$C$.  If $B^-=A$ and $d=0$, take stage zero to be $T_n$, terminal stage
$C$, and core $A$ from $B_C$, using the zero-index clause of
\noderef{HierarchyStarredStage}.  Relation (1), with the length and exact
entry content of \noderef{TreeSequence} and
\noderef{TreeSequenceInitialSegment}, supplies every nesting and
strictness field.  In no case is a zero-rung ETT used.

Thus define $K_C$ over $B_C$ with exactly $d$ dividers, the old stages
through $A$, and terminal stage $C$.  Every starred stage lies in
$B^-$ by \noderef{HierarchyStarredStage}, prefix transitivity in
\noderef{TreeSequenceInitialSegment}, and vertex-set inclusion from
\noderef{TreeSequenceVertexSet}; there edge colors and vertex missing sets
agree.  Correspondence gives the same raw stored sets.  The vertexwise
clause of \noderef{StableColoring}, read through
\noderef{TreeMissingColors}, first identifies the two terminal-ladder
missing sets; expanding \noderef{TashkinovStoredColors} and
\noderef{HierarchyStoredColorsAt}, and using the assumed missing-set
equality on the remaining starred-stage vertices, then gives equality of
every indexed stored set.  Use the old Gamma family.  Pair cardinality,
disjointness, and unique refresh therefore copy.  Every internal boundary
identity has an inside endpoint by \noderef{BoundaryEdges}; the stage
vertex-set inclusion and \noderef{TreeIncidentEdge} therefore fix its
exact color.  The applicable prefix-repackaging result supplies the last
internal clause, whose meaning is the one in
\noderef{TreeColorSetMinusClosed}.  In PE, the rebuilt base and the
transported closure above copy the parallel-base cardinality and the
non-minus-closed clause of \noderef{TreeColorSetMinusClosed}; in non-PE
the implication is vacuous.

It remains to check terminal avoidance.  Expand
\noderef{HierarchySegmentColors} and the totalized
\noderef{HierarchyFirstMissingPosition}.  At indices below $d$, the next
stage lies in $B^-$ and all relevant data are unchanged.  At index $d$,
all displayed edges of $C$ retain their colors: by
\noderef{TreeSequenceInitialSegment}, its old entries are the displayed
entries of $B^-$, whose attachment fields in \noderef{TreeSequence} and
the definition of \noderef{TreeIncidentEdge} make them incident with
$B^-$; the last identity $g$ is incident with $B^-$ by the same attachment
field in the adjoin certificate.  For a color outside
$\{\gamma,\beta\}$, the assumed comparison of missing sets on $C$, the
length/index data of \noderef{TreeSequence}, and terminal prefixhood in
\noderef{TreeSequenceInitialSegment} make the new segment a subset of the
old one.

For $\gamma$, \noderef{ColoredVertexSetElementary}, the inclusion
$V(C)\subseteq V(T)$ supplied by \noderef{TreeSequenceInitialSegment} and
\noderef{TreeSequenceVertexSet}, and its old missingness at the last
vertex $b$ make $b$ its unique missing vertex in $T$.  The strict prefix
$B^-\prec C$ and the distinct-vertex clause of \noderef{TreeSequence}
show that $b\notin V(B^-)$.  No vertex of $B^-$ therefore misses $\gamma$
under either coloring, and the stated
incident $\gamma$-edge under $\mu$, together with
\noderef{PresentColors} and \noderef{MissingColors}, says that $b$ does
not miss it under $\mu$.  Since $C=B^-+g$, the exact-entry and vertex-set
descriptions in \noderef{TreeSequence} and
\noderef{TreeSequenceVertexSet} now show that no vertex of $C$ misses
$\gamma$.  The old first position and new no-candidate fallback, together
with the length/index data of \noderef{TreeSequence}, both cap the relevant
segment at the last position.  For $\beta$, the witness-union definition
in \noderef{TreeMissingColors} turns the hypothesis that it is not missing
on $T$ into its absence from every old vertex missing set.  Its new
missingness at $b$, the prefix and vertex-set inclusions from
\noderef{TreeSequenceInitialSegment} and
\noderef{TreeSequenceVertexSet}, and the assumed equality on $B^-$ make
$b$ the first new missing vertex.  The new segment is again only the
unchanged portion before the last position.
Old avoidance implies new avoidance in every case.  We have now checked
every field of a genuine \noderef{GoodHierarchy} witness $\mathcal G_C$.

The old exclusion of $\gamma$ transfers through the equality of indexed
stored sets and Gamma pairs.  If $\beta$ belonged to the last indexed
Gamma union, \noderef{GoodHierarchyReservedDisjoint} would make it
missing on $A$ and hence on $T$, contradicting the hypothesis.  Thus
$\beta$ is excluded as well.  Finally, correspondence identifies the
replacement last ladder tree with the exact old $T_n$, and the vertex
clause of \noderef{StableColoring}, together with the vertex-union
definition in \noderef{TreeMissingColors} and exact vertex-set identity in
\noderef{TreeSequenceVertexSet}, identifies its missing-color set under
$\mu$ with the old one.  Taking cardinalities transports the large
estimate.  The
objects $\mathcal C_C,B_C,K_C,\mathcal G_C$, (2), and (3) therefore
satisfy every conclusion.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Claim 4.3(b2), missing-color clauses]
Let \(\mathcal R\) satisfy
\noderef{ExtensionBaseRestorationInvariant}, with initial coloring
\(\sigma_n\), output \(\varrho_1\), terminal support \(v_n\), and ladder
tree \(T_j\) containing \(v_n\).  Assume the following two explicit
preparations.
\begin{enumerate}
\item If \(v\in V(T_j)\) is not the support of an active slot, then
      \(\overline{\sigma}_n(v)=\overline{\varphi}_{j-1}(v)\).
\item For each nonterminal support \(w\in V(T_j)\), let
      \(i_0<\cdots<i_p\) be the exact support class, with exact fields
      \(\gamma_{i_a},\delta_{i_a}\).  Then
      \[
       \gamma_{i_{a+1}}=\delta_{i_a},
      \]
      the list
      \(\gamma_{i_0},\delta_{i_0},\ldots,\delta_{i_p}\) is injective,
      and
      \[
       \overline{\varphi}_{j-1}(w)
       =(\overline{\sigma}_n(w)\setminus\{\delta_{i_p}\})
          \cup\{\gamma_{i_0}\}.                               \tag{1}
      \]
\end{enumerate}
The first preparation and (1) are the two conclusions of
\noderef{ExtensionBaseClaimMissingColors}; the chain and injectivity are
the conclusions of \noderef{ExtensionBaseSupportClassColors}.  Then
\[
 \overline{\varrho}_1(v)=\overline{\varphi}_{j-1}(v)
       \quad(v\in V(T_j)\setminus\{v_n\}),                    \tag{2}
\]
and
\[
             \overline{\varrho}_1(v_n)
             =\overline{\sigma}_n(v_n).                       \tag{3}
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Write the colorings in \(\mathcal R\) as
\(\sigma^0,\ldots,\sigma^r\).  Unpacking
\noderef{ExtensionBaseRestorationTrace}, its initial-coloring field gives
\(\sigma^0=\sigma_n\), its output is
\(\sigma^r=\varrho_1\), and at position \(a<r\) it records the exact
slot \(t_a\), support \(v_{t_a}\), colors
\(\gamma_{t_a},\delta_{t_a}\), and the transition
\[
 \sigma^{a+1}=
 \noderef{TwoColorComponentSwap}
   (\sigma^a,\gamma_{t_a},\delta_{t_a},v_{t_a}).                \tag{A}
\]
Its enumeration field says that the set of the \(t_a\)'s is exactly
\(L^*\), and its strict-descent field says
\(a<b\Rightarrow t_b<t_a\).  In particular the \(t_a\)'s are distinct.

We first derive two consequences that will be used at every transition.
Unpack \noderef{ExtensionBaseRestorationInvariant} at position \(a\).
It says that \(T_j\) is an initial segment of \(T_{t_a}\), that
\(v_{t_a}\in V(T_{t_a})\), that
\(\delta_{t_a}\in\overline{\sigma^a}(v_{t_a})\), and that the
\((\gamma_{t_a},\delta_{t_a})\)-component \(K_a\) rooted at
\(v_{t_a}\) is a path whose vertex-node intersection with
\(V(T_{t_a})\) is exactly \(\{v_{t_a}\}\).
By \noderef{TreeSequenceInitialSegment}, every ordered vertex entry of
\(T_j\) occurs as the same entry of \(T_{t_a}\).  Unfolding
\noderef{TreeSequenceVertexSet} (the image of the ordered vertex map)
therefore gives
\[
                         V(T_j)\subseteq V(T_{t_a}).            \tag{B}
\]

Let \(x\in V(T_j)\) with \(x\ne v_{t_a}\).  By (B) and the exact
intersection clause, the vertex node \(x\) is not in \(K_a\).  We claim
that no edge incident with \(x\) has its edge node in \(K_a\).  Indeed,
unfold \noderef{TwoColorComponent}: membership means membership in the
connected component of the root in
\noderef{TwoColorIncidenceGraph}.  If an edge node \(f\) is in this
component, then, since it is not the vertex root, the last adjacency on
a walk from the root to \(f\) shows from the incidence-graph definition
that \(\sigma^a(f)\) is one of
\(\gamma_{t_a},\delta_{t_a}\).  If \(f\) is also incident with \(x\),
the same definition makes \(x\) adjacent to \(f\).  Connected-component
closure under this adjacency would put the vertex node \(x\) in \(K_a\),
a contradiction.  Thus all edges incident with \(x\) lie outside
\(K_a\), and the edgewise definition of
\noderef{TwoColorComponentSwap} in (A) leaves every one of their colors
unchanged.  By \noderef{PresentColors}, the sets of colors occurring on
incident edges are equal before and after the transition; by
\noderef{MissingColors}, taking their complements in the full color set
gives
\[
              \overline{\sigma^{a+1}}(x)
                    =\overline{\sigma^a}(x).                  \tag{C}
\]

At the root \(x=v_{t_a}\), unpacking
\noderef{TwoColorPathComponent} supplies an edge node in \(K_a\).
Because \(K_a\) is the connected component of the vertex root by
\noderef{TwoColorComponent}, a walk from the root to that edge has a
first edge node adjacent to the root.  The adjacency formula in
\noderef{TwoColorIncidenceGraph} says that this is an incident edge of
color \(\gamma_{t_a}\) or \(\delta_{t_a}\).  But the invariant says
that \(\delta_{t_a}\) is missing; after unfolding
\noderef{MissingColors} and \noderef{PresentColors}, no incident edge
has that color.  The distinct-color clause of
\noderef{TwoColorPathComponent} therefore shows that
\(\gamma_{t_a}\) is present at the root.
Moreover, every incident edge of color \(\gamma_{t_a}\) is adjacent to
the root in the incidence graph and therefore has its edge node in
\(K_a\); there is no incident edge of color \(\delta_{t_a}\).
The edgewise clauses of \noderef{TwoColorComponentSwap} consequently
change all incident gamma edges to delta, change no incident delta edge
to gamma, and preserve every other incident-edge color.  Thus the new
present-color set is obtained by deleting gamma and adjoining delta.
Taking its complement using \noderef{MissingColors} gives the exact root
exchange
\[
 \overline{\sigma^{a+1}}(v_{t_a})
   =(\overline{\sigma^a}(v_{t_a})
        \setminus\{\delta_{t_a}\})
          \cup\{\gamma_{t_a}\}.                              \tag{6}
\]
This derives, rather than assumes, the missing-color effect of each
transition.

We now identify which transitions occur at a fixed nonterminal support.
Unfold \noderef{ExtensionBaseActiveIndices} and
\noderef{ExtensionBaseSupportClass}: a slot belongs to \(L(w)\) exactly
when it is active and its exact chosen vertex is \(w\).  Unfolding
\noderef{ExtensionBaseRestorationIndices} gives
\[
                         L^*=L\setminus L(v_n).                \tag{D}
\]
Let \(w\in V(T_j)\) and \(w\ne v_n\).  If an active slot is supported
at \(w\), it cannot lie in \(L(v_n)\), since its exact chosen-vertex
field cannot be both \(w\) and \(v_n\).  Hence it lies in \(L^*\) by
(D), and the enumeration field of
\noderef{ExtensionBaseRestorationTrace} produces a trace position having
that exact slot; its exact chosen-support field then makes the recorded
support equal to \(w\).  Conversely, if a trace position has support
\(w\), the per-step slot clause of
\noderef{ExtensionBaseRestorationInvariant} puts its slot in \(L^*\),
hence in \(L\), while the exact support field of
\noderef{ExtensionBaseRestorationTrace} puts that slot in \(L(w)\).
Thus the trace enumeration proves the equivalence
\[
 \{t_a:v_{t_a}=w\}=L(w)                                      \tag{E}
\]
including both inclusions.  In particular, the existence of any active
slot supported at \(w\) supplies the antecedent of preparation 2.

Take its exact increasing enumeration
\(i_0<\cdots<i_p\) and exact fields
\(\gamma_{i_a},\delta_{i_a}\).  Equation (E), trace enumeration, and
distinctness of the trace slots show that each \(i_a\) occurs exactly
once among the \(t_b\)'s.  Since the trace is strictly descending, the
transitions supported at \(w\) occur in exactly the order
\[
                         i_p,i_{p-1},\ldots,i_0.               \tag{5}
\]
Here the descending-order field is the one in
\noderef{ExtensionBaseRestorationTrace}; together with its enumeration
field it also proves that there are no omitted or repeated class slots.
There are no additional transitions at \(w\).  At a transition supported
at another vertex, transport \(w\in V(T_j)\) through
\noderef{TreeSequenceInitialSegment} and
\noderef{TreeSequenceVertexSet} as in (B), then apply the exact component
intersection, \noderef{TwoColorComponent},
\noderef{TwoColorIncidenceGraph}, and
\noderef{TwoColorComponentSwap} as in (C).  Such a transition preserves
the missing-color set at \(w\).

At the transition with slot \(i_a\), the exact support, gamma, and delta
fields in \noderef{ExtensionBaseRestorationTrace} identify (6) with
\[
 \overline{\sigma}^{\,\mathrm{after}}(w)
   =(\overline{\sigma}^{\,\mathrm{before}}(w)
        \setminus\{\delta_{i_a}\})\cup\{\gamma_{i_a}\}.
\]
The prepared chain says
\(\gamma_{i_a}=\delta_{i_{a-1}}\) for \(a>0\).  Hence the first
transition in (5) deletes \(\delta_{i_p}\) and adjoins the delta needed
by the next transition; each following transition deletes precisely that
adjoined delta and adjoins the next one.  We also check that no deletion
removes a color which was initially missing.  For \(a>0\), the root-path
argument preceding (6), using \noderef{TwoColorPathComponent}, says that
\(\gamma_{i_a}=\delta_{i_{a-1}}\) is present immediately before the
step at \(i_a\).  Every earlier trace step supported at \(w\) has
index \(i_b\) with \(b>a\), and by (6) it touches only
\(\delta_{i_b}\) and
\(\gamma_{i_b}=\delta_{i_{b-1}}\).  Injectivity of
\(\gamma_{i_0},\delta_{i_0},\ldots,\delta_{i_p}\) makes both colors
different from \(\delta_{i_{a-1}}\); intervening steps at other supports
preserve every incident-edge color at \(w\) by (C).  The latter color is
therefore unchanged from \(\sigma_n\) until the step at \(i_a\), so it was already
present, rather than missing, under \(\sigma_n\).  The same injectivity
ensures that no transition deletes the final color \(\gamma_{i_0}\),
and that unrelated colors are untouched.  Induction down the complete
list (5), using (6), therefore
gives
\[
 \overline{\varrho}_1(w)
   =(\overline{\sigma}_n(w)\setminus\{\delta_{i_p}\})
        \cup\{\gamma_{i_0}\}.                                \tag{7}
\]
Preparation (1) identifies the right side with
\(\overline{\varphi}_{j-1}(w)\).

It remains to treat the other two cases.  Let \(v\in V(T_j)\) be
unsupported by every active slot.  Every trace slot lies in \(L^*\) and
hence is active by (D), by the per-step clause of
\noderef{ExtensionBaseRestorationInvariant}; its exact support field in
\noderef{ExtensionBaseRestorationTrace} therefore makes its support
different from \(v\).
At every transition transport \(v\) by
\noderef{TreeSequenceInitialSegment} and
\noderef{TreeSequenceVertexSet}, and apply (C), whose component and swap
steps were justified by \noderef{TwoColorComponent},
\noderef{TwoColorIncidenceGraph}, and
\noderef{TwoColorComponentSwap}.  Induction over the trace, followed by
its initial-coloring equality, yields
\[
                 \overline{\varrho}_1(v)
                 =\overline{\sigma}_n(v)
                 =\overline{\varphi}_{j-1}(v),                \tag{4}
\]
where the second equality is preparation 1.  Every
\(v\in V(T_j)\setminus\{v_n\}\) is either in this unsupported case or
has an active supporting slot and is covered by (7).  This proves (2).

Finally, no restoration slot is supported at \(v_n\).  Indeed, every
trace slot lies in the set difference (D) by
\noderef{ExtensionBaseRestorationInvariant}; if its stored support were
\(v_n\), the exact chosen-vertex field of
\noderef{ExtensionBaseRestorationTrace}, together with the definition
\noderef{ExtensionBaseSupportClass}, would put it in \(L(v_n)\),
contradicting (D).  At every transition, transport the given
\(v_n\in V(T_j)\) through \noderef{TreeSequenceInitialSegment} and
\noderef{TreeSequenceVertexSet}; it differs from the current support, so
the exact intersection and connected-component argument using
\noderef{TwoColorComponent} and \noderef{TwoColorIncidenceGraph}, followed
by the edgewise rule in \noderef{TwoColorComponentSwap}, preserves all
incident-edge colors.  Unfolding \noderef{PresentColors} and then
\noderef{MissingColors} turns that preservation into equality of missing
sets.  Induction through the trace and its initial-coloring field now
derives
\[
             \overline{\varrho}_1(v_n)
                =\overline{\sigma}_n(v_n).                    \tag{3}
\]
which is (3) and completes the missing-color clauses of Claim 4.3(b2).
\end{proof}

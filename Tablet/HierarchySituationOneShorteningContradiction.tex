\begin{lemma}[Situation 7.1: shortening at the last exact edge]
Fix the Section 7 hierarchy counterexample and its supplied rung-local
\noderef{HierarchyMinimumCounterexampleContext}.  Assume
\(\noderef{HierarchyPathNumber}=1\) and suppose
\[
 \ell-1\in\noderef{HierarchyEligibleCommonMissingIndices}.
\]
Then these hypotheses contradict the supplied rung-local lexicographic
minimality and hence are impossible.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Write \(H=C.\mathit{hierarchy}\),

\[
 A=\noderef{HierarchyStarredStage}
       (H,\operatorname{Fin.last}(H.\mathit{dividerCount})),
 \qquad
 \ell=\noderef{HierarchyFullTailLength}(C)=|T|-|A|.
                                                               \tag{1}
\]

Unfold the filter in
\noderef{HierarchyEligibleCommonMissingIndices} at the assumed index
\(\ell-1\).  It supplies a
\noderef{HierarchyReplacementPresentation} \(P\) and a color \(\alpha\).
Put
\[
 \mu_n=P.\mathit{coloring},\quad
 \mathcal E_P=P.\mathit{corresponding}.\mathit{replacementETT},\quad
 C_P=P.\mathit{counterexample},\quad
 H_P=C_P.\mathit{hierarchy}.
\]
The defining membership also says that \(\alpha\) is \(\mu_n\)-missing at
the last vertices of
\[
 B=\noderef{HierarchyFullTailPrefix}(C,\ell-1)
 \quad\hbox{and}\quad T.                                     \tag{2}
\]
The first component of \(P.\mathit{sameGood}\), by
\noderef{SameGoodHierarchy} and
\noderef{SameHierarchyStages}, identifies the entire stage and divider
families of \(H\) and \(H_P\), including their last starred stage.  Since
their exact terminal tree is the same \(T\), (1), all full-tail prefixes,
and \(\noderef{HierarchyPathNumber}\) are the same for these two
presentations.  In particular
\[
                 \noderef{HierarchyPathNumber}(H_P)=1.        \tag{3}
\]

Apply \noderef{HierarchyCounterexampleTailAtLeastTwo} to
\(\mathcal E_P\), \(P.\mathit{replacementBase}\), \(C_P\), and the
properness, unique-uncoloredness, lower-rung, fewer-divider, minimality, and
large-missing-set fields of \(P.\mathit{minimumContext}\).  Its starred
stage is the one in (1), so
\[
                              2\leq\ell.                       \tag{4}
\]
The exact nesting in \noderef{TashkinovHierarchy}, together with the PE
and non-PE alternatives of \noderef{HierarchyBase} and the
\noderef{OrderedTreeUnion} left-segment field in the PE alternative, also
gives
\[
 T_n\preccurlyeq A\preccurlyeq
 X^-:=\noderef{HierarchyFullTailPrefix}(C,\ell-2)
 \preccurlyeq B\preccurlyeq T,                               \tag{5}
\]
where \(T_n\) is the terminal ladder tree of \(\mathcal E_P\).  Expanding
\noderef{HierarchyFullTailPrefix} and its exact
\noderef{TreeSequencePrefix}, and using
\(|A|+\ell=|T|\), which follows from (1) and the terminal inclusion, gives
\[
 |X^-|=|T|-2,\qquad |B|=|T|-1,\qquad |B|=|X^-|+1.             \tag{6}
\]
Every relation in (5) is the exact ordered relation
\noderef{TreeSequenceInitialSegment}; hence it retains every displayed
vertex and individual edge identity.

Define the last edge index by
\[
 t_\ell:=\langle |T|-1,\ |T|-1<|T|\rangle\in\operatorname{Fin}|T|,
 \qquad e_\ell:=T.\mathit{edge}(t_\ell).                     \tag{7}
\]
Thus \(t_\ell\) is an edge index of value \(|T|-1\); it is not the vertex
index \(\operatorname{Fin.last}|T|\in\operatorname{Fin}(|T|+1)\).
Let \(y_{\ell-1}\) and \(y_\ell\) be the last vertices of \(B\) and \(T\),
respectively.  From (3), the nonempty candidate branch in the definition of
\noderef{HierarchyPathNumber} has least candidate (1): the fallback is
\(\ell\ne1\) by (4).  Applying the candidate condition to the index (7),
whose value is \(|A|+\ell-1\geq |A|+1\), yields the exact endpoint equation
\[
                  G.\mathit{ends}(e_\ell)
                    =\{y_{\ell-1},y_\ell\}.                  \tag{8}
\]

We next extract the color of this exact edge and its earlier missing
witness.  Inspect the certificate at the final positive index in
\noderef{TashkinovSeries} and
\noderef{TashkinovSeriesStep} for \(\mathcal E_P\).  If its kind is PE,
the final \noderef{TashkinovClosure} supplies a
\(\mu_n\)-\noderef{TashkinovAugmentationSequence} from \(T_n\) to the
final closure.  If its kind is SE or RE, that certificate supplies an exact
edge \(f_n\), an exact
\noderef{TreeSequenceAdjoinEdge} seed \(S=T_n+f_n\), the singleton
connecting-edge equation \(F_n=\{f_n\}\), and an augmentation sequence from
this seed to the final closure.  Denote the source by \(S\), taking
\(S=T_n\) in the PE case.

The upper-containment field of
\noderef{CorrespondingExtendedTashkinovTree}, equivalently the
\(\noderef{ExtendedTashkinovTree}\) carried by it, puts \(T\) and hence
\(B\) inside that same final closure.  In the PE case (5) gives
\(S=T_n\preccurlyeq B\).  In the SE/RE case the seed has length
\(|T_n|+1\).  If the divider count is zero, the non-PE clause of
\noderef{HierarchyBase} gives \(A=T_n\), so (4)--(6) give
\(|S|\leq|B|\); if the divider count is positive, strict nesting in
\noderef{TashkinovHierarchy} gives the same inequality.  Comparing the
entries of the common final closure then gives the exact relation
\(S\preccurlyeq B\).  Therefore
\noderef{TashkinovAugmentationSequenceRestrict}, first at \(T\) and then at
\(B\), gives exact old-color routes
\[
                            S\longrightarrow T,
              \qquad       S\longrightarrow B.               \tag{9}
\]

Peel the last step from the first finite route in (9).  This can be checked
directly by induction on the reflexive-transitive chain in
\noderef{TashkinovAugmentationSequence}: every
\noderef{TashkinovAugmentation} raises the length by one and retains the
old sequence exactly.  Since \(S\preccurlyeq B\prec T\) and
\(|T|=|B|+1\), the chain is nonempty; its last predecessor has length
\(|T|-1\), and comparison inside the common target \(T\) identifies that
predecessor with the exact prefix \(B\).  Thus its last step is
\[
                     \noderef{TashkinovAugmentation}(\mu_n,B,T). \tag{10}
\]
Unfolding (10) supplies a color \(\beta\) such that
\[
 \mu_n(e_\ell)=\operatorname{some}\beta,
 \qquad \beta\in\noderef{TreeMissingColors}(\mu_n,B).         \tag{11}
\]
Choose, using the definitions of \noderef{TreeMissingColors} and
\noderef{TreeSequenceVertexSet}, a displayed vertex \(z\in V(B)\) at which
\(\beta\) is missing.

Equation (8) makes \(e_\ell\) incident with \(y_{\ell-1}\), while (11)
colors that edge \(\beta\).  The properness field of
\(P.\mathit{minimumContext}\), together with
\noderef{IsProperEdgeColoring}, \noderef{Incident}, and
\noderef{MissingColors}, therefore excludes
\(z=y_{\ell-1}\): the color \(\beta\) is present there on the exact edge
\(e_\ell\), and so cannot be missing there.  By (6), \(B\) is obtained
from \(X^-\) by adding precisely its last displayed edge and vertex.
The exact vertex equalities in \noderef{TreeSequenceInitialSegment} and
the vertex injectivity field of \noderef{TreeSequence} show that every
vertex of \(B\) other than \(y_{\ell-1}\) lies in \(V(X^-)\).  Hence
\[
             z\in V(X^-),\qquad
             \beta\in\noderef{MissingColors}(G,\mu_n,z).      \tag{12}
\]
This is the required earlier witness; it was not asserted directly by the
last augmentation.

Define \(\sigma_n\) by
\[
 \sigma_n(e)=
 \begin{cases}
   \operatorname{some}\alpha,&e=e_\ell,\\
   \mu_n(e),&e\ne e_\ell.
 \end{cases}                                                  \tag{13}
\]
By (2), \(\alpha\) is missing under \(\mu_n\) at both endpoints in
(8).  Consequently no other edge incident with either endpoint has color
\(\alpha\).  For two exact adjacent edges in the sense of
\noderef{AdjacentEdges}, if neither is \(e_\ell\), properness is inherited
from \(\mu_n\); if one is \(e_\ell\), (8) and the two missingness statements
give the required color inequality.  Thus (13) satisfies
\noderef{IsProperEdgeColoring}.

The index \(t_\ell\) in (7) is different from the first edge index: by
(4), positivity of \(A\), and (1), its value is positive.  Edge injectivity
and the first-edge field of \noderef{TreeSequence} therefore give
\(e_\ell\ne e_0\).  Equation (11), (13), and the unique-uncoloredness of
\(\mu_n\) now show directly from \noderef{IsOneEdgeUncolored} that
\(\sigma_n(e_0)=\mathsf{none}\) and every other exact graph edge remains
colored.  Hence \(\sigma_n\) also leaves exactly \(e_0\) uncolored.

Neither endpoint in (8) lies in \(V(X^-)\): they are the two strictly later
displayed vertices, and \noderef{TreeSequence}.vertex_injective excludes
their equality with an earlier entry.  Thus \(e_\ell\) is not incident
with \(X^-\), and (13) gives
\[
 \noderef{MissingColors}(G,\sigma_n,v)
   =\noderef{MissingColors}(G,\mu_n,v)
       \quad(v\in V(X^-)).                                    \tag{14}
\]
Likewise, every displayed edge of (B) is fixed.  Indeed, its corresponding
index in \(T\) has value less than \(|B|=|T|-1\), so equality with
\(e_\ell=T.\mathit{edge}(t_\ell)\) would contradict the edge injectivity
field of \noderef{TreeSequence}.  Hence
\[
              \sigma_n(B.\mathit{edge}(t))
                =\mu_n(B.\mathit{edge}(t))\quad(t<|B|).       \tag{15}
\]

In particular \(T_n\preccurlyeq X^-\) by (5).  Equation (14) gives
vertexwise missing-set equality on \(T_n\), and the same no-incidence
argument says that every \noderef{TreeIncidentEdge} of \(T_n\) is different
from \(e_\ell\) and is fixed by (13).  Both clauses of
\noderef{StableColoring} therefore show that \(\sigma_n\) is
\((T_n,D_n,\mu_n)\)-stable, for the terminal stored set
\(D_n=\noderef{TashkinovStoredColors}\) of \(\mathcal E_P\).
Apply \noderef{Td} to \(\mathcal E_P\), its maximum property, its positive
rung, and the complete lower-rung field of
\(P.\mathit{minimumContext}\).  With the properness,
unique-uncoloredness, and stability just proved, it yields
\[
                         \noderef{ModColoring}(\mathcal E_P,\sigma_n).
                                                               \tag{16}
\]

We now construct the shortened ETT under the new coloring.  In each of the
three terminal-kind cases, take the old route \(S\to B\) in (9) and apply
\noderef{TashkinovAugmentationSequenceColoringTransport} with
\[
       \varphi=\mu_n,\quad \sigma=\sigma_n,\quad
       X=B,\quad\hbox{penultimate prefix }X^- .                \tag{17}
\]
The required exact prefix and unit length equation are (5)--(6).  The
fixed-edge premise follows from (15), even without its lower bound on edge
positions, and the vertexwise missing-set premise is exactly (14).  We
obtain the identical exact route
\[
                              S\longrightarrow B
                    \quad\hbox{under }\sigma_n.                \tag{18}
\]
The relation \(T_n\prec B\) follows from (4)--(6).  In the PE case, (18)
starts at \(T_n\).  In the SE/RE cases it starts at the same exact seed
\(T_n+f_n\), and the singleton equation \(F_n=\{f_n\}\) and
\noderef{TreeSequenceAdjoinEdge} certificate were retained from the final
series step.  Thus (18) supplies precisely the appropriate PE or SE/RE
alternative in the universal clause of (16).  By
\noderef{ModColoring} there is an actual
\[
 \mathcal C_\sigma:
   \noderef{CorrespondingExtendedTashkinovTree}
       (G,k,e_0,\mathcal E_P,\sigma_n,B).                      \tag{19}
\]
Set
\(\mathcal E_\sigma=\mathcal C_\sigma.\mathit{replacementETT}\).
This ETT is genuinely generated by \(\sigma_n\); its input and certified
series are those in (19), not those of an old-color prefix package.
Applying \noderef{MaximumPropertyTransport} to (16), (19), and
\(P.\mathit{maximum}\) gives maximum property for
\(\mathcal E_\sigma\).                                       \tag{20}

For the remaining structural bookkeeping, apply
\noderef{GoodHierarchyTerminalPrefixRepackage}, with
\(P.\mathit{maximum}\), to the old-color tuple
\((\mathcal E_P,P.\mathit{replacementBase},H_P,C_P.\mathit{good})\)
and the exact prefix \(B\).  The strict starred-stage inclusion required
there is (4)--(6), and \(B\preccurlyeq T\) is (5).  Retain from its output
only the shortened stage family, divider positions, core, closure, support
position, and Gamma family, together with their proved old-color
properties.  We do not use its old-color ETT or its maximum-property output
as the witness in (19)--(20).

We next rebuild a \noderef{HierarchyBase} for
\(\mathcal E_\sigma\) under \(\sigma_n\), using those retained core,
closure, and support data.  The corresponding certificate (19) identifies
all ordered ladder trees, raw stored sets, exact connecting-edge sets, and
extension kinds with those of \(\mathcal E_P\).  In a non-PE terminal
case the old base has core \(T_n\), so ladder-tree agreement gives the
required new non-PE clause.

Suppose the terminal kind is PE.  Inspect the final PE constructors in
\noderef{TashkinovSeriesStep} for the old and replacement series.  Each
constructor has a \noderef{MaximumDefectiveChoice}; its exact connecting
edge is a singleton, its selected vertex belongs to \(V(T_n)\) by
\noderef{DefectiveVertex}, its other endpoint lies outside \(V(T_n)\), and
it records the unordered endpoint pair.  Connecting-edge-set agreement in
(19) identifies the two singleton edge identities.  The two endpoint
equations for that same edge, with one endpoint inside \(T_n\) and the other
outside, therefore have only one orientation; the replacement chosen
vertex equals the old one.  The old PE field of
\noderef{HierarchyBase} identifies it with the retained support position.

It remains in this PE case to transport the retained closure \(R_n\).
The old \noderef{OrderedTreeUnion} says
\(V(T_n\vee R_n)=V(T_n)\cup V(R_n)\), and this union is the retained core,
which is contained in \(A\preccurlyeq X^-\).  Hence neither endpoint of
\(e_\ell\) lies in \(R_n\), and (13)--(14) fix every edge and every
vertex-missing set occurring in its old augmentation route.  Induction on
that route, using \noderef{TashkinovAugmentation}, transports each exact
step to \(\sigma_n\); since \(e_\ell\) is not incident with \(R_n\), the
old closedness condition also remains true.  Thus the retained \(R_n\) is
a \(\sigma_n\)-\noderef{TashkinovClosure} of the same support prefix.
The old ordered-union certificate is coloring-independent.  This proves
all PE fields of the new base.  Terminal missing-color compatibility is
reflexive because both the ETT generating coloring and base coloring are
\(\sigma_n\).  Denote the resulting base by \(\mathcal B_\sigma\).

Build a \noderef{TashkinovHierarchy} \(H_\sigma\) on \(B\) and
\(\mathcal B_\sigma\) by copying the shortened stage family and divider
positions from the prefix package.  Its base-stage equation uses the exact
terminal-ladder agreement in (19), its core-inside-first equation uses the
retained core, and its terminal stage is now \(B\).  Every nestedness,
strictness, and divider-prefix equation is one of the exact equations
already proved by the prefix package.  Consequently
\[
 H_\sigma.\mathit{dividerCount}=H_P.\mathit{dividerCount},     \tag{21}
\]
all nonterminal ordinary and starred stages agree, and only the terminal
tree has changed from \(T\) to \(B\).

Use the retained Gamma function as the Gamma function for \(H_\sigma\).
We verify every field of \noderef{GoodHierarchy}, rather than transporting
it across a change of generating coloring.  First, agreement in (19) of
the raw stored sets, together with (14) on \(T_n\), identifies the two
terminal \noderef{TashkinovStoredColors} sets.  Every starred stage and
ordinary hierarchy stage lies in \(A\preccurlyeq X^-\), so (14) identifies
all \noderef{HierarchyStoredColorsAt} and
\noderef{HierarchyStageMissingColors} sets for the old-color prefix package
and \(H_\sigma\).

It remains to compare the segment-color sets, whose bound uses
\noderef{HierarchyFirstMissingPosition} on the terminal tree \(B\).
For a color other than \(\alpha,\beta\), all missing-color memberships on
\(B\) are unchanged.  For \(\beta\), (12) is an unchanged missing witness
strictly before the last vertex, and the recoloring changes missingness only
at the later vertex \(y_{\ell-1}\); hence the least missing position is
unchanged.  For \(\alpha\), if there is an earlier missing witness, its
least position is unchanged by (14).  If there is none, under \(\mu_n\)
the first missing position is the last vertex \(y_{\ell-1}\) by (2), while
under \(\sigma_n\) there is no missing witness in \(B\) and the totalized
fallback is that same last position.  Therefore every color has the same
first missing position.  Combining this with the exact displayed-edge
equality (15) and the retained starred and next-stage bounds gives
\[
 \noderef{HierarchySegmentColors}(H_\sigma,j,\eta)
   =\noderef{HierarchySegmentColors}(H_{P,B},j,\eta)           \tag{22}
\]
for every stage \(j\) and color \(\eta\), where \(H_{P,B}\) is the
old-color shortened hierarchy supplied by the prefix package.

The two-color cardinalities, avoidance, disjointness, and unique-refresh
fields now copy using these exact stored, stage-missing, segment, and Gamma
equalities.  The positive-stage
\noderef{TreeColorSetMinusClosed} clauses also copy: all their stages lie
in \(X^-\), so \(e_\ell\) is not incident with them and every other edge is
fixed.  In the PE parallel-base clause, the core and \(R_n\) likewise lie
in \(X^-\), so their missing sets and every boundary edge value are
unchanged; both the non-minus-closed assertion and the cardinality bound
copy from the old-color prefix package.  These checks construct
\[
                         \Gamma_\sigma:
                         \noderef{GoodHierarchy}(H_\sigma).   \tag{23}
\]

Finally, (12) says that \(z\in V(X^-)\subseteq V(B)\) still misses
\(\beta\) under \(\sigma_n\), because \(e_\ell\) is not incident with
\(z\).  Equations (11) and (13) remove the only \(\beta\)-colored edge
\(e_\ell\) incident with \(y_{\ell-1}\); properness of \(\mu_n\) says
there was no second incident \(\beta\)-edge, so \(y_{\ell-1}\) also misses
\(\beta\) under \(\sigma_n\).  The two vertices are distinct by
\noderef{TreeSequence}.vertex_injective.  Thus their missing-color sets
intersect, and the pairwise-disjointness definition
\noderef{ColoredVertexSetElementary} shows that \(V(B)\) is not elementary.
Together with the positive rung from (19), (20), (21), and (23), this
constructs a genuine
\[
 C_\sigma:\noderef{HierarchyCounterexample}(\mathcal B_\sigma) \tag{24}
\]
whose ETT is generated by \(\sigma_n\).

It remains to apply the stated minimum, not a newly assumed one.  The rung
agreement in (19) gives
\(\mathcal E_\sigma.\mathit{rounds}=\mathcal E_P.\mathit{rounds}\),
and (21) gives equal divider counts.  The path number of \(C_P\) is one by
(3).  Its candidate \(1\) retains the consecutive-edge endpoint equation
on every edge still displayed in \(B\), so it is also a candidate for
\(H_\sigma\).  The candidate interval in
\noderef{HierarchyPathNumber} starts at \(1\), and the shortened tail has
positive length by (4); hence the path number of \(C_\sigma\) is exactly
one.

Let \(T_{n,q}\) be the last ordinary nonterminal stage.  It is unchanged in
(21), and strict nestedness gives \(|T_{n,q}|<|B|\).  With (6), direct
natural-number arithmetic gives
\[
 \noderef{HierarchyCounterexampleMeasure}(C_\sigma)
   =\bigl(1,\ |T|-1-|T_{n,q}|\bigr)
   <_{\rm hier}
    \bigl(1,\ |T|-|T_{n,q}|\bigr)
   =\noderef{HierarchyCounterexampleMeasure}(C_P),             \tag{25}
\]
where the strict relation is exactly
\noderef{HierarchyCounterexampleLess}.  Applying the
same-rung, equal-divider lexicographic-minimality field of
\(P.\mathit{minimumContext}\) to (24)--(25) is a contradiction.  Therefore
the assumed eligible index \(\ell-1\) is impossible.
\end{proof}

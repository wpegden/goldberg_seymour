\begin{lemma}[Claim 9: zero-divider PE normalization]
Fix the Section 7 minimum-counterexample data and a
\noderef{HierarchyReplacementPresentation} \(P\) on the same exact terminal
tree \(T\).  Assume every proper coloring leaving exactly \(e_0\) uncolored
has a missing color at every vertex.  Suppose the replacement hierarchy has
zero dividers, its terminal series step is PE, and a color \(\alpha\) is
missing on its starred core and at the final vertex \(y_\ell\), but not on
its terminal ladder tree \(T_n\).  Suppose also that no index
\(1\leq i\leq\ell-1\) is already common-missing with \(y_\ell\) under the
coloring of \(P\).

Then either the finite set
\noderef{HierarchyEligibleCommonMissingIndices} is already nonempty, or
there are another replacement presentation \(P'\) and a color \(\beta\)
which is missing under \(P'\) on \(T_n\), on the starred core, and at
\(y_\ell\), while no eligible tail index is common-missing with \(y_\ell\)
under \(P'\).  All ladder trees, hierarchy stages, and terminal-tree edge
identities in \(P'\) are the exact ones in \(P\) and in the originally fixed
counterexample.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Write \(\mu_n=P.\mathit{coloring}\), let \(\mathcal E_\mu\) be the
replacement ETT in \(P\), let \(\mathcal B_\mu\) be its replacement base,
and write
\[
 H=P.\mathit{counterexample}.\mathit{hierarchy},\qquad
 A=\noderef{HierarchyStarredStage}(H,0).
\]
The fields of \noderef{HierarchyReplacementPresentation} supply an actual
\noderef{CorrespondingExtendedTashkinovTree}, maximum property for
\(\mathcal E_\mu\), the hierarchy counterexample with its
\noderef{GoodHierarchy} witness, the
\noderef{SameGoodHierarchy} comparison with the originally fixed
counterexample, and a transported
\noderef{HierarchyMinimumCounterexampleContext}.  In particular
\(\mu_n\) is proper, leaves exactly \(e_0\) uncolored, and carries the
complete lower-rung, fewer-divider, rung-local minimality, and \(2n+11\)
data.

Apply \noderef{HierarchyCounterexamplePenultimateElementary} and
\noderef{HierarchyCounterexampleTailAtLeastTwo} to those transported
context fields.  Same-good-hierarchy data identify the exact base core,
divider count, stages, and divider positions with the original ones.
Consequently the resulting full-tail length and prefix are
\[
 \ell=\noderef{HierarchyFullTailLength}(C),\qquad
 B=\noderef{HierarchyFullTailPrefix}(C,\ell-1),
\]
and we have
\[
        2\leq\ell,\qquad
        V(B)\text{ is elementary under }\mu_n.                 \tag{1}
\]
The ordered prefix equations in \noderef{TreeSequencePrefix} and
\noderef{TreeSequenceInitialSegment} give
\[
 A\preccurlyeq B\prec T,\qquad |B|=|T|-1.                     \tag{2}
\]
Thus the final displayed vertex
\(y_\ell=T.\mathit{vertex}(|T|)\) is not in \(V(B)\), by vertex
injectivity in \noderef{TreeSequence}.

Since the divider count is zero,
\noderef{HierarchyStarredStage} identifies \(A\) with the base core.
The terminal PE hypothesis and the parallel clause of
\noderef{HierarchyBase} then give an exact
\noderef{OrderedTreeUnion}
\[
                         A=T_n\vee R_n                         \tag{3}
\]
and a \(\mu_n\)-\noderef{TashkinovClosure} \(R_n\) of the recorded
supporting prefix of \(T_n\).  In particular
\[
 V(A)=V(T_n)\cup V(R_n),\qquad T_n\preccurlyeq A.              \tag{4}
\]
The color \(\alpha\) is missing on \(A\), but is not missing on \(T_n\).
Expanding \noderef{TreeMissingColors} in (3)--(4) therefore supplies an
actual vertex \(v_\alpha\in V(R_n)\) at which \(\alpha\) is missing.
It lies outside \(V(T_n)\).

Let \(D_{n,0}=\noderef{HierarchyStoredColorsAt}(H,0)\), and let
\[
 \Gamma^0=\bigcup_{\eta\in D_{n,0}}
                  P.\mathit{counterexample}.\mathit{good}.\Gamma^0_\eta .
\]
The two-colors and disjoint-pairs fields of
\noderef{GoodHierarchy} make this a disjoint union of two-element sets.
By \noderef{HierarchyStoredColorsCardinality},
\[
                 |\Gamma^0|=2|D_{n,0}|\leq2n.                 \tag{5}
\]
The large-set field of the transported minimum context says
\[
                   2n+11\leq|\overline{\mu_n}(T_n)|.
\]
Finite cardinality comparison with (5) therefore supplies a color
\[
          \beta\in\overline{\mu_n}(T_n)\setminus\Gamma^0.      \tag{6}
\]
Because \(\alpha\notin\overline{\mu_n}(T_n)\), (6) also makes
\(\beta\ne\alpha\).  Old avoidance at index zero says that each Gamma
pair is contained in
\(\noderef{HierarchyStageMissingColors}(H,0)
 =\overline{\mu_n}(T_n)\).  Hence
\[
                         \alpha,\beta\notin\Gamma^0.           \tag{7}
\]
Notice that (7) uses avoidance for \(\alpha\) and the choice (6) for
\(\beta\); it makes no unsupported assertion that either color is absent
from the whole terminal stored set.

We next prove the component separation required for the Kempe change.
Let
\[
 v_\beta=T.\mathit{vertex}
   (\noderef{HierarchyFirstMissingPosition}(\mu_n,T,\beta)).
\]
Because \(\beta\) already has a missing witness in \(T_n\), the
least-position definition places \(v_\beta\) in \(V(T_n)\) and makes
\(\beta\) missing there.  Likewise choose \(v_\alpha\) to be the first
\(\alpha\)-missing vertex; since \(\alpha\) is missing on \(A\), it lies
in \(V(A)\).  This choice may replace the witness selected above, and
\(\alpha\notin\overline{\mu_n}(T_n)\) still places it in
\(V(R_n)-V(T_n)\).

Apply clause (iv) of \noderef{interchange} with the ordered color pair
\((\beta,\alpha)\).  Its mixed hypotheses have exactly this orientation:
\(\beta\) is missing on \(T_n\), while \(\alpha\) is missing on \(R_n\).
The coloring premise is the reflexive
\noderef{CombinedStableColoring}: both constituent
\noderef{StableColoring} relations compare \(\mu_n\) with itself.
The maximum, positivity, properness, exactly-one-uncolored, and lower-rung
premises are the corresponding fields of \(P\).  We conclude that
\[
          \beta,\alpha\text{ are interchangeable on }A
          \text{ under }\mu_n.                                \tag{8}
\]

By \noderef{MissingColorIncidenceEndpoint}, the vertex-nodes
\(\operatorname{inl}v_\beta\) and
\(\operatorname{inl}v_\alpha\) have degree at most one in the
\((\beta,\alpha)\)-incidence graph.  For \(v_\alpha\), apply the lemma
with the colors reversed and use the literal symmetry of
\noderef{TwoColorIncidenceGraph}.  Thus their components are
\noderef{TwoColorPathComponent}s.  Both meet \(A\), so (8) and the
definition of \noderef{TreeInterchangeable} identify them.  Call their
common full incidence component \(K_A\).

Let
\[
 Q=\noderef{TwoColorComponent}
      (G,\mu_n,\beta,\alpha,\operatorname{inl}y_\ell).
\]
The hypothesis that \(\alpha\) is missing at \(y_\ell\), together with
\(\beta\ne\alpha\) and
\noderef{MissingColorIncidenceEndpoint}, makes \(Q\) a possibly isolated
\noderef{TwoColorPathComponent}.  We claim that \(Q\ne K_A\).
Suppose otherwise.  Then \(Q\) contains the three vertex-nodes
\[
       \operatorname{inl}v_\alpha,\quad
       \operatorname{inl}v_\beta,\quad
       \operatorname{inl}y_\ell,                              \tag{9}
\]
each of degree at most one.  The last one is distinct from the first two
by (2), since both first-missing vertices lie in \(A\subseteq B\).
If \(v_\alpha=v_\beta\), both affected colors are missing at that vertex;
unfolding \noderef{MissingColors} and
\noderef{TwoColorIncidenceGraph} shows that its incidence-graph degree is
zero.  Its component is therefore the isolated singleton, contrary to
the additional distinct node \(\operatorname{inl}y_\ell\) in (9).

If \(v_\alpha\ne v_\beta\), all three nodes in (9) are distinct.
By \noderef{TwoColorComponentSpanningPath}, the component \(Q\) is the
support of a simple path.  A non-end node on that path has its two
distinct path neighbors, while
\noderef{TwoColorIncidenceDegreeAtMostTwo} bounds its full degree by two.
Consequently the only nodes of full degree at most one are the two path
ends.  The three distinct degree-at-most-one nodes in (9) are impossible.
This proves \(Q\ne K_A\).  The equal-or-disjoint conclusion of
\noderef{TwoColorComponentsDisjoint} now gives
\[
                         Q\cap K_A=\varnothing.                \tag{10}
\]
This is the required component argument; it does not follow from the
no-common-tail hypothesis.

Define
\[
 \sigma_n=\noderef{TwoColorComponentSwap}
       (G,\mu_n,\beta,\alpha,\operatorname{inl}y_\ell).
\]
By \noderef{TwoColorComponentSwapProper}, \(\sigma_n\) is proper.
The swap fixes every uncolored value and only transposes the two displayed
colored values, so \noderef{IsOneEdgeUncolored} shows that it still leaves
exactly \(e_0\) uncolored.

We now prove the vertexwise assertion actually needed on the penultimate
prefix:
\[
 \overline{\sigma_n}(v)=\overline{\mu_n}(v)
                 \quad\text{for every }v\in V(B).              \tag{11}
\]
Fix \(v\in V(B)\).  First suppose that
\(\operatorname{inl}v\in Q\).  By (10), \(v\) is neither
\(v_\alpha\) nor \(v_\beta\).  Elementarity in (1) and the missing
witnesses \(v_\alpha,v_\beta\in V(A)\subseteq V(B)\), interpreted via
\noderef{ColoredVertexSetElementary}, imply that neither
\(\alpha\) nor \(\beta\) is missing at \(v\).  Expanding
\noderef{MissingColors} and \noderef{PresentColors}, each of the two
colors is therefore carried by an incident edge identity.  Properness,
in the local uniqueness form of \noderef{IsProperEdgeColoring}, makes that
identity unique for each color.  Its edge-node is adjacent to
\(\operatorname{inl}v\), hence belongs to the same component \(Q\).
The swap transposes these two incident values.  Thus both colors remain
present at \(v\), while every color outside
\(\{\alpha,\beta\}\) is fixed by
\noderef{TwoColorComponentSwap}.  The missing-color set at \(v\) is
unchanged.

Suppose instead that \(\operatorname{inl}v\notin Q\).  If an edge-node
of \(Q\) were incident with \(v\), then its old value would be
\(\alpha\) or \(\beta\) and the definition of
\noderef{TwoColorIncidenceGraph} would make it adjacent to
\(\operatorname{inl}v\), forcing that vertex-node into \(Q\).  Therefore
no edge incident with \(v\) is changed, and its missing-color set is again
unchanged.  These cases prove (11).  Importantly, vertices of \(B\) may
belong to \(Q\); (11) was proved by the degree and elementarity argument,
not by claiming that \(Q\) is outside \(B\).

We can now invoke \noderef{StableChangeCasesOneTwo}, with its ordered
colors again chosen as \((\beta,\alpha)\).  Both are missing on \(B\):
\(\beta\) has its witness in \(T_n\subseteq A\subseteq B\), and
\(\alpha\) has its witness in \(A\subseteq B\).  The path-component
premise is the component through \(y_\ell\), and (10) is exactly its
disjointness from the component through the first \(\beta\)-missing
vertex.  The Cases 1/2 premise is its zero-divider alternative with
\(\beta\in\overline{\mu_n}(T_n)\).  Supply (1), all transported context
fields, and the defining equation for \(\sigma_n\).  The lemma yields
\[
\begin{array}{ll}
\text{(S1)}&
 \sigma_n\text{ is }\noderef{HierarchyWeaklyStable}\text{ at }A,\\
\text{(S2)}&
 \sigma_n\text{ is a }\noderef{ModColoring}\text{ of }\mathcal E_\mu .
\end{array}                                                     \tag{12}
\]
Since the divider count is zero and the terminal kind is PE, unfolding
\noderef{HierarchyWeaklyStable} in (S1) gives precisely
\[
 \noderef{CombinedStableColoring}
    (T_n,R_n,D_n,\mu_n,\sigma_n).                              \tag{13}
\]

It remains to construct the full ETT and hierarchy under \(\sigma_n\).
Inspect the actual last positive certificate in the
\noderef{TashkinovSeries} of \(\mathcal E_\mu\).  The terminal-kind
identity selects the PE constructor of
\noderef{TashkinovSeriesStep}.  Its
\(\mu_n\)-\noderef{TashkinovClosure}, together with the final
initial-segment field of \noderef{ExtendedTashkinovTree}, supplies a
\(\mu_n\)-\noderef{TashkinovAugmentationSequence} from \(T_n\) to the
certified final output.  Apply
\noderef{TashkinovAugmentationSequenceRestrict} first at the exact prefix
\(T\), and then at the intermediate exact prefix \(A\) from
(2)--(4).  We obtain an old route
\[
            T_n\longrightarrow A\longrightarrow T             \tag{14}
\]
whose steps retain every individual edge identity.

For completeness, the suffix \(A\to T\) in (14) is obtained inside the
finite reflexive-transitive chain, not postulated.  Each
\noderef{TashkinovAugmentation} raises the length by exactly one and
retains the preceding ordered entries.  Starting at \(T_n\), take the
first member whose length is at least \(|A|\).  Since \(A\) and every
chain member are exact prefixes of \(T\), the one-step length equation
and equality of same-length exact prefixes make that member exactly
\(A\).  Factoring the chain there gives the displayed prefix and suffix.

Transport first the route \(T_n\to A\).  The two
\noderef{StableColoring} clauses in (13) preserve vertexwise missing sets
on \(T_n\) and \(R_n\).  The ordered-union vertex equation (3) therefore
preserves the missing witness of every old augmentation predecessor
inside \(A\).  For its exact new edge, the left-prefix part of
\noderef{OrderedTreeUnion} handles the retained \(T_n\) entries, while
the new-entry-from-right field identifies every later displayed identity
of \(A\) with an identity of \(R_n\).
\noderef{CombinedStableClosurePreserved}, applied to (13), says explicitly
that all displayed identities of \(T_n\) and \(R_n\) have the same values
under \(\mu_n\) and \(\sigma_n\).  Thus the old new-edge color and its
missing witness are both preserved at every step.  Induction through
\noderef{TashkinovAugmentationSequence} proves
\[
                  T_n\longrightarrow A
                  \quad\text{under }\sigma_n.                  \tag{15}
\]

Equation (11) in particular preserves the missing witnesses
\(v_\alpha,v_\beta\in V(A)\).  Hence
\[
       \alpha,\beta\in\overline{\sigma_n}(A).                  \tag{16}
\]
Apply the new canonical transport lemma
\noderef{TashkinovAugmentationSequenceComponentSwap} to the old suffix
\(A\to T\) in (14), with the component rooted at \(y_\ell\).
Its two missing-color hypotheses are exactly (16), and its coloring is
the displayed \(\sigma_n\).  It follows, without any assertion that the
tail-edge values are individually fixed, that the same exact suffix is a
\(\sigma_n\)-augmentation sequence:
\[
                  A\longrightarrow T
                  \quad\text{under }\sigma_n.                  \tag{17}
\]
Concatenating (15) and (17) gives a
\(\sigma_n\)-Tashkinov augmentation sequence from \(T_n\) to \(T\).

The universal clause of (S2), applied to this PE route and the strict
ordered inclusion \(T_n\prec T\), produces an actual
\noderef{CorrespondingExtendedTashkinovTree} on the exact ordered tree
\(T\) generated by \(\sigma_n\).  Applying
\noderef{MaximumPropertyTransport} to (S2), this certificate, and the
maximum property of \(\mathcal E_\mu\) gives maximum property for its
replacement ETT.  To retain the dependent hierarchy data, apply
\noderef{HierarchySplitter} to (13) and the same full route.  Choose its
corresponding certificate \(\mathcal C_\sigma\), its maximum property,
its replacement base \(\mathcal B_\sigma\), and its hierarchy \(K\).
The conclusion gives
\[
                 \noderef{SameHierarchyStages}(H,K).           \tag{18}
\]
In particular the new base core is the same exact \(A\), its PE closure
is the same exact \(R_n\), and \(K\) has divider count zero with the same
terminal stage \(T\).

We rebuild goodness rather than treating (18) as a goodness certificate.
The corresponding-ETT fields identify every raw stored set and every
positive ladder tree.  Expanding
\noderef{TashkinovStoredColors}, raw-set agreement together with the
vertexwise \(T_n\)-stability in (13) identifies the computed terminal
stored sets under \(\mu_n\) and \(\sigma_n\).  Equation (11) and
\(A\subseteq B\) identify the colors missing on the common starred core
\(A\).  Expanding \noderef{HierarchyStoredColorsAt} therefore gives
\[
                 D^K_{n,0}=D^H_{n,0}.                         \tag{19}
\]
Likewise, the zero branch of
\noderef{HierarchyStageMissingColors} is the common terminal ladder tree,
so (13) identifies the two stage-missing sets.

Define the sole Gamma function of \(K\) to be the old Gamma function,
using the divider-count equality in (18).  The two-colors and
disjoint-pairs fields of \noderef{GoodHierarchy} copy immediately after
(19).  There are no unique-refresh or minus-closed obligations because
the divider count is zero.

We verify avoidance pointwise.  Fix
\(\eta\in D^K_{n,0}=D^H_{n,0}\).  By
\noderef{HierarchyStoredColorsAt}, \(\eta\) is not missing on \(A\).
Since both \(\alpha\) and \(\beta\) are missing on \(A\), this gives
\(\eta\notin\{\alpha,\beta\}\).  The component swap fixes the presence
and missingness of \(\eta\) at every graph vertex, so
\[
 \noderef{HierarchyFirstMissingPosition}(\sigma_n,T,\eta)
 =
 \noderef{HierarchyFirstMissingPosition}(\mu_n,T,\eta).        \tag{20}
\]
Now take \(c\) in the copied pair \(\Gamma^0_\eta\).  By (7),
\(c\notin\{\alpha,\beta\}\).  Thus the component swap fixes the value of
every edge having color \(c\), in both directions.  If \(c\) belonged to
the new \noderef{HierarchySegmentColors} set, its witnessing exact
position would have the same range by (20), and the same edge would have
old color \(c\); it would therefore belong to the old segment-color set,
contradicting old avoidance.  The required stage-missing membership
transports by the stage-missing equality above.  This proves the full
avoidance field even though some tail edges colored \(\alpha\) or
\(\beta\) may have been recolored.

It remains to verify the PE parallel-base field.  Its cardinality clause
involves only the missing-color sets on \(T_n\) and \(R_n\), the common
stored set (19), and the copied Gamma pairs.  The two vertexwise clauses
of (13) identify those finite sets exactly, so the old cardinality-at-least
four inequality transports.

For the non-minus-closed clause, expand
\noderef{TreeColorSetMinusClosed} and
\noderef{TreeColorSetClosed}.  The old failure supplies an individual
\noderef{BoundaryEdges} identity \(f\) of \(A\), a color
\(c\in\overline{\mu_n}(A)-\Gamma^0\), and
\(\mu_n(f)=c\).  If \(c\notin\{\alpha,\beta\}\), the swap fixes \(f\)'s
value and (11) preserves its missing witness in \(A\).  If
\(c\in\{\alpha,\beta\}\), the new value of \(f\) is either \(c\) or the
other affected color; both affected colors are missing on \(A\) by
(16), and both lie outside \(\Gamma^0\) by (7).  In all cases \(f\)
remains the same boundary identity and belongs to the appropriate
\noderef{BoundaryColorEdges} set for a new color missing on \(A\) but
outside \(\Gamma^0\).  Hence \(A\) is still not
\((\Gamma^0)^-\)-closed.  All five fields of
\noderef{GoodHierarchy} have now been checked; denote the resulting
witness by \(g_\sigma\).

The base core, PE closure, divider count, stage family, and divider
positions agree by (18); the only indexed stored set agrees by (19), and
the Gamma function was copied there.  These are exactly the clauses of
\noderef{SameGoodHierarchy}(P.\mathit{counterexample}.\mathit{good},
g_\sigma)\), and transitivity with the same-good data in \(P\) gives
same-good data relative to the originally fixed \(C\).

The vertex \(v_\beta\in V(T_n)\subseteq V(B)\) remains
\(\beta\)-missing by (11).  At \(y_\ell\), the component swap turns the
old missing \(\alpha\) into a missing \(\beta\): by the local uniqueness
clause of \noderef{IsProperEdgeColoring}, if a \(\beta\)-edge is present
there it lies in \(Q\) and becomes the unique \(\alpha\)-edge,
while if no such edge is present then both colors were already missing.
The two vertices are distinct by (2).  Thus \(V(T)\) is not elementary
under \(\sigma_n\).  Positivity follows from rung agreement in
\(\mathcal C_\sigma\); together with its maximum property, \(K\), and
\(g_\sigma\), this constructs a genuine
\noderef{HierarchyCounterexample} \(C_\sigma\) on the
\(\sigma_n\)-generated replacement ETT.

We next transport every field of the minimum context.  The rung agreement
in \(\mathcal C_\sigma\) rewrites the lower-rung and fewer-divider
quantifiers to those in \(P.\mathit{minimumContext}\).
Equation (18) fixes the divider count, base core, complete stage family,
and terminal tree.  Therefore both coordinates of
\noderef{HierarchyCounterexampleMeasure}, including the path-number
coordinate, are equal for \(C_\sigma\) and
\(P.\mathit{counterexample}\).  Any same-rung, equal-divider
counterexample smaller than \(C_\sigma\) in
\noderef{HierarchyCounterexampleLess} would consequently contradict the
old rung-local minimality field.  Finally, ladder-tree agreement and the
\(T_n\) vertexwise clause of (13) identify the two terminal-ladder
missing-color sets, so the \(2n+11\) estimate transports.  Properness and
exact one-uncoloredness are also fields of
\(\mathcal C_\sigma\).  These data form the required
\noderef{HierarchyMinimumCounterexampleContext}.

We have thus constructed a replacement presentation \(P'\) with coloring
\(\sigma_n\), corresponding ETT \(\mathcal C_\sigma\), replacement base,
counterexample \(C_\sigma\), same-good comparison, and transported
minimum context.

It remains to check the stated no-common-tail conclusion.  Fix
\(1\leq i\leq\ell-1\), and let \(y_i\) be the last vertex of the exact
prefix \(\noderef{HierarchyFullTailPrefix}(C,i)\).  It lies in \(B\), so
(11) identifies its old and new missing sets.  Colors outside
\(\{\alpha,\beta\}\) also have the same missing status at \(y_\ell\);
the original no-common hypothesis excludes them.  If \(\alpha\) is still
missing at \(y_\ell\), then it was missing there before the swap, and the
same original hypothesis excludes it at \(y_i\).  Finally, \(\beta\) is
missing at \(v_\beta\in V(T_n)\subseteq V(B)\).  The vertex \(y_i\) is a
later displayed vertex outside \(A\), so it is distinct from \(v_\beta\);
elementarity of \(B\) in (1) excludes \(\beta\) from its missing set.
Hence the missing sets at \(y_i\) and \(y_\ell\) are disjoint under
\(\sigma_n\).

The same-stage comparison identifies the new starred core with \(A\).
Thus \(\beta\) is missing under \(P'\) on \(T_n\), on that starred core,
and at \(y_\ell\), and the preceding paragraph proves the required
universal disjointness.  These are exactly the witnesses for the second
alternative of the lemma, and hence prove the asserted disjunction.
\end{proof}

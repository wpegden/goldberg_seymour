\begin{lemma}[Step 1, Subcase 2.1: restricted augmentation and minimality]
Let $G$ be a finite loopless multigraph retaining individual parallel-edge
identities, let $e_0$ be distinguished, and let $[k]$ be the color set.
Let $T$ be a positive-rung certified ETT with generating coloring
$\varphi$, maximum property, properness, and exactly $e_0$ uncolored.
Assume the complete six-part lower-rung package.  Let $H$ be a good
hierarchy over a hierarchy base, with $d<q_0$ dividers.  Assume uniformly
that every same-rung maximum ETT built from an arbitrary certified input
and generating coloring and having a good hierarchy with fewer than
$q_0$ dividers is elementary.  Assume the large bound
$|\overline\varphi(T_n)|\geq2n+11$, the separate terminal certificate
\[
 T\text{ is }
 \left(\bigcup_{\eta\in D^H_{n,d+1}}\Gamma^d_\eta\right)^-
 \text{-closed under }\varphi,
\]
and elementarity of $V(T)$.  Put $A=H.\mathit{starred}(d)$.

Let $\alpha\ne\beta$, with $\alpha$ missing and singleton-closed on $T$
and $\beta$ not missing on $T$.  Let $P_1,P_2,P_3$ be pairwise
support-disjoint exact exit paths for $\alpha,\beta$.  Their outside ends
$a_1,a_2,a_3$ and tree ends $b_1,b_2,b_3$ are pairwise distinct and
$b_1\prec_T b_2\prec_T b_3$.  Retain, for every $j$, the exact
$\beta$-colored boundary-edge identity $f_j$ on $P_j$ incident with
$b_j$.  Suppose $b_3\notin V(A)$.

Assume the following universal secondary-minimality property, ranging
over arbitrary certified replacement inputs rather than only over paths
on the current display.  Let $\sigma$ be any proper coloring leaving
exactly $e_0$ uncolored and stable from $\varphi$ on the final ladder
tree.  Let $T'$ be the exact terminal tree of any corresponding
same-rung ETT under $\sigma$, with maximum property, a hierarchy base,
a good hierarchy $K$ with exactly $d$ dividers, its own large estimate,
and its separate terminal minus-closed certificate.  For distinct colors
$a,b$, suppose $a$ is missing and singleton-closed on $T'$ and two exact
support-disjoint $(a,b)$ exit legs have distinct outside ends.  Such a
replacement is forbidden if either
\begin{enumerate}
\item $b$ is missing on $T'$, so its secondary coordinate is zero; or
\item $b$ is not missing and there is a third exact exit leg, pairwise
support-disjoint from the first two and with a third outside end, such
that the total of the three exact walk lengths is strictly less than
$|P_1|+|P_2|+|P_3|$.
\end{enumerate}
Assume in addition that the following prefix-plus-two-edges package has
been constructed.  There are a color $\gamma\ne\beta$, a proper coloring
$\mu$ leaving exactly $e_0$ uncolored, and an exact ordered tree $J$,
together with a corresponding same-rung ETT $\mathcal E_J$ under $\mu$,
a maximum-property certificate, and a good hierarchy $K_J$ on $J$ with
exactly $d$ dividers.  The coloring $\mu$ is
$(T_n,D_n,\varphi)$-stable and is a mod-coloring of the original ETT; the
replacement has the same large missing-color estimate; neither $\gamma$
nor $\beta$ belongs to the union of the last indexed Gamma pairs of
$K_J$; and $\beta$ is missing at $b_3$.  There are three exact
$(T,\mu,\{\gamma,\beta\})$-exit paths $R_1,R_2,R_3$ with the same
respective endpoints, supports, and lengths as $P_1,P_2,P_3$, so their
supports remain pairwise disjoint.  Finally $C$ is the exact prefix of
$T$ ending at $b_3$, $J_1=C+f_1$, $J=J_1+f_2$ by exact
\noderef{TreeSequenceAdjoinEdge} certificates, $b_1,b_2,b_3\in V(J)$,
and both endpoints of each exact identity $f_1,f_2$ lie in $V(J)$.
Then the displayed Subcase 2.1 data are contradictory.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Unpack the asserted prefix-plus-two-edges package and retain its exact
witnesses, writing them as
$\gamma,\mu_5,C,J_1,J,\mathcal C_J,B_J,K_J,\mathcal G_J$ and
$R_1,R_2,R_3$.  Retain separately all of the package's conjuncts:
properness and exact one-uncoloredness of $\mu_5$, its stability from
$\varphi$ on $T_n$, its mod-coloring certificate, maximum property of
$\mathcal C_J.\mathit{replacementETT}$, the equality
$K_J.\mathit{dividerCount}=H.\mathit{dividerCount}$, the replacement
large estimate, the two last-Gamma-union exclusions, the missingness of
$\beta$ at $b_3$, the three \noderef{TreeExitPath} certificates and their
support and length data, and every exact prefix, adjoin-edge, endpoint,
and vertex-membership assertion involving $C,J_1,J,f_1,f_2$.

Apply \noderef{GoodHierarchyRestrictedAugmentation} to the original ETT,
its given maximum-property certificate, the seed mod-coloring $\mu_5$,
and the objects $J,\mathcal C_J,B_J,K_J,\mathcal G_J$.  Unpack the entire
conclusion.  It gives a number $m$, an exact chain
\[
                         U_0=J,U_1,\ldots,U_m=U,               \tag{12}
\]
and the following named data: every successor is an exact restricted
Tashkinov augmentation; every chain member has the asserted length; each
earlier member is an exact \noderef{TreeSequenceInitialSegment} of each
later member; each successor has strictly larger vertex set; and the
whole chain is the certified exact augmentation sequence from $J$ to $U$.
Retain also its terminal avoidance and maximality clauses.  Most
importantly, the same conclusion gives a corresponding ETT certificate
$\mathcal C_U$, maximum property of
$\mathcal C_U.\mathit{replacementETT}$, a reconstructed base $B_U$, a
hierarchy $K_U$, the equality
\[
 K_U.\mathit{dividerCount}=K_J.\mathit{dividerCount},          \tag{13}
\]
equality of all canonically indexed nonterminal stages, starred stages,
stored sets, stage-missing sets, and divider positions, and a good witness
$\mathcal G_U$ whose indexed Gamma pairs equal those of $\mathcal G_J$.
Finally retain, as two separate returned clauses,
\[
 D^{K_U}_{n,d+1}\subseteq D^{K_J}_{n,d}                       \tag{14}
\]
and
\[
 U\text{ is }
 \left(\bigcup_{\eta\in D^{K_U}_{n,d+1}}
       \mathcal G_U.\Gamma^d_\eta\right)^-
 \text{-closed under }\mu_5.                                  \tag{15}
\]
Here $d=K_U.\mathit{dividerCount}$ in the left-hand notation of (14),
and the canonical identification in (13) sends the last index of $K_U$
to the last index of $K_J$.

By the defining same-rung field of
\noderef{CorrespondingExtendedTashkinovTree},
$\mathcal C_U.\mathit{replacementETT}$ has rung number equal to that of
the original ETT.  Its maximum property was just returned.  Moreover,
(13), the seed equality
$K_J.\mathit{dividerCount}=H.\mathit{dividerCount}$, and
$H.\mathit{dividerCount}<q_0$ give
$K_U.\mathit{dividerCount}<q_0$.  The uniform fewer-divider hypothesis,
applied to this actual replacement ETT, $B_U,K_U$, and $\mathcal G_U$,
therefore gives
\[
          \noderef{ColoredVertexSetElementary}(G,\mu_5,V(U)). \tag{16}
\]

We next record two consequences of (14) before considering the paths.
Suppose, for example, that $\gamma$ belonged to the terminal union in
(15).  Then some
$\eta\in D^{K_U}_{n,d+1}$ would have
$\gamma\in\mathcal G_U.\Gamma^d_\eta$.  Inclusion (14) puts the
canonically corresponding $\eta$ in $D^{K_J}_{n,d}$, and indexed Gamma
equality changes the latter membership to
$\gamma\in\mathcal G_J.\Gamma^d_\eta$.  This contradicts the seed
exclusion of $\gamma$ from the last Gamma union of $K_J$.  The identical
argument with $\beta$ and the other seed exclusion proves
\[
 \{\gamma,\beta\}\cap
 \bigcup_{\eta\in D^{K_U}_{n,d+1}}
       \mathcal G_U.\Gamma^d_\eta=\varnothing.                 \tag{17}
\]

The exact segment $J\preccurlyeq U$ is the $r=0,s=m$ instance returned
in (12).  Unfolding \noderef{TreeSequenceInitialSegment} and
\noderef{TreeSequenceVertexSet} transports each seed membership
$b_i\in V(J)$ to $b_i\in V(U)$ and transports every vertex of $J$,
including every endpoint of the exact identities $f_1,f_2$, to $V(U)$.
In particular the seed fact $\beta\in\noderef{MissingColors}(G,\mu_5,b_3)$
and the union definition in \noderef{TreeMissingColors} show that
$\beta$ is missing on $U$.

We claim that none of $a_1,a_2,a_3$ lies in $V(U)$.  Suppose instead that
$a_j\in V(U)$ for some $j$.  Unfolding the seed
\noderef{TreeExitPath} certificate for $R_j$ says that $a_j$ misses
$\gamma$ or $\beta$.  The same definition puts every $a_i$ outside
$V(T)$ and every $b_i$ inside $V(T)$, so $a_j\ne b_3$.  Since $b_3$ in
$V(U)$ misses $\beta$, elementarity (16) rules out $\beta$ missing at
$a_j$; hence $\gamma$ is missing at $a_j$, and thus on $U$ by
\noderef{TreeMissingColors}.

Now unfold \noderef{TreeColorSetMinusClosed}: (15) is
\noderef{TreeColorSetClosed} for the colors missing on $U$ outside its
terminal reserved union.  The missingness of $\beta$ and $\gamma$ just
proved, together with (17), puts both colors in that difference.  The
definition of \noderef{TreeColorSetClosed} therefore gives
\[
 \noderef{BoundaryColorEdges}(G,\mu_5,V(U),\gamma)=\varnothing,
 \qquad
 \noderef{BoundaryColorEdges}(G,\mu_5,V(U),\beta)=\varnothing. \tag{18}
\]

Fix $i$.  If $a_i\notin V(U)$, traverse $R_i$ backwards from
$b_i\in V(U)$ toward $a_i$ and take the first transition from a
vertex-node in $V(U)$ to a vertex-node outside it.  By the adjacency
definition in \noderef{TwoColorIncidenceGraph}, the intervening edge-node
is an individual edge identity $e$ incident with both vertices and
$\mu_5(e)$ is either $\gamma$ or $\beta$.  Unfolding
\noderef{BoundaryEdges}, the two endpoint memberships put
$e\in\partial_G(V(U))$; unfolding \noderef{BoundaryColorEdges} then puts
$e$ in one of the two sets in (18), a contradiction.  Hence every
$a_i$ lies in $V(U)$.  The missing-color alternative in each seed
\noderef{TreeExitPath}, elementarity with the distinct vertex $b_3$ that
misses $\beta$, and the fact that $a_i\notin V(T)$ whereas
$b_3\in V(T)$ force every $a_i$ to miss $\gamma$.  The distinct vertices
$a_1,a_2$ in $V(U)$ now both miss $\gamma$, contradicting (16).  This
proves
\[
                         a_1,a_2,a_3\notin V(U).               \tag{19}
\]

For each $j$, write the node list of the simple incidence path $R_j$ in
its given direction from $a_j$ to $b_j$.  Since $a_j\notin V(U)$ by
(19) and $b_j\in V(U)$, there is a least position $s_j$ occupied by a
vertex-node $t_j\in V(U)$.  The bipartite adjacency definition in
\noderef{TwoColorIncidenceGraph} shows that vertex- and edge-nodes
alternate.  Thus $s_j$ is a positive even position, in particular
$s_j\geq2$.  Let $L_j$ be the walk prefix through that occurrence of
$t_j$.  A prefix of the simple path $R_j$ is simple, its length is
$s_j\geq2$, its outside end is $a_j\notin V(U)$, its other end is
$t_j\in V(U)$, and the seed missing-color alternative at $a_j$ is
unchanged.  Minimality of $s_j$ says that no earlier vertex-node of the
prefix belongs to $V(U)$, while simplicity says the final occurrence of
$t_j$ is unique.  These are, field by field, precisely
\[
 L_j:\noderef{TreeExitPath}(U,\mu_5,\gamma,\beta,a_j,t_j)
 \quad(j=1,2,3).                                               \tag{20}
\]
Because every $L_j$ is a walk prefix of $R_j$, its support is contained
in the support of $R_j$.  The seed support-disjointness therefore makes
the three $L_j$ pairwise support-disjoint, and their outside ends remain
the seed's pairwise distinct $a_1,a_2,a_3$.

For the strict comparison, use the exact edge identity $f_1$, not an
equality of node lists.  The seed says that $\operatorname{inr}(f_1)$
occurs in the support of $R_1$ and that every endpoint incident with
$f_1$ lies in $V(J)$, hence in $V(U)$.  When $R_1$ is traversed from
$a_1$, the vertex-node immediately preceding its occurrence of
$\operatorname{inr}(f_1)$ is therefore already in $V(U)$, by the
incidence clause of \noderef{TwoColorIncidenceGraph}.  The least entry
position $s_1$ occurs strictly before that edge-node.  Thus $L_1$ is a
proper walk prefix of $R_1$, whereas $L_2,L_3$ are walk prefixes of
$R_2,R_3$.  Consequently
\[
 |L_1|<|R_1|=|P_1|,\qquad
 |L_2|\leq|R_2|=|P_2|,\qquad
 |L_3|\leq|R_3|=|P_3|.                                       \tag{21}
\]
The three displayed comparisons, added term by term, give
\[
 |L_1|+|L_2|+|L_3|<|P_1|+|P_2|+|P_3|.                        \tag{22}
\]

It remains to instantiate the universal no-better-presentation
hypothesis.  Take $\sigma=\mu_5$, terminal tree $U$, corresponding
certificate $\mathcal C_U$, base $B_U$, hierarchy $K_U$, and good witness
$\mathcal G_U$.  Properness, exact one-uncoloredness, and stability on the
original terminal ladder tree are seed clauses.  The helper returned
maximum property and (15), while (13) together with the seed count equality
gives exactly the required equality with the $d$ dividers of $H$.
For the large estimate, the defining fields of
\noderef{CorrespondingExtendedTashkinovTree} identify the rung numbers,
terminal ladder tree, and generating coloring of both
$\mathcal C_J.\mathit{replacementETT}$ and
$\mathcal C_U.\mathit{replacementETT}$ with those of the original ETT;
hence the seed estimate for $\mathcal C_J$ is exactly the required estimate
for $\mathcal C_U$.

Use first comparison color $\beta$ and second comparison color $\gamma$.
They are distinct.  The missingness of $\beta$ on $U$, (17), (15), and
the unfolded definitions of \noderef{TreeColorSetMinusClosed} and
\noderef{TreeColorSetClosed} give singleton-$\beta$-closedness on $U$.
The adjacency predicate in \noderef{TwoColorIncidenceGraph} is symmetric
in the two colors, and the outside-missing alternative in
\noderef{TreeExitPath} is a symmetric disjunction.  Thus each certificate
in (20), with the same exact walk and all the same remaining fields, is
also a $(\beta,\gamma)$ exit-path certificate.

If $\gamma\in\overline{\mu_5}(U)$, use $L_1,L_2$ with their inherited
support disjointness and $a_1\ne a_2$ in the zero-secondary-coordinate
alternative of the no-better hypothesis.  If
$\gamma\notin\overline{\mu_5}(U)$, use all three paths, all three support
and outside-end inequalities, and the strict sum (22) in its other
alternative.  Both alternatives yield the forbidden replacement
presentation.  This final contradiction proves that the Subcase 2.1 data
cannot occur.
\end{proof}

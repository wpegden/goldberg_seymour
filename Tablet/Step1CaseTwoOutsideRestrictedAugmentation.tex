\begin{lemma}[Step 1, Subcase 2.1: restricted augmentation and minimality]
Let $G$ be a finite loopless multigraph retaining individual parallel-edge
identities, let $e_0$ be distinguished, and let $[k]$ be the color set.
Let $T$ be a positive-rung certified ETT with generating coloring
$\varphi$, maximum property, properness, and exactly $e_0$ uncolored.
Assume the complete six-part lower-rung package.  Let $H$ be a good
hierarchy over a hierarchy base, with $d<q_0$ dividers.  Assume uniformly
that every same-rung maximum ETT built from an arbitrary certified input
and generating coloring and having a good hierarchy with fewer than
$q_0$ dividers is elementary.  Assume the large bound
$|\overline\varphi(T_n)|\geq2n+11$, the separate terminal certificate
\[
 T\text{ is }
 \left(\bigcup_{\eta\in D^H_{n,d+1}}\Gamma^d_\eta\right)^-
 \text{-closed under }\varphi,
\]
and elementarity of $V(T)$.  Put $A=H.\mathit{starred}(d)$.

Let $\alpha\ne\beta$, with $\alpha$ missing and singleton-closed on $T$
and $\beta$ not missing on $T$.  Let $P_1,P_2,P_3$ be pairwise
support-disjoint exact exit paths for $\alpha,\beta$.  Their outside ends
$a_1,a_2,a_3$ and tree ends $b_1,b_2,b_3$ are pairwise distinct and
$b_1\prec_T b_2\prec_T b_3$.  Retain, for every $j$, the exact
$\beta$-colored boundary-edge identity $f_j$ on $P_j$ incident with
$b_j$.  Suppose $b_3\notin V(A)$.

Assume the following universal secondary-minimality property, ranging
over arbitrary certified replacement inputs rather than only over paths
on the current display.  Let $\sigma$ be any proper coloring leaving
exactly $e_0$ uncolored and stable from $\varphi$ on the final ladder
tree.  Let $T'$ be the exact terminal tree of any corresponding
same-rung ETT under $\sigma$, with maximum property, a hierarchy base,
a good hierarchy $K$ with exactly $d$ dividers, its own large estimate,
and its separate terminal minus-closed certificate.  For distinct colors
$a,b$, suppose $a$ is missing and singleton-closed on $T'$ and two exact
support-disjoint $(a,b)$ exit legs have distinct outside ends.  Such a
replacement is forbidden if either
\begin{enumerate}
\item $b$ is missing on $T'$, so its secondary coordinate is zero; or
\item $b$ is not missing and there is a third exact exit leg, pairwise
support-disjoint from the first two and with a third outside end, such
that the total of the three exact walk lengths is strictly less than
$|P_1|+|P_2|+|P_3|$.
\end{enumerate}
Assume in addition that the following prefix-plus-two-edges package has
been constructed.  There are a color $\gamma\ne\beta$, a proper coloring
$\mu$ leaving exactly $e_0$ uncolored, and an exact ordered tree $J$,
together with a corresponding same-rung ETT $\mathcal E_J$ under $\mu$,
a maximum-property certificate, and a good hierarchy $K_J$ on $J$ with
exactly $d$ dividers.  The coloring $\mu$ is
$(T_n,D_n,\varphi)$-stable and is a mod-coloring of the original ETT; the
replacement has the same large missing-color estimate; neither $\gamma$
nor $\beta$ belongs to the union of the last indexed Gamma pairs of
$K_J$; and $\beta$ is missing at $b_3$.  There are three exact
$(T,\mu,\{\gamma,\beta\})$-exit paths $R_1,R_2,R_3$ with the same
respective endpoints, supports, and lengths as $P_1,P_2,P_3$, so their
supports remain pairwise disjoint.  Finally $C$ is the exact prefix of
$T$ ending at $b_3$, $J_1=C+f_1$, $J=J_1+f_2$ by exact
\noderef{TreeSequenceAdjoinEdge} certificates, $b_1,b_2,b_3\in V(J)$,
and both endpoints of each exact identity $f_1,f_2$ lie in $V(J)$.
Then the displayed Subcase 2.1 data are contradictory.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Unpack the asserted prefix-plus-two-edges package and retain its exact
witnesses, writing them as
$\gamma,\mu_5,C,J_1,J,\mathcal C_J,B_J,K_J,\mathcal G_J$ and
$R_1,R_2,R_3$.  We now perform the paper's finite restricted augmentation.
At a current tree $U$, append an exact boundary identity $h$ only when
its $\mu_5$-color is missing on $U$ and, for every color $\eta$ in the
last indexed stored set of $K_J$, the segment of $U+h$ before its first
$\eta$-missing vertex uses no color in the corresponding last Gamma pair
of $\mathcal G_J$.  The boundary
identity supplies one old and one new endpoint, so together with its
missing-color witness this checks every field of
\noderef{TashkinovAugmentation}; the additional condition is exactly the
last \noderef{HierarchySegmentColors} avoidance test.  Each step adds a
new vertex to the ordered list.  Since $V$ is finite, iteration stops;
choose a maximal finite sequence and call its last tree $T'$.

We certify every intermediate tree $U$, including $T'$, by induction.
Compose its accumulated exact steps with the exact terminal-seed route
through $J$ supplied by $\mathcal C_J$; this is a
\noderef{TashkinovAugmentationSequence}.  The universal mod-coloring
clause gives a corresponding ETT $\mathcal E_U$, and
\noderef{MaximumPropertyTransport} gives maximum property.  At this
point we again reconstruct, rather than copy, its base.  In non-PE its
core is the common $T_n$.  In PE compare the two actual terminal
constructors of $\mathcal E_J$ and $\mathcal E_U$: equality of their
singleton connecting-edge sets, both identified by correspondence with
the original exact set, fixes the individual defective edge.  The
inside/outside endpoint fields of their maximum-defective choices then
fix the replacement support vertex.  Reuse the exact closure in $B_J$:
it is already a \noderef{TashkinovClosure} under $\mu_5$, and the same
support prefix is forced by the support equality just proved.  The
unchanged coloring-free ordered-union witness from $B_J$ supplies the
core.  This checks every \noderef{HierarchyBase} field at every restricted
augmentation.

Take the internal stages of $K_J$ and replace only its terminal stage by
$U$.  They satisfy
all \noderef{TashkinovHierarchy} fields and have exactly $d$ dividers.
The stored-set, Gamma, unique-refresh, internal minus-closed, and
parallel-base arguments are unchanged.  The induction hypothesis gives
old avoidance, and the selection condition is precisely avoidance for
the newly appended edge, including the totalized first-missing fallback.
Hence every intermediate tree has a good witness $\mathcal G_U$ with all
fields.  Apply this construction at $T'$ and denote its hierarchy by
$K'$ and its corresponding certificate by $\mathcal C'$.

We now derive the separate terminal certificate.  Correspondence
identifies the raw stored sets throughout the restricted sequence.
Because every internal stage is copied from $K_J$, unfolding
\noderef{TashkinovStoredColors},
\noderef{HierarchyStoredColorsAt}, and
\noderef{HierarchyTerminalStoredColors} gives
\[
 D^{K'}_{n,d+1}=D_n\setminus\overline{\mu_5}(T')
       \subseteq D_n\setminus\overline{\mu_5}
          (K_J.\mathit{starred}(d))
       =D^{K_J}_{n,d}.                                         \tag{12}
\]
This is the terminal subtraction argument recorded abstractly by
\noderef{HierarchyStoredColorsAntitone}.

Suppose that a color $c$ missing on $T'$ and outside
$\bigcup_{\eta\in D^{K'}_{n,d+1}}\Gamma^d_\eta$ had an exact
$c$-colored boundary identity.  It is a permissible next Tashkinov
augmentation.  For $\eta\in D^{K_J}_{n,d}$ not in the terminal set, the
definition in (12) says that $\eta$ is already missing on $T'$, so its
first missing position precedes the proposed new edge.  For $\eta$ in
the terminal set, the proposed color $c$ is outside
$\Gamma^d_\eta$, and every older segment already passes the avoidance
test.  Thus the proposed edge satisfies the restricted selection rule,
contrary to maximality.  Therefore
\[
 T'\text{ is }
 \left(\bigcup_{\eta\in D^{K'}_{n,d+1}}\Gamma^d_\eta\right)^-
 \text{-closed under }\mu_5.                                  \tag{13}
\]
The ETT $\mathcal E_{T'}$ has the original rung, maximum property, and
the good hierarchy $K'$ with $d<q_0$ dividers.  The stated same-rung
hypothesis consequently gives
\[
          \noderef{ColoredVertexSetElementary}
          (G,\mu_5,V(T')).                                    \tag{14}
\]

Before using minimality, we locate all three outside endpoints.  Suppose
$a_j\in V(T')$ for some $j$.  The seed path $R_j$ says it misses
$\gamma$ or $\beta$.  It cannot miss $\beta$, since
$b_3\in J\subseteq T'$ already misses $\beta$ by the seed package,
$a_j\ne b_3$, and (14) forbids a repeated missing color.
Thus $a_j$ misses $\gamma$.  Both $\gamma$ and $\beta$ lie outside the
reserved union in (13): (12) reduces this to the two last-pair exclusions
in the seed package.  Equation (13), together with the missing
witnesses $a_j$ and $b_3$, therefore makes both singleton color classes
closed on $T'$.

For every $i$, the path $R_i$ meets $T'$ at $b_i\in J$.  If its outside
end $a_i$ were outside $T'$, traverse the simple incidence walk from
$b_i$ toward $a_i$ and take the first edge-node crossing from a vertex in
$T'$ to one outside.  By \noderef{TwoColorIncidenceGraph} and
\noderef{BoundaryEdges}, this is an exact $\gamma$- or $\beta$-colored
boundary identity, contradicting the two closedness conclusions.  Hence
all three $a_i$ lie in $T'$.  None can miss $\beta$ because $b_3$ does,
so all three miss $\gamma$ by their seed paths, contradicting (14) already at the
distinct vertices $a_1,a_2$.  This contradiction proves
\[
                         a_1,a_2,a_3\notin V(T').              \tag{15}
\]

For each $j$, traverse $R_j$ from $a_j$ toward $b_j$ and let $t_j$ be
the first vertex-node belonging to $V(T')$; it exists because
$b_j\in J\subseteq T'$, and (15) makes its position positive.  Let
$L_j$ be the initial subwalk from $a_j$ through $t_j$.  A subwalk of a
simple walk is simple; it has positive length, the same outside endpoint
$a_j$, and tree endpoint $t_j$.  By first choice of $t_j$, that endpoint
is its unique vertex-node in $T'$.  Its edges still alternate between
$\gamma$ and $\beta$ under $\mu_5$, and the outside-end missing
alternative is exactly the one in the seed path.  These are every
field of
\[
 L_j:\operatorname{TreeExitPath}(T',\mu_5,\gamma,\beta,a_j,t_j)
 \quad(j=1,2,3).                                               \tag{16}
\]
Each support is a subset of the corresponding seed-path support, so the
three remain pairwise support-disjoint and their outside ends remain
pairwise distinct.

The tree $J\subseteq T'$ contains both endpoints of the exact edge
$f_1$ by the seed endpoint clause.  Consequently the first $T'$ vertex
encountered from $a_1$ occurs
strictly before the walk reaches $f_1$; hence $f_1$ is absent from
$L_1$ although it belongs to the support of $R_1$.  Thus $L_1$ is a
proper initial subwalk of $R_1$, while $L_2,L_3$ are initial subwalks of
$R_2,R_3$.  The latter have exactly the original node lists of
$P_2,P_3$, and likewise for the first, by the seed equalities.  Therefore
\[
 |L_1|+|L_2|+|L_3|<|P_1|+|P_2|+|P_3|.                        \tag{17}
\]

We finally enumerate the premises of the universal no-better-presentation
hypothesis.  Take $\sigma=\mu_5$, terminal tree $T'$, and the actual
certificate $\mathcal C'$.  Properness, exact one-uncoloredness, and
stability on the original final ladder tree are clauses of the seed
package; maximum property, the rebuilt base,
$K'$, its good witness, equality of divider counts, and the separate
certificate (13) were proved above.  The seed package already contains
the replacement large estimate, and every later corresponding ETT has
the same exact ladder tree and generating coloring $\mu_5$; hence that
same estimate is the one required for $\mathcal C'$.

Use first comparison color $\beta$ and second color $\gamma$.  They are
distinct; the seed missingness at $b_3\in J\subseteq T'$ makes $\beta$
missing on $T'$, and its exclusion from the
reserved union together with (13) makes $T'$ singleton-$\beta$-closed.
The adjacency predicate in \noderef{TwoColorIncidenceGraph} and the
outside-end alternative in \noderef{TreeExitPath} are symmetric in their
two displayed colors.  Thus the three fieldwise certificates (16) may
equally be read as $(\beta,\gamma)$ exit paths.  Their inherited pairwise
support disjointness and the three distinct outside ends supply every
path premise.  If $\gamma$ is missing on $T'$, choose any two $L_j$ and
use the first, zero-secondary-coordinate alternative.  If it is not
missing, use all three and (17) in the second alternative.  Each is
forbidden by the stated universal hypothesis.  This final contradiction
proves that the Subcase 2.1 data cannot occur.
\end{proof}

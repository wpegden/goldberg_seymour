\begin{lemma}[Balanced triples, part (b); Scheide Lemma 3.12(b)]
Let $(T,e_0,\varphi)$ be normal with $h(T)=h(G)$.  There is a Tashkinov
tree $T'$ on the same vertex set such that $(T',e_0,\varphi)$ is balanced,
and every color used on $T'$ was already used on $T$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Write
\[
 T=(y_0,e_1,y_1,\ldots,e_{n-1},y_{n-1})
 \quad\hbox{and}\quad p=h(G)=h(T).
\]
The normal data in \noderef{NormalGlobalMaximumTriple} have a global
field.  By \noderef{GlobalMaximumTriple}, this says simultaneously that
$(G,e_0,\varphi)$ is a $k$-triple, that $T$ is a Tashkinov tree, and that
$|V(T)|=t(G)$.  By \noderef{ScheideGraphHeightOdd}, $p$ is odd.  Let
$Q=\noderef{NormalTripleTrunk}(T)$ be the prescribed initial trunk, with
vertices $y_0,\ldots,y_{p-1}$ and edge identities
$e_1,\ldots,e_{p-1}$.

Consider the Tashkinov tree-sequences $S$ for the fixed pair
$(e_0,\varphi)$ which begin with exactly $Q$, use on their colored tree
edges only colors in $\varphi\langle T\rangle$ as defined by
\noderef{TreeUsedColors}, and, after $Q$, have consecutive pairs
\[
 (e_p,e_{p+1}),\ (e_{p+2},e_{p+3}),\ldots
\]
of equally colored edges.  This family is nonempty.  Indeed, restrict the
Tashkinov condition for $T$ to the prefix $Q$: every retained edge keeps
the same earlier missing-color witness, so \noderef{TashkinovTree} holds
for $Q$; the tree-sequence clauses in \noderef{TreeSequence} are inherited
by a prefix.  Its order is the odd number $p$, and it has no post-trunk
edge.  Every tree-sequence has pairwise distinct vertices by
\noderef{TreeSequence}, hence has order at most $|V(G)|$.  Thus the set of
odd orders attained by this nonempty family is a nonempty finite set of
natural numbers.  Choose a member $S$ with maximum odd order $i$.

We first locate this maximum relative to $t(G)$.  The $k$-triple equality
in \noderef{KTriple}, obtained from the global field above, is
$\chi'(G)=k+1$, and hence $k=\chi'(G)-1$.  Every candidate $S$ has the same
$k$-triple and is a Tashkinov tree.  Consequently its cardinality is one
of the cardinalities over which \noderef{TashkinovOrder} takes its
maximum, so $|V(S)|=i\leq t(G)$.  On the other hand
$|V(T)|=t(G)$ by \noderef{GlobalMaximumTriple} (equivalently, by the first
conclusion of \noderef{ScheideGlobalMaximumOrder}).

Suppose for a contradiction that $i<t(G)$.  Since
$|V(S)|<|V(T)|$, some vertex of $T$ is not in $V(S)$; let $y_r$ be the
first one.  Both $y_0$ and $y_1$ belong to $V(S)$: the first edge identity
of both sequences is $e_0$, and the attachment clause of
\noderef{TreeSequence} says that its two ends are precisely the first two
vertices.  Thus $r\geq2$, and every $y_j$ with $j<r$ lies in $V(S)$.
The Tashkinov condition for $T$ supplies a color $\gamma$ and an index
$a<r$ such that
\[
 \varphi(e_r)=\gamma
 \quad\hbox{and}\quad
 \gamma\in\overline\varphi(y_a).
\]
The attachment clause supplies an index $b<r$ such that $e_r$ has ends
$y_r$ and $y_b$.  Both $y_a$ and $y_b$ belong to $V(S)$.

Append the edge identity $e_r$ and then $y_r$ to $S$.  We check all data
rather than merely calling this an append.  The vertex $y_r$ is fresh by
its choice.  The identity $e_r$ is also fresh: every old tree edge has
both ends in $V(S)$ by \noderef{TreeSequence}, whereas $e_r$ has the end
$y_r\notin V(S)$.  It attaches the new vertex to the earlier vertex
$y_b\in V(S)$.  The old vertices and edge identities remain pairwise
distinct, the first edge remains $e_0$, and every old Tashkinov witness is
unchanged.  Finally the displayed witness $y_a\in V(S)$ occurs earlier
than the new edge and misses its color $\gamma$.  Hence the enlarged
sequence, say $S^+$, satisfies both \noderef{TreeSequence} and
\noderef{TashkinovTree}.  Its vertex set $U=V(S^+)$ has even cardinality
$i+1$.  Moreover $\gamma\in\varphi\langle T\rangle$: it colors the
original edge $e_r$ with $r\geq2$, exactly as required by
\noderef{TreeUsedColors}.

Apply \noderef{TashTree} to $S^+$, using the unchanged $k$-triple.  It
says that $U$ is elementary in the sense of
\noderef{ColoredVertexSetElementary}.  At least one vertex of $U$, namely
$y_a$, misses $\gamma$.  No second vertex of $U$ can miss $\gamma$, since
elementariness makes the missing-color sets of two distinct vertices
disjoint.  Thus exactly one vertex of $U$ misses $\gamma$.

We now give the incidence count.  By \noderef{MissingColors} and
\noderef{PresentColors}, the unique missing vertex is incident with no
$\gamma$-colored edge, while every other vertex of $U$ is incident with at
least one $\gamma$-colored edge.  It is incident with at most one: if two
distinct $\gamma$-colored edge identities met there, they would be
adjacent by \noderef{AdjacentEdges}, contrary to the properness clause of
\noderef{KTriple} and \noderef{IsProperEdgeColoring}.  Therefore the
number of pairs
\[
 (v,f),\qquad v\in U,\quad v\hbox{ incident with }f,
 \quad\varphi(f)=\gamma,
\]
is exactly $|U|-1=i$, which is odd.

Here incidence has its literal endpoint meaning from
\noderef{Incident}.  Thus the following partition counts exactly the
pairs just displayed, rather than only endpoint pairs with multiplicity
forgotten.

Partition the $\gamma$-colored edge identities meeting $U$ according as
both ends lie in $U$ or exactly one end lies in $U$.  If $a_0$ and $b_0$
are the respective numbers of identities, then the incidence count is
$2a_0+b_0$.  Indeed, an internal identity contributes its two distinct
ends, distinct by the loopless condition in
\noderef{LooplessMultigraph}; an identity in the second class contributes
its unique end in $U$, and every other edge contributes zero.  The second
class is precisely the set of $\gamma$-colored identities in
$\noderef{BoundaryEdges}(G,U)$.  Since $2a_0+b_0=i$ is odd, $b_0$ is odd
and in particular is nonzero.  Choose such a boundary identity $f$, write
its ends as $u\in U$ and $w\notin U$, and note that
$\varphi(f)=\gamma$.

Append $f$ and $w$ to $S^+$.  Again, all clauses are explicit.  The
vertex $w$ is fresh because it is outside $U$.  The edge identity $f$ is
fresh because every edge identity already in $S^+$ has both ends in $U$,
whereas $f$ is a boundary edge.  It attaches $w$ to the earlier vertex
$u\in U$.  All earlier tree and Tashkinov clauses are unchanged, and the
unique vertex of $U$ missing $\gamma$ is an earlier missing-color witness
for the new $\gamma$-colored edge.  Hence the twice-enlarged sequence is a
Tashkinov tree-sequence by \noderef{TreeSequence} and
\noderef{TashkinovTree}.  Its two new edges $e_r,f$ have the common color
$\gamma\in\varphi\langle T\rangle$, so they form exactly the next
post-trunk pair; all earlier pairs and the used-color restriction remain
unchanged.  Its order is the odd number $i+2$, contradicting the choice of
$S$.  We have therefore ruled out $i<t(G)$, and the already proved bound
$i\leq t(G)$ yields $i=t(G)$.

Set $T'=S$.  Before comparing its vertex set with that of $T$, we verify
the fixed-pair maximal hypothesis needed below.  The global field gives
that $T$ is Tashkinov.  Let $R$ be any Tashkinov tree for
$(e_0,\varphi)$ with $V(T)\subseteq V(R)$.  As above,
\noderef{TashkinovOrder} gives $|V(R)|\leq t(G)=|V(T)|$.  Finite-set
inclusion gives the reverse cardinal inequality, hence equality of the
two cardinalities, and inclusion plus equal cardinality gives
$V(R)=V(T)$.  In particular $V(R)\subseteq V(T)$.  This proves, clause by
clause, that $T$ is fixed-pair maximal according to
\noderef{FixedPairMaximalTashkinovTree}.  We may now apply
\noderef{ScheideMaximalContainsEveryTree} to the $k$-triple, this
fixed-pair maximal tree $T$, and the Tashkinov tree $T'$, obtaining
$V(T')\subseteq V(T)$.  Both sets have cardinality $t(G)$, so finite-set
equality gives
\[
 V(T')=V(T).
\]

It remains to construct the asserted normal and balanced data, rather
than infer it solely from the common vertex set.  The vertex maps in a
tree-sequence are injective, and \noderef{TreeSequenceVertexSet} is their
finite image, so
$|V(T')|=T'.\operatorname{length}+1$ and
$|V(T)|=T.\operatorname{length}+1$; their equal orders give equal
lengths.  Define $N'$ using the following fields of
\noderef{NormalGlobalMaximumTriple}.
\begin{itemize}
\item Its global field consists of the original $k$-triple, the proved
Tashkinov property of $T'$, and $|V(T')|=t(G)$, and is therefore a
\noderef{GlobalMaximumTriple}.
\item Take its height to be $p$.  The lower bound $2\leq p$ is copied from
$N$, and the upper bound follows because the prescribed $p$-vertex trunk
is an initial segment of $T'$.  Take the same colors $\alpha,\beta$.
Since $T'$ begins with exactly the prescribed trunk, its first two
vertices are the same $y_0,y_1$, so the two missing-color fields are
unchanged.
\item Take the same closing edge identity and the index of value $p-1$.
The entire first $p$ vertices and first $p-1$ edge identities of $T'$ are
those of $T$.  Therefore the closing-edge endpoint equation and every
trunk-edge endpoint equation are literally the corresponding equations
from $N$.
\item Take the same closing incidence path.  Its simplicity and length
$2(p-1)$ are unchanged.  Its vertex-node support is exactly the first
$p$ vertices of $T'$ because that ordered prefix agrees with $T$, and its
edge-node support is exactly the same closing identity together with the
same trunk edge identities.  Thus both support equivalences required by
\noderef{NormalGlobalMaximumTriple} hold for $N'$.
\end{itemize}
These checks construct normal globally maximum data $N'$ of exact height
$p=h(G)$.

Finally consider any one-based post-trunk pair indexed by $2j$ with
$p<2j<T'.\operatorname{length}+1$.  The defining choice of the family
gives
$\varphi(e_{2j-1})=\varphi(e_{2j})$; after shifting to Lean's zero-based
edge indices, this is exactly the quantified pair clause in
\noderef{BalancedGlobalMaximumTriple}.  Together with $N'.h=p=h(G)$ it
proves that $N'$ is balanced.  Only after this balance check, use the
family's color restriction and \noderef{TreeUsedColors}: every colored
tree edge of $T'$ has a color in $\varphi\langle T\rangle$, so
$\varphi\langle T'\rangle\subseteq\varphi\langle T\rangle$.  This proves
the balanced conclusion, equality of vertex sets, and the required
used-color inclusion.
\end{proof}

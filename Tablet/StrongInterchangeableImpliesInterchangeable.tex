\begin{lemma}[Strong interchangeability implies interchangeability]
Let slot \(t\) be a PE with supporting vertex \(v_t\).  If the distinct
colors \(\alpha,\beta\) are \(T_t\)-strongly interchangeable under
\(\varphi\), then they are \(T_t\)-interchangeable under \(\varphi\):
there is at most one \((\alpha,\beta)\)-path component meeting \(T_t\).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Expand \noderef{ETTStrongInterchangeable}.  For every
\(x\in V(T_t)\setminus\{v_t\}\), it says that the entire component
\(K_x\) through \(x\) is a cycle in the precise sense of
\noderef{TwoColorComponentCycle}.  A simple closed walk has two incident
walk edges at every node in its support.  Since its support is the whole
connected component, no node of that component has a subsingleton
neighbor set.  Hence \(K_x\) cannot satisfy
\noderef{TwoColorPathComponent}, whose last clause requires a node of the
component with subsingleton neighbor set.

Now let \(K\) be any \((\alpha,\beta)\)-path component meeting \(T_t\),
and choose a tree vertex \(x\in K\cap V(T_t)\).  The preceding paragraph
rules out \(x\ne v_t\); therefore \(x=v_t\).  Thus every path component
meeting \(T_t\) contains the same incidence-graph node
\(\operatorname{inl}(v_t)\).  By the connected-component definition
\noderef{TwoColorComponent}, any two such components are equal.
This is exactly the universal equality required by
\noderef{TreeInterchangeable}.
\end{proof}

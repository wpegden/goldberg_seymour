\begin{lemma}[Elementary ordered union at a parallel extension]
Let $T$ be a positive-rung ETT satisfying maximum property, let its last
step be a PE, and write $T_n$, $v_n$, $\varphi_n$, and $D_n$ for the
terminal ladder tree, supporting vertex, generating coloring, and stored
colors.  Keep $G,k,e_0$ fixed but allow the certified input coloring to
vary.  Assume that for every positive-rung ETT $T'$ with arbitrary
certified input coloring, maximum property, and rung number $s<n$, the
following six assertions hold.
\begin{enumerate}
\item $V(T')$ is elementary under its generating coloring
$\varphi'_s$.
\item Under every proper $(T'_{s+1},D'_s,\varphi'_s)$-stable coloring
leaving exactly $e_0$ uncolored, $T'_{s+1}$ is interchangeable for any
two colors of which at least one is missing on it.
\item At every PE slot, stability descends unconditionally to the prefix before the
support; when the support's first-appearance index is greater than one, it
also descends to the preceding ladder stage.  Stored pairs at distinct PE slots with distinct supports
and equal first-appearance indices are disjoint.
\item At a terminal PE, the stored two-color component through its
support meets the terminal ladder tree only at that support.
\item Every nonminimum tree endpoint of a defective boundary color
precedes each later PE support or SE extension vertex permitted by its
first-appearance index.
\item Every proper terminal stable coloring leaving exactly $e_0$
uncolored is a $\varphi'_s\bmod T'_s$ coloring.
\end{enumerate}

Let $\sigma_n$ be a proper $(T_n,D_n,\varphi_n)$-stable coloring leaving
exactly $e_0$ uncolored.  If $J_n$ is a closure of $T_n(v_n)$ under
$\sigma_n$ and $T_n\vee J_n$ is their ordered union, then
$V(T_n\vee J_n)$ is elementary under $\sigma_n$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Put $n=r(T)$.  The generating-color equality in
\noderef{ExtendedTashkinovTree} identifies the displayed $\varphi_n$
with the coloring in the last positive slot.  Eliminate that slot in
\noderef{TashkinovSeries} using the hypothesis $\Theta_n=PE$.  The PE
constructor of \noderef{TashkinovSeriesStep} supplies a selected coloring
$\pi_{n-1}$, a second coloring $\pi'_{n-1}$, an exact connecting edge
$f_n$, colors $\delta_n,\gamma_n$, and the support $v_n$.  In particular,
\[
 \varphi_n=\pi'_{n-1}/P_{v_n}(\gamma_n,\delta_n,\pi'_{n-1}),   \tag{3}
\]
$\pi_{n-1}$ is $(T_n,D_{n-1},\varphi_{n-1})$-stable, and
$\pi'_{n-1}$ is
$(T_n,D_{n-1}\cup\{\delta_n\},\pi_{n-1})$-stable.  Both are proper
and leave exactly $e_0$ uncolored.  The
\noderef{MaximumDefectiveChoice} fields say that $f_n$ is a
$\delta_n$-colored defective boundary edge incident with $v_n$, while
\noderef{ParallelExtensionColor} says that $\gamma_n$ is missing at
$v_n$ under $\pi_{n-1}$.  The vertex and edge clauses of
\noderef{StableColoring} give the same two facts under $\pi'_{n-1}$;
thus $\gamma_n\ne\delta_n$.

When $n>1$, apply \noderef{CertifiedETTRestriction} with target
$r=n-1$.  It returns an actual positive-rung ETT on the original input,
with rung exactly $n-1$, displayed tree exactly the old $T_n$,
generating coloring exactly $\varphi_{n-1}$, and inherited maximum
property.  Its pointwise raw-data equalities, computed stored-set
equalities, and heterogeneous step-certificate equalities identify the
entire retained series with the original one.  Lower assertion (i) on
this ETT makes $V(T_n)$ elementary under $\varphi_{n-1}$.  When $n=1$, the same
claim is the elementarity conclusion of
\noderef{TechnicalTheoremBase} for the original initial tuple.  The two
vertex clauses of the stable relations just extracted therefore make
$V(T_n)$ elementary under $\pi'_{n-1}$ as well.

We also need the pre-switch closedness which prevents $\delta_n$ from
already being missing elsewhere on $T_n$.  The terminal PE constructor
says that $V(T_n)$ is closed under $\varphi_{n-1}$ in the sense of
\noderef{ColoredVertexSetClosed}.  Suppose a boundary identity $g$ had
$\pi'_{n-1}(g)=c$ with $c$ missing on $T_n$ under $\pi'_{n-1}$.
The vertex clauses transport this missing color first to $\pi_{n-1}$ and
then to $\varphi_{n-1}$.  A boundary edge is incident with the tree by
\noderef{TreeIncidentEdge}.  Apply the reverse controlled-value criterion
\noderef{sc1} to the two certified stable relations, or equivalently use
symmetry and transitivity from \noderef{sc2}; it gives
$\varphi_{n-1}(g)=\pi_{n-1}(g)=\pi'_{n-1}(g)=c$, contrary to the old
closedness.  Thus $T_n$ is closed under $\pi'_{n-1}$.  Since $f_n$ is a
$\delta_n$-colored boundary edge under $\pi'_{n-1}$, it follows that
\[
          \delta_n\notin\overline\pi'_{n-1}(T_n).              \tag{4}
\]

We now justify the effect of the switch in (3).  Apply
\noderef{ExtensionBase} to the current ETT and the displayed lower-rung
package, taking its stable coloring to be $\varphi_n$ itself.  Properness
and unique-uncoloredness come from the PE constructor, and reflexivity of
the required stable relation is the reflexive clause of \noderef{sc2}.
We obtain
\[
 P_{v_n}(\gamma_n,\delta_n,\varphi_n)\cap V(T_n)=\{v_n\}.       \tag{5}
\]
The definition \noderef{TwoColorComponentSwap} only transposes
$\gamma_n$ and $\delta_n$ on edge-nodes of the selected component.  It
does not change which edge-nodes carry one of these two colors, and hence
does not change the two-color incidence graph or any of its connected
components.  Consequently the component in (3) and the component in (5)
have exactly the same node support.  In particular, the pre-switch
component in (3) also meets $V(T_n)$ only in $v_n$.

If $x\in V(T_n)-\{v_n\}$ and an edge of the switched component were
incident with $x$, the incidence rule in
\noderef{TwoColorIncidenceGraph} would put the vertex-node $x$ in that
component, contradicting (5).  Thus the switch fixes every edge incident
with every such $x$, and so fixes its entire missing-color set.  At
$v_n$, $\gamma_n$ is missing and $f_n$ is the unique incident
$\delta_n$-edge by properness; the switch changes $f_n$ to
$\gamma_n$ and changes the missing color at $v_n$ from $\gamma_n$ to
$\delta_n$.  Before the switch, elementarity says that $\gamma_n$ is
missing nowhere else, while (4) says that $\delta_n$ is missing nowhere
on the tree.  Therefore the switch creates no repeated missing color, and
$V(T_n)$ is elementary under $\varphi_n$.  The vertex clause of the
assumed $(T_n,D_n,\varphi_n)$-stability then gives
\[
             V(T_n)\text{ is elementary under }\sigma_n.       \tag{1}
\]
It also records for later use that $\delta_n$ is missing at $v_n$ under
both $\varphi_n$ and $\sigma_n$.

We next construct the lower-rung presentation of $J_n$.  The PE clause of
\noderef{HierarchyBase}, together with the chosen-vertex equation just
extracted, identifies its support with $v_n$, says that $J_n$ is a
\noderef{TashkinovClosure} under $\sigma_n$ of the ordered prefix
$T_n(v_n)$, and gives the exact ordered union certificate for
$T_n\vee J_n$.  Let $j=m(v_n)$.  The least-index definition
\noderef{ETTFirstAppearanceIndex} and the entrywise relations
\noderef{TreeSequenceInitialSegment} identify this final prefix with
$T_j(v_n)$.  Apply current assertion (iii), namely
\noderef{ExtensionRules} with the displayed lower package.  It says that
$\sigma_n$ is stable relative to $(D_{j-1},\varphi_{j-1})$ on every
vertex and incident edge strictly before $v_n$; it also gives stability
on $T_{j-1}$ when $j>1$.  The proof below uses that latter conclusion only
in its explicit $j>1$ branch.

We verify the displayed edge colors needed to copy the construction of
$T_j(v_n)$.  Let $g$ be a colored displayed edge of $T_j$.
\noderef{HkuTreeColorsControlled} puts its $\varphi_{j-1}$-color in
$\overline\varphi_{j-1}(T_j)\cup D_{j-1}$.  Both endpoints of a
displayed tree edge lie in $V(T_j)$, so $g$ is internal to that vertex
set in the sense of \noderef{InternalEdges}.  The internal-edge conclusion of
\noderef{SameColorSeriesPreserved}, applied from slot $j-1$ through slot
$n-1$, therefore gives
\[
                  \varphi_n(g)=\varphi_{j-1}(g).               \tag{6}
\]
The terminal conclusion of \noderef{HkuTreeColorsControlled} and the
assumed stability give $\sigma_n(g)=\varphi_n(g)$, because the ordered
ladder inclusions put the same exact edge identity in $T_n$.  Thus
$\sigma_n(g)=\varphi_{j-1}(g)$ for every colored displayed edge of
$T_j$.  The remaining displayed identity is the root $e_0$, and both
colorings take value uncolored there by
\noderef{IsOneEdgeUncolored}.  Hence equality holds on every displayed
edge required below.

Now inspect the constructor which produces $T_j$ in
\noderef{TashkinovSeriesStep}.  It contains an explicit
\noderef{TashkinovAugmentationSequence} under $\varphi_{j-1}$ from the
prescribed seed to $T_j$: the seed is $T_{j-1}$ in the PE case and the
exact one-edge extension $T_{j-1}+f_{j-1}$ in the SE or RE case; when
$j=1$, the seed is the initial one-edge tree $T_0$.  Stop that finite
sequence at the prefix $T_j(v_n)$.  At one of its steps, let $g$ be the
new displayed edge and let $c=\varphi_{j-1}(g)$ be missing at the old
witness vertex $x$.  The witness $x$ occurs strictly before the newly
adjoined vertex and hence strictly before $v_n$.  The before-$v_n$
stability from \noderef{ExtensionRules} preserves the missingness of
$c$ at $x$, while (6) and terminal edge control give
$\sigma_n(g)=c$.  These are exactly the color and missing-witness clauses
of \noderef{TashkinovAugmentation}; the ordered-prefix and one-new-entry
clauses are unchanged.  Repeating this verification at every position
gives a $\sigma_n$-augmentation sequence from the same prescribed seed
to $T_j(v_n)$.  Compose it with the augmentation sequence in the
$\sigma_n$-closure of $T_j(v_n)$ supplied by
\noderef{HierarchyBase}.  Transitivity of
\noderef{TashkinovAugmentationSequence}, together with the final
closedness field of \noderef{TashkinovClosure}, proves that $J_n$ is a
$\sigma_n$-closure of that exact prescribed seed.

Suppose first that $j>1$.  Apply
\noderef{CertifiedETTRestriction} with target rung $r=j-1$, which is
positive and below $n$.  Call its returned maximum ETT $B$.  Its terminal
ladder stage is exactly $T_{j-1}$, its displayed next tree is exactly
$T_j$, its generating coloring is $\varphi_{j-1}$, and its retained
arrays, computed stored sets, and certificates agree with the original
ones under the canonical inclusions.  Lower assertion (vi), applied to
$B$ and the $T_{j-1}$-stability already obtained, says precisely that
$\sigma_n$ is a \noderef{ModColoring} of $B$.  Only now do we apply the
universal extension clause of that definition: the exact seed and the
augmentation sequence just checked make $J_n$ an eligible strict
extension: the first-appearance definition puts $v_n$ outside
$T_{j-1}$ but inside $J_n$.  It returns an actual
\noderef{CorrespondingExtendedTashkinovTree} with displayed tree $J_n$,
replacement input coloring, replacement series, and generating coloring
$\sigma_n$.  By \noderef{MaximumPropertyTransport}, its replacement ETT
has maximum property.  Lower assertion (i), instantiated with that actual
replacement input and ETT, makes $V(J_n)$ elementary under $\sigma_n$.

For interchangeability, let $L$ be the final ladder closure in this
replacement series.  The \noderef{ExtendedTashkinovTree} certificate makes
$J_n$ an ordered initial segment of $L$ in the sense of
\noderef{TreeSequenceInitialSegment}.  A color missing at a vertex of
$J_n$ is therefore also missing on $L$ by the vertex-union definition
\noderef{TreeMissingColors}.  Apply lower assertion (ii) to the reflexive
stable coloring $\sigma_n$ of $L$; it makes $L$ interchangeable for every
pair of which one color is missing on $J_n$.  To restrict this conclusion,
fix arbitrary roots $z,w$ whose two-color components are path components
meeting $J_n$; this includes isolated vertex components.  Choose a meeting
vertex from each component.  Entrywise agreement in the ordered
initial-segment certificate, together with the vertex-union definition
\noderef{TreeSequenceVertexSet}, puts both chosen vertices in $V(L)$.
Thus both full two-color components meet $L$, and the universal
\noderef{TreeInterchangeable} conclusion on $L$ identifies their full
connected-component node sets.  Since $z,w$ were arbitrary, this is exactly
$\noderef{TreeInterchangeable}$ on $J_n$.

If $j=1$, no positive lower ETT exists, so we construct the initial
series rather than infer it from the base theorem.  Retain the original
one-edge seed $T_0$, set its input and initial coloring equal to
$\sigma_n$, keep $S_0=F_0=\varnothing$ and all optional initial fields
absent, and take the $\sigma_n$-closure of $T_0$ ending at $J_n$ which
was proved above.  The graph, criticality, chromatic-index, and degree
clauses of the original \noderef{KTriple} do not depend on its coloring;
the assumed properness and exact unique-uncoloredness supply the two
coloring clauses.  Hence these data satisfy the initial constructor of
\noderef{TashkinovSeriesStep} and form an actual initial certified series
whose $T_1$ is $J_n$.  Applying \noderef{TechnicalTheoremBase} to this
constructed series gives both elementarity of $J_n$ under $\sigma_n$ and,
using reflexive stability, its interchangeability for every pair with a
color missing on it.

In both cases the original hierarchy-base closure already supplies
closedness.  We have therefore proved
\[
 V(J_n)\text{ is elementary and closed under }\sigma_n,        \tag{2}
\]
and every pair of colors of which one is missing on $J_n$ is
$J_n$-interchangeable.

We record one further consequence needed for the final path argument.
Let $a,b$ be colors missing on $T_n$ and suppose
$a,b\notin\{\gamma_n,\delta_n\}$.  Apply \noderef{ExtensionBase} again,
now to the given coloring $\sigma_n$ and its stated properness,
unique-uncoloredness, and stability.  It gives the exact singleton
intersection of the $(\gamma_n,\delta_n)$-component of $v_n$ under
$\sigma_n$ with $V(T_n)$.  All hypotheses of
\noderef{ParallelExtensionUndoStable} are now present.  Put
\[
 \tau=\sigma_n/P_{v_n}(\gamma_n,\delta_n,\sigma_n).
\]
That lemma makes $\tau$
$(T_n,D_{n-1},\varphi_{n-1})$-stable.  Since
$\gamma_n\ne\delta_n$,
\noderef{TwoColorComponentSwapProper} gives properness of $\tau$ from
properness of $\sigma_n$; and \noderef{TwoColorComponentSwap} fixes every
uncolored value and sends every colored value to a colored value, so the
exact statement \noderef{IsOneEdgeUncolored} is preserved as well.
The same swap definition changes only edge values
$\gamma_n,\delta_n$.  Since $a,b$ avoid those values, every edge has
color $a$ or $b$ under $\tau$ exactly when it has the same color under
$\sigma_n$.  Thus the two $(a,b)$-incidence graphs, their connected
components, their nontriviality, and the colors missing at their vertices
are identical.  In particular $a,b$ are still missing on $T_n$ under
$\tau$.  If $n>1$, lower assertion (ii) for the certified rung-$(n-1)$
ETT supplied above by \noderef{CertifiedETTRestriction} makes $T_n$
interchangeable under $\tau$ for $a,b$; if $n=1$, this
is the interchangeability conclusion of
\noderef{TechnicalTheoremBase} for the original initial tuple.  Equality
of the two incidence graphs transports that conclusion back to
$\sigma_n$:
\[
 \begin{split}
 &a,b\in\overline\sigma_n(T_n),\quad
   a,b\notin\{\gamma_n,\delta_n\}\quad\Longrightarrow\\
 &\hspace{45mm}a,b\text{ are $T_n$-interchangeable under }\sigma_n.
                                                               \tag{7}
 \end{split}
\]

Suppose, for a contradiction, that the ordered union is not elementary.
By (1), (2), and the exact vertex-set equality in
\noderef{OrderedTreeUnion}, two vertices with a common missing color
cannot both lie in either constituent.  Hence there are
\[
 u\in V(T_n)-V(J_n),\qquad v\in V(J_n)-V(T_n)
\]
and a color $\alpha$ missing at both $u$ and $v$.  The support $v_n$
belongs to the prefix contained in both trees, and $\delta_n$ is missing
there under $\sigma_n$ by the calculation following (5).  Elementarity
of $J_n$ and $v\ne v_n$ give $\alpha\ne\delta_n$.

The first conclusion of \noderef{HkuParallelBoundaryStructure} says that
the $\gamma_n$-boundary of $T_n$ under $\varphi_n$ is the nonempty
singleton $\{f_n\}$.  If $\gamma_n$ were missing on $T_n$, then, since
$\gamma_n\ne\delta_n$, the conditional conclusion of the same lemma and
the pre-step elementarity proved above would make this boundary empty.
Thus $\gamma_n$ is not missing on $T_n$ under $\varphi_n$, and the vertex
clause of terminal stability gives the same fact under $\sigma_n$.
Since $\alpha$ is missing at $u\in V(T_n)$, we also have
$\alpha\ne\gamma_n$.

We next produce the paper's vertex $w$.  The selected edge is defective
in the sense of \noderef{DefectiveEdge}; expanding
\noderef{DefectiveColor} and the fields of
\noderef{MaximumDefectiveChoice} supplies a
$\delta_n$-boundary identity $g\ne f_n$ under $\pi_{n-1}$.  The enlarged
stable relation fixes its value under $\pi'_{n-1}$.  This edge-node cannot
belong to the component in (3): otherwise its inside endpoint would be a
tree vertex in that component, hence $v_n$ by (5), but properness makes
$f_n$ the unique incident $\delta_n$-edge there.  Therefore the switch
fixes $g$, so it remains a $\delta_n$-boundary identity under
$\varphi_n$.  The unconditional delta-boundary conclusion of
\noderef{HkuParallelBoundaryStructure} gives an inside endpoint
$w\prec_{T_n}v_n$.  By \noderef{TreeSequencePrefix}, this vertex lies in
$T_n(v_n)$, hence in its closure $J_n$; of course it also lies in $T_n$.

The numerical clause of \noderef{KTriple} gives
$k\geq\Delta(G)+1$, and \noderef{MaximumDegree} gives
$d_G(w)\leq\Delta(G)$.  Under the proper coloring $\sigma_n$, at most
$d_G(w)<k$ colors are present at $w$.  The complementary definition
\noderef{MissingColors} therefore supplies a color $\beta$ missing at
$w$.  Elementarity of $J_n$, together with the missing
$\delta_n$ at the distinct vertex $v_n$, gives
$\beta\ne\delta_n$.  The fact that $\gamma_n$ is missing nowhere on
$T_n$ gives $\beta\ne\gamma_n$.  Finally, elementarity of either
constituent, using $u\ne w$ or $v\ne w$, gives $\alpha\ne\beta$.

Apply (7) to $a=\alpha$ and $b=\beta$: both colors are missing on
$T_n$, at $u$ and $w$, and both avoid the terminal PE pair.  Thus they
are $T_n$-interchangeable under $\sigma_n$.  They are also
$J_n$-interchangeable by (2), since they are missing there at $v$ and
$w$.

Consider first the two-color components through $v$ and $w$.  At $v$,
$\alpha$ is missing; because $\beta$ is missing at the distinct vertex
$w$ and $J_n$ is elementary, $\beta$ is present at $v$.  Similarly,
$\beta$ is missing and $\alpha$ is present at $w$.  By
\noderef{PresentColors}, each component is nontrivial.  Properness in
\noderef{IsProperEdgeColoring} makes the vertex-nodes $v,w$ have degree
exactly one in the $(\alpha,\beta)$-incidence graph.
\noderef{TwoColorComponentSpanningPath} supplies a spanning simple path
for each component, so the two nontrivial components qualify for the
definition \noderef{TreeInterchangeable}.  Interchangeability identifies
them, and either spanning path has the common component as its support.
Its two degree-one ends are therefore $v$ and $w$.

The same argument in $T_n$, using (1) and (7), identifies the components
through $u$ and $w$ and gives a spanning path with degree-one ends $u$
and $w$.  Both common components are the component through $w$, so they
are one and the same connected component.  The three vertices $u,v,w$
are distinct by their memberships in the two set differences and the
intersection.  Each has degree one in this component, but a simple path
has only its two ends of degree one.  This contradicts the spanning-path
conclusion.  Therefore no two distinct vertices of $T_n\vee J_n$ have a
common missing color, and the ordered union is elementary under
$\sigma_n$, as required.
\end{proof}

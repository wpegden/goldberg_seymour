\begin{lemma}[Missing colors are excluded from an elementary tail]
Let $(T,\varphi_n)$ be a positive-rung extended Tashkinov tree in a
finite loopless multigraph with individual edge identities, let
$\mathcal B$ be a hierarchy base under $\varphi_n$, and let $H$ be a
hierarchy on this base.  Put
\[
 q=H.\mathit{dividerCount},\qquad
 A=\noderef{HierarchyStarredStage}(H,q),\qquad
 \ell=|T|-|A|,
\]
and, for a natural number $j$, let
\[
 T(y_j)=\noderef{TreeSequencePrefix}
       \bigl(T,\min\{|A|+j,|T|\}\bigr).
\]
Let $n$ be the terminal rung index and let $T_n$ be the terminal ladder
tree.  Assume that $V(T(y_{\ell-1}))$ is elementary under $\varphi_n$.

Fix $i$ with $1\leq i<\ell$, and suppose that a color $\beta$ is missing
at the exact last vertex $y_i$ of $T(y_i)$.  If $t$ is an edge position
satisfying
\[
             |A|\leq t<|A|+i
 \quad\hbox{and}\quad
             \varphi_n(T(y_i).\mathit{edge}(t))=\beta,
\]
then all of the following exceptional conclusions hold:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $q=0$, $A=\mathcal B.\mathit{core}=T_n$, and $t=|A|$;
\item the actual certified step at index $n$ is an SE or RE constructor;
\item for an individual edge identity $f_n$, one has
\[
 F_n=\{f_n\},\qquad
 T(y_i).\mathit{edge}(t)=f_n,\qquad
 \varphi_n(f_n)=\beta\in D_n,
\]
where $D_n=\noderef{TashkinovStoredColors}(\mathcal T,n)$.
\end{enumerate}
Thus no $\beta$-colored exact edge occurs from $A$ through $y_i$, apart
from the first displayed SE/RE connecting seed in precisely the stated
zero-divider case.  The assertion concerns edge identities, not endpoint
pairs, so it remains valid in the presence of parallel edges.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Write $B=T(y_{\ell-1})$.  The range $1\leq i<\ell$ gives
$i\leq\ell-1$.  Unfold \noderef{HierarchyStarredStage} at the last
divider index.  If $q=0$, its zero branch says
$A=\mathcal B.\mathit{core}$; the core-inside-first field of
\noderef{TashkinovHierarchy}, followed by the terminal-stage identity,
places $A$ as an exact ordered initial segment of $T$.  If $q>0$, its
successor branch identifies $A$ with the ordinary stage $T_{n,q}$;
repeated use of the nested-stage field through the terminal stage
$T_{n,q+1}=T$ gives the same conclusion.  Thus $|A|\leq |T|$.  By the
definition $\ell=|T|-|A|$, subtraction now gives $|A|+\ell=|T|$.
Consequently $i<\ell$ makes the minimum defining $T(y_i)$ equal to
$|A|+i$, and $i\leq\ell-1$ makes $T(y_i)$ an exact ordered initial
segment of $B$.
By \noderef{TreeSequencePrefix}, its last vertex is the entry of $T$ at
index $|A|+i$.  All vertex and edge equalities used below are the
entrywise equalities of \noderef{TreeSequenceInitialSegment}; no equality
of endpoint pairs is substituted for an edge identity.

Let $n=\operatorname{Fin.last}(\mathit{ett.rounds})$.  Since the ETT has
positive rung, $n$ is a positive series index.  Inspect the actual
certificate $\mathcal T.\mathit{certified}(n)$ supplied by
\noderef{TashkinovSeries}.  The initial constructor of
\noderef{TashkinovSeriesStep} is impossible at this positive index, so
the certificate is PE, SE, or RE.  The generating-coloring field of
\noderef{ExtendedTashkinovTree} identifies its current coloring with
$\varphi_n$.

We next locate the certified Tashkinov-augmentation route.  In the PE
case, the constructor supplies a
$\varphi_n$-\noderef{TashkinovClosure} from $T_n$ to the final certified
output.  Thus it supplies a
$\varphi_n$-\noderef{TashkinovAugmentationSequence} from $T_n$ to that
output.  The PE clause of \noderef{HierarchyBase} supplies an ordered
union of $T_n$ and the auxiliary closure whose union is the base core.
The left-segment field in the definition of \noderef{OrderedTreeUnion}
therefore makes $T_n$ an exact ordered initial segment of the base core.
The hierarchy places the core inside $A$, then $T(y_i)$, then $T$, and
finally the certified output.  Applying
\noderef{TashkinovAugmentationSequenceRestrict} at these exact intermediate
prefixes gives the route through $A$ and $T(y_i)$.

In an SE or RE constructor, the certificate instead supplies an
individual edge $f_n$, the singleton equation $F_n=\{f_n\}$, the exact
one-edge seed $S=T_n+f_n$ in the sense of
\noderef{TreeSequenceAdjoinEdge}, and a $\varphi_n$-Tashkinov closure from
$S$ to the final output.  If $q>0$, the exact conclusion of
\noderef{HierarchyNonparallelFirstEdge} places this same identity $f_n$
at position $|T_n|$ of the first positive hierarchy stage.  The seed and
that stage are exact prefixes of the same certified output, while
$|S|=|T_n|+1$ and hierarchy strictness gives
$|T_n|+1\leq |T_{n,1}|$.  At every vertex or edge index of $S$, the two
initial-segment equations identify the entry of $S$ and the entry of
$T_{n,1}$ with the same entry of the certified output.  Hence $S$ is an
exact prefix of $T_{n,1}$.  The positive branch of
\noderef{HierarchyStarredStage} and hierarchy nesting place
$T_{n,1}$ inside $A=T_{n,q}$, so $S$ is a prefix of $A$.  Restriction
again gives the augmentation route through $A$ and $T(y_i)$.

If $q=0$ in the SE/RE case, the nonparallel clause of
\noderef{HierarchyBase}, together with the zero branch of
\noderef{HierarchyStarredStage}, gives
\[
                              A=T_n.                           \tag{1}
\]
Here the only displayed edge immediately after $A$ that is not itself a
Tashkinov-augmentation edge is the adjoined seed $f_n$.  Every later
edge lies in the closure route from $S$ and is an augmentation edge.
For completeness, this last assertion follows directly by induction on
the finite augmentation chain.  Every step increases the length by one
and retains every earlier entry.  If the chain starts at a length at
most $t$ and ends at $T(y_i)$, whose length is greater than $t$, take
the first member whose length is greater than $t$.  Its predecessor has
length at most $t$, and the one-step length equation forces the two
lengths to be $t$ and $t+1$.  The new last edge of that step is therefore
the exact displayed identity at position $t$.
We have therefore proved the following exact dichotomy for the position
$t$ in the statement:
\[
 \begin{array}{l}
 \text{either the edge at }t\text{ is the new last edge of a certified }
 \varphi_n\text{-Tashkinov augmentation,}\quad\text{or}\\[1mm]
 q=0,\ A=T_n,\ t=|A|,\ \Theta_n\in\{\mathrm{SE},\mathrm{RE}\},
 \ F_n=\{f_n\},\text{ and the edge at }t\text{ is }f_n.
 \end{array}                                                   \tag{2}
\]
For the second line, \noderef{HierarchyNonparallelFirstEdge} places the
individual identity $f_n$ at position $|T_n|$ of $T_{n,1}$; when $q=0$,
the terminal-stage field says $T_{n,1}=T$.  Together with (1), this is the
exact $e_1=f_n$ equality required in the manuscript.

We rule out the first line of (2).  Expand the corresponding one-step
\noderef{TashkinovAugmentation}.  Its predecessor $X$ has length $t$,
its new last edge is the exact edge at position $t$, and its
$\varphi_n$-color $c$ belongs to
$\noderef{TreeMissingColors}(\varphi_n,X)$.  The assumed color equation
for the same individual edge gives $c=\beta$.  Unfolding
\noderef{TreeMissingColors}, this membership in the finite union supplies
a vertex $z\in V(X)$ with $\beta\in\overline\varphi_n(z)$.  Thus
\[
             \beta\in\overline\varphi_n(z).                   \tag{3}
\]
Now unfold \noderef{TreeSequenceVertexSet}: membership means membership
in the image of the displayed vertex map.  Since $|X|=t$, the witness
$z\in V(X)$ has the form $X.\mathit{vertex}(s)$ for an index
$s\leq t$.  The exact-prefix equations from $X$ to $T(y_i)$ and then to
$B$ identify this with the entry of $B$ at index $s$, so $z\in V(B)$ by
the same image definition.  Likewise, the restricted vertex map in
\noderef{TreeSequencePrefix} identifies $y_i$ with the entry of $B$ at
index $|A|+i$, and hence $y_i\in V(B)$.  Since
$s\leq t<|A|+i$, the two indices are different; vertex injectivity in
\noderef{TreeSequence} therefore gives $z\ne y_i$.  Equation (3) and the
assumed $\beta\in\overline\varphi_n(y_i)$ now contradict the elementarity
of $B$ as defined by \noderef{ColoredVertexSetElementary}.  Therefore
only the exceptional second line of (2) can occur.  This proves $q=0$,
(1), $t=|A|$, the actual certified SE/RE alternative,
$F_n=\{f_n\}$, the exact edge identity, and
$\varphi_n(f_n)=\beta$.

It remains to verify the asserted membership in $D_n$, rather than
merely label the seed exceptional.  In the SE constructor, the fields of
the actual \noderef{TashkinovSeriesStep} certificate and its
\noderef{MaximumDefectiveChoice} identify $f_n$ with the selected
defective edge, identify its color with the selected color
$\delta_n=\beta$, and put $\delta_n$ in the raw stored set $S_n$.  In the
RE constructor, write $i=n-1$.  The
\noderef{RevisitingExtensionCertificate} supplies a
\noderef{TwoColorCycle} for the preceding coloring $\varphi_i$ and the
two certified colors $\gamma_h,\delta_h$, and it supplies a return walk
in that cycle's incidence graph beginning at the exact edge-node $f_n$
and ending at a graph-vertex node.  Unfolding
\noderef{TwoColorCycle} identifies the ambient graph of that walk with
\noderef{TwoColorIncidenceGraph}$(G,\varphi_i;\gamma_h,\delta_h)$.
The endpoints have different sum tags, so the return walk is nonempty;
its first adjacency joins the edge-node $f_n$ to a vertex-node.
Unfolding \noderef{TwoColorIncidenceGraph} at that adjacency gives the
exact alternative
\[
        \varphi_i(f_n)=\gamma_h\quad\hbox{or}\quad
        \varphi_i(f_n)=\delta_h.
\]
The RE current-coloring equation in the actual
\noderef{TashkinovSeriesStep} certificate says
$\varphi_n=\varphi_i$.  Since the already proved exact equation is
$\varphi_n(f_n)=\beta$, it follows that
$\beta\in\{\gamma_h,\delta_h\}=S_n$.  Thus in either case
\[
                              \beta\in S_n.                    \tag{4}
\]

We claim that $\beta$ is not missing anywhere on $T_n$.  In the RE
constructor, the current coloring $\varphi_n$ is exactly the preceding
coloring and the constructor states that $T_n$ is closed under that
coloring.  The exact edge $f_n$ is a boundary edge of $T_n$ carrying
$\beta$.  The definition \noderef{ColoredVertexSetClosed} therefore
immediately excludes $\beta$ from the missing-color set of every vertex
of $T_n$.

For SE, write $\psi$ for the preceding coloring and $\pi=\varphi_n$ for
the selected coloring.  The constructor gives that $T_n$ is closed under
$\psi$, that $\pi$ is
$\bigl(T_n,D_{n-1},\psi\bigr)$-stable, that $\pi$ is proper, and that
$f_n$ is a boundary edge incident with the selected inside vertex $v$
and has $\pi(f_n)=\beta$.  Suppose that $\beta$ were missing somewhere
on $T_n$ under $\pi$.  The vertexwise clause of
\noderef{StableColoring} then makes $\beta$ missing at the same vertex
of $T_n$ under $\psi$; after unfolding \noderef{TreeMissingColors}, this
says $\beta\in\overline\psi(T_n)$.  The maximum-defective certificate
also places its selected vertex $v$ in $V(T_n)$ and makes $f_n$ incident
with $v$.  Since $\pi(f_n)=\beta$, the definition of
\noderef{PresentColors} makes $\beta$ present, and hence by
\noderef{MissingColors} not missing, at $v$ under $\pi$.  Vertexwise
stability says it is also not missing at $v$ under $\psi$.  Expanding
\noderef{MissingColors} as the complement of
\noderef{PresentColors}, and then expanding presentness, supplies an
individual edge $g$ incident with $v$ such that $\psi(g)=\beta$.

Because $v\in V(T_n)$ and $g$ is incident with $v$, the definition of
\noderef{TreeIncidentEdge} says that this exact edge $g$ is incident with
$T_n$.  We also have $\beta\in\overline\psi(T_n)$.  Therefore the
protected-edge clause of \noderef{StableColoring} fixes $g$ and gives
$\pi(g)=\psi(g)=\beta$.  The common incident vertex $v$ witnesses
\noderef{AdjacentEdges}$(G,f_n,g)$.  If $f_n\ne g$, properness of $\pi$
from \noderef{IsProperEdgeColoring}, applied to these adjacent exact
edges with the common color $\beta$, gives a contradiction.  Hence
$g=f_n$ and $\psi(f_n)=\beta$.  This contradicts the
$\psi$-closedness of $T_n$: the maximum-defective certificate makes
$f_n$ a boundary edge, while $\beta$ is missing at a vertex of $T_n$.
This proves in SE as well that
\[
                    \beta\notin\overline\varphi_n(T_n).        \tag{5}
\]
Finally, \noderef{TashkinovStoredColors} defines $D_n$ as the union of
the raw stored sets through the positive index $n$, with
$\overline\varphi_n(T_n)$ removed.  Combining (4) and (5) gives
$\beta\in D_n$.  This completes every clause of the exceptional
conclusion.
\end{proof}

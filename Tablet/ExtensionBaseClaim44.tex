\begin{lemma}[Claim 4.4: output of Algorithm (B)]
Retain the Case 1 data.  Let \(v_n\in V(T)\), and let the zero-based Lean
predecessor slot \(j_{\rm slot}\) satisfy the exact first-appearance identity
\[
 j_{\rm slot}.\mathrm{val}+1
   =\noderef{ETTFirstAppearanceIndex}(v_n)=j=m(v_n).
\]
Thus
\(\mathtt{ett.series.data.tree\ jSlot.succ.castSucc}\) is the exact stored
stage tree \(T_j\), while
\(\mathtt{ett.series.data.coloring\ jSlot.castSucc}\) is the exact reference
coloring \(\varphi_{j-1}\).  Choose a positive occurrence position of \(v_n\)
in this \(T_j\), certify that the vertex at that position is exactly \(v_n\),
and identify the closure seed by the equality
\[
\mathtt{seed}
   =\noderef{TreeSequencePrefix}(T_j,\mathtt{terminalPosition.val})
   =T_j(v_n).
\]
Let the Lean color-set parameter \(C\) be the manuscript set \(D_{j-1}\).
Write the support class in its manuscript order as
\(L_1=\{1_1<\cdots<1_{p+1}\}\), represented in Lean by
\(\mathtt{indices}:\operatorname{Fin}(p+1)\to\operatorname{Fin}(\mathtt{ett.rounds})\).
Retain the strict enumeration premise
\[
 a<c\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 \mathtt{indices}(a).\mathrm{val}<\mathtt{indices}(c).\mathrm{val}.
\]
Also retain equation (4)'s distinctness assertion in its exact Lean form:
\[
 \mathtt{Fin.cons}(\gamma_0,\delta):
   \operatorname{Fin}(p+2)\longrightarrow\operatorname{Fin}(k)
 \quad\text{is injective}.
\]
Equivalently, the colors
\(\gamma_{1_1},\delta_{1_1},\ldots,\delta_{1_{p+1}}\) are pairwise
distinct; together with the chain identities, this is precisely the list
of distinct colors displayed in equation (4).  In particular,
\(\gamma_0=\gamma_{1_1}\) is the color named in (c3), and two nonadjacent
positions cannot collapse to the same trace slot.
Let \(T'_j\) be a \noderef{TashkinovClosure} of this exact ordered prefix under
\(\varrho_1\), let \(u\in V(T'_j)\setminus V(T_j)\), and let
\(\mathcal B\) be an
\noderef{ExtensionBaseAlgorithmBTrace} satisfying
\noderef{ExtensionBaseAlgorithmBInvariant}.  Write \(\varrho_1\) for
its initial coloring and \(\varrho_2\) for its output.  Assume Claim
4.3(b1), namely that \(\varrho_1\) is
\((T_j(v_n)-v_n,D_{j-1},\varphi_{j-1})\)-stable.  Assume the exact
Case 1 consequence of (14)--(15) saying that no color
used by Algorithm (B) is missing on \(T'_j(u)-u\), and that \(T'_j\) is a closure
of the displayed exact \(T_j(v_n)\) under \(\varrho_1\).  Finally retain the
least missing gamma at \(u\), the exact gamma and delta field equalities,
the exact chain identities, and the exact stored-pair identities.

Then:
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(c1)] \(\varrho_2\) is
 \((T_j(v_n)-v_n,D_{j-1},\varphi_{j-1})\)-stable;
\item[(c2)]
 \(\overline\varrho_2(v)=\overline\varrho_1(v)\) for every
 \(v\in V(T_j)\cup V(T'_j(u)-u)\), and
 \(\varrho_2(f)=\varrho_1(f)\) for every exact displayed edge identity
 \(f\) on \(T_j\) or on the prefix \(T'_j(u)\); and
\item[(c3)] \(\gamma_{1_1}\in\overline\varrho_2(u)\).
\end{enumerate}
In the Lean statement, \(V(T'_j(u)-u)\) is
\noderef{TreeVerticesBefore}, and an edge of \(T'_j(u)\) is expressed by
its exact edge index lying strictly before the unique vertex index of
\(u\).  Thus parallel edge identities are not collapsed.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Write the trace as
\[
 \varrho^0=\varrho_1,\varrho^1,\ldots,\varrho^r=\varrho_2,
\]
and let \(t_a\) be the exact slot processed at step \(a\).  We first
record the consequence of invariant (16) that will be used repeatedly:
\[
 P_u(\gamma_{t_a},\delta_{t_a},\varrho^a)
 \text{ contains at most }v_n\text{ from }T_{t_a},
 \text{ and }v_n\text{ is not an end}.                        \tag{18}
\]
The invariant also gives \(T_j\subseteq T_{t_a}\) as an ordered initial
segment and says that every \(\varrho^a\) is proper.

We prove (c1).  Let \(x\) occur before \(v_n\) in the exact stage-\(j\) tree
order.  By the definition of this prefix \(x\in V(T_j)\), hence
\(x\in V(T_{t_a})\), and \(x\ne v_n\).  Equation (18) therefore says
that the switched component contains no vertex node \(x\).  It contains
no edge node incident with \(x\) either: such an edge node is adjacent
to \(x\) in \noderef{TwoColorIncidenceGraph}, so connectedness would
put \(x\) in the component.  Thus every step fixes every edge incident
with the before-\(v_n\) vertex set and fixes every missing-color set on
that set.  Induction over the trace gives
\[
 \varrho_2(f)=\varrho_1(f)\text{ on all such incident edges},
 \qquad
 \overline\varrho_2(x)=\overline\varrho_1(x).                \tag{19}
\]
Expanding \noderef{StableColoringBeforeVertex}, compose (19) with the
two clauses of the assumed stability of \(\varrho_1\).  The missing sets
still agree with those of \(\varphi_{j-1}\), and every controlled
incident edge still has its \(\varphi_{j-1}\)-color.  This is precisely
(c1), the before-vertex form of the stability transitivity used in the
paper.

For the first assertion of (c2), begin with a vertex \(x\in V(T_j)\).
If \(x\ne v_n\), (18) says that the component avoids \(x\), so the switch
does not alter any incident edge or missing color there.  If \(x=v_n\),
then the component either avoids \(v_n\) or passes through it.  In the
latter case (18) says that \(v_n\) is not an end.  By
\noderef{IsProperEdgeColoring} and
\noderef{TwoColorIncidenceDegreeAtMostTwo}, a proper two-color path
then has one incident edge of each of its two colors at \(v_n\); the
switch exchanges them, so the set of colors present, and hence
\noderef{MissingColors}, is unchanged.  Induction proves missing-color
equality on all of \(V(T_j)\).

Now let \(x\) occur in \(T'_j(u)-u\).  At each step,
\(\delta_{t_a}\) is missing at \(u\), so \(u\) is one end of the path.
If the other end also occurred before \(u\) in \(T'_j\), then one of
\(\gamma_{t_a},\delta_{t_a}\) would be missing at a vertex of that
prefix.  Both colors belong to the stored-color union over \(L_1^*\),
contradicting the hypothesis obtained from equations (14)--(15).
Inductively, earlier switches have already preserved the missing sets on
this prefix, so the same argument applies at every step.  Hence every
prefix vertex on the path is internal; its two incident path colors are
merely exchanged and its missing set is unchanged.  This proves the
vertex half of (c2).

We next prove the exact edge equalities.  If an edge identity displayed
on \(T_j\) belonged to a switched component, both of its distinct
endpoints would be vertices of \(T_j\subseteq T_{t_a}\).  The graph is
loopless and \noderef{TreeSequence} retains both endpoint identities, so this
would put two vertices of \(T_{t_a}\) on the component, contrary to
(18).  Therefore every displayed edge of \(T_j\) is fixed at every
step.

Consider an edge of the prefix \(T'_j(u)\).  If it belongs to the seed,
the displayed equality of the seed with
\(\noderef{TreeSequencePrefix}(T_j,\mathtt{terminalPosition.val})\), together
with the defining edge map of that prefix, identifies this exact edge with
the edge of \(T_j\) having the same cast index.  It is therefore fixed by the
preceding paragraph.  Otherwise, the
\noderef{TashkinovClosure} certificate and
\noderef{TashkinovAugmentationSequence}, through
\noderef{TashkinovAugmentation}, show that this exact edge was
adjoined by TAA under \(\varrho_1\).  Its \(\varrho_1\)-color was missing
at a vertex of the tree prefix present immediately before it was added.
Because the edge itself occurs before \(u\), that witnessing vertex also
occurs before \(u\).  Equations (14)--(15) say that no color used by
Algorithm (B) is missing there.  Thus the edge's \(\varrho_1\)-color is
not one of the two colors at any switch.  It never belongs to a switched
component and retains its exact value.  This proves the edge half of
(c2), including the individual identities of parallel edges.

Finally prove (c3).  If \(L_1^*=\varnothing\), then the selected position
is the first position and the trace has length zero; the Case 1 selection
already says that \(\gamma_{1_1}=\gamma_0\) is missing at \(u\) under
\(\varrho_1=\varrho_2\).  Otherwise Algorithm (B) processes
\(1_b,1_{b-1},\ldots,1_1\).  Before switching at \(1_a\), invariant
(16) says that \(\delta_{1_a}\) is missing at \(u\).  The switch turns
the incident \(\gamma_{1_a}\)-edge into a
\(\delta_{1_a}\)-edge, so \(\gamma_{1_a}\) becomes missing.  The chain
identity identifies this new missing color with the delta of the next
slot.  Descending induction therefore ends with \(\gamma_{1_1}\)
missing at \(u\) under \(\varrho_2\).  This is (c3), and all three parts
of Claim 4.4 follow.
\end{proof}

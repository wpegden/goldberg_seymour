\begin{lemma}[Scheide Proposition 4.2, one-tail-pair case]
Let $(T,e_0,\varphi)$ be a balanced normal globally maximum $k$-triple in
a finite loopless multigraph $G$, and suppose every vertex of $G$ is
incident with an edge.  If $h(G)+2=t(G)$, then $G$ is elementary.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Put $p=h(G)$.  The balanced condition
\noderef{BalancedGlobalMaximumTriple} identifies $p$ with the chosen
normal height.  The global equality in \noderef{GlobalMaximumTriple},
vertex injectivity in \noderef{TreeSequence}, and
\noderef{TreeSequenceVertexSet} give
\[
 \operatorname{length}(T)+1=|V(T)|=t(G)=p+2.
\]
Thus $T$ consists of the $p$ trunk vertices
$y_0,\ldots,y_{p-1}$ followed by precisely two vertices $u_1,u_2$, with
corresponding tail edges $f_1,f_2$.

The first tail edge has index $p-1\geq1$.  The Tashkinov condition contained
in \noderef{GlobalMaximumTriple} and defined in
\noderef{TashkinovTree} therefore supplies a color $\gamma$ on $f_1$ which
is missing at an earlier vertex.  Every earlier vertex lies in the trunk.
By \noderef{ScheideGraphHeightOdd}, $p$ is odd.  Taking
$j=(p+1)/2$ in the paired-tail clause of
\noderef{BalancedGlobalMaximumTriple} is legitimate because
$p<2j=p+1<p+2$; that clause gives
$\varphi(f_2)=\varphi(f_1)=\gamma$.  Hence $\gamma$ is missing on the
trunk and both tail edges have color $\gamma$.

We verify carefully that $\gamma$ is not either trunk color.  Global
maximality makes $T$ maximal for its fixed pair.  Indeed, if a Tashkinov
tree $S$ for the same pair contained $V(T)$ but not conversely, finiteness
would give $|V(S)|>|V(T)|=t(G)$, contrary to the maximum in
\noderef{TashkinovOrder} and the equality in
\noderef{GlobalMaximumTriple}.  Thus the containment implication in
\noderef{FixedPairMaximalTashkinovTree} holds for $T$.  Apply
\noderef{ScheideMaximalKempePath} to this fixed-pair maximum, to the
Tashkinov tree $T$, to its first two vertices, and to the colors
$\alpha,\beta$ recorded in \noderef{NormalGlobalMaximumTriple}.  It says
that the full $(\alpha,\beta)$ component of those vertices is a path.
Every edge-node of the normal closing path is colored $\alpha$ or $\beta$
and is joined to its incident endpoint vertex-nodes, so by
\noderef{TwoColorIncidenceGraph} this is another simple path in that same
component with the same ends.  A path has a unique simple path between its
ends, so the normal closing path is the full component.  Its recorded
vertex support is exactly $\{y_0,\ldots,y_{p-1}\}$.

The first tail edge attaches $u_1$ to an earlier, hence trunk, vertex by
\noderef{TreeSequence}.  If $\gamma$ were $\alpha$ or $\beta$, then the
colored identity $f_1$, incident with its trunk attachment and with $u_1$,
would give two successive adjacencies in
\noderef{TwoColorIncidenceGraph}.  Since the attachment vertex is in the
full $(\alpha,\beta)$ component, both the edge-node $f_1$ and its other
endpoint vertex-node $u_1$ would belong to that component.  This contradicts
the preceding support equality and the fact from \noderef{TreeSequence}
that $u_1$ is distinct from every earlier trunk vertex.  Thus
$\gamma\notin\{\alpha,\beta\}$.  The second tail edge initially attaches
$u_2$ either to a trunk vertex or to $u_1$.  The latter is impossible:
$f_1\ne f_2$ by edge injectivity in \noderef{TreeSequence}, and both
identities would then be incident with $u_1$, hence adjacent by
\noderef{AdjacentEdges}.  The globally maximum field in
\noderef{GlobalMaximumTriple} contains a $k$-triple; \noderef{KTriple}
supplies its proper coloring, and \noderef{IsProperEdgeColoring} says that
the two distinct adjacent colored identities must receive different
colors.  This contradicts
$\varphi(f_1)=\varphi(f_2)=\gamma$.  Consequently both tail edges attach
their new vertices directly to the trunk.

Every non-first trunk edge is an edge-node of the normal
$(\alpha,\beta)$ closing path and is therefore colored $\alpha$ or
$\beta$ by \noderef{TwoColorIncidenceGraph}.  Since $\gamma$ differs from
both, all earlier non-first trunk edges avoid $\gamma$.  We have now
established the ordered block data,
equal block color, attachment-to-base condition, missing-on-base condition,
and earlier-edge avoidance required by
\noderef{ScheideStructuralConfiguration} with $s=1$, base equal to the
trunk, and the singleton color list $\Gamma=\{\gamma\}$.  The singleton
list is injective automatically.

It remains to verify the exit-covering field.  By
\noderef{ScheideGlobalMaximumOrder}, $V(T)$ is elementary.  Therefore each
of $\alpha,\beta,\gamma$ is missing at a unique tree vertex.  Existence for
$\alpha$ at $y_0$ and $\beta$ at $y_1$ is part of
\noderef{NormalGlobalMaximumTriple}, and existence for $\gamma$ at a trunk
vertex was proved above from \noderef{TashkinovTree}; these are memberships
in the sets defined by \noderef{MissingColors}.  For uniqueness, if one of
these colors were missing at two distinct tree vertices, their missing-color
sets would intersect, contrary to the disjointness required by
\noderef{ColoredVertexSetElementary}.  Denote the unique vertex missing
$\gamma$ by $y_r$.  The used tree colors are contained in
$\{\alpha,\beta,\gamma\}$, as just proved.

Every vertex other than $y_r$ misses a free color.  Indeed, a vertex other
than the two ends of $e_0$ misses at least one color by
\noderef{ScheideGlobalMaximumMissingCounts}.  Such a vertex is distinct
from $y_0,y_1,y_r$, so uniqueness and \noderef{MissingColors} exclude all
three used colors from its missing-color set.  At either end of $e_0$, the
same counting lemma supplies at least two missing colors.  By
\noderef{TreeSequence} these ends are the distinct vertices $y_0,y_1$.
If that end is not $y_r$, uniqueness says that $\gamma$ is not missing
there, while \noderef{ColoredVertexSetElementary} and
\noderef{MissingColors} say that only its own one of $\alpha,\beta$ can be
missing there.  Hence another missing color lies outside the three used
colors.  In every case the color obtained is missing on $V(T)$ and absent from
\noderef{TreeUsedColors}, hence belongs to
\noderef{TreeFreeColors}.

Now let $v\in\noderef{ScheideExitVertices}(T,\varphi)$.  By the definition
\noderef{ScheideExitVertex}, $v\in V(T)$.  The theorem
\noderef{ScheideExitNoFree} says that $v$ misses no free color.  The
preceding paragraph forces $v=y_r$, and therefore $\gamma$ is missing at
$v$.  This proves that every exit is covered by the sole color in
$\Gamma$.

Finally, the stated hypothesis supplies the no-isolated-vertex field, and
the original balanced normal triple supplies the globally maximum field.
Thus all fields, including the numerical length identity, of
\noderef{ScheideStructuralConfiguration} have been proved for $s=1$.
Applying \noderef{ScheideStructuralLemma} to this exact configuration
gives that $G$ is elementary.
\end{proof}

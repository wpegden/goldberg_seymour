\begin{lemma}[Defective-chain structure, part (a)]
Let $(T,e_0,\varphi)$ be globally maximum, let $\alpha$ be defective, and
let the free color $\gamma$ be missing at $u\in V(T)$.  The full
$(\alpha,\gamma)$-component through $u$ is a path from $u$ to a vertex
$z\notin V(T)$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Write $X=V(T)$.  By
\noderef{ScheideGlobalMaximumBoundaryFacts}, $X$ is closed, no
$\gamma$-colored edge identity leaves $X$, and at least three
$\alpha$-colored edge identities leave $X$.  By
\noderef{ScheideGlobalMaximumUnused}, neither $\alpha$ nor $\gamma$ is
used on a tree edge.  The $k$-triple clause of
\noderef{GlobalMaximumTriple} supplies properness.

The defective color $\alpha$ occurs on a boundary edge.  Closedness
therefore implies that $\alpha$ is missing at no vertex of $X$.  Hence the
vertex-node $u$ has one incident $\alpha$-edge and no
$\gamma$-edge, so it has degree one in its two-color component.  At every
other vertex of $X$, elementariness from
\noderef{ScheideGlobalMaximumBoundaryFacts} implies that $\gamma$ is
present, and closedness again implies that $\alpha$ is present; properness
makes the two incident edge identities unique.  Thus those vertex-nodes
have degree two.

By \noderef{TwoColorComponentSpanningPath}, the finite component has a
simple path spanning its full incidence support.  Its degree-one
vertex-node $u$ must be one end.  Its other end cannot be another vertex of
$X$, since all those vertices have degree two.  It also cannot be an
edge-node: every colored edge-node has exactly its two distinct endpoint
neighbors, because the graph is loopless.  Therefore the other end is a
vertex-node $z\notin X$.  Orienting the spanning path from $u$ to $z$
gives exactly the full defective-chain path of
\noderef{ScheideDefectiveChainPath}.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Scheide defective no-cross property (4)]
For every defective color $\delta$ in a defective-color setup, no
$\delta$-colored edge identity joins
$U_{\gamma(\delta)}$ to $Z_{\gamma(\delta)}$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Write $U=U_{\gamma(\delta)}$, $Z=Z_{\gamma(\delta)}$, and suppose that a
$\delta$-edge $g_1$ joins $u^1_{\gamma(\delta)}$ to $Z$, exchanging the
two indexed vertices if necessary.  Since $\alpha_0$ is missing in $Z$
and differs from $\gamma(\delta)$,
\noderef{ScheideStructuralInternalPair} supplies an internal
$\alpha_0$-edge $g_2$ of $U$.  Properness then permits at most one
$\delta$-boundary edge incident with $U$.  By
\noderef{ScheideGlobalMaximumBoundaryFacts}, a defective color has at least
three boundary edge identities, so there is a $\delta$-edge
$g_3\ne f^0_\delta$ joining $Z$ to a vertex outside $V(T)$.

Interchange $\alpha_0$ and $\gamma(\delta)$ on edges with both ends
outside $V(T)$.  Neither color occurs on the boundary of the closed tree,
so checking the two ends of each edge shows that the new coloring is
proper and still leaves exactly $e_0$ uncolored.  Under it, the terminal
subpath from $v^0_\delta$ on the recorded
$(\alpha_0,\delta)$ path is a $(\gamma(\delta),\delta)$ chain.  Switch that
full incidence component; properness is preserved by
\noderef{TwoColorComponentSwapProper}, and its edge-nodes are colored, so
$e_0$ remains uniquely uncolored.

Delete the $\gamma(\delta)$ block.  No retained tree edge changes:
$\delta$ is unused on the original tree by
\noderef{ScheideGlobalMaximumUnused}, and the only
$\gamma(\delta)$ tree edges were deleted.  At $v^0_\delta$, the missing
witness changes from $\gamma(\delta)$ to $\delta$.  Thus the remaining
sequence is Tashkinov for the recoloring.  The three unchanged edges
$g_1,g_2,g_3$, with colors respectively
$\delta,\alpha_0,\delta$, can be appended successively, adding the two
deleted vertices of $U$ and then a new outside vertex.  This produces a
Tashkinov tree with more vertices than the globally maximum tree in
\noderef{ScheideDefectiveSetup}, a contradiction.  Hence no such
$\delta$-edge exists.
\end{proof}

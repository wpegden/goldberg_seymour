\begin{lemma}[Change lemma, mixed location: largest closed starred stage]
Fix a finite loopless multigraph with its exact edge set, a palette of
$k$ colors, and a distinguished edge $e_0$.  Let $(T,\varphi_n)$ be a
positive-rung extended Tashkinov tree.  Let $\mathcal B$ be a hierarchy
base and let
\[
 T_n=T_{n,0}\subset T_{n,1}\subset\cdots\subset
 T_{n,q}\subset T_{n,q+1}=T
\]
be a good hierarchy under $\varphi_n$, where $q$ is its last divider
index and
\[
 A=T_{n,q}^{*}=\noderef{HierarchyStarredStage}(H,q).
\]
Assume that $T$ has maximum property, that $V(T)$ is not elementary,
that $\varphi_n$ is proper and leaves exactly $e_0$ uncolored, and that
the rung number is positive.  These data form a
\noderef{HierarchyCounterexample} $C$.

Assume the six lower-rung assertions of the technical theorem, uniformly
for every positive-rung maximum ETT on the same graph, palette, and
distinguished edge, with arbitrary certified input and generating
colorings and with rung number smaller than that of $T$: lower-rung
elementarity, terminal interchangeability, the two PE stability and
stored-pair assertions, the terminal PE component assertion, the
defective-endpoint order assertion, and the mod-coloring assertion.
Assume also the same-rung induction hypothesis that every maximum ETT
with a good hierarchy having fewer than $q$ dividers is elementary.
Finally assume the large-color estimate
\[
 2n+11\leq |\overline\varphi_n(T_n)|.
\]

Put $\ell=|T|-|A|$, the number of exact tail additions above $A$, and let
\[
 T(y_{\ell-1})=T\big|_{\min\{|A|+(\ell-1),|T|\}}
\]
be the exact ordered prefix supplied by \noderef{TreeSequencePrefix}.
Assume (6.6): $V(T(y_{\ell-1}))$ is elementary under $\varphi_n$.
Assume moreover that $C$ is lexicographically minimal among all
\noderef{HierarchyCounterexample}s on the fixed graph, palette, and
$e_0$ which have exactly $q$ dividers, allowing their certified input
colorings, generating colorings, bases, ETTs, and good hierarchies to
vary.  Explicitly, no such counterexample $C'$ satisfies
\[
 \noderef{HierarchyCounterexampleLess}
  \bigl(\noderef{HierarchyCounterexampleMeasure}(C'),
        \noderef{HierarchyCounterexampleMeasure}(C)\bigr).
\]

Let $\alpha\ne\beta$ be colors missing on $T(y_{\ell-1})$.  Write
\[
 i_c=\noderef{HierarchyFirstMissingPosition}(\varphi_n,T,c),
 \qquad v_c=T.\mathit{vertex}(i_c).
\]
If at least one of $\alpha,\beta$ is not missing on $A$, assume both
\[
 v_\alpha\prec v_\beta                                      \tag{a}
\]
and the exact edge-identity exclusion
\[
 \alpha\notin
 \left\{c:\exists t\in\operatorname{Fin}|T|,
       |A|\leq t<i_\beta,
       \ \varphi_n(T.\mathit{edge}(t))=c\right\}.             \tag{b}
\]
Thus (b) is precisely
$\alpha\notin\varphi_n\langle T(v_\beta)-A\rangle$; it is not
an endpoint-only abbreviation.


Assume in addition that the first $\alpha$-missing vertex lies in the
last starred stage and the first $\beta$-missing vertex lies outside it:
\[
        v_\alpha\in V(A),\qquad v_\beta\notin V(A).
\]
Assume that $q>0$.  Let $r$ satisfy $0\leq r<q$, assume that
$\{\alpha\}$ is closed on $T_{n,r}^{*}$, and assume explicitly that
for every $s$ with $r<s\leq q$, the singleton $\{\alpha\}$ is not
closed on $T_{n,s}^{*}$.  Thus $r$ is the largest closed starred-stage
index; no missingness at stage $r$ is assumed.  Then
\[
 \noderef{TwoColorComponent}(G,\varphi_n,\alpha,\beta,
       \operatorname{inl}v_\alpha)
 =
 \noderef{TwoColorComponent}(G,\varphi_n,\alpha,\beta,
       \operatorname{inl}v_\beta).
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Put $H=C.\mathit{hierarchy}$, $A=T_{n,q}^{*}$, and
\[
 K_x=\noderef{TwoColorComponent}
       (G,\varphi_n,\alpha,\beta,\operatorname{inl}x).
\]
The nestedness fields of \noderef{TashkinovHierarchy} put every
ordinary and starred stage through $q$ inside $A$ as an exact ordered
prefix.  The same zero-tail argument as follows directly from the
statement is needed here.  If $\ell=|T|-|A|$ were zero, then
\noderef{TreeSequenceInitialSegment} would identify $A=T$, and the
prefix \noderef{TreeSequencePrefix} in (6.6) would be the full tree,
contradicting \noderef{HierarchyCounterexample}.  Hence $\ell\geq1$,
so every named stage is also a prefix of $T(y_{\ell-1})$.  Its assumed
\noderef{ColoredVertexSetElementary} property restricts to each stage.

The least-candidate definition
\noderef{HierarchyFirstMissingPosition} says that $v_\alpha$ misses
$\alpha$ and $v_\beta$ misses $\beta$.  Since $v_\alpha\in A$,
$q>0$, and \noderef{HierarchyStarredStage} gives $A=T_{n,q}$,
the color $\alpha$ is missing on $T_{n,q}$.  The two mixed locations
make the vertices distinct.  Elementarity of $T(y_{\ell-1})$ then
shows that neither vertex misses the other color, so
\noderef{MissingColors}, \noderef{PresentColors}, and properness make
$K_{v_\alpha}$ and $K_{v_\beta}$ nontrivial paper path components,
including the exact edge nodes.

Apply \noderef{rutcor}.  Its full-tail hypothesis is exactly the
displayed prefix of (6.6), not a prefix indexed by the hierarchy path
number.  Its pre-step elementarity premise comes from lower assertion
(i), after restricting the given maximum ETT through the preceding
rung, or from \noderef{TechnicalTheoremBase} at rung one.  Since
$T_{n,r}^{*}$ is closed and $r$ is largest, the first conclusion of
\noderef{rutcor} gives the exact edge exclusion
\[
 \alpha\notin
   \varphi_n\langle T_{n,q}-T_{n,r}^{*}\rangle.                \tag{1}
\]

We now derive the missingness needed for interchangeability; closedness
alone is not used as a missingness assertion.  We prove by descending
induction that $\alpha$ is missing on every $T_{n,s}^{*}$ for
$r\leq s\leq q$.  At $s=q$, the witness is the actual vertex
$v_\alpha\in A$.  Suppose $r<s\leq q$ and $\alpha$ is missing on
$T_{n,s}^{*}=T_{n,s}$ but not on $T_{n,s-1}^{*}$.  A vertex witnessing
the first fact lies in the exact vertex difference
$V(T_{n,s})-V(T_{n,s-1}^{*})$.  The stage $T_{n,s}$ is elementary, so
\noderef{GoodHierarchyNewMissingClosed} makes $\alpha$ closed on
$T_{n,s}=T_{n,s}^{*}$.  This contradicts the stated nonclosedness at
every index strictly larger than $r$.  Descending to $s=r+1$ proves
\[
       \alpha\in\overline\varphi_n(T_{n,r}^{*}).               \tag{2}
\]
Choose a vertex of $T_{n,r}^{*}$ witnessing (2).  The least-candidate
property of \noderef{HierarchyFirstMissingPosition}, together with the
entrywise prefix relation, places $v_\alpha$ itself in
$T_{n,r}^{*}$.

If $r>0$, apply \noderef{step1} at this exact stage using (2), the
assumed singleton closedness, the restricted elementarity, maximum
property, the lower-rung and fewer-divider hypotheses, and the
large-color estimate.  If $r=0$ and the terminal extension is PE,
apply conclusion (iii) of \noderef{interchange} to the exact core with
the identity instance of \noderef{CombinedStableColoring}.  If $r=0$
and the terminal extension is SE or RE, use the lower-rung terminal
interchangeability assertion, with \noderef{sc2} in SE, or use
\noderef{TechnicalTheoremBase} at rung one.  Thus
\[
 \alpha,\beta\text{ are }T_{n,r}^{*}\text{-interchangeable}.  \tag{3}
\]

Suppose for contradiction that $K_{v_\alpha}\ne K_{v_\beta}$.
The first component meets $T_{n,r}^{*}$, so (3),
\noderef{TreeInterchangeable}, and
\noderef{TwoColorComponentsDisjoint} show that $K_{v_\beta}$ is
vertex-disjoint from that stage.  Set
$\sigma_n=\varphi_n/K_{v_\beta}$ using
\noderef{TwoColorComponentSwap}.  The theorem
\noderef{TwoColorComponentSwapProper} gives properness, and the switch
preserves the unique uncolored exact edge.  It leaves $v_\alpha$
missing $\alpha$ and makes $v_\beta$ missing $\alpha$.

Let $B=T(v_\beta)$ be the exact prefix ending at the displayed vertex
$v_\beta$.  If $s_B=|B|-|A|$, the mixed location and the inclusion
$v_\beta\in T(y_{\ell-1})$ give
\[
                       1\leq s_B\leq\ell-1<\ell.              \tag{4}
\]
Both vertices are in $B$, so $B$ is non-elementary under $\sigma_n$.

Before applying any transport result, apply
\noderef{GoodHierarchyTerminalPrefixRepackage} to
$A\prec B\preccurlyeq T$.  It returns the old-color maximum ETT
$\mathcal E_B$ on the actual ordered prefix $B$, its copied base, the
same-divider hierarchy with unchanged nonterminal and starred stages,
and a genuine good witness with the old Gamma pairs.  In particular it
proves equality of the computed stored and stage-missing sets.  Its
avoidance proof treats the totalized first-missing fallback and gives the
needed new-to-old segment containment, without asserting equality of
the full \noderef{HierarchySegmentColors} functions.

The switched component is outside $T_{n,r}^{*}$ but may meet later
stages, including $A$.  Therefore we do not invoke an outside-terminal
good-hierarchy transport theorem.  We first establish only the
terminal-ladder data.  The ladder tree $T_n$ is an ordered prefix of
$T_{n,r}^{*}$ by \noderef{HierarchyBase}; hence
\noderef{OutsideKempeRecoloringStable} gives
\[
       (T_n,D_n,\varphi_n)\text{-stability}                   \tag{5}
\]
and equality on every exact edge incident with $T_n$.  In PE, both
constituents of the exact core lie in $T_{n,r}^{*}$.  Applying the same
argument to each constituent gives
\noderef{CombinedStableColoring}, and
\noderef{CombinedStableClosurePreserved} transports $R_n$ as the same
exact closure.

Combine (1) with the ordered-unused exclusion from $A$ to $v_\beta$.
They are adjacent displayed index intervals, so
\[
 \alpha\notin\varphi_n\langle B-T_{n,r}^{*}\rangle.            \tag{6}
\]
The first-missing definition excludes $\beta$ from every ordinary
augmentation edge before $v_\beta$.  There is one exact exception to
check: when $r=0$, $T_{n,0}^{*}=T_n$, and the first displayed edge is
the SE or RE connecting seed $f_n$ with $\beta\in D_n$.  The SE
\noderef{MaximumDefectiveChoice} or the
\noderef{RevisitingExtensionCertificate} in the corresponding
constructor of \noderef{TashkinovSeriesStep} makes this individual edge
incident with $T_n$.  The edge clause of (5) fixes
$\sigma_n(f_n)=\varphi_n(f_n)$ before reconstruction.  Thus (6), the
ordinary \noderef{TashkinovAugmentation} rule, and this first-seed
check show that every exact edge in the appropriate seed and subsequent
route to $B$ keeps its old color.

The missing-color witnesses along that route are also pointwise
unchanged.  Before $v_\beta$, no vertex misses $\beta$.  By elementarity,
$v_\alpha$ is the only prefix vertex missing $\alpha$; it belongs to
the other component.  Hence any vertex of $K_{v_\beta}$ before
$v_\beta$ has both colors present, and the switch exchanges them.
All other vertices are fixed.  The old augmentation witnesses therefore
remain witnesses.  The required restricted exact route is certified by
\noderef{TashkinovAugmentationSequenceRestrict}.

Apply \noderef{Td} to (5), using the stated lower-rung package and
maximum property, to obtain \noderef{ModColoring}.  In PE, now apply
\noderef{HierarchySplitter} to the already constructed old-color
prefix ETT $\mathcal E_B$, with the combined stability, transported
closure, and exact augmentation route.  It returns a
\noderef{CorrespondingExtendedTashkinovTree}, maximum property, and
\noderef{SameHierarchyStages}.  In SE or RE, use the exact one-edge
seed and route in the universal clause of \noderef{ModColoring}; the
replacement keeps the non-PE kind and base, and
\noderef{MaximumPropertyTransport} supplies maximum property.

We must still construct a new \noderef{GoodHierarchy}.  Use the same
Gamma pairs.  At every hierarchy-stage vertex in the switched
component, both $\alpha$ and $\beta$ are present: $v_\alpha$ lies in
the other component and $v_\beta$ is later than $A$.  Thus the switch
preserves the missing-color set at every stage vertex, even when the
component meets $A$.  Pair size and pairwise disjointness are literal.

For avoidance, (6) and the exact first-seed check show that each
displayed segment edge used by the hierarchy has its old color.
The pointwise missing-set equality on every stage difference then
preserves unique refresh.  The combined stability and transported
closure preserve the two clauses of the PE parallel-base field.

It remains to check every positive-stage minus-closed field.  Through
stage $r$ the component has no vertex in the starred stage, so every
incident $\alpha$- or $\beta$-edge is fixed and the old field applies.
For a stage $j>r$, the descending argument above shows that $\alpha$
is missing there, while the branch hypothesis says it is not closed.
The old minus-closed field in \noderef{GoodHierarchy} therefore forces
\[
 \alpha\in
 \bigcup_{\eta\in D_{n,j}}\Gamma^{j-1}_\eta.                  \tag{7}
\]
The color $\beta$ is not missing on that stage because its first
missing vertex is $v_\beta\notin A$.  Under the switch, a changed
boundary edge has color $\alpha$ or $\beta$; by (7), $\alpha$ is among
the colors excluded from the minus-closed demand, and $\beta$ is not
among its missing colors.  Every other boundary color is fixed.
Hence the minus-closed field holds pointwise at every later stage.

These checks construct every field of the new good witness.  Only now
apply \noderef{SameGoodHierarchy} to record equality of the stages,
stored sets, and Gamma pairs.  The reconstructed maximum ETT on $B$,
its base and hierarchy, this good witness, and the two vertices missing
$\alpha$ give a same-divider
\noderef{HierarchyCounterexample} $C'$.

Finally compare the precise
\noderef{HierarchyCounterexampleMeasure}s.  Let
$p=\noderef{HierarchyPathNumber}(H)$ and $p'$ be the path number of
the truncated hierarchy.  If $s_B<p$, then $p'\leq s_B<p$.  If
$p\leq s_B$, the old path tail beginning at $p$ restricts to a path
tail of $B$, including the singleton endpoint case, and $p'\leq p$.
If equality holds, writing $d=|A|-|T_{n,q}|$ gives
\[
 |T|-|T_{n,q}|=d+\ell,\qquad
 |B|-|T_{n,q}|=d+s_B<d+\ell
\]
by (4).  Thus
\noderef{HierarchyCounterexampleLess} holds from $C'$ to $C$, contrary
to the minimality assumption.  Therefore
$K_{v_\alpha}=K_{v_\beta}$.
\end{proof}

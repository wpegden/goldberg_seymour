\begin{lemma}[Step 1, Subcase 2.2 at a PE zero divider]
Let $G,e_0,[k],T,\varphi$ be a finite loopless multigraph with exact edge
identities, distinguished uncolored edge, color set, positive-rung
certified ETT, and generating coloring.  Assume maximum property,
properness, exact one-uncoloredness, and the complete six-part lower-rung
package, whose assertion (iii) gives unconditional stability before each PE
support, predecessor-ladder stability only when its first-appearance index is
greater than one, and unconditional equal-index stored-pair disjointness.  Let $H$ be a good hierarchy over a hierarchy base, with
$d<q_0$ dividers.  Assume the same-rung fewer-than-$q_0$ elementarity
hypothesis for arbitrary certified inputs and generating colorings, the
large bound $|\overline\varphi(T_n)|\geq2n+11$, the separate terminal
certificate
\[
 T\text{ is }
 \left(\bigcup_{\eta\in D^H_{n,d+1}}\Gamma^d_\eta\right)^-
 \text{-closed under }\varphi,
\]
and elementarity of $V(T)$.  Put $A=H.\mathit{starred}(d)$.

Let $\alpha\ne\beta$, with $\alpha$ missing and singleton-closed on $T$
and $\beta$ not missing on $T$.  Let $P_1,P_2,P_3$ be pairwise
support-disjoint exact $(T,\varphi,\{\alpha,\beta\})$-exit paths.  Their
outside ends $a_1,a_2,a_3$ and tree ends $b_1,b_2,b_3$ are pairwise
distinct, with $b_1\prec_T b_2\prec_T b_3$.  Retain the exact
$\beta$-colored boundary-edge identity $f_j$ of each path incident with
$b_j$.  Suppose $b_3\in V(A)$.

Assume that a selected color $\theta\ne\alpha,\beta$ is missing and
singleton-closed on $A$ and singleton-closed on $T$.  Assume also the
explicit no-smaller-presentation consequence of the global minimum: for
every proper, exactly one-uncolored coloring $\sigma$ stable from
$\varphi$ on the final ladder tree, arbitrary replacement input,
corresponding same-rung maximum ETT $T'$, good hierarchy with fewer than
$d$ dividers, its own large estimate, and its separate terminal
minus-closed certificate, there do not exist two support-disjoint exact
exit legs with distinct outside ends for a missing singleton-closed first
color.  Assume additionally that $d=0$ and that the terminal extension
kind is PE.  Then the displayed Subcase 2.2 data are contradictory.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Write $n$ for the rung number, $T_n$ for the last ladder tree, $D_i$ for
the exact cumulative stored-color set at slot $i$, and $\varphi_i$ for
the exact coloring at that slot.  Thus $\varphi=\varphi_n$ by the
generating-coloring field of \noderef{ExtendedTashkinovTree}.  We first
make the three exit legs used in every branch completely explicit.

The starred tree $A$ is an ordered initial segment of $T$.  Unfold
\noderef{TashkinovHierarchy}.  If $d>0$, then
\noderef{HierarchyStarredStage} identifies $A$ with the $d$-th hierarchy
stage, and repeated use of the nestedness field of the hierarchy gives
the required initial-segment map to its terminal stage $T$.  If $d=0$,
the same conclusion follows from the core-inside-first field and then the
nestedness and terminal-stage fields of the hierarchy.  All these are
entrywise statements by \noderef{TreeSequenceInitialSegment}.

We record the positions carefully, since the two order hypotheses initially
give two possibly different witnesses for $b_2$.  Unfold the first
\noderef{TreeVertexOrder}: there are positions $r_1<s_2$ in $T$ displaying
$b_1$ and $b_2$.  Unfold the second one: there are positions $r_2<s_3$ in
$T$ displaying $b_2$ and $b_3$.  The vertex map of the
\noderef{TreeSequence} $T$ is injective.  Since the entries at $s_2$ and
$r_2$ are both $b_2$, injectivity gives
\[
                         s_2=r_2,\qquad r_1<s_2=r_2<s_3.       \tag{1}
\]
Unfolding \noderef{TreeSequenceVertexSet} in $b_3\in V(A)$ gives a
position $t_3$ of $A$ displaying $b_3$.  The vertex-entry clause of the
initial-segment relation says that the canonical image of $t_3$ is a
position of $T$ displaying $b_3$.  Injectivity of the vertex map of $T$
identifies that image with $s_3$.  Hence the numerical value of $s_3$ is
within the initial block occupied by $A$.  By (1), the numerical values of
$s_2=r_2$ and $r_1$ are strictly smaller.  The positions of $A$ having
these two numerical values therefore exist, and the same entrywise
initial-segment equalities say that they display $b_2$ and $b_1$,
respectively.  Together with the assumed membership of $b_3$, this proves
\[
                         b_1,b_2,b_3\in V(A).                  \tag{2}
\]

Unfold each given \noderef{TreeExitPath}.  The edge-node immediately
preceding the terminal vertex-node $b_j$ represents an exact boundary
edge of $T$: its other adjacent vertex-node on the path is outside $T$,
because $b_j$ is the only vertex-node of the path in $V(T)$.  By
\noderef{TwoColorIncidenceGraph} that edge has color $\alpha$ or
$\beta$.  It cannot have color $\alpha$, since
\noderef{TreeColorSetClosed} says that the exact $\alpha$-colored
boundary set is empty.  Hence this last boundary edge is
$\beta$-colored.  The separately retained identity $f_j$ has the same
two properties by hypothesis; unfolding \noderef{BoundaryColorEdges}
and \noderef{BoundaryEdges} shows in particular that its end other than
$b_j$ lies outside $T$.  Since $b_j\in V(A)\subseteq V(T)$, the same
identity $f_j$ is also a $\beta$-colored boundary edge of $A$.

We next justify which $\alpha,\theta$ components may be exchanged.  If an
$\alpha$-edge node occurs on $P_j$, it is not incident with $T$:
otherwise one of its incident vertex-nodes on this alternating path
would be in $T$, hence would be $b_j$, and the edge would be an
$\alpha$-colored boundary edge, contrary to the preceding closedness.
More generally, its full \noderef{TwoColorComponent} for
$\alpha,\theta$ contains no vertex of $T$.  For if it did, take a
shortest incidence-graph walk from the external edge to a vertex-node in
$T$ and its first vertex-node in $T$.  The preceding exact edge identity
crosses the boundary of $T$ and, by
\noderef{TwoColorIncidenceGraph}, has color $\alpha$ or $\theta$.
This contradicts respectively the assumed singleton closedness of
$\alpha$ or of $\theta$ on $T$.  The identical first-crossing argument
shows that every $\alpha,\theta$ component containing an outside vertex
is wholly outside $T$.

Exchange $\alpha$ and $\theta$ on every full $\alpha,\theta$ component
outside $T$, and call the result $\nu$.  Finiteness makes this a finite
choice of roots, and the preceding paragraph verifies the outside-support
clause of \noderef{OutsideKempeRecoloring}.  By
\noderef{OutsideKempeRecoloringStable}, $\nu$ is proper, leaves exactly
$e_0$ uncolored, fixes every exact edge incident with $T$, preserves the
missing-color set at each vertex of $T$, and is
$(T,C,\varphi_n)$-stable for every chosen control set $C$.  We now verify
the restriction to $T_n$, including both clauses.  The
above-penultimate field of \noderef{ExtendedTashkinovTree} and
\noderef{TreeSequenceInitialSegment} give the canonical entrywise
inclusion
\[
                         V(T_n)\subseteq V(T).                  \tag{3}
\]
Indeed, a position witnessing membership in
\noderef{TreeSequenceVertexSet} for $T_n$ is sent to the position with the
same numerical value in $T$, and the two displayed vertex entries agree.
If an exact edge $f$ is incident with $T_n$, unfold
\noderef{TreeIncidentEdge} and retain an endpoint $v\in V(T_n)$ incident
with $f$; (3) makes the same $v$ witness that $f$ is incident with $T$.
Likewise, unfolding \noderef{TreeMissingColors} gives
\[
              \overline{\varphi_n}(T_n)
                    \subseteq\overline{\varphi_n}(T):          \tag{4}
\]
if a color is missing at a witnessing vertex $v\in V(T_n)$, then (3) puts
that same vertex in $V(T)$, and its \noderef{MissingColors} membership is
unchanged.

Unfold \noderef{StableColoring}.  For its edge clause on $T_n$, let $f$
be incident with $T_n$ and suppose that its old color belongs to
$\overline{\varphi_n}(T_n)\cup C$.  The preceding incident-edge transport
makes $f$ incident with $T$, while (4) puts that old color in
$\overline{\varphi_n}(T)\cup C$.  The edge clause of stability on $T$
therefore gives $\nu(f)=\varphi_n(f)$.  For the vertex clause, take
$v\in V(T_n)$, use (3) to regard it as a vertex of $T$, and apply the
vertex clause on $T$ to obtain
$\overline\nu(v)=\overline{\varphi_n}(v)$.  Thus both clauses hold, and
$\nu$ is $(T_n,C,\varphi_n)$-stable for every $C$.

For $j=1,2,3$, form a walk $Q_j$ with the same ordered vertex- and
edge-nodes as $P_j$.  Each old $\beta$-edge remains $\beta$ because
$\beta\ne\alpha,\theta$.  Every old $\alpha$-edge lies in one of the
selected outside components just proved and becomes $\theta$.  Thus
every successive pair of nodes of $P_j$ is adjacent in
$I_G(\nu;\theta,\beta)$, so $Q_j$ is a walk there; it is the same simple
node list and has the same positive length and support as $P_j$.
Its outside end still misses one of its two colors.  Explicitly, if
$\beta$ was missing at $a_j$, then the definition
\noderef{MissingColors}, together with \noderef{PresentColors}, says
that no incident edge had color $\beta$; none acquires that color in an
$\alpha,\theta$ exchange, so $\beta$ remains missing.  If instead
$\alpha$ was missing, then no incident edge had color $\alpha$.  The
whole $\alpha,\theta$ component through the outside vertex $a_j$ was
selected, so the $\theta$-edges incident with $a_j$ become $\alpha$ and
the new $\theta$-edges are exactly its old $\alpha$-edges; hence
$\theta$ is missing there under $\nu$.  We already proved that all three
tree ends lie in $A$, while $a_j\notin V(T)$ implies
$a_j\notin V(A)$.  Finally, the only vertex-node of $Q_j$ in $A$ is
$b_j$, because the corresponding claim for $P_j$ excludes every other
vertex even from the larger tree $T$.  This checks every field of
\[
       \operatorname{TreeExitPath}(A,\nu,\theta,\beta,a_j,b_j,Q_j).
       \tag{5}
\]
The supports remain pairwise disjoint, the three outside ends remain
pairwise distinct, and no claim about separation of the three
transformed full components has been made.

Apply \noderef{HierarchyLEMStable} to the just constructed outside
recoloring.  It supplies a corresponding ETT $\mathcal T^\nu$ displayed
on $T$, a hierarchy base $B^\nu$, a hierarchy $H^\nu$, and a good
hierarchy witness $g^\nu$ on it.  Retain also the supplied maximum
property, transported terminal minus-closed certificate, and the proof
that the old and new witnesses satisfy \noderef{SameGoodHierarchy}.
The first conjunct in that definition is precisely
\noderef{SameHierarchyStages}$(H,H^\nu)$.  Its first field gives the
equality of the two hierarchy-base cores
\[
                         B.\mathit{core}=B^\nu.\mathit{core}.   \tag{6}
\]
This equality is obtained from the explicit same-stages field, not inferred
from the equality of divider counts, stored-color sets, or Gamma pairs.
The \noderef{CorrespondingExtendedTashkinovTree} certificate separately
retains the rung, every ordered ladder tree, each exact connecting-edge
set, each raw stored set, and each extension kind.  The stability proved
above identifies the missing-color sets on $T_n$ under $\nu$ and
$\varphi_n$.  Consequently the finite-union-and-deletion formula in
\noderef{TashkinovStoredColors} identifies the replacement terminal
control set with the same $D_n$: the cumulative raw stored sets agree and
the deleted terminal tree-missing set agrees.  The large bound is unchanged
for the same reason.

By hypothesis $d=0$.  The unique index $\operatorname{last}(d)$ is
therefore zero, and direct evaluation of
\noderef{HierarchyStarredStage} at zero gives
\[
                              A=B.\mathit{core}.                \tag{7}
\]
Combining (6) and (7) proves the exact equality of ordered tree-sequences
\[
                              B^\nu.\mathit{core}=A.            \tag{8}
\]

The terminal extension is PE by hypothesis.  Equality of terminal kinds
in \noderef{CorrespondingExtendedTashkinovTree} makes the terminal
extension of $\mathcal T^\nu$ PE as well.  Apply the `parallel' field of
\noderef{HierarchyBase} to the transported base $B^\nu$.  Let $r$ be its
positive support position and let $x$ be the vertex displayed at position
$r$ in the transported terminal ladder tree.  The parallel field states
all of the following exact data: the chosen last-slot vertex is $x$;
$R_n:=B^\nu.\mathit{closure}$ is a
\noderef{TashkinovClosure} under $\nu$ of the
\noderef{TreeSequencePrefix} of that ladder tree through $x$; and there is
an \noderef{OrderedTreeUnion} certificate whose first two trees are that
ladder tree and $R_n$ and whose union is $B^\nu.\mathit{core}$.  Equality
of the terminal ladder trees in the corresponding certificate identifies
the transported ladder tree with the original ordered $T_n$.  Rewriting
the union by (8) therefore gives the exact certificate
\[
                         A=T_n\vee R_n.                         \tag{9}
\]

The position $r$ itself witnesses $x\in V(T_n)$.  By the entrywise formula
in \noderef{TreeSequencePrefix}, the prefix through $r$ contains $x$ as
its last displayed vertex.  The augmentation sequence in the closure,
after unfolding \noderef{TashkinovAugmentationSequence}, is a finite
reflexive-transitive chain of steps from \noderef{TashkinovAugmentation}.
Each step retains the preceding ordered tree as an initial segment.
Induction along this chain, including the empty-chain case, therefore
retains the support prefix in $R_n$, so $x\in V(R_n)$.  The vertex-set
field of the \noderef{OrderedTreeUnion} certificate (9) also gives
$x\in V(A)$.

We now prove that a palette color is missing at $x$.  Inspect the
certificate at index zero of the replacement series in $\mathcal T^\nu$,
which \noderef{TashkinovSeries} supplies at every index.  The SE, PE, and
RE constructors of \noderef{TashkinovSeriesStep} all certify a positive
successor index, so none can occur at index zero.  Eliminating the
certificate leaves the initial constructor and its initial
\noderef{KTriple}.  Unfolding the latter gives
\[
                              \Delta(G)+1\leq k.                \tag{10}
\]
Let $C=\noderef{PresentColors}(G,\nu,x)$, and let $I_x$ be the finite set
of exact edge identities incident with $x$.  For every $z\in C$, the
definition of `PresentColors' supplies an edge identity $e_z\in I_x$ with
$\nu(e_z)=z$.  Choose one such identity for each $z$.  If
$e_z=e_{z'}$, then the single value of the partial coloring on that exact
edge is both $z$ and $z'$, whence $z=z'$.  The map $z\mapsto e_z$ is
therefore injective, and
\[
 |C|\leq |I_x|=\noderef{Degree}(G,x)
          \leq\noderef{MaximumDegree}(G)=\Delta(G).            \tag{11}
\]
The last inequality follows directly from the defining maximum, which
contains the degree of $x$.  If
\noderef{MissingColors}$(G,\nu,x)$ were empty, its definition as the
complement of $C$ in $[k]$ would give $C=[k]$ and hence $|C|=k$.
Equations (10) and (11) would then imply
$\Delta(G)+1\leq\Delta(G)$, a contradiction.  Choose
$c\in\overline\nu(x)$.  Since $x$ belongs to both constituent trees, the
biunion definition \noderef{TreeMissingColors} gives
\[
                 c\in\overline\nu(T_n)\cap\overline\nu(R_n).  \tag{12}
\]

The pre-step elementarity required next is available for these exact
transported data.  If $n>1$, put $m=n-1$.  Then $m>0$, and
\noderef{CertifiedETTPredecessor} applied to $\mathcal T^\nu$ supplies an
actual rung-$m$ ETT whose displayed terminal tree is this same $T_n$, whose
generating coloring is the replacement series coloring at slot $n-1$, and
which has maximum property.  Lower assertion (i), applied to that certified
predecessor, makes $V(T_n)$ elementary under precisely this pre-step
coloring.  If $n=1$, $T_n$ is the tree $T_1$ of the replacement series'
initial tuple and its pre-step coloring is the slot-zero coloring;
the elementarity conclusion of \noderef{TechnicalTheoremBase} applies to
that exact tuple and gives the same conclusion.  Thus in both cases the
explicit pre-step elementarity premise of
\noderef{ParallelCommonMissingClosed} is satisfied.  Apply that lemma to
$\mathcal T^\nu$ and $B^\nu$.  In view of (8)--(9), it makes every color
common-missing on $T_n$ and $R_n$ singleton-closed on the exact core $A$;
by (12), this holds in particular for $c$.

The retained identity $f_3$ is still a $\beta$-colored boundary edge of
$A$: its inside endpoint is $b_3\in V(A)$, its other endpoint is outside
$T$ and hence outside $A$, and the first outside recoloring fixed its
color.  Consequently $c\ne\beta$.  Moreover $\beta$ is present at $x$.
For if it were missing there, then $x\in V(T_n)\cap V(R_n)$ would put
$\beta$ in both tree-missing sets; a second application of
\noderef{ParallelCommonMissingClosed} would make $A$
singleton-$\beta$-closed, contradicting the exact boundary identity
$f_3$.  Unfolding \noderef{MissingColors} and
\noderef{PresentColors} therefore supplies an exact $\beta$-colored edge
incident with $x$.

The first outside recoloring fixes every exact edge incident with $T$ and
every vertex missing-color set on $T$.  Since $A$ is an initial segment of
$T$, the original $\theta$-missingness and singleton-$\theta$-closedness
on $A$ therefore hold under $\nu$.  We have just proved singleton-$c$-
closedness there.  If $\theta=c$, put $\rho=\nu$.  Suppose instead that
$\theta\ne c$.  If a full $\theta,c$ component containing an outside
vertex met $A$, choose a shortest incidence-graph walk to its first
vertex-node in $A$.  The edge-node immediately before that first vertex
would be an exact boundary edge of $A$ colored $\theta$ or $c$, contrary
to the two singleton closedness statements.  Thus every such component is
wholly outside $A$.  Exchange $\theta,c$ on all full components outside
$A$; finiteness and the preceding first-crossing argument verify every
field of \noderef{OutsideKempeRecoloring}, and define the result to be
$\rho$.

In the nonidentity case, (9) gives
$V(A)=V(T_n)\cup V(R_n)$, so every selected component is outside both
constituents.  Apply \noderef{OutsideKempeRecoloringStable} to $T_n$ with
control set $D_n$, and separately to $R_n$ with empty control.  This makes
$\rho$ proper, leaves exactly $e_0$ uncolored, and gives simultaneously
\[
 \rho\text{ is }(T_n,D_n,\nu)\text{-stable},\qquad
 \rho\text{ is }(R_n,\varnothing,\nu)\text{-stable}.           \tag{13}
\]
In the identity case, properness and exact one-uncoloredness are those of
$\nu$, and both relations in (13) follow directly from reflexivity in the
two clauses of \noderef{StableColoring}.  Thus in either case (13) is
exactly the \noderef{CombinedStableColoring} relation relative to the
transported generating coloring $\nu$.  Its two vertex clauses, together
with (9), preserve the missing-color set at every vertex of $A$.

In particular $c$ remains missing at $x$, and $x\in V(A)$ gives
$c\in\overline\rho(A)$ by \noderef{TreeMissingColors}.  In the
nonidentity case \noderef{OutsideKempeRecoloringStable} fixes every exact
edge incident with each constituent, hence, by (9), every exact edge
incident with $A$; the identity case is immediate.  The empty $c$-colored
boundary under $\nu$ therefore remains empty under $\rho$, so $A$ is
singleton-$c$-closed under $\rho$.  The exact $\beta$-edge incident with
$x$ is also fixed, so $\beta$ remains present at $x$.

We next construct the three final exit paths rather than importing any
component identity through the recolorings.  In the identity case put
$\widehat Q_j=Q_j$.  In the nonidentity case give $\widehat Q_j$ the same ordered node list
as $Q_j$.  Every $\beta$-edge remains $\beta$, since both $\theta$ and $c$
are distinct from $\beta$.  Consider a $\theta$-edge on $Q_j$.  It cannot
be incident with $A$: the only vertex-node of $Q_j$ in $A$ is $b_j$, and
an incident $\theta$-edge would be a $\theta$-colored boundary edge of
$A$, contrary to singleton-$\theta$-closedness.  The first-crossing
argument of the preceding paragraph then shows that its full $\theta,c$
component is outside $A$, so that component was selected and the edge
becomes $c$.  Consequently the same successive node pairs form a walk
$\widehat Q_j$ in $I_G(\rho;c,\beta)$, with the same simplicity, positive length,
and support as $Q_j$.

The outside-end missing-color clause also transports explicitly.  The
certificate (5) says that either $\beta$ or $\theta$ is missing at $a_j$
under $\nu$.  If $\beta$ is missing, no incident edge has that color by
\noderef{MissingColors} and \noderef{PresentColors}, and the
$\theta,c$ swap creates no $\beta$-edge, so it remains missing.  If
$\theta$ is missing, the full $\theta,c$ component through the outside
vertex $a_j$ is selected; after its swap, the new $c$-edges incident with
$a_j$ are exactly its old $\theta$-edges, of which there were none.  Thus
$c$ is missing at $a_j$.  In the identity case these conclusions are just
the corresponding clauses of (5), since $\theta=c$.  The tree end remains
$b_j\in V(A)$, the outside end remains outside $A$, and the only
vertex-node of the unchanged support belonging to $A$ remains $b_j$.
This proves every field of
\[
          \operatorname{TreeExitPath}(A,\rho,c,\beta,a_j,b_j,\widehat Q_j)
          \qquad(j=1,2,3).                                    \tag{14}
\]
The three supports in (14) are the old supports, so they remain pairwise
disjoint; the three outside ends remain pairwise distinct.  No old
two-color component equality or inequality has been transported.

It remains to prove the component contradiction locally.  Put
$I=\noderef{TwoColorIncidenceGraph}(G,\rho;c,\beta)$.  At the vertex-node
$x$, the color $c$ is missing and an exact $\beta$-colored incident edge
was retained.  Hence $x$ has at least one neighbor in $I$ and no neighbor
arising from a $c$-edge.  Properness allows at most one incident
$\beta$-edge: two distinct such exact identities would be adjacent edges
of the same nonempty color, contrary to
\noderef{IsProperEdgeColoring}.  Therefore $x$ has degree exactly one in
$I$.

For each $j=1,2,3$, unfold the exact exit-path certificate (14).  Its
incidence-graph length is at least two, so its first edge-node is a
neighbor of the outside vertex-node $a_j$ in $I$.  The certificate also
says that one of $c,\beta$ is missing at $a_j$.  By
\noderef{MissingColors} and \noderef{PresentColors}, no incident edge at
$a_j$ has that missing color; properness permits at most one incident edge
of the other color.  Thus $a_j$ also has degree exactly one.  This sharper
local calculation is consistent with the global conclusion of
\noderef{TwoColorIncidenceDegreeAtMostTwo}, which gives degree at most two
for every node of $I$ under the proper coloring $\rho$.

The four vertex-nodes $x,a_1,a_2,a_3$ are pairwise distinct.  Indeed,
$x\in V(A)$ while every $a_j\notin V(A)$ by (14), and the three outside
ends are pairwise distinct by hypothesis.  Let
\[
 K_x=\noderef{TwoColorComponent}(G,\rho,c,\beta,\operatorname{inl}x),
 \qquad
 K_j=\noderef{TwoColorComponent}(G,\rho,c,\beta,\operatorname{inl}a_j).
\]
Each root belongs to its own full connected component.  Since
$c\ne\beta$ and each of the four displayed roots has at most one neighbor,
the root itself supplies the leaf witness required by
\noderef{TwoColorPathComponent}.  Thus $K_x,K_1,K_2,K_3$ are certified
full two-color path components, not merely the components of the displayed
exit subwalks.

Suppose for contradiction that $K_1=K_x$, $K_2=K_x$, and $K_3=K_x$.
Apply \noderef{TwoColorComponentSpanningPath} to $K_x$.  It supplies a
simple path whose support is all of $K_x$, so all four distinct nodes
$x,a_1,a_2,a_3$ occur on this path.  If any one occurred at an internal
position, its two adjacent path nodes would be distinct neighbors in $I$,
contradicting its degree being one.  Hence all four would have to lie in
the two-element set of the path's endpoints, which is impossible.  We may
therefore choose $j\in\{1,2,3\}$ such that
\[
                              K_j\ne K_x.                       \tag{15}
\]
This alternative asserts no pairwise distinction among $K_1,K_2,K_3$.

Both components in (15) meet $A$.  The component $K_x$ contains its root
$x\in V(A)$.  The walk $\widehat Q_j$ connects $a_j$ to $b_j$, so every node on
that walk is in $K_j$; its endpoint $b_j\in V(A)$ proves that $K_j$ meets
$A$.  Finally apply conclusion (iii) of \noderef{interchange} to
$\mathcal T^\nu$, its PE base $B^\nu$, the maximum property supplied by
\noderef{HierarchyLEMStable}, the complete lower-rung package, the
\noderef{CombinedStableColoring} (13), and the proved $c$-missing and
singleton-$c$-closed properties on $A$.  Its conclusion is formulated on
$B^\nu.\mathit{core}$, which is exactly $A$ by (8), and says that
$c,\beta$ are $A$-interchangeable under $\rho$.  Applying
\noderef{TreeInterchangeable} to the two certified full path components
$K_x,K_j$ and their displayed witnesses in $A$ forces $K_x=K_j$,
contradicting (15).  This completes Subcase 2.2.
\end{proof}

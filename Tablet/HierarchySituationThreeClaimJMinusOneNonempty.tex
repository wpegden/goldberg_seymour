\begin{lemma}[Situation 7.3, Claim \(j-1\): nonempty case]
Fix a rung-local minimum hierarchy counterexample \(C\), set

\[
 j=\noderef{HierarchyPathNumber}(C.\mathit{hierarchy}),\qquad
 \ell=\noderef{HierarchyFullTailLength}(C),
\]

and assume \(2\le j<\ell\).  Assume every vertex has a missing color
under every proper exactly-one-uncolored replacement coloring.  If
\(\noderef{HierarchyEligibleCommonMissingIndices}\) is nonempty, then
there are an actual \noderef{HierarchyReplacementPresentation} \(P\)
and a color \(\alpha\) missing under \(P.\mathit{coloring}\) both at the
terminal vertex and somewhere in the exact prefix \(T(y_{j-2})\), such
that one of the following holds.  Writing \(H=P.H\), \(q\) for its last
stored index, \(D=D_{n,q}\),
\(\Gamma^q=\bigcup_{\eta\in D}P.\mathit{good}.\Gamma^q_\eta\), and
\(M_0\) for the colors missing on
\(P.\mathit{replacementBase}.\mathit{core}-T_n\), either

\[
                       \alpha\notin\Gamma^q\cup M_0,          \tag{A}
\]

or there is an \(\eta\in D\) such that
\(\alpha\in P.\mathit{good}.\Gamma^q_\eta\) and the first vertex missing
\(\eta\) occurs no later than \(y_{j-2}\).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Let \(I\) denote the finite eligible-index set and choose
\(i=\max I\).  Then \(i\in I\), and
\noderef{HierarchySituationThreeEligibleIndexBound} gives

\[
                         1\le i<j.                            \tag{1}
\]

Unfolding membership in \(I\), choose the displayed replacement
presentation \(P\) and color \(\alpha\) missing at both \(y_i\) and the
terminal vertex \(y_\ell\).  Put \(\mu=P.\mathit{coloring}\), write
\(H=P.H\), let \(q\) be its last stored index, and set

\[
 A=\noderef{HierarchyStarredStage}(H,q),\quad
 B=T(y_{j-2}),\quad B^+=T(y_{j-1}),\quad
 D=\noderef{HierarchyStoredColorsAt}(H,q).                    \tag{2}
\]

Here and below the \(T(y_s)\) are the exact
\noderef{HierarchyFullTailPrefix}es.  The
\noderef{SameGoodHierarchy} field of \(P\) identifies \(A\), all these
prefixes, the stored sets, and the Gamma pairs with the original
presentation.  Apply
\noderef{HierarchyCounterexamplePenultimateElementary} to the six
fields of \(P.\mathit{minimumContext}\).  We obtain

\[
              V(T(y_{\ell-1}))\text{ is elementary under }\mu.\tag{3}
\]

Since \(y_i\) lies after \(A\), (3) implies that \(\alpha\) is not
missing on \(A\).  Every color in a terminal Gamma pair is missing on
the corresponding hierarchy stage by the avoidance field of
\noderef{GoodHierarchy} and
\noderef{HierarchyStageMissingColors}; every color in the set \(M_0\)
is missing on the base core outside \(T_n\), which is contained in \(A\)
by \noderef{HierarchyBase} and \noderef{TashkinovHierarchy}.  Therefore

\[
                  \alpha\notin\Gamma^q\cup M_0.              \tag{4}
\]

If \(i\le j-2\), then the witness \(y_i\) belongs to \(B\), so
\(P,\alpha\), and (4) give the required conclusion.  By (1), the only
remaining case is

\[
                             i=j-1.                           \tag{5}
\]

We now make the paper's color choice (2) on the exact prefix \(B\).
Instantiate \noderef{NineN} with the replacement ETT, base, hierarchy,
goodness, the elementary prefix (3), and the large-missing-set field of
\(P.\mathit{minimumContext}\), at index \(j-2\).  The inequalities
\(2\le j<\ell\) verify its index premise.  Unfold its available set,
its tail-used set, and \(M_0\).  If more than four available colors
remain after deleting \(\Gamma^q\cup D\), the one exact edge of
\(B^+-B\) uses at most one of them; choose a remaining color \(\beta\).
Otherwise the second conclusion of \noderef{NineN} supplies seven
distinct stored colors \(\eta_h\in D\cap
\noderef{TreeMissingColors}(\mu,B)\) whose three-color sets

\[
                   P.\mathit{good}.\Gamma^q_{\eta_h}
                              \cup\{\eta_h\}                 \tag{6}
\]

are disjoint from the tail through \(B\).  The Gamma pairs are pairwise
disjoint by \noderef{GoodHierarchy}, and the seven \(\eta_h\) are
distinct.  The single new edge in \(B^+-B\) has one color, so at least
one set (6) also avoids that edge.  Calling its stored color \(\beta\),
we have exactly one of

\[
\begin{split}
 &\beta\in\noderef{TreeMissingColors}(\mu,B),\quad
 \beta\notin M_0\cup\mu\langle B^+-A\rangle
                  \cup(\Gamma^q\cup D),                     \tag{7a}\\
 &\beta=\eta_h\in D\cap\noderef{TreeMissingColors}(\mu,B),
 \quad (P.\mathit{good}.\Gamma^q_{\eta_h}\cup\{\eta_h\})
                  \cap\mu\langle B^+-A\rangle=\varnothing, \tag{7b}
\end{split}
\]

and in (7b) the first \(\eta_h\)-missing position is at most \(|B|\).
This is the exact finite choice in manuscript assertion (2); the use of
the single edge \(B^+-B\) is explicit.

We record a reconstruction argument that will be invoked for each switch
below.  Suppose a proper current replacement coloring \(\psi\) has
\(V(T(y_{\ell-1}))\) elementary, two affected colors have their first
missing witnesses in that prefix, and the component through those
witnesses is disjoint from the component \(Q\) switched at \(y_\ell\).
For \(v\) in the elementary prefix but outside \(Q\), adjacency in
\noderef{TwoColorIncidenceGraph} shows that no affected incident edge is
switched.  If \(v\in Q\), elementarity and component disjointness say
that neither affected color is missing there.  Properness supplies one
incident edge of each affected color; both edge-nodes lie in \(Q\), so
\noderef{TwoColorComponentSwap} exchanges them and fixes every other
incident color.  Thus the full missing-color set is unchanged at every
prefix vertex.  This pointwise proof allows internal vertices of \(Q\)
in the prefix and does not infer prefix avoidance from component
separation.

For the route, inspect the actual terminal
\noderef{TashkinovSeriesStep} supplied by
\(\mathcal E.\mathit{series}.\mathit{certified}
(\operatorname{Fin.last}\mathcal E.\mathit{rounds})\), and use
\noderef{ExtendedTashkinovTree}.\mathit{belowFinal} to place the desired
split prefix below its output.  In PE, retain the terminal-ladder closure
route and use \noderef{HierarchyBase}.parallel for the eligible core and
closure.  In SE/RE, retain the individual connecting edge, its singleton
set, the literal \noderef{TreeSequenceAdjoinEdge} seed, and its route.
At positive divider count the seed lies below \(A\) by
\noderef{HierarchyNonparallelFirstEdge}; at divider zero it is kept as
the source.  Restrict the old route at the split prefix with
\noderef{TashkinovAugmentationSequenceRestrict}, transport each step
using the exact edge equalities and the pointwise missing-set equality,
and concatenate the pieces using the reflexive-transitive definition of
\noderef{TashkinovAugmentationSequence}.  Transport the remaining suffix
by the component swap, since both affected colors are missing at the
split.  Together with equality of all stored first missing positions and
the relevant Gamma cutoff, these are precisely the premises of
\noderef{StableChangeCasesOneTwoSplitTail}.  Its conclusion supplies an
actual corresponding same-rung ETT on \(T\), maximum property, rebuilt
base, hierarchy, goodness, and same-good data, separately in PE,
divider-zero SE/RE, and positive-divider SE/RE.                    \(\tag{8}\)

We finish by distinguishing whether the common color in (5) is stored.
First suppose \(\alpha\notin D\).  Since \(\alpha\) is not missing on
\(A\), the identity
\(D=D_n\setminus\noderef{TreeMissingColors}(\mu,A)\) gives
\(\alpha\notin D_n\).  Equations (4) and (7) exclude \(\alpha,\beta\)
from the exact displayed interval through \(B^+\), including the
divider-zero seed.  Apply \noderef{ChangeLemma} to their first missing
witnesses.  The identified component is disjoint from the component
through \(y_\ell\): equality would put three distinct missing endpoints
in one spanning two-color path, contrary to
\noderef{MissingColorIncidenceEndpoint},
\noderef{TwoColorComponentSpanningPath}, and
\noderef{TwoColorIncidenceDegreeAtMostTwo}.  Switch the terminal
component.  Apply \noderef{StableChangeCasesOneTwo} and then the complete
reconstruction (8), with split \(B^+\).  The terminal switch makes
\(\beta\) missing at \(y_\ell\), while its old missing witness in \(B\)
is in the disjoint component and remains missing.

Package the returned data as a replacement presentation \(P'\).  The
minimum context is rebuilt field by field: properness follows from
\noderef{TwoColorComponentSwapProper}; the uncolored value is fixed;
correspondence transports the complete lower-rung package and the
\(2n+11\) estimate; same-good equality transports the fewer-divider
assertion; and equality of path number, stages, and terminal excess
identifies \noderef{HierarchyCounterexampleMeasure}, transporting
lexicographic minimality.  Alternative (7a) gives (A) for \(\beta\),
while (7b) gives the stated Gamma-pair alternative and its first-position
bound.  Thus \(P',\beta\) prove the claim in the unstored case.

Now suppose \(\alpha=\eta_t\in D\).  Choose the two distinct colors
\(\gamma_1,\gamma_2\) of
\(P.\mathit{good}.\Gamma^q_{\eta_t}\) using the two-colors field of
\noderef{GoodHierarchy}.  Since \(\eta_t\) is missing at \(y_{j-1}\),
Gamma avoidance and \noderef{HierarchyTailMissingColorExclusion} give

\[
 \{\gamma_1,\gamma_2\}\cap\mu\langle B^+-A\rangle=\varnothing,
\]

and \(\eta_t\) itself is absent there except possibly on the exact first
SE/RE seed when \(q=0\).  Apply \noderef{ChangeLemma} to
\((\gamma_1,\eta_t)\); the first witnesses lie in \(A\) and at
\(y_{j-1}\).  Separate their component from the terminal component by
the three-endpoint argument, switch the latter, and apply
\noderef{StableChangeCasesOneTwo} and reconstruction (8).  In the
divider-zero exceptional case, the stable-change terminal-seed clause
fixes the individual seed before route transport.  Call the resulting
presentation \(P_1\) and its coloring \(\mu_1\).  The pointwise argument
above, not component separation alone, shows that all missing sets on
\(T(y_{\ell-1})\) agree with those under \(\mu\).  In particular the first
positions of every stored color agree.  The color \(\gamma_1\) is now
missing at the terminal vertex and still at its witness in \(A\).

Relative to \(P_1\), apply
\noderef{HierarchyCounterexamplePenultimateElementary} again; this
rederives prefix elementarity from \(P_1.\mathit{minimumContext}\).
Equations (7a) or (7b) continue to hold because the first switch fixes
the tail interval and preserves its missing sets pointwise.  Apply
\noderef{ChangeLemma} to \((\gamma_1,\beta)\), separate the terminal
component, and switch it.  The complete reconstruction (8), now using
the actual terminal certificate of \(P_1.\mathit{replacementETT}\),
produces \(P_2\), not merely a coloring.  The switch makes \(\beta\)
missing at \(y_\ell\) and preserves its witness in \(B\).  Rebuild every
field of \(P_2.\mathit{minimumContext}\) exactly as for \(P'\); the
correspondence composition and same-good composition are taken relative
to the original \(P\), so \(P_2\) is a presentation in the original
family \(I\).  Finally (7a) gives (A), and (7b) gives the Gamma-pair and
first-position alternative.  Hence \(P_2,\beta\) prove the claim also in
the stored case.
\end{proof}
